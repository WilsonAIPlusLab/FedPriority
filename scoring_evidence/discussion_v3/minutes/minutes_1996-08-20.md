# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1996-08-20

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Unsegmented pre-2009 format with some mixing of staff and participant content. I will score only participant-attributed language ("members commented," "members observed," "members focused," etc.).

**Era context**: Mid-1990s expansion. Economy growing above trend, unemployment near or below NAIRU estimates, yet inflation surprisingly subdued. The "new economy" period where productivity gains and competitive pressures were keeping inflation low despite tight labor markets.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all but one" (hold policy), "members" (general consensus language used throughout), "a number" (real interest rates not low), "some members" (benefit cost economies), "one" (dissent for tightening). The dominant quantifier is broad "members" language indicating general consensus, with one formal dissenter (Stern).

**Dimension-by-dimension reasoning**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Members note "broad measures of price inflation... did not exhibit any uptrend" and "some price measures suggested inflation had trended lower." However, there is significant discussion of upside risks: "inflation risks in the outlook clearly seemed to be to the upside," labor costs rising, tighter labor markets feeding through. Current inflation is subdued but risks are tilted upward. The center of gravity is: inflation is currently contained but upside risks are clearly perceived. This places it slightly below neutral on current readings but with upward risk perception. Score should be around 48-52 -- current measures are benign but risk perception is elevated. I'll weight the explicit "risks clearly to the upside" language and wage pressures to push this to ~52.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: "Slowing in the growth of economic activity from a very rapid pace during the spring," "high levels of resource use," "growth of the economy continued to outstrip its potential." This is clearly above-potential language. The bright-line rule says above potential = must be >50. The economy was running "very rapid" but is slowing -- members see it as still above potential. Score ~55-58.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Increasing indications of tightness," "essentially full employment conditions," "the rate of unemployment" discussed in relation to labor compensation being below norms but catching up. Unemployment at 5.4%. Members see the labor market as tight with wage pressures building. Score ~58-62.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Very rapid pace during the spring" but slowing. "Large increases in spending for consumer durables, housing, and business equipment" but expected to moderate. "Real consumer spending weakened somewhat." The net picture is above-trend but decelerating. Score ~55-58.

5. **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Generally supportive financial conditions" is the key phrase. Interest rates mentioned as having earlier increases that would slow interest-sensitive sectors. "Real interest rates were not unusually low." Conditions described as supportive, not tight. Score ~35-40 (somewhat loose to neutral, leaning supportive).

6. **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All but one" supported unchanged policy. But the directive included a tightening bias. Members emphasized readiness to tighten if inflation risks materialize. One dissenter (Stern) wanted tightening now. The center of gravity is hold with a tightening bias -- lean hawkish. Score ~55-58.

7. **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal dissent (Stern). The majority was relatively cohesive in holding with a tightening bias. "All but one" supported. This is a standard one-dissent meeting with modest disagreement. Score ~28-32.

8. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Very little discussion of financial stability per se. "Generally supportive financial conditions" is a passing mention. Concern about market overreaction to tightening is tactical, not stability-focused. Consistent with 1993-96 anchor of 1-10. Score ~8.

9. **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The directive has a tightening bias ("somewhat greater reserve restraint would be acceptable"). Members agreed on "desirability of a directive that remained biased toward possible tightening." "Tightening should be implemented promptly if developments were perceived as pointing to rising inflation." This is a mild tightening bias, data-dependent. Score ~60-63.

10. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The extent of the slowing remained uncertain," "unclear at this juncture whether the expansion would slow sufficiently," multiple open questions about wages, productivity, profit margins. Members express meaningful uncertainty about whether inflation will materialize, but the overall tone is not deeply uncertain -- it's more watchful. Score ~38-42.

**Cross-dimension coherence checks**:
- OUTPUT_GAP (56) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (60): Both above 50, consistent. Labor tightness slightly higher because members emphasize it more as the key pressure point.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (56) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (62): Forward guidance slightly more hawkish because the directive explicitly includes a tightening bias, while current action is hold. This divergence makes sense -- hold now but signal readiness to tighten.

**Polarity checks**:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says subdued currently but upside risks. 52 = modestly above neutral. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says above potential but slowing. 56 = modestly above. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says "supportive." 37 = somewhat loose. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 52 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "broad measures of price inflation, adjusted to exclude their volatile food and energy components, did not exhibit any uptrend despite robust growth in economic activity this year and high levels of resource use"
- "the inflation risks in the outlook clearly seemed to be to the upside, with the potential for more inflation stemming from rising labor compensation costs"
- "Upward wage adjustments were becoming more evident and increases in overall compensation had edged up"

**Center-of-gravity**: Members see current inflation as contained but perceive clear upside risks from labor costs and resource pressures, placing the net assessment just slightly above neutral.

**Minority views**: None truly downplaying inflation risk; the one dissenter (Stern) saw even greater inflation risk, pulling the band upward.

---

### OUTPUT_GAP: 56 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "slowing in the growth of economic activity from a very rapid pace during the spring"
- "high levels of resource use"
- "growth of the economy continued to outstrip its potential"

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is operating above potential with robust resource use, though growth is decelerating from a very rapid pace.

**Minority views**: Broad agreement on above-potential operation; uncertainty about whether slowing will be sufficient.

---

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "increasing indications of tightness that might at some point feed through to greater inflation"
- "essentially full employment conditions"
- "acceleration of wage increases this year might well augur faster advances"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is clearly tight with full employment and building wage pressures, though passthrough to prices remains uncertain.

**Minority views**: Broad agreement on tightness; some noted offsetting factors (benefit cost moderation, productivity).

---

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 56 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "much of the stimulus for the strong expansion in the first half of the year had been provided by large increases in spending for consumer durables, housing, and business equipment"
- "growth in such spending could be expected to slow in the context of increasingly satisfied pent-up demands"
- "Real consumer spending weakened somewhat on balance over June and July following several months of robust growth"

**Center-of-gravity**: Spending was very strong in the first half and is now decelerating, but still above trend levels.

**Minority views**: Some upside risk from potential inventory accumulation if final demand stays strong.

---

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 37 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "generally supportive financial conditions" (cited as a factor supporting continued expansion)
- "lagged effects of earlier increases in intermediate- and long-term interest rates on these interest-sensitive sectors"
- "a number also commented that real interest rates were not unusually low"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are characterized as supportive/accommodative -- somewhat loose to neutral, with some drag from earlier rate increases but not tight.

**Minority views**: Limited discussion; no one described conditions as tight.

---

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 56 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all but one of the members supported a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance"
- "they also agreed on the desirability of a directive that remained biased toward possible tightening"
- "any tightening should be implemented promptly if developments were perceived as pointing to rising inflation"
- Mr. Stern dissented favoring "modestly more restrictive" policy

**Center-of-gravity**: Hold with a clear tightening bias. The majority chose patience but signaled readiness to tighten. One dissenter wanted immediate action.

**Minority views**: Stern's dissent for immediate tightening pulls the uncertainty band slightly hawkish.

---

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all but one of the members supported" the hold decision
- "Mr. Stern dissented because he believed that policy should become modestly more restrictive"
- The majority showed broad agreement on rationale (wait and watch for inflation signals)

**Center-of-gravity**: One formal dissent with clear rationale, but the majority is cohesive. Standard one-dissent meeting.

**Minority views**: Stern's dissent was principled but isolated; no other members expressed strong reservations.

---

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 8 +/-4 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "generally supportive financial conditions" (passing contextual mention)
- Concern about "excessive reaction in financial markets" to a tightening move (tactical, not stability-focused)

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability is barely discussed. The brief mentions are contextual rather than substantive. Consistent with mid-1990s low emphasis.

**Minority views**: None.

---

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "directive that remained biased toward possible tightening in the intermeeting period ahead"
- "tightening should be implemented promptly if developments were perceived as pointing to rising inflation"
- "the Committee remained committed to a policy that would resist a rise in inflation"

**Center-of-gravity**: Mild tightening bias with data-dependent posture. The forward signal is clearly in the tightening direction but conditional on incoming data.

**Minority views**: Stern wanted immediate action; majority preferred conditional forward lean.

---

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 40 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The extent of the slowing remained uncertain, and it was unclear at this juncture whether the expansion would slow sufficiently"
- "Improvements in productivity were likely to offset part of such increases, but how much remained an open question"
- "the extent to which they might narrow to absorb increasing labor costs was difficult to predict"

**Center-of-gravity**: Members express somewhat elevated uncertainty about the growth-inflation tradeoff, particularly whether the slowdown will be sufficient and whether wage pressures will translate to prices. Multiple open questions identified, but not deep structural uncertainty.

**Minority views**: Broad agreement on the uncertainty itself.
