# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2006-05-10

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format, but participant language cues are clear throughout ("participants saw," "members believed," "some participants noted," etc.). Staff content is minimal/absent in this extract.

**Era context**: May 2006 -- late in the tightening cycle that began in June 2004. The economy was growing strongly, housing was beginning to cool, and inflation concerns were rising. The fed funds rate had been raised at 16 consecutive meetings. This meeting raised rates another 25bp.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all members" (25bp appropriate), "most participants" (core inflation to remain near recent levels), "some participants" (housing cooling, spending slowing, price concern), "several participants" (business investment robust, core inflation at upper end of acceptable range), "a number of factors" (upside inflation risks). No "a few" or "one or two" language appears prominently.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as somewhat above comfort zone. "Core consumer inflation lately had been a little higher than expected." "Core inflation was now around the upper end of what they viewed as an acceptable range." Energy prices surging, commodity prices climbing, dollar declining. But participants also cite restraining factors: moderate compensation growth, wide profit margins, productivity gains. "Most participants expected... core inflation would probably remain around the levels experienced on average over the past year." Upside risks to inflation had "risen somewhat." Direction: modestly above target with rising risks. Score should be in the 58-65 range. Polarity check: higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says elevated with upside risks. Matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "The economy appeared to be operating at a relatively high level of resource utilization." GDP grew at ~5% annual rate in Q1. "The expansion appeared likely to moderate, it evidently remained solid." "Pressures on productive resources would most likely continue to be limited" if growth moderates. Direction: above potential currently but expected to moderate. The "relatively high level of resource utilization" language places this above 50. Score ~55-58. Polarity check: higher = more above potential. Matches.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Business contacts had reported continued shortages of certain types of skilled labor and related wage pressures in some occupations." But "growth of total compensation on balance appeared to remain moderate." Selective tightness in skilled labor but overall compensation moderate. Direction: somewhat tight, around 55-58. Polarity check: higher = tighter. Evidence says selective shortages but moderate overall wages. Matches.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending "likely to slow," housing cooling. But business investment "robust," nonresidential construction "accelerating notably," equipment/software "expanding at a solid rate." "Consumption spending was viewed as most likely to expand at a moderate pace." Net: above trend but moderating. Score ~55-58. Polarity check: higher = stronger. Evidence says solid but moderating from very strong. Matches.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Ready availability of financing on attractive terms" for business investment. Nontraditional mortgage products mentioned but "household sector seemed likely to remain in sound financial condition overall." "Recent rise in longer-term interest rates" noted as a potential drag. Conditions still relatively loose despite cumulative tightening. Score ~30-35 (somewhat loose). Polarity check: higher = tighter. Evidence suggests still fairly loose conditions despite rate hikes. Score in low range matches.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All members agreed on 25bp hike. Discussion ranged from hold to +50bp, but all settled on +25bp. "The Committee decided to repeat the indication... that some further policy firming could be required." But "members were uncertain about how much, if any, further tightening would be needed." This is a hawkish action (another hike) with an increasingly uncertain forward lean. Score ~65-68. Polarity check: higher = more hawkish. Evidence: unanimous hike, retain tightening bias but with growing uncertainty. Matches.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All members believed that an additional 25 basis point firming of policy was appropriate." Discussion ranged from hold to 50bp, indicating some spread, but the final action was unanimous. "Members debated the appropriate characterization of inflation expectations." Broad agreement on action with moderate differences on outlook. Score ~22-28. Polarity check: higher = more disagreement. Unanimous action but range of discussion. Low-moderate disagreement. Matches.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Housing cooling discussed as economic sector issue, not stability concern. Nontraditional mortgage products mentioned briefly ("potential to cause financial difficulties for some households"). Some discussion of asset prices (housing), equity not discussed. Financial conditions mentioned in passing. Moderate but not dominant. Score ~35-40. Polarity check: higher = more attention. Some housing-related financial mentions but not a primary focus. Matches.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Some further policy firming could be required" retained from March. But "the extent and timing of any such firming will depend importantly on the evolution of the economic outlook." Members "uncertain about how much, if any, further tightening would be needed." This is a mild tightening bias with heavy data dependence -- moving toward potential pause. Score ~62-65. Polarity check: higher = more hawkish forward signal. Retain tightening bias but emphasize conditionality. Matches.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Members were uncertain about how much, if any, further tightening would be needed." "Whether economic growth would moderate to a sustainable pace was not yet clear." Upside risks to inflation, downside risks to growth. Inflation expectations discussion shows uncertainty. But this is not extreme uncertainty -- more "somewhat elevated." Score ~40-45. Polarity check: higher = more uncertainty. Genuine uncertainty about path forward. Matches.

### Cross-dimension coherence:

- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (66) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (63): Both moderately hawkish, with forward guidance slightly less hawkish due to "if any" language. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 62 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Core consumer inflation lately had been a little higher than expected."
- "Several members remarked that core inflation was now around the upper end of what they viewed as an acceptable range."
- "Recent developments suggested that upside risks to inflation had risen somewhat since the time of the March meeting."

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants perceive inflation as modestly above comfort levels with rising upside risks from energy, commodities, and a weaker dollar, though restraining factors (moderate wages, productivity, wide margins) provide some offset.

**Minority views**: Most expected core inflation to remain near recent levels (tempering concern), but the risk distribution was tilted upward. Uncertainty band reflects the range between those emphasizing restraining factors and those stressing upside risks.

---

### OUTPUT_GAP: 56 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The economy appeared to be operating at a relatively high level of resource utilization."
- "Real GDP increasing at around a 5 percent annual rate" in Q1.
- "Pressures on productive resources would most likely continue to be limited" if growth moderates as expected.

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is running modestly above potential with strong recent growth, though expected moderation should limit further resource pressure.

**Minority views**: Some saw downside risks to growth (housing, cumulative policy tightening), while the strong Q1 GDP print suggested even higher utilization. Narrow band reflects broad agreement.

---

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 56 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Business contacts had reported continued shortages of certain types of skilled labor and related wage pressures in some occupations."
- "Growth of total compensation on balance appeared to remain moderate."

**Center-of-gravity**: Selective tightness in skilled labor categories with wage pressures in some occupations, but aggregate compensation growth remains moderate, indicating a labor market that is tight but not overheating.

**Minority views**: Limited divergence discussed. Band reflects uncertainty between pockets of tightness and moderate aggregate wages.

---

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 56 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Several participants remarked that business investment spending was robust."
- "Consumption spending was viewed as most likely to expand at a moderate pace in coming quarters."
- "Nonresidential construction was accelerating notably."

**Center-of-gravity**: Business investment is robust and nonresidential construction is accelerating, while consumer spending is expected to grow at a moderate pace with housing cooling acting as a drag. Net position: somewhat above trend.

**Minority views**: Consumer side faces headwinds (housing, energy, rates) that some participants emphasized more than others.

---

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 32 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Capital formation was likely to continue to be supported by... ready availability of financing on attractive terms."
- "Certain features of recently popular nontraditional mortgage products had the potential to cause financial difficulties for some households."
- "The cumulative effect of past monetary policy actions and the recent rise in longer-term interest rates" could weigh on activity.

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions remain loose despite 16 rate hikes. Credit is freely available on attractive terms. Longer-term rates have risen but have not significantly tightened conditions. Consistent with the 2005-06 era anchor of 10-20, though somewhat tighter given cumulative hikes.

**Minority views**: Some recognized emerging risks in nontraditional mortgages, but the dominant view was that conditions were supportive.

---

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 66 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All members believed that an additional 25 basis point firming of policy was appropriate today."
- "The Committee discussed policy approaches ranging from leaving the stance of policy unchanged at this meeting to increasing the federal funds rate 50 basis points."
- "Members were uncertain about how much, if any, further tightening would be needed after today's action."

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous support for another 25bp hike with retained tightening bias, but growing uncertainty about whether more tightening is needed. Moderately hawkish with some conditional softening.

**Minority views**: The range from "hold" to "+50bp" indicates meaningful internal debate about the appropriate pace, even though all converged on +25bp. The "if any" language signals a contingent of members closer to pausing.

---

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 25 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All members believed that an additional 25 basis point firming of policy was appropriate."
- "The Committee discussed policy approaches ranging from leaving the stance of policy unchanged at this meeting to increasing the federal funds rate 50 basis points."
- "Members debated the appropriate characterization of inflation expectations in the statement."

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous vote on action with broad agreement on assessment, though the range of policy approaches considered (hold to +50bp) and debate on statement language reveal moderate underlying differences.

**Minority views**: No formal dissent. Differences were about magnitude and forward path, not fundamental direction.

---

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 37 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Certain features of recently popular nontraditional mortgage products had the potential to cause financial difficulties for some households and erode mortgage loan performance for some lenders."
- "Some cooling of housing markets" discussed at length as an economic and potential financial issue.
- "Ready availability of financing on attractive terms" -- implicit loose-conditions awareness.

**Center-of-gravity**: Housing market dynamics received meaningful attention, including nascent financial concerns about nontraditional mortgages. But financial stability was not a primary focus -- it was secondary to inflation and growth assessment. Consistent with the 2006-07 pre-crisis anchor range.

**Minority views**: Limited.

---

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 63 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The Committee decided to repeat the indication... that some further policy firming could be required."
- "The extent and timing of any such firming will depend importantly on the evolution of the economic outlook as implied by incoming information."
- "Members were uncertain about how much, if any, further tightening would be needed."

**Center-of-gravity**: Retained mild tightening bias but added strong conditionality language. The "if any" phrasing signals the Committee is approaching the end of the tightening cycle and may pause, softening the forward guidance relative to previous meetings.

**Minority views**: Some members likely favored a stronger tightening signal (given inflation risks), while others preferred signaling a potential pause.

---

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 43 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Members were uncertain about how much, if any, further tightening would be needed after today's action."
- "Whether economic growth would moderate to a sustainable pace was not yet clear."
- "It was possible, though, that investors' uncertainty regarding inflation prospects, not just inflation expectations themselves, had risen."

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants express genuine uncertainty about the economic outlook, particularly whether growth will moderate as expected, the inflation trajectory, and how much more tightening is needed. This is somewhat elevated above normal but not extreme.

**Minority views**: The range of policy options discussed (hold to +50bp) itself reflects meaningful disagreement about the appropriate response to uncertainty.

---

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 57 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Inflation expectations had risen somewhat--a development that would have to be taken into account in policymaking and warranted close monitoring--but remained contained."
- "Some surveys suggested that inflation expectations had risen in recent weeks, but others implied that expectations were little changed."
- "Measures of inflation compensation based on the difference between yields on nominal Treasury securities and inflation-indexed issues had edged higher."

**Center-of-gravity**: Expectations have drifted up modestly but remain anchored within recent ranges. The rise is enough to warrant monitoring and inform policy but not alarming.

---

### CREDIT_STRESS: 28 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Ready availability of financing on attractive terms."
- "Nontraditional mortgage products had the potential to cause financial difficulties."

**Center-of-gravity**: Credit conditions are loose with easy availability. Nascent concerns about mortgage product risk but no stress evident.

---

### PRODUCTIVITY: 58 +/-5 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Relatively wide profit margins could buffer the effect on prices... this might be accomplished in part through further productivity gains, which had remained fairly strong on balance in recent quarters."

**Center-of-gravity**: Productivity growth perceived as somewhat above historical trend, serving as a buffer against cost pressures.
