# FOMC Meeting Scoring — 2006-03-28 (Minutes, Independent)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Unsegmented (pre-2009), so I must focus on participant-attributed language ("meeting participants," "members," "several," "some," "a few") and ignore staff-attributed content.

**Era context**: March 2006 — late in the "measured pace" tightening cycle that began June 2004. The economy is near full employment, housing is starting to cool, and the FOMC is approaching the end of its rate-hiking campaign. The funds rate was raised to 4.75% at this meeting.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all members" (raise 25bp, retain risk language), "most members" (end of tightening likely near), "several" (labor market strengthening; housing cooling; concern about market misinterpretation), "some" (surprise at limited pass-through; concern about tightening too much; inflation expectations at upper end). The language shows broad consensus with nuanced differences at the margin.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly contained but with upside risks. Core inflation "flat or even a bit lower by some measures." "Underlying inflation was not in the process of moving higher." BUT "some participants held that core inflation and inflation expectations were already toward the upper end of the range consistent with price stability." Energy prices remain "elevated." The center of gravity is: inflation is near but slightly above comfort, with upside risks acknowledged. Direction: slightly above neutral. Score in the 53-58 range.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Current levels of labor and product market resource utilization were in a zone consistent with little or no remaining economic slack." "The economy could well be producing in the neighborhood of its sustainable potential." This is textbook "at potential" or slightly above. The bright-line rule says "at potential" = 48-52, but "little or no remaining slack" nudges slightly above 50. Score ~52-54.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "The labor market had continued to strengthen." "Now showing some signs of tightness, consistent with a relatively low jobless rate." "Anecdotal reports of shortages of skilled labor in a few sectors." But "participants expressed uncertainty about how much slack remained" and compensation growth is "still-moderate." Direction: modestly tight, near full employment. Score ~54-57.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "growing at a solid pace." "Contacts in the retail sector reported strong demand." Business capital expenditures had "considerable momentum." Nonresidential construction picking up. Housing cooling is a drag but the expansion is "broad-based." Net: above trend. Score ~58-62.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Financial market conditions remained supportive of growth, with long-term rates relatively low, risk spreads in corporate debt markets narrow, and banks seeking lending opportunities." M&A strong, private equity rapid. This is clearly loose/accommodative. Score ~20-25.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All favored 25bp hike. "Most members thought that the end of the tightening process was likely to be near." "Some expressed concerns about the dangers of tightening too much." But also "checking upside risks to inflation was important." This is a hike at the late stage of the cycle — moderately hawkish but with signals of approaching the end. Score ~63-66.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: All voted for 25bp hike. "Members expressed some difference in views about the appropriate level of detail to include in the statement." There's nuance on communication but no formal dissents and broad agreement on action. Score ~22-28.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Housing cooling discussed at length but more as an economic variable than a financial stability risk. Financial conditions mentioned as "supportive." No real discussion of systemic risks, bubbles, or financial vulnerabilities per se. The housing discussion has financial stability undertones but is framed as an economic outlook issue. Score ~35-40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Most members thought that the end of the tightening process was likely to be near." Retained "some further policy firming may be needed" but with concern it could be "misconstrued as suggesting several further tightening steps." This signals a mild tightening bias with the end in sight — the forward signal is hawkish but fading. Score ~62-67.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The magnitude of the adjustment [in housing] and its effects on household spending were hard to predict." "Participants expressed uncertainty about how much slack remained." Some upside and downside risks noted. But overall the tone is fairly confident. Score ~35-42.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (~53) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (~55): Both modestly above 50, consistent with "at or slightly above potential" and "some signs of tightness." Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (~65) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (~64): Both in the moderate hawkish zone, with the forward tone suggesting the cycle is near its end. Coherent.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (~22) is loose, which is consistent with the era (pre-crisis ease, anchor 10-20 for 2005-06). My score of ~22 is at the upper edge of that anchor, reasonable given some tightening effects noted.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says "not in the process of moving higher" but "upper end of range." Modestly above neutral. Score above 50 is correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. "Little or no remaining slack" = at/slightly above. Score ~53 correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Conditions are "supportive" / loose. Score ~22 correct (low = loose).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 55 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Data over the intermeeting period, including measures of inflation expectations, suggested that underlying inflation was not in the process of moving higher."
- "Some participants held that core inflation and inflation expectations were already toward the upper end of the range that they viewed as consistent with price stability, making them particularly vigilant about upside risks to inflation."
- "With energy prices remaining high, and prices of some other commodities continuing to rise, the risk of at least a temporary impact on core inflation remained a concern."

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants see inflation as roughly contained near target but with meaningful upside risks from energy prices and resource utilization; "some" view it as already at the upper end of acceptable.

**Minority views**: Some see inflation expectations already uncomfortably high (would push score higher); others are surprised at limited pass-through (suggesting pass-through risk is lower than feared).

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 53 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Current levels of labor and product market resource utilization were in a zone consistent with little or no remaining economic slack."
- "The economy could well be producing in the neighborhood of its sustainable potential."
- "The economy seemed to be on track to grow near a sustainable pace."

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants view the economy as operating at or very slightly above potential — essentially in the neighborhood of balance with a slight positive tilt.

**Minority views**: Some see robust non-residential investment and foreign growth as potential upside, while housing is a potential downside.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 55 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The labor market was now showing some signs of tightness, consistent with a relatively low jobless rate."
- "There were anecdotal reports of shortages of skilled labor in a few sectors, such as health care, technology, and finance."
- "Participants expressed uncertainty about how much slack remained. Pressures on unit labor costs appeared contained."

**Center-of-gravity**: The labor market is modestly tight — showing "some signs of tightness" with scattered shortages, but compensation growth remains moderate and unit labor costs are contained.

**Minority views**: Uncertainty about remaining slack creates a moderate band.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 59 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending appeared to be growing at a solid pace... Contacts in the retail sector reported strong demand."
- "Business capital expenditures, especially on equipment and software, appeared to have considerable momentum, supported by strong corporate profits, exceptionally liquid balance sheets, and greater business optimism."
- "The economic expansion appeared to be broad-based."

**Center-of-gravity**: Spending is above trend — consumer demand is "solid," business investment has "considerable momentum," and the expansion is "broad-based." Housing cooling provides a partial offset but does not dominate.

**Minority views**: Housing slowdown and auto sector weakness partially offset the strong readings; growth expected to "moderate to a more sustainable pace."

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 22 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Financial market conditions remained supportive of growth, with long-term rates relatively low, risk spreads in corporate debt markets narrow, and banks seeking lending opportunities."
- "Merger and acquisition activity was strong and infusions of private equity continued at a rapid pace."
- "The effects on spending of the substantial increase in short- and intermediate-term rates since June 2004 had probably not yet been fully felt."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly loose and accommodative despite 21 months of rate hikes. Spreads are narrow, banks are actively lending, and M&A/private equity are booming.

**Minority views**: Some note ARM rate increases weighing on housing and suggest full effects of past tightening not yet felt, which nudges slightly toward neutral.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 65 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All members favored raising the target federal funds rate 25 basis points to 4¾ percent at this meeting."
- "Most members thought that the end of the tightening process was likely to be near, and some expressed concerns about the dangers of tightening too much."
- "Keeping rates unchanged would run an unacceptable risk of rising inflation."

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous 25bp hike with moderate hawkish lean — still tightening but clearly approaching the end of the cycle. The action is hawkish but the forward posture is increasingly cautious.

**Minority views**: "Some" worried about overtightening (dovish pull); others remained focused on inflation upside risks (hawkish pull).

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 25 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- All members voted for the 25bp hike (unanimous).
- "Members expressed some difference in views about the appropriate level of detail to include in the statement."
- "Several members were concerned that market participants might not fully appreciate the extent to which future policy action will depend on incoming economic data."

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on the rate action with no formal dissents, but minor differences on communication strategy and the forward outlook. This is typical one-dissent-or-fewer territory.

**Minority views**: Differences are about communication, not the underlying economic assessment or policy action.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 38 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Extended discussion of housing market cooling (inventories, cancellations, house prices), with financial conditions implications.
- "Risk spreads in corporate debt markets narrow, and banks seeking lending opportunities" — noted but not flagged as a concern.
- "An abrupt rise in long-term interest rates... could weigh on both household and business spending."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are discussed but primarily in terms of their implications for growth and inflation, not as a standalone stability concern. The housing discussion has some financial stability flavor but is framed as an economic variable. Moderate attention but not a dominant theme.

**Minority views**: Limited — most discussion is about financial conditions supporting growth rather than posing risks.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 64 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Most members thought that the end of the tightening process was likely to be near."
- Retained "some further policy firming may be needed to keep the risks... roughly in balance."
- "Some members expressed concern that retention of the phrase... could be misconstrued as suggesting that the Committee thought that several further tightening steps were likely to be necessary."

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is mildly hawkish — retaining a tightening bias — but clearly signaling that the cycle is near its end. The guidance leans hawkish but with diminishing conviction.

**Minority views**: Some wanted to soften the forward language more, others were comfortable with the existing phrasing.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 38 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The economy's sustainable potential output could not be observed directly or estimated with precision."
- "The magnitude of the adjustment [in housing] and its effects on household spending were hard to predict."
- "Participants expressed uncertainty about how much slack remained."

**Center-of-gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty — participants acknowledge genuine difficulty in assessing housing trajectory and remaining slack, but overall the tone is fairly confident about the broad outlook. This is above routine hedging but not deeply uncertain.

**Minority views**: Housing uncertainty is the main source of dispersion.
