# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1999-10-05

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is **minutes** (synthesized summary of participant views). It is unsegmented (pre-2009), so I must score only portions with participant language cues ("members noted," "members commented," "several observed") and ignore staff-attributable content.

### Era Context
October 1999: Late 1990s expansion/boom. The Fed had raised rates by 25bp in June 1999 after holding through the Asian/LTCM crisis. Economy growing rapidly, productivity acceleration narrative in play, labor markets very tight, but core inflation still subdued. Y2K concerns are present. This meeting resulted in no rate change but adopted an asymmetric directive biased toward restraint.

### Quantifier Distribution
- "all the members" — favored/could accept unchanged policy
- "most members" — viewed some increase in core inflation as a definite possibility; concluded to continue announcement practice
- "a majority" — favored directive biased toward restraint
- "a number of members" — emphasized benign price behavior
- "several members" — hearing fewer comments about inability to raise prices
- "some members" — saw possibility of steeper dollar drop; some pickup from subdued inflation possible
- "other members" — uncertain about timing of firming, preferred more open future course

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as currently subdued but with rising risks. "Most measures of inflation had remained subdued" but "members generally saw some risk of rising inflation going forward." Energy prices up sharply, import price declines ebbing, health care costs rising. However, "labor cost increases had not turned up and core inflation continued to edge lower." The center of gravity is: inflation is currently low but risks are tilting up. This is below-target-but-approaching territory. Direction: slightly below midpoint but with upward tilt. Score ~45-48. The language is not "elevated" — it's "subdued" with risks.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "The expansion had been considerably stronger in recent months than many had anticipated." "A level of resource use that based on the historical record exceeded the economy's sustainable capacity—perhaps by even more than at present." "Already high level of economic activity." This is clearly above-potential language. Must be >50 per bright-line rule. The language says "exceeded sustainable capacity" — this is firmly above potential. Score ~58-62.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Tight labor markets and rising labor costs" mentioned. "Upward pressures on labor costs could at some point outpace gains in productivity." "Rising healthcare benefit costs and possible increases in the minimum wage." Labor markets described as tight, consistent with late 1990s era. But labor cost increases "had not turned up" yet despite tightness. Score ~65-68.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumer spending... was continuing to rise at a brisk pace." "Business fixed investment appeared to have accelerated to a surprising extent in the third quarter from an already robust pace." "Continued high levels of activity in all regions." This is clearly above-trend, strong/robust language. Score ~63-67.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Somewhat less accommodative conditions in financial markets, including the increases that had occurred in interest rates over the past several months and the steadying of stock market prices." "Financing became less attractive in response to the rise that had occurred in market interest rates." Conditions have tightened somewhat from very loose levels. This is moving toward neutral/slightly tight. Score ~50-55.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All favored unchanged stance at this meeting. But "a majority favored associating an unchanged policy stance with a directive that was biased toward restraint." "They believed that the Committee probably would need to move to a less accommodative policy stance in the relatively near future, possibly at the November meeting." This is hold-with-tightening-bias. Score ~58-62.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: All favored or could accept unchanged policy. Disagreement was about the asymmetric directive: "a majority" favored it, "other members... could accept" it though preferring more openness. No formal dissents. Minor tactical differences. Score ~20-25.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Stock market mentioned briefly (pause in stock market, steadying of prices). Dollar discussed by "some members" regarding potential steep drop. Y2K concerns mentioned. Not a central topic. Score ~20-28.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "A majority... believed that the Committee probably would need to move to a less accommodative policy stance in the relatively near future, possibly at the November meeting." Clear tightening bias in the forward signal. The asymmetric directive biased toward restraint is explicitly a hawkish signal. Score ~65-70.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "A great deal of uncertainty surrounded the behavior of productivity growth going forward." "Considerable uncertainty surrounded expectations of rising inflation." "Substantial uncertainty surrounded the outlook for aggregate supply and aggregate demand going forward." Multiple uses of "considerable" and "substantial" uncertainty. Score ~50-58.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (60) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (67): Both above 50, consistent — late 1990s boom.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (60) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (67): Aligned — hold now but tightening bias forward. Forward guidance slightly more hawkish than current lean, which makes sense given they held but signaled likely November move.

### Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says "subdued" with upward risks. 47 = slightly below midpoint. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says "exceeded sustainable capacity." 60 = clearly above. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says "somewhat less accommodative" conditions. 52 = slightly tighter than neutral. Matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 47 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Most measures of inflation had remained subdued."
- "Members generally saw some risk of rising inflation going forward, but they also recognized that similar forecasts in recent years had proved wrong and that considerable uncertainty surrounded expectations of somewhat higher core inflation."
- "Labor cost increases had not turned up and core inflation continued to edge lower."

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation currently subdued but members see upward risks from energy, ebbing import price declines, and tight labor markets. The "subdued" present condition pulls the score below 50 while forward risks pull it toward 50.

**Minority views**: "A number of members emphasized that the behavior of prices had remained surprisingly benign for an extended period" — these members are more sanguine, pulling the band lower.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 60 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The expansion had been considerably stronger in recent months than many had anticipated."
- "A level of resource use that based on the historical record exceeded the economy's sustainable capacity—perhaps by even more than at present."
- "Expanding aggregate supply, boosted by accelerating productivity, had remained in reasonable balance with rapidly growing aggregate demand despite an already high level of economic activity."

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy clearly above potential, with "considerably stronger" growth than anticipated. The productivity acceleration narrative tempers alarm somewhat — supply is partially keeping up — but the level of resource use is described as exceeding sustainable capacity.

**Minority views**: The third quote suggests some members see more balance due to productivity gains, pulling the band slightly lower.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 67 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Tight labor markets and rising labor costs."
- "Upward pressures on labor costs could at some point outpace gains in productivity."
- "Labor compensation would come under greater pressures as a result of rising healthcare benefit costs and possible increases in the minimum wage."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market described as tight with rising costs, though productivity gains have so far contained unit labor cost increases. Consistent with late-1990s tight labor market conditions.

**Minority views**: Limited; broad agreement on tightness.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 65 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending, notably for light motor vehicles, was continuing to rise at a brisk pace."
- "Business fixed investment appeared to have accelerated to a surprising extent in the third quarter from an already robust pace earlier in the year."
- "Continued high levels of activity in all regions and few indications of moderating growth."

**Center-of-gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment are well above trend. "Brisk," "accelerated," "robust," "surprising extent" all point to strong above-trend spending. Some expected moderation ahead tempers slightly.

**Minority views**: Nonresidential construction outside tech was "relatively sluggish," pulling the band slightly lower.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 52 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Somewhat less accommodative conditions in financial markets, including the increases that had occurred in interest rates over the past several months and the steadying of stock market prices."
- "Financing became less attractive in response to the rise that had occurred in market interest rates."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions have moved from accommodative toward neutral. Interest rates have risen, stock market has leveled off, but no language suggesting stress or significantly tight conditions. Slightly above neutral.

**Minority views**: Limited explicit discussion of financial conditions beyond these references.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 60 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All the members indicated that they favored or could accept an unchanged policy stance."
- "A majority of the members favored associating an unchanged policy stance with a directive that was biased toward restraint."
- "They believed that the Committee probably would need to move to a less accommodative policy stance in the relatively near future, possibly at the November meeting."

**Center-of-gravity**: Hold with clear tightening bias. A majority expect tightening soon. No one is advocating easing. This is a lean-hawkish hold.

**Minority views**: "Other members... were quite uncertain about the timing of any additional firming... preferred to leave the Committee's possible future course of action more open." These members still accepted the asymmetric directive, so the disagreement is on timing not direction.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 22 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All the members indicated that they favored or could accept an unchanged policy stance."
- "A majority of the members favored... a directive that was biased toward restraint... Other members... could accept an asymmetric directive in light of the consensus."
- Disagreement was only about directive tilt and timing, not fundamental direction.

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on holding rates, with minor differences on the tilt of the directive and timing of future action. No formal dissents. "Other members" could accept the majority view.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 25 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The pause in the stock market, should it persist, and the attendant effects on financial wealth were expected with some lag to damp further gains in consumer expenditures."
- "Some members saw the possibility of a steeper drop in the dollar... as an added source of risk."
- Brief mention of Y2K concerns and market volatility from announcement practices.

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions mentioned as context for growth outlook (stock market wealth effects, interest rates) and dollar risks, but not as a standalone stability concern. Limited, contextual discussion.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 67 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "They believed that the Committee probably would need to move to a less accommodative policy stance in the relatively near future, possibly at the November meeting."
- "An asymmetrical directive would help convey the message that policy adjustments might not yet be completed for the balance of this year."
- "The Committee remained concerned about potential inflationary developments in coming months."

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear tightening signal looking forward. The asymmetric directive is explicitly intended to signal likely near-term tightening. This is a mild-to-moderate tightening bias in forward guidance.

**Minority views**: Some members wanted to keep future options more open, but even they accepted the signal.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "A great deal of uncertainty surrounded the behavior of productivity growth going forward."
- "Considerable uncertainty surrounded expectations of somewhat higher core inflation."
- "Substantial uncertainty surrounded the outlook for aggregate supply and aggregate demand going forward."

**Center-of-gravity**: Multiple explicit references to "considerable" and "substantial" uncertainty, primarily around whether productivity acceleration would continue and whether inflation would actually rise. This is elevated uncertainty, above normal hedging.

**Minority views**: Some members were more confident inflation would remain benign; others more confident it would rise.
