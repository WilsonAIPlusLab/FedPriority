# FOMC Meeting Scoring — 2020-01-29 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented — participant discussion section is clearly separated from staff review sections. Uses standard quantifier language ("most," "many," "several," "some," "a few," "a couple").

**Era context**: January 2020, just before COVID-19 hit. The U.S. economy was in a late-cycle expansion with strong labor market, moderate GDP growth, and inflation running persistently below the 2% target. The Fed had cut rates three times in 2019 ("mid-cycle adjustment") and was on hold. Trade tensions had eased somewhat with the phase-one China deal. The coronavirus had just emerged as a new risk but was not yet a dominant concern.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants note overall and core PCE "continued to run below 2 percent." They "generally expected inflation to move closer to 2 percent" but it had not yet arrived. "A few participants expressed less confidence" that inflation would return to 2%. Some alternative measures like trimmed mean were at 2%, but center of gravity is clearly below target. Direction: below target. Magnitude: "somewhat below" — not dramatically low, just persistently short of 2%. Score range: 38-42.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants say economy is growing at a "moderate rate," labor market is "strong," unemployment at "50-year low." The staff forecast says output is "a rate a little above the staff's estimate of its potential rate of growth" and unemployment "below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." A few participants observed "actual level of employment might still be below maximum employment." The center of gravity treats the economy as at or modestly above potential. Direction: near/slightly above potential. Score range: 50-54.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at "50-year low," "continued strong labor demand," "difficulties in finding qualified workers," "tightness in the labor market." However, aggregate wage measures "continued to rise at a moderate pace" — not explosively. Several participants noted "absence of stronger broad-based wage pressures" and potential for labor force participation to rise further. The labor market is clearly tight but not generating major inflationary pressures. Direction: tight. Score range: 57-63.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Split picture. Consumer spending: "moderated in the fourth quarter" but expected to remain "on a firm footing." Residential investment: "significant pickup." Business investment: "remained weak," manufacturing "declined over the past year." Exports "remained weak." Net: consumption solid but not booming, investment weak. The overall picture is moderate/mixed. Direction: near trend but mixed. Score range: 46-50.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Financial conditions described as "accommodative" and "more accommodative over the intermeeting period." Equity prices rose notably. Corporate borrowing costs "near historical lows." Financing conditions "remained supportive of spending." Mortgage rates declined. This is clearly loose/accommodative. Direction: loose. Score range: 25-32.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Participants "judged that it would be appropriate to maintain the target range." The "current stance of policy as likely to remain appropriate for a time." They are on hold with no lean to tighten. Several want inflation to overshoot 2% modestly. A few think employment may still be below maximum. The overall lean is hold-steady with a slight dovish bias (willingness to let inflation run above 2%, no rush to tighten). Direction: balanced to slightly dovish. Score range: 45-50.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Broad agreement on holding rates. No formal dissents indicated. Some differences of emphasis — "a few" see less confidence in inflation returning to 2%, "a couple" worry about financial imbalances from tight labor markets, "several" skeptical about limited impact of trade deal. These are minor emphasis differences, not fundamental disagreements. Direction: broad agreement. Score range: 18-25.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Substantial discussion. There is a dedicated section on financial stability where "several participants observed that equity, corporate debt, and CRE valuations were elevated," concerns about "overvaluation and excessive indebtedness," bank capital buffers, cyber attacks. Additionally, the first part of the meeting was a special briefing/discussion on monetary policy and financial stability interactions. This is more than typical — financial stability was a prominent topic at this meeting. Direction: moderate-to-prominent. Score range: 50-60.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Current stance of policy as likely to remain appropriate for a time." No signal of either tightening or easing. Pure data-dependent hold. Market expectations were for ~30bp of cuts over the year, but participants did not endorse that. Several participants wanted inflation to modestly exceed 2%. The forward signal is neutral with perhaps a slight dovish lean. Direction: neutral. Score range: 45-50.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Uncertainties about the outlook remained, including those posed by the outbreak of the coronavirus." Distribution of risks "somewhat more favorable than at the previous meeting, although a number of downside risks remained prominent." Trade uncertainty "to remain somewhat elevated." Coronavirus "warranted close watching." This is moderate uncertainty — somewhat elevated but reduced from prior meetings. Direction: somewhat elevated. Score range: 38-45.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (~52) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (~60): Labor market is tighter than the output gap suggests, which is consistent with the text — labor market is very strong while business investment is weak, creating a mixed GDP picture. Plausible divergence.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (~48) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (~48): Both neutral with slight dovish lean. Consistent.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (~28) being loose while HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (~48) is neutral-dovish: Consistent — loose conditions are partly why they can afford to hold steady.

### Quantifier distribution:
- "Participants agreed/generally judged" — used for main conclusions (hold rates, strong labor market, moderate growth)
- "Many" — trade deal effects on business confidence, residential investment pickup, strong labor demand, wage moderation
- "Several" — difficulties finding workers, trade deal skeptics, inflation overshooting, financial stability concerns
- "Some" — upbeat consumer surveys, trade uncertainties diminished, financial imbalances
- "A few" — less confidence in inflation returning to 2%, employment still below maximum, energy/agriculture challenges

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 40 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "recent readings on overall and core PCE price inflation, measured on a 12-month basis, had continued to run below 2 percent"
- "Participants generally expected inflation to move closer to 2 percent in the coming months" but "a few participants expressed less confidence in this outlook"
- "a couple of participants...noted that some alternative inflation indicators, including trimmed mean measures, suggested that there had been a modest step-up in underlying inflation during 2019"

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation running below target but expected to gradually converge; not alarming but persistently below objective. Consistent with the 2018-2019 "below target" anchor range (38-42).

**Minority views**: A few see less confidence inflation will return to 2%; a couple see trimmed mean already near target. Modest spread.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 52 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "economic activity had risen at a moderate rate"
- Staff forecast: output expanding "at a rate a little above the staff's estimate of its potential rate of growth"
- "business fixed investment and exports had remained weak" offset by strong labor market and solid consumer spending

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy near potential, perhaps modestly above. Growth is moderate, labor market is strong, but business investment is weak. Net assessment: approximately at potential with a slight lean above.

**Minority views**: A few think employment is still below maximum, implying output gap could be near zero or slightly negative. Mild divergence.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 60 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "unemployment rate at a 50-year low and continued solid job gains"
- "contacts reported difficulties in finding qualified workers or that observed wage growth might currently understate the degree of tightness"
- "aggregate measures of nominal wages continued to rise at a moderate pace broadly in line with productivity growth" — moderating the tightness signal
- "many participants pointed to the strong performance of labor force participation" and "possibility that there was some room for labor force participation to rise further"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market clearly tight — historically low unemployment, reports of worker shortages — but wage growth is moderate and participation is strong, tempering the degree of tightness. Scores well above 50 but not in the "severe shortages" range.

**Minority views**: Several note absence of strong wage pressures, some suggest labor market may not be as tight as unemployment rate implies. A couple worry tight labor markets could lead to imbalances.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 47 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "spending growth had moderated in the fourth quarter" but expected to remain "on a firm footing, supported by strong labor market conditions, rising incomes, and healthy household balance sheets"
- "business investment and exports remained weak and that manufacturing output had declined over the past year"
- "many participants were encouraged by the significant pickup since last summer in residential investment"

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending is moderate/solid, residential investment is improving, but business fixed investment and exports are weak. The net position is close to trend — consumer strength roughly offsets business weakness. Slightly below trend due to the weak investment drag.

**Minority views**: Some cautiously optimistic that trade developments will boost business confidence; several skeptical the trade deal will have much effect.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 27 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "U.S. financial conditions became more accommodative over the intermeeting period, with equity prices rising notably"
- "accommodative monetary and financial conditions"
- "Financing conditions for nonfinancial firms remained accommodative, on balance, with corporate borrowing costs staying near historical lows"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions clearly loose/accommodative. Equity prices up, borrowing costs at lows, mortgage rates down. Well below the neutral midpoint.

**Minority views**: Late-period coronavirus concerns caused some retracement but did not change the overall accommodative assessment.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 48 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "participants judged that it would be appropriate to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 1½ to 1¾ percent"
- "current stance of policy as likely to remain appropriate for a time, provided that incoming information about the economy remained broadly consistent with this economic outlook"
- "Several participants suggested that inflation modestly exceeding 2 percent for a period would be consistent with the achievement of the Committee's longer-run inflation objective"
- "a few participants observed that the actual level of employment might still be below maximum employment"

**Center-of-gravity**: Firm hold with no interest in tightening and a mild dovish lean via inflation-overshoot tolerance and sense that there may be room to run. Not actively dovish — no one is calling for cuts — but the lean is slightly below balanced.

**Minority views**: "A couple of other participants expressed concern that tight labor markets have in the past been associated with economic and financial imbalances" — a hawkish minority voice.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 20 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Broad agreement on holding rates — no mention of formal dissents
- "Participants agreed that the labor market had remained strong" and "economic activity had risen at a moderate rate"
- Minor differences: "a few" less confident on inflation, "a couple" worried about financial imbalances from tight labor markets, "several" skeptical on trade deal impact

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement with minor emphasis differences. Typical low-dissent meeting.

**Minority views**: N/A — differences are of emphasis rather than prescription.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 55 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Dedicated discussion section: "In their discussion of financial stability, participants acknowledged the staff report...several participants observed that equity, corporate debt, and CRE valuations were elevated"
- "Some participants expressed the concern that financial imbalances—including overvaluation and excessive indebtedness—could amplify an adverse shock"
- The first part of the meeting included a special briefing on monetary policy and financial stability interactions, with extensive participant discussion

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability received meaningful and extended discussion — both the special topic briefing and the standard assessment section. More attention than a typical meeting, but not a crisis-level concern. The discussion was analytical/prospective rather than alarmed.

**Minority views**: Range from "moderate vulnerabilities" to concerns about elevated valuations and bank capital declines.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 48 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "current stance of policy as likely to remain appropriate for a time"
- "maintaining the current stance of policy would give the Committee time for a fuller assessment"
- "Several participants suggested that inflation modestly exceeding 2 percent for a period would be consistent with the achievement of the Committee's longer-run inflation objective"
- "if developments emerged that led to a material reassessment of the outlook, an adjustment to the stance of monetary policy would be appropriate"

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is essentially neutral — hold for now, data-dependent. The slight dovish lean comes from the inflation-overshoot tolerance language, but there is no explicit easing signal. Neutral with a mild dovish tinge.

**Minority views**: No explicit tightening signals from any participants in the forward-looking discussion.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 40 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "uncertainties about the outlook remained, including those posed by the outbreak of the coronavirus"
- "distribution of risks to the outlook for economic activity as somewhat more favorable than at the previous meeting, although a number of downside risks remained prominent"
- "participants generally expected trade-related uncertainty to remain somewhat elevated"
- "the threat of the coronavirus...had emerged as a new risk to the global growth outlook, which participants agreed warranted close watching"

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty somewhat elevated but reduced from prior meetings. Several downside risks remain (trade, coronavirus, global growth) but some risks had diminished (phase-one deal, Brexit). The tone is watchful but not deeply uncertain.

**Minority views**: Some more concerned about coronavirus and trade; overall uncertainty tilted to downside risks.
