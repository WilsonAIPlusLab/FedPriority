# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1999-06-29

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). This is an unsegmented pre-2009 minutes document, so I must distinguish participant language ("members commented," "most members," "members noted") from staff content. The text is almost entirely participant discussion, with only minor staff references (e.g., "a staff projection indicating that growth of the debt aggregate").

**Era context**: June 1999 -- late 1990s expansion/boom. Strong GDP growth, very tight labor markets, subdued inflation thanks to a productivity acceleration. The Fed had cut rates during the 1998 LTCM/Asian crisis episode and was now facing an economy running hot. The fed funds rate was at 4.75% (had been raised 25bp at the prior meeting in May 1999, though this document doesn't explicitly state that). This is a classic late-cycle overheating concern period.

**Quantifier distribution**: "most" (members saw tightening risk, members worried about overheating), "all" (favored retaining ranges), "unanimously" (retaining debt range), "a number of" (indicators on housing). The language is fairly unified around concern about overheating risks.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as currently subdued but with significant upside risk. "Inflation as reflected in a wide range of statistical measures and anecdotal reports remained remarkably subdued." However, "most members had become increasingly worried about the risks of an overheating economy and rising inflation over time." The trend in hourly earnings "appeared to have tilted up." Oil/commodity prices firming. The CENTER OF GRAVITY is: inflation is currently low but pressure is building -- members see it as below target now but risks tilted strongly upward. Current measured inflation is subdued (~41-48 range) but the forward risk perception and the tilting-up wage evidence push this toward 48-52. Given the weight on "increasingly worried" and rising wage/commodity signals, I'll score 50 -- balanced between currently subdued readings and clearly building pressures. Actually, the rubric says score what participants PERCEIVE NOW. They perceive subdued inflation currently but rising pressures. The key phrase is "remarkably subdued despite" tight labor markets. But also "trend in average hourly earnings appeared to have tilted up" and "risks of added price pressures as having risen further." This is a mixed picture leaning toward modest pressure building. Score: 48. Subdued current readings with acknowledged upside risks push it near the midpoint.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Vigorous expansion," "very tight labor markets," growth slowing from "outsized advances." Members questioned whether moderation was enough to bring activity in line with "the economy's estimated output potential." "Most members saw tightening labor markets and an updrift in measured inflation as a significant risk" -- implying above-potential operation. The economy is running above potential: "overheating economy" language is used explicitly. Bright-line rule: "overheating" = must be > 50. The late 1990s anchor is 57-63. Growth was slowing somewhat but still above trend. Score: 60. Clearly above potential, members use "overheating" language.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Very tight labor markets across the nation" -- repeated multiple times. "Growing scarcities of labor." "Labor utilization was likely to continue to rise." Unemployment forecast 4-4.25% (well below NAIRU estimates of the time). "Scarcities of labor and some building supplies." The anchor for 1999-2000 is 65-75. Score: 70. Very tight, severe shortages, rising wage pressure beginning.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Robust growth in consumer spending," "exceptional growth in expenditures for producers' durable equipment," business investment "picked up in recent months from an already rapid pace." Some signs of moderation from "outsized" earlier pace but still very strong. "Robust" and "rapid" language dominates. Anchor for above trend is 55-65; this is solidly above trend. Score: 64. Well above trend but with some early signs of moderation from a very high level.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Limited direct discussion. "Somewhat higher levels of market interest rates that business borrowers now faced." "Backup that had occurred in mortgage rates." But also "ample availability of capacity" and strong equity wealth effects. Financial conditions are mildly tighter from rate backup but not restrictive. Score: 48. Roughly neutral, with slight tightening from higher rates but offset by strong equity/wealth effects.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "In the absence of some policy firming most of the members saw tightening labor markets and an updrift in measured inflation as a significant risk." This is a clear hawkish lean -- most members see the need for firming. No member voices are described as favoring easing. The vote was to raise rates 25bp at this meeting (the outcome). Members are unified on tightening. Score: 65. Moderate hawkish -- consensus for tightening, "conditions warrant increase."

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All the members favored retaining the current ranges." "Unanimously in favor." The discussion shows broad agreement on the outlook and risks. No dissenting views are described. Members appear united on the need for firming. Score: 15. Near-consensus; no dissents mentioned, all views align.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Stock market wealth effects are mentioned ("outlook for stock market prices and their effects on consumer resources"), some mention of overbuilding in nonresidential construction. But no substantial discussion of financial stability risks per se -- it's more about wealth effects on spending. Very limited. Anchor for 1993-96 typical is 1-10. Score: 15. Brief passing references to stock market wealth and overbuilding, not a focused stability discussion.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Members explicitly say that "in the absence of some policy firming" inflation risks are significant. "Risks of added price pressures as having risen further." The forward signal is clearly hawkish -- more tightening may be needed. But there's also "considerable uncertainty" about timing. The signal is: we just tightened, and risks remain tilted toward needing more. Score: 68. Mild-to-moderate tightening bias going forward.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Timing and extent of a potential rise in inflation were subject to considerable uncertainty." "How long faster gains in productivity could continue to offset increases in labor costs" -- genuine uncertainty about the productivity puzzle. "Marked degree of uncertainty in the outlook for productivity as well as velocity." Multiple references to genuine uncertainty. "Considerable uncertainty" maps to 65-80 per the rubric. But it's focused on a few specific channels (productivity, timing of inflation). Score: 58. Elevated uncertainty, focused on productivity trends and inflation timing.

### Cross-dimension coherence check:
- OUTPUT_GAP (60) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70): Both well above 50, coherent with a boom period. Labor tightness higher than output gap because labor constraints are described as more severe than the overall gap.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (65) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (68): Aligned, both moderately hawkish. Coherent.

### Polarity verification:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (48): Higher = more pressure. Evidence shows subdued current readings but building risks. 48 is slightly below midpoint, reflecting "remarkably subdued" current state tempered by rising risks. Correct direction.
- OUTPUT_GAP (60): Higher = more above potential. "Overheating" language. Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70): Higher = tighter. "Very tight labor markets." Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (48): Higher = tighter conditions. Conditions are roughly neutral with slight tightening. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 48 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "inflation as reflected in a wide range of statistical measures and anecdotal reports remained remarkably subdued despite the persisting strength of the expansion and very tight labor markets"
- "most members had become increasingly worried about the risks of an overheating economy and rising inflation over time"
- "the trend in average hourly earnings appeared to have tilted up in recent months"

**Center-of-gravity**: Current inflation readings are subdued, but upside pressure is building via wages, commodity prices, and tightening labor markets. The score sits just below the midpoint, reflecting the tension between benign current readings and rising forward-looking risks.

**Minority views**: None explicitly stated; views appear unified on the risk assessment.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 60 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "vigorous expansion" with "outsized advances" in prior quarters
- "most of the members saw tightening labor markets and an updrift in measured inflation as a significant risk" -- implying above-potential operation
- "increasingly worried about the risks of an overheating economy"

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is running clearly above potential, with strong demand outpacing estimated output potential, though some moderation is beginning to appear.

**Minority views**: Members acknowledged "limited indications of some moderation" but this is presented as insufficient to close the gap.

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor markets are very tight with widespread shortages. Wage pressures are emerging. This is a consensus view with no dissent.

**Center-of-gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment are well above trend, though showing early signs of moderation from extremely high levels. The net picture is strong.
