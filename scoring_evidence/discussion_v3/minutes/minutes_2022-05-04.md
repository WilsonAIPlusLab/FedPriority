# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2022-05-04 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented format -- participant discussion is clearly separated from staff content.

**Era context**: May 2022. Inflation running at multi-decade highs (CPI above 8%). The Fed had just begun its tightening cycle with a 25bp hike in March 2022. This meeting delivered the first 50bp hike. Russia-Ukraine war ongoing, China COVID lockdowns disrupting supply chains. Labor market extremely tight with near-50-year low unemployment.

**Quantifier distribution**: Heavy use of "all" and "participants" (universal agreement on key assessments). "Most" used for 50bp at next couple meetings. "Many" for inflation expectations, expediting removal of accommodation. "Several" for upside inflation risks, financial stability, challenges of restoring price stability. "A few" for labor supply signs, business contacts reporting higher prices hurting sales, business contacts hesitant to expand. "A couple" for housing demand, commodities risk.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as far above target. "Inflation remained elevated," "inflation continued to run well above the Committee's longer-run goal," "inflation pressures were evident in a broad array of goods and services," "all participants concurred...inflation was very high and well above the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective." Risks "skewed to the upside." Some noted price pressures "may no longer be worsening" but "too early to be confident that inflation had peaked." This is very strong inflation language. Higher = more pressure. The anchor for 2022 Q2-Q3 peak is 82-88. The language here is extremely strong with unanimous concern -- "very high," broadening, upside risks. Score: ~85.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: GDP edged down in Q1 but participants judged this reflected volatile components (net exports, inventories) and that "household spending and business fixed investment had remained strong." They saw "significant underlying momentum in the domestic economy" and expected "real GDP would grow solidly in the current quarter." Growth "near or above its longer-run rate." "All participants concurred that the U.S. economy was very strong." "Imbalance between aggregate demand and aggregate supply" -- demand outpacing supply. The bright-line rule: demand exceeding supply = above potential, must be >50. The anchor for 2022 is 57-63. "Very strong" economy with demand-supply imbalance suggests the higher end. Score: ~60.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor market was extremely tight." "Demand for labor continued to outstrip available supply." "Various indicators pointed to a very tight labor market." Unemployment at "near-50-year low," "quits and job openings had remained extremely elevated," "nominal wages had continued to rise rapidly." "Exceptionally high ratio of vacancies to job searchers." Anchor for 2022-23 is 65-75. "Extremely tight" language pushes toward higher end. Score: ~75.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Household spending and business fixed investment had remained strong." "Robust growth in consumption spending" expected. "Strong household balance sheets, wide availability of jobs." Business sector: "robust consumer demand, healthy household balance sheets, and inventory rebuilding" supportive. But supply constraints limiting ability to meet demand. A few contacts hesitant to expand capacity. Net position: strong/robust spending. Anchor for 2021 Q3-2022 Q1 surge is 62-68. Language is clearly "strong" and "robust" but not "unsustainably rapid." Score: ~63.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Considerable increases in Treasury yields across maturities" with "rising interest rates faced by households, particularly rates on home mortgages." "Many participants assessed that the Committee's previous communications had been helpful...and had contributed to the tightening of financial conditions." Several noted "potential for unanticipated effects on financial market conditions" from balance sheet reduction. Conditions have tightened from policy but no stress language. Anchor for 2022 rate hikes is 60-70. Score: ~62.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All participants agreed that it was appropriate to raise the target range...50 basis points." "All participants reaffirmed their strong commitment and determination to take the measures necessary to restore price stability." "Committee should expeditiously move the stance of monetary policy toward a neutral posture." "Most participants judged that 50 basis point increases...would likely be appropriate at the next couple of meetings." Balance sheet reduction starting June 1. A number discussed potential future MBS sales. "A restrictive stance of policy may well become appropriate." This is very hawkish -- 50bp hike with more signaled, plus QT launch. Anchor for late 2022 75bp hikes is 80-85. This meeting is 50bp with 50bp more signaled, not yet at 75bp pace. Score: ~80.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Unanimous vote. "All participants concurred," "all participants agreed," "all participants supported." No dissents, no expressed reservations mentioned. Very little disagreement language. "A number of participants" discussed possible future MBS sales vs. others presumably not -- minor tactical difference. Score: ~12.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: "Several participants who commented on issues related to financial stability" -- limited to several. Discussion covered Treasury market liquidity, commodities risk from Ukraine, CCP margin requirements. But it was a secondary topic, not dominant. Score: ~30.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Most participants judged that 50 basis point increases in the target range would likely be appropriate at the next couple of meetings." "Ongoing increases in the target range for the federal funds rate would be warranted." "Expeditiously move the stance of monetary policy toward a neutral posture." "A restrictive stance of policy may well become appropriate." Balance sheet reduction starting. Strongly hawkish forward signal. Anchor for 2022 aggressive hiking is 80-95. The signal is clear: multiple 50bp hikes ahead, possible move to restrictive. Score: ~85.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Economic outlook was highly uncertain." "Implications for the U.S. economy were highly uncertain." "High degree of uncertainty surrounding the economic outlook." "Risk-management considerations would be important." Russia-Ukraine and China lockdowns as sources of uncertainty. "Timing and magnitude of these effects were uncertain." Language is "highly uncertain" -- genuine, repeated. Anchor for 2015-16 China slowdown is 55-65. "Highly uncertain" is stronger. Score: ~68.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (60) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (75): Both above 50, labor tighter than output gap reading. This makes sense because the labor market was arguably tighter than the overall economy (Q1 GDP technically declined but labor was at historic tightness). Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (80) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (85): Both strongly hawkish. Forward guidance slightly more hawkish because the signal of multiple 50bp hikes and possible restrictive territory ahead is very aggressive. Coherent.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. "Very high," "well above." Score 85. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Demand exceeds supply, "very strong." Score 60. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Conditions have tightened, yields up. Score 62. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 85 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All participants concurred that...inflation was very high and well above the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective."
- "Inflation pressures were evident in a broad array of goods and services."
- "Risks to inflation were skewed to the upside."
- "Most participants indicated that their business contacts had continued to report that substantial increases in wages and input prices were being passed through into higher prices."

**Center of gravity**: Unanimous view that inflation is very high, broadening, with upside risks. Near the peak of the 2022 inflation episode.
**Minority views**: "A number of participants observed that recent monthly data might suggest that overall price pressures may no longer be worsening" -- but even they "emphasized that price pressures remained elevated and that it was too early to be confident that inflation had peaked." This does not significantly lower the center.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 60 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All participants concurred that the U.S. economy was very strong."
- "Household spending and business fixed investment had remained strong" despite Q1 GDP decline.
- "Significant underlying momentum in the domestic economy."
- "Imbalance between aggregate demand and aggregate supply" acknowledged.

**Center of gravity**: Economy clearly above potential with demand exceeding supply, despite technical Q1 GDP dip attributed to volatile components.
**Minority views**: Q1 GDP decline and supply constraints introduce some downside nuance, but participants largely dismissed the GDP print as noise.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 75 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Labor market was extremely tight."
- "Demand for labor continued to outstrip available supply across many parts of the economy."
- "Unemployment rate had fallen to a near-50-year low, quits and job openings had remained extremely elevated, and nominal wages had continued to rise rapidly."
- "Exceptionally high ratio of vacancies to job searchers."

**Center of gravity**: Unanimous view of extreme labor market tightness across multiple indicators.
**Minority views**: "A few participants noted...signs that the pandemic-related factors that had held back labor supply might be abating further" -- marginal softening at the edges.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 63 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Household spending and business fixed investment had remained strong."
- "Robust growth in consumption spending" expected, supported by "strong household balance sheets, wide availability of jobs."
- "Robust consumer demand, healthy household balance sheets, and inventory rebuilding" supporting business activity.
- "A few participants indicated that some of their business contacts were reportedly hesitant to expand capacity."

**Center of gravity**: Strong/robust spending and investment, clearly above trend but not described as unsustainable.
**Minority views**: A few contacts hesitant to expand capacity; supply constraints limiting firms' ability to meet demand.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 62 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Considerable increases in Treasury yields across maturities...associated with rising interest rates faced by households, particularly rates on home mortgages."
- "Many participants assessed that the Committee's previous communications...had contributed to the tightening of financial conditions."
- "Several participants noted the potential for unanticipated effects on financial market conditions" from balance sheet reduction.

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions have notably tightened from policy actions and communications, but no stress or crisis language.
**Minority views**: Limited discussion; some uncertainty about how further tightening and QT would affect markets.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 80 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All participants agreed that it was appropriate to raise the target range...50 basis points."
- "All participants reaffirmed their strong commitment and determination to take the measures necessary to restore price stability."
- "Committee should expeditiously move the stance of monetary policy toward a neutral posture."
- "Most participants judged that 50 basis point increases...would likely be appropriate at the next couple of meetings."
- All supported beginning balance sheet reduction on June 1.

**Center of gravity**: Very strong hawkish consensus -- 50bp hike, more 50bp hikes signaled, QT launch, possible move to restrictive territory. Unanimous support.
**Minority views**: Essentially none -- remarkable uniformity of hawkish intent.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 12 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The Committee voted unanimously."
- "All participants concurred," "all participants agreed," "all participants supported" -- repeated throughout.
- No reservations, reluctance, or alternative preferences expressed.

**Center of gravity**: Near-total consensus. One of the most unified meetings in the 2022 cycle.
**Minority views**: Minor tactical differences (e.g., some discussed future MBS sales, others did not) but no substantive disagreement.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 30 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Several participants who commented on issues related to financial stability noted that the tightening of monetary policy could interact with vulnerabilities related to the liquidity of markets for Treasury securities."
- "A couple of participants pointed to increased risks in financial markets linked to commodities following Russia's invasion of Ukraine."
- Discussion of CCP margin requirements and risk-management practices.

**Center of gravity**: Limited discussion by "several" participants -- present but not a prominent focus of the meeting.
**Minority views**: None -- financial stability was a secondary topic.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 85 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Most participants judged that 50 basis point increases in the target range would likely be appropriate at the next couple of meetings."
- "Ongoing increases in the target range for the federal funds rate would be warranted."
- "A restrictive stance of policy may well become appropriate depending on the evolving economic outlook."
- "Many participants judged that expediting the removal of policy accommodation would leave the Committee well positioned later this year to assess the effects of policy firming."

**Center of gravity**: Very hawkish forward signal -- explicit guidance for continued 50bp hikes, with door open to restrictive territory beyond neutral.
**Minority views**: Data-dependence caveats ("policy decisions should be data dependent") provide some flexibility but do not meaningfully soften the hawkish signal.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 68 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants agreed that the economic outlook was highly uncertain."
- "Implications for the U.S. economy were highly uncertain" (regarding Russia-Ukraine).
- "In light of the high degree of uncertainty surrounding the economic outlook, participants judged that risk-management considerations would be important."
- "Uncertainty about real activity was also seen as elevated."

**Center of gravity**: Repeatedly expressed high uncertainty driven by Russia-Ukraine war, China lockdowns, and the path of inflation. Genuine, not rhetorical.
**Minority views**: Despite uncertainty, the policy direction was clear, suggesting uncertainty was more about the speed/magnitude of adjustment than the direction.

### Optional Dimensions:

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Discussed -- supply chain disruptions, goods price pressures. "Inflation pressures were evident in a broad array of goods and services." Supply bottlenecks exacerbated by Ukraine and China lockdowns. Score: 80 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Mentioned as part of broad-based inflation but not specifically separated from goods in detailed discussion. Score: N/A

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: "A couple of participants reported that their business contacts continued to see robust housing demand and elevated home prices despite higher mortgage interest rates." Limited discussion. Score: 70 +/-10 [Low confidence]

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Many participants commented that measures of short-term inflation expectations were elevated or that far-forward measures of inflation compensation were near the upper edge of their historical range." "Several participants judged that measures of longer-term inflation expectations...still appeared to be broadly consistent with the Committee's longer-run inflation objective." "Some participants emphasized that persistently high inflation heightened the risk that longer-term inflation expectations could become unanchored." Mixed: short-term elevated, longer-term still anchored but at risk. Score: 62 +/-8 [High confidence]

**CREDIT_STRESS**: Not prominently discussed by participants. Score: N/A

**EQUITY_STRESS**: Not discussed. Score: N/A

**BANKING_STRESS**: Not discussed as stress to banking system per se. Score: N/A

**TREASURY_STRESS**: "Vulnerabilities related to the liquidity of markets for Treasury securities" mentioned by several participants. Limited but present. Score: 55 +/-10 [Low confidence]

**DOLLAR_CONCERN**: Not discussed. Score: N/A

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Not discussed. Score: N/A
