# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2019-01-30

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented format with clearly delineated "Participants' Views on Current Conditions and the Economic Outlook" and "Committee Policy Action" sections separate from staff reviews.

**Era context**: January 2019. The Fed had hiked to 2.25-2.50% by December 2018. Financial markets had sold off sharply in Q4 2018. Global growth was slowing (China, Europe). The Committee was pivoting from a tightening cycle to a "patient" posture. This is the famous "Powell pivot" meeting where the Committee dropped "further gradual increases" language.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all" (hold rates, statement agreement), "almost all" (stop balance sheet reduction), "many" (muted inflation pressures, uncertainty language), "several" (nudged down outlooks, tight labor markets, yield curve flat, rate increases later), "some" (consumer sentiment softening, weakening business optimism, inflation factors), "a few" (housing weakness, energy CapEx, inflation expectations concerns, SEP concerns).

### Core Dimension Reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as near but slightly below target. "Both overall inflation and inflation for items other than food and energy remained near 2 percent." But "many participants commented that upward pressures on inflation appeared to be more muted than they appeared to be last year despite strengthening labor market conditions." Market-based inflation compensation had "moved lower." The statement change explicitly references "muted inflation pressures." Direction: slightly below midpoint. Anchor: 2013-19 below target range is 38-42. "Near 2 percent" but "muted" pressures suggests 40-43.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic activity had been rising at a solid rate." Growth was expected to "step down somewhat from the pace seen over 2018 to a rate closer to their estimates of longer-run growth." The staff projected unemployment "to decline somewhat further below the staff's estimate of its longer-run natural rate." Participants described activity as "solid" not "strong" -- they changed the statement language from "strong" to "solid." The economy was at or modestly above potential. Direction: modestly above midpoint. Bright-line: unemployment below natural rate = above potential = must be >50. Score 52-55.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor market had continued to strengthen," "unemployment rate had remained low," "strong job gains," "labor force participation rate had moved up." "Several participants noted solid rates of hiring or other indicators of tight labor market conditions." "Some participants commented on recent indicators ... suggesting a pickup in wage growth." The labor market is clearly tight. Direction: above midpoint, moderate tightness. Score 57-62.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Household spending had continued to grow strongly" BUT "growth of business fixed investment had moderated from its rapid pace earlier last year." Manufacturing contacts in "a number of Districts" were "delaying or deferring capital expenditures." Housing sector showed "continued weakness." Mixed picture: strong consumer spending offset by moderating business investment and weak housing. Direction: near trend, slightly above. Score 50-54.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Key language: "financial conditions were materially tighter than they had been several months ago, with lower equity prices and wider corporate risk spreads." "Financial conditions had tightened, on net, since September." "Financing conditions for businesses and households tightened a bit further." However, conditions were "generally supportive of spending." This is meaningfully tighter than neutral but not severely restrictive. Direction: above midpoint. Score 58-63.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All participants expressed the view that it would be appropriate for the Committee to maintain the target range." The Committee replaced "some further gradual increases" with "be patient." "Many participants suggested that it was not yet clear what adjustments to the target range for the federal funds rate may be appropriate later this year." "Several of these participants argued that rate increases might prove necessary only if inflation outcomes were higher than in their baseline outlook." This is a clear dovish pivot -- hold with patient/data-dependent posture. But not easing. Direction: lean dovish to balanced. Score 42-46.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Unanimous decision to hold. All agreed to the statement. However, there was a range of views on future path: "several other participants indicated that... it would be appropriate to raise the target range later this year" while others thought hikes only if inflation higher. "A few" had concerns about SEP. Moderate but not severe disagreement about forward path. Direction: broad agreement with some differences. Score 25-30.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: "Among those participants who commented on financial stability, a number expressed concerns about the elevated financial market volatility." Discussion of liquidity, capital adequacy, leveraged lending, business debt levels. This was a meaningful but not dominant topic. Several paragraphs devoted to it. Direction: moderate discussion. Score 40-48.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The Committee moved from "further gradual increases" to "patient" -- a clear dovish shift in forward guidance. "Many participants observed that if uncertainty abated, the Committee would need to reassess the characterization of monetary policy as 'patient'." This is easing lean with data dependence. Direction: below midpoint. Score 35-40.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The economic outlook had become more uncertain." Multiple uncertainties cited: global growth, trade policy, Brexit, government shutdown, fiscal policy, financial conditions. "Participants noted that some risks to the downside had increased." "A patient approach" was explicitly tied to managing uncertainty. The removal of "roughly balanced" risks language signals elevated uncertainty. Direction: above midpoint. Score 50-58.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (53) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (59): Both above 50, consistent. Labor tightness somewhat higher than output gap makes sense -- the labor market was very tight even as growth was moderating.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (44) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (37): Both lean dovish, with forward guidance more dovish than current stance. This makes sense -- they held rates but signaled patience/no more hikes for now, a dovish pivot.

### Polarity verification:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says "muted" = less pressure. Score 42 < 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says "materially tighter." Score 60 > 50. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says dovish pivot to patience. Score 44 < 50. Correct.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence says "patient" replacing "further gradual increases." Score 37 < 50. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 42 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Both overall inflation and inflation for items other than food and energy remained near 2 percent on a 12-month basis."
- "Many participants commented that upward pressures on inflation appeared to be more muted than they appeared to be last year despite strengthening labor market conditions and rising input costs for some industries."
- Statement references "muted inflation pressures."

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants viewed inflation as near but slightly below the 2% target, with upward pressures fading despite a tight labor market, placing this just below the neutral midpoint.

**Minority views**: "A few participants expressed concern that longer-run inflation expectations may be lower than levels consistent with the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective" -- pulling slightly lower. Uncertainty band reflects the range of views on inflation compensation interpretation.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 53 +/-4 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants agreed that over the intermeeting period the labor market had continued to strengthen and that economic activity had been rising at a solid rate."
- "Participants generally continued to expect the growth rate of real GDP in 2019 to step down somewhat from the pace seen over 2018 to a rate closer to their estimates of longer-run growth."
- Members agreed to change characterization from "strong" to "solid," indicating moderation.

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy was operating modestly above potential -- unemployment below the natural rate, growth still above trend -- but the pace was decelerating toward potential. The shift from "strong" to "solid" signals convergence toward potential from above.

**Minority views**: "Several participants commented that they had nudged down their outlooks for output growth" -- slightly wider range.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 59 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The labor market had continued to strengthen... Job gains had been strong, on average, in recent months, and the unemployment rate had remained low."
- "Several participants noted solid rates of hiring or other indicators of tight labor market conditions in their Districts."
- "Some participants commented on recent indicators at the national or District levels as suggesting a pickup in wage growth."

**Center-of-gravity**: The labor market was clearly tight with low unemployment, strong job gains, rising participation, and emerging wage pressures, placing it solidly in the "tight" range.

**Minority views**: No dissent on the tightness assessment; views were uniform.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 51 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Household spending had continued to grow strongly" and "recent data on spending had been strong, supported by a strong job market and rising incomes."
- "Growth of business fixed investment had moderated from its rapid pace earlier last year." "Manufacturing contacts in a number of Districts indicated that such factors were causing them to delay or defer capital expenditures."
- "A few participants also pointed to continued weakness in the housing sector."

**Center-of-gravity**: Strong consumer spending was offset by moderating business investment, some deferred capital expenditures, and housing weakness. Net position is roughly at trend -- solid but no longer above trend.

**Minority views**: "A few participants commented that business optimism had increased among contacts in their Districts, or that they were planning new capital expenditures" -- some upside variation.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Financial conditions were materially tighter than they had been several months ago, with lower equity prices and wider corporate risk spreads."
- "Overall financial conditions had tightened since September."
- "Financing conditions for businesses and households tightened a bit further over the intermeeting period but remained generally supportive of spending."

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants consistently noted a meaningful tightening in financial conditions since September 2018, though conditions remained supportive of growth. "Materially tighter" places this above neutral but well short of crisis territory.

**Minority views**: "A couple of participants noted that the rise in credit spreads over recent months, if it were to persist, could restrain future economic activity" -- some concern about further tightening.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 44 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All participants expressed the view that it would be appropriate for the Committee to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 2-1/4 to 2-1/2 percent."
- Replaced "some further gradual increases" with "be patient as it determines what future adjustments to the target range for the federal funds rate may be appropriate."
- "Many participants suggested that it was not yet clear what adjustments to the target range for the federal funds rate may be appropriate later this year."

**Center-of-gravity**: This is a dovish pivot -- unanimous hold with a shift from tightening guidance to patience. However, they are not easing or signaling easing; they are pausing. This places the stance as lean-dovish to balanced.

**Minority views**: "Several other participants indicated that, if the economy evolved as they expected, they would view it as appropriate to raise the target range for the federal funds rate later this year" -- a hawkish minority keeping the score from going lower.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 25 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All participants expressed the view that it would be appropriate for the Committee to maintain the target range."
- "All participants agreed with the proposed statement."
- However, views diverged on forward path: some thought hikes likely, others uncertain, some thought only if inflation higher.

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous on the current decision and statement language. Disagreement was limited to the forward outlook -- whether and when to hike again. This is broad agreement with minor emphasis differences.

**Minority views**: Range from "several" wanting hikes later this year to others seeing no clear need.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 43 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Among those participants who commented on financial stability, a number expressed concerns about the elevated financial market volatility and the apparent decline in investors' willingness to bear risk."
- "A couple of participants highlighted the role that decreased liquidity at the end of the year appeared to play in exacerbating changes in financial market conditions."
- "A few participants highlighted the importance of ensuring that financial institutions were able to withstand adverse financial market events."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability received moderate attention. A dedicated paragraph addressed it with "a number" of participants expressing concerns. Discussion covered volatility, liquidity, capital adequacy, and business debt, but it was not a dominant theme.

**Minority views**: The qualifier "among those participants who commented" suggests not all participants addressed this topic.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 37 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Replaced "some further gradual increases in the target range" with "be patient as it determines what future adjustments... may be appropriate."
- "Participants also supported a proposal to remove from the statement the characterization of risks to the economic outlook as 'roughly balanced.'"
- "Many participants observed that if uncertainty abated, the Committee would need to reassess the characterization of monetary policy as 'patient.'"

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal shifted decidedly dovish -- from explicit tightening bias to a neutral/data-dependent "patient" posture. This is an easing lean with data dependence. The removal of "balanced risks" adds to the dovish tilt. However, the conditional language about reassessing patience if uncertainty abates prevents this from being a strong easing signal.

**Minority views**: "Several other participants indicated that... they would view it as appropriate to raise the target range for the federal funds rate later this year" -- a hawkish contingent.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 55 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The economic outlook had become more uncertain."
- "Participants noted that some risks to the downside had increased, including the possibilities of a sharper-than-expected slowdown in global economic growth... a rapid waning of fiscal policy stimulus, or a further tightening of financial market conditions."
- "An increase in some foreign and domestic government policy uncertainties, including those associated with Brexit, an escalation in international trade policy tensions, and the potential for additional extended federal government shutdowns were also cited as downside risks."

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty was elevated and explicitly cited as a key reason for the patient posture. Multiple sources of uncertainty were identified (global growth, trade, Brexit, shutdown, fiscal). The removal of "roughly balanced" risks language confirms above-normal uncertainty. However, participants still expected sustained expansion as the most likely outcome.

**Minority views**: Broad agreement on elevated uncertainty.

**Center-of-gravity**: Market-based measures had declined, raising concern about expectations potentially drifting below target, though survey measures remained stable. The split in interpretation creates uncertainty. Slightly below anchored-at-target.
