# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2010-12-14

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Segmented format (post-2009), with clear participant vs. staff separation. Quantifier language is the primary signal.

**Era context**: December 2010, roughly 18 months into recovery from the GFC. The Fed launched QE2 in November 2010 ($600B in Treasury purchases). Unemployment was still around 9.8%. The European sovereign debt crisis was intensifying. This is a period of "recovery but still deep slack."

**Quantifier distribution**: "participants" (general/most), "several," "some," "a number of," "a few," "many," "nearly all," "one member" (dissent).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW target. "Underlying inflation had continued trending downward," "slowdown in price increases evident across categories," "most participants expected that underlying measures of inflation would bottom out around current levels." Several saw deflation risk as receding but inflation still well below mandate-consistent levels. Commodities rising but businesses had "little ability to pass these increases on." Direction: clearly below 50. The language is "trending downward," "below levels judged most consistent... with price stability" -- this is subdued/muted territory. Score ~22-28.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see economy BELOW potential. "Significant slack in the economy," "slow progress toward maximum employment," recovery "gradual," housing "depressed." Some positive signals (retail sales, manufacturing), but the dominant message is persistent slack. "Below potential" language is clear. Score ~33-37.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see labor market as LOOSE. "Pace of hiring was still sluggish," "unemployment rate had edged higher," "employment-population ratio remained very low." Some improvement noted (declining jobless claims, job openings, workweek uptick) but the level is still clearly slack. Score ~28-32.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed but below trend. Retail sales "somewhat stronger than expected," "reasonably upbeat reports" on holiday spending. But housing "depressed," contacts "cautious about hiring and investment," nonresidential construction "very weak." Net: modestly below trend with some brightening. Score ~38-42.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Credit conditions had eased further," equity prices rose moderately, credit spreads roughly unchanged. But interest rates backed up substantially. The backup was attributed largely to improved outlook expectations and fiscal package, not tightening per se. Overall conditions described as somewhat accommodative to neutral. Score ~35-40.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Nearly all Committee members agreed to continue expanding" asset purchases. Maintaining QE2 at $75B/month pace. "Extended period" language retained. "Some noted that more time was needed" before considering adjustments. "Some indicated a fairly high threshold for making changes." One dissent on the hawkish side. This is firmly in the QE-era dovish range. Score ~18-22.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal dissent. "Members generally felt" and "nearly all" language suggests broad agreement with minority reservations. "A few" saw upside risks to inflation from balance sheet expansion. The dissent plus scattered hawkish minority views suggest moderate but not deep disagreement. Score ~30-35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: European sovereign debt concerns discussed by "participants" (multiple references). Treasury rate backup discussed at length. But financial stability is not the dominant topic -- it's contextual. Score ~35-42.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate for an extended period" reiterated. QE2 continuing. "Some indicated a fairly high threshold for making changes to the program." A few mentioned exit planning. The forward signal is clearly dovish/accommodative. Score ~10-15.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Recovery remained subject to some downside risks." "Range of views about the risks." European spillover concerns, housing downside risk, state/local fiscal concerns. But also some upside possibilities. Moderate-to-somewhat-elevated uncertainty. Score ~40-48.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (~35) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (~30): Both below 50, consistent -- economy in slack territory.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (~20) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (~12): Both dovish, consistent -- maintaining QE2 and "extended period" language.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says pressure is low/trending down. Score below 50. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says below potential. Score below 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says conditions eased further. Score below 50. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 24 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Underlying inflation had continued trending downward... slowdown in price increases evident across categories of goods and services"
- "Most participants expected that underlying measures of inflation would bottom out around current levels and then move gradually higher as the recovery progresses"
- "Inflation would remain for some time below levels judged to be most consistent... with maximum employment and price stability"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants see inflation well below target, with broad-based deceleration, though deflation risk is seen as receding. This places the reading in the "meaningful deflation risk; well below target; subdued/muted" range.

**Minority views**: "A few participants pointed to the risk that... the Federal Reserve's balance sheet and the sustained low level of short-term interest rates could trigger undesirable increases in inflation expectations." This small minority expands the band slightly upward.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Significant slack in the economy"
- "The most likely outcome was a gradual pickup in growth with slow progress toward maximum employment"
- "Progress toward the Committee's dual objectives of maximum employment and price stability was disappointingly slow"

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy clearly operating below potential with persistent slack, but some improvement noted (retail, manufacturing). Consistent with the 2010-14 recovery anchor range.

**Minority views**: "A few mentioned the possibility that growth could pick up more rapidly than expected" -- small upside minority.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 28 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The pace of hiring was still sluggish; indeed, the unemployment rate had edged higher in November, and the employment-population ratio remained very low"
- "Conditions in the labor market appeared to be improving on balance" with "declining number of new jobless claims, an increase in job openings"
- "The high level of unemployment was limiting gains in wages"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market very slack with high unemployment and sluggish hiring, though some early improvement signs. Firmly in the "high unemployment; significant slack" range.

**Minority views**: None prominent; broad agreement on slack conditions.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 38 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Incoming data on retail sales were somewhat stronger than expected, and there were some reasonably upbeat reports from business contacts regarding holiday spending"
- "Many contacts remained cautious about hiring and investment"
- "The housing sector, including residential construction and home sales, continued to be depressed"; "nonresidential construction remained very weak"

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumption showing some brightening but still below trend; investment cautious and construction weak. Net position is below trend but not deeply so.

**Minority views**: Mixed signals create moderate uncertainty -- retail improving but housing/construction dragging.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 37 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Credit conditions had eased further, although nonfinancial corporations continued to hold very high levels of cash"
- "Interest rates at intermediate and longer maturities rose substantially over the intermeeting period, while credit spreads were roughly unchanged and equity prices rose moderately"
- The backup in rates attributed to improved outlook expectations and fiscal package, not fundamental tightening

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions somewhat loose overall -- credit easing, spreads stable, equities rising. The rate backup was seen as reflecting improved expectations rather than restrictive tightening. Conditions "generally supportive."

**Minority views**: The rate backup creates some ambiguity, widening the band.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 20 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Nearly all Committee members agreed to continue expanding the Federal Reserve's holdings of longer-term securities"
- "The Committee also decided to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 0 to 1/4 percent and to reiterate its expectation that economic conditions are likely to warrant exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate for an extended period"
- "Some indicated that they had a fairly high threshold for making changes to the program"

**Center-of-gravity**: Strong dovish consensus maintaining QE2 and near-zero rates. The Committee is in active accommodation mode. One hawkish dissent does not shift the center of gravity meaningfully.

**Minority views**: "One member dissented from the Committee's policy decision, judging that, in light of the improving economy, a continued high level of [asset purchases was unwarranted]." A few also flagged inflation risks from balance sheet expansion.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "One member dissented from the Committee's policy decision"
- "Nearly all Committee members agreed" (implies near-consensus with one outlier)
- "A few participants pointed to the risk that the ongoing expansion of the Federal Reserve's balance sheet... could trigger undesirable increases in inflation"
- "Some noted that more time was needed to accumulate information on the economy before considering any adjustment"

**Center-of-gravity**: One formal dissent with a small minority expressing hawkish reservations, but broad agreement on the policy action. This is moderate disagreement with limited scope.

**Minority views**: The dissent plus "a few" with inflation concerns widen the band modestly.

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability/conditions discussed at moderate length -- European contagion risks and Treasury yield dynamics received meaningful attention from multiple participants, though not dominating the meeting.

**Minority views**: N/A.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 12 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:

- "Some indicated that they had a fairly high threshold for making changes to the program"
- "The Committee should continue its planning for the eventual exit" (noted but no urgency)

**Center-of-gravity**: Strong easing bias in forward guidance -- "extended period" language, ongoing QE2, high bar for changes. The forward signal is firmly accommodative.

**Minority views**: A few noted exit planning, but this is precautionary, not directional.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

**Evidence**:

- "They held a range of views about the risks to that outlook"
