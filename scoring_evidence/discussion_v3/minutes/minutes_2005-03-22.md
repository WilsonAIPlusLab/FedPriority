# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2005-03-22

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format, but participant language is clearly identifiable via "participants," "members," "many," "some," "a few," etc. Staff content is minimal/absent in this excerpt.

**Era context**: March 2005, mid-tightening cycle. The Fed had been raising rates in "measured" 25bp increments since June 2004 (from 1.00% to 2.50%), now going to 2.75%. The economy was expanding solidly, inflation was contained but showing some uptick, and "measured pace" language was central to forward guidance.

**Quantifier distribution**: "many" appears frequently (momentum, investment stronger, inflation to remain low, uncertainty about inflation risen). "All" for the 25bp vote. "Some" for pricing power, housing speculation, labor force participation. "Several" for dollar effects, upside inflation surprise concern. "A few" for housing speculation evidence, productivity surprises.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly above the comfortable zone but not alarming. Key: "pressures on inflation have picked up in recent months and pricing power is more evident." But "many participants stated that they expected total inflation to diminish and any rise in core consumer inflation to be limited." Core inflation had moved up "only modestly." The distribution was "tilted a little to the upside." This is a situation where inflation is slightly elevated with rising concern, but expectations remain contained. Direction: somewhat above midpoint. Score ~57.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Economy has "appreciably more forward momentum than previously perceived." Capacity utilization rising. But "a degree of economic slack apparently remained." Slack is diminishing but still present. The bright-line rule says slack remains = below 50. However, the language is that slack is "gradually diminishing" and activity has momentum — suggesting approaching potential. Score ~47.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor market conditions were reported to be stable to improving," "slack in labor markets was gradually diminishing." "Business executives indicated that labor was readily available" though some specific shortages. Wages "continued to increase moderately." This is below full employment but approaching it. Score ~44.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Investment "running considerably stronger" than anticipated, "underlying trends in capital spending appeared to be more robust." Consumer spending "expected to continue to advance at a solid pace." Housing at record levels. Some concerns about motor vehicle sales slowing and energy price restraint. Net: clearly above trend. Score ~60.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Unusually supportive financial conditions" mentioned. "Monetary conditions evidently were still quite accommodative." Low interest rates spurring housing. This is loose conditions. Score ~18.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All favored 25bp hike. "The required amount of cumulative tightening may have increased." But "accelerated pace of policy tightening did not appear necessary at this time." Some discomfort with "measured pace" language as potentially constraining. Members felt measured removal appropriate "for now." This is a steady tightening meeting with hints the pace might need to increase but not yet. Score ~67.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: All members favored 25bp. Agreement on action was unanimous. Some debate about language — "measured pace" concerns, risk assessment language. "A number of members believed that formulaic language by its nature was too rigid." But no dissents and no sharp divisions on the actual policy decision. Score ~25.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Brief mentions: "unusually supportive financial conditions," "a few participants cited some evidence of speculative activity in the housing market." These are passing references, not a sustained discussion focus. Score ~22.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Measured pace" language retained but with growing discomfort. "The odds that the Committee might need to step up the pace of policy firming were thought to have increased." Forward signal is clearly toward continued tightening, with hawkish lean growing. Score ~70.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Many participants indicated that their uncertainty about the intensity of inflation pressures had risen." "Considerable uncertainty persisted about longer-run prospects for productivity growth." Participants had "become less certain of that outlook for the next few quarters." This is elevated but specific to inflation — not broad existential uncertainty. Score ~48.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (47) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (44): Both slightly below 50, consistent — slack diminishing but still present.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (67) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (70): Aligned — steady tightening with hints of possibly faster pace. Forward guidance slightly more hawkish because of the "odds of stepping up" language.

### Polarity verification:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says pressures picked up, tilted to upside. 57 > 50. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says "quite accommodative," "unusually supportive." 18 < 50. Matches (loose conditions = low score).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 57 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "pressures on inflation have picked up in recent months and pricing power is more evident"
- "many participants indicated that their uncertainty about the intensity of inflation pressures had risen... the distribution of possible inflation outcomes was now tilted a little to the upside"
- "many participants stated that they expected total inflation to diminish and any rise in core consumer inflation to be limited"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants acknowledged modestly rising inflation pressures and increased upside risk, but the majority still expected inflation to remain contained. This places the score modestly above the neutral midpoint.

**Minority views**: "A few saw a distinct possibility of further positive productivity surprises, representing a downside risk to the inflation outlook" (dovish minority). Conversely, "several participants indicated that... they viewed an upside surprise to inflation as potentially more harmful" (hawkish-leaning concern).

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 47 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "a degree of economic slack apparently remained"
- "economic activity appeared to have more momentum than had previously been perceived"
- "slack in labor markets was gradually diminishing"; capacity utilization "pushing higher"

**Center-of-gravity**: Some slack remains (bright-line rule: below 50), but the economy is rapidly approaching potential with "appreciably more forward momentum."

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 44 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "slack in labor markets was gradually diminishing"
- "business executives indicated that labor was readily available, although... workers with certain skills and in certain occupations... were becoming increasingly difficult to hire"
- "wages generally continued to increase moderately"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market slack still present but shrinking; generally available labor with pockets of tightness. Below full employment but not far.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 60 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "investment was running considerably stronger in the first quarter of the year than had previously been anticipated"
- "Underlying trends in capital spending appeared to be more robust than had earlier been recognized"
- "Consumer expenditures were expected to continue to advance at a solid pace"; housing starts at "record levels"

**Center-of-gravity**: Investment is clearly above trend ("considerably stronger"), consumer spending solid, housing booming. Net spending picture is firmly above trend.

**Minority views**: Motor vehicle sales slowing; possibility of rising saving rate cited as downside risks.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 18 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "unusually supportive financial conditions"
- "Monetary conditions evidently were still quite accommodative"
- "residential investment expenditures apparently continued to be spurred by relatively low interest rates"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions described as clearly loose and accommodative, consistent with the era anchor of 10-20 for 2005-06 pre-crisis ease.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 67 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "all of the members favored boosting the target for the federal funds rate by 25 basis points to 2-3/4 percent"
- "the required amount of cumulative tightening may have increased"
- "the measured removal of policy accommodation was appropriate for now"

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous 25bp hike with growing sentiment that more tightening may be needed, though not at an accelerated pace yet. Solidly in the moderate hawkish range.

**Minority views**: "Some expressed the view that such language could constrain future policy inappropriately" — these members lean more hawkish, wanting optionality for faster tightening.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 25 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "all of the members favored boosting the target" — unanimous on action
- "Some expressed the view that such language could constrain future policy inappropriately" — debate on communication
- "a number of members believed that formulaic language by its nature was too rigid" and "some of these members believed that the risk assessment should be discontinued"

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous on the rate decision with moderate disagreement on communication strategy (measured pace language, risk assessment format). No formal dissents.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 22 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "A few participants cited some evidence of speculative activity in the housing market in several regions"
- "unusually supportive financial conditions" mentioned as a factor in investment strength
- No sustained discussion of financial stability risks

**Center-of-gravity**: Brief, passing references to housing speculation and accommodative conditions but not a prominent discussion topic.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 70 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the odds that the Committee might need to step up the pace of policy firming were thought to have increased"
- Retained "measured pace" language but with explicit discomfort: "such language could constrain future policy inappropriately; while these concerns were not new, they were now felt to be more pressing"
- "the required amount of cumulative tightening may have increased"

**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal clearly pointed toward continued tightening with growing hawkish lean. The "measured pace" was retained but under increasing strain, signaling the possibility of faster tightening ahead.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 48 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "many participants indicated that their uncertainty about the intensity of inflation pressures had risen"
- "considerable uncertainty persisted about longer-run prospects for productivity growth, unit labor costs, and cost pressures"
- "they had become less certain of that outlook for the next few quarters"

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty elevated specifically around inflation and productivity, with "many" participants noting increased uncertainty. Not crisis-level but notably above normal baseline.
