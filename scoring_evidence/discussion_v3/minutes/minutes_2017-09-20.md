# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2017-09-20

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented -- participant discussion section is clearly separated from staff review.

**Era context**: September 2017. The FOMC was in a gradual hiking cycle (had raised rates in Dec 2015, Dec 2016, March 2017, June 2017). The economy was near full employment, but inflation had softened notably in 2017, running below the 2% target. The Committee announced the start of balance sheet normalization at this meeting. Hurricanes Harvey, Irma, and Maria were a prominent near-term disruption factor.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all" (economy evolved as anticipated; appropriate to maintain rate; monitor data), "nearly all" (gradual approach warranted), "many" (accommodative financial conditions supportive; another hike this year likely warranted; inflation concerns about persistence; gradual balance sheet reduction), "several" (persistence of low inflation concerning; noted convergence taking longer; others' decision dependent on data), "some" (room for further labor utilization; patience in removing accommodation; worried about upside inflation risks), "a few" (no further increases near term; low expectations measures; upside risks from tight labor), "a couple" (accommodative conditions pose fin stability risks; broader wage acceleration may have begun).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target. PCE at ~1.5%, "running below 2 percent." "Many" believed cyclical pressures would show through but acknowledged soft readings. "Several" noted convergence taking longer than anticipated. "Some" discussed secular trends muting inflation. The center of gravity is clearly below target with genuine concern about persistence. However, many still expected a return to 2% over the medium term. Direction: below midpoint. Magnitude: "somewhat below" and persistent softness = 15-20 pts below 50. Score around 35. Polarity check: higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says below-target inflation with concerns about persistence. Score below 50 is correct.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "economy operating above its potential" is stated directly by participants. Economic activity "rising moderately." The text explicitly references "an economy that was projected to continue operating above its longer-run potential" and "tightening resource utilization." Bright-line rule: "above potential" language -> must be >50. But the language is moderate -- "moderately" expanding, not "running hot." Score ~53. Polarity check: higher = more above potential. Evidence says above potential. Score >50 is correct.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment "below participants' estimates of its longer-run normal level." Labor market "continued to strengthen." Job openings, quits returned to pre-recession levels. "Most participants expected wage increases to pick up." "Some participants still saw room for further increases in labor utilization" but this is the minority. Center of gravity: tight labor market, at or modestly beyond full employment. Wage pressures still "subdued" broadly but anecdotally emerging. Score ~55. Polarity check: higher = tighter. Evidence says tight. Correct.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "expanding at a moderate rate," supported by solid fundamentals. Business investment "picked up during 2017." Equipment investment on "steady uptrend." Business contacts "more confident." This is at-trend to slightly above trend. "Moderate" is the key word for consumption. Investment is the bright spot. Score ~52. Polarity check: higher = stronger. Evidence says moderate with improving investment. Score slightly above 50 is correct.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Many participants viewed accommodative financial conditions" as supportive. Equity prices increased, longer-term rates declined, dollar depreciated. Financial conditions remained loose even as the Committee raised rates. "A couple expressed concern that persistence of highly accommodative financial conditions could pose risks to financial stability." Direction: conditions are loose/accommodative, meaning TIGHTNESS is low. Score ~30. Polarity check: higher = tighter. Evidence says accommodative/loose. Score well below 50 is correct.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Held rates steady; announced balance sheet normalization (itself a tightening step). "Many" thought another hike this year "likely warranted." "Several others" said decision depends on incoming inflation data. "A few" thought no further increases near term. "Some other participants" worried about upside inflation risks and pace being too slow. Center of gravity: lean hawkish -- maintaining gradual tightening path, balance sheet normalization beginning, but tempered by inflation concerns causing some to counsel patience. Score ~60. Polarity check: higher = more hawkish. Evidence supports lean hawkish with balance sheet normalization plus likely another hike. Correct.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All" agreed to maintain rate. "Nearly all" supported gradual approach language. But there are clear differences: some want patience/no more hikes near term, others worried about being too slow. The range spans from "a few" wanting to defer increases to "some" worried about overshooting. No formal dissents noted. Moderate spread of views on the outlook but consensus on the action. Score ~30. Polarity check: higher = more disagreement. Evidence shows broad agreement on action but notable differences on outlook/pace. Correct.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: "A couple" expressed concern about accommodative conditions posing financial stability risks. "Some" cautioned about financial imbalances. Financial conditions discussed by "many" but primarily as supportive context, not as a stability concern. Limited but present. Score ~28. Polarity check: higher = more attention. Evidence shows limited discussion -- a couple specifically raised stability, but not a major theme. Correct.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Many" thought another increase this year "likely warranted." "Nearly all" supported indicating gradual approach. Balance sheet normalization announced. But "several" said decision depends on data, "a few" wanted to defer. Forward signal is mildly hawkish -- gradual tightening path continuing, with data-dependence caveat. Score ~65. Polarity check: higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence: gradual tightening continuing, likely another hike, balance sheet normalization. Correct.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Near-term risks appeared roughly balanced." Hurricanes added near-term uncertainty but explicitly judged as not altering medium-term outlook. Inflation uncertainty was the main source -- participants debated whether softness was transitory or persistent. "Several" noted uncertainty around inflation outlook. This is moderate uncertainty, focused on one dimension (inflation). Score ~35. Polarity check: higher = more uncertainty. Evidence shows focused uncertainty on inflation but otherwise relatively confident outlook. Correct.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (53) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (55): Both modestly above 50, consistent -- economy at/slightly above potential with tight labor market.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (60) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (65): Both lean hawkish, forward guidance slightly more so because of "many" expecting another hike and balance sheet normalization. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 35 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "PCE price inflation over the 12 months ending in August was estimated to be about 1-1/2 percent, remaining below the Committee's longer-run objective."
- "Many participants continued to believe that the cyclical pressures... were likely to show through to higher inflation over the medium term."
- "Several noted that... convergence to the Committee's symmetric 2 percent inflation objective might take somewhat longer than they anticipated earlier."

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation clearly running below target at 1.5% with genuine concern about whether the softness is persistent or transitory. The majority still expects a return to 2% but with diminished conviction.

**Minority views**: "A few" participants saw upside risks from tight labor markets (would push score higher); "some" discussed secular trends muting inflation further (would push lower). Reflected in +/-7 band.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 53 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "an economy that was projected to continue operating above its longer-run potential"
- "economic activity had been rising moderately so far this year"
- "the unemployment rate... remained below participants' estimates of its longer-run normal level"

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy modestly above potential. Growth described as "moderate" -- not overheating but clearly beyond the sustainable longer-run level.

**Minority views**: Narrow range of views; "some" saw room for further labor utilization increases, suggesting slightly less above potential. Band is narrow.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 55 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the unemployment rate... remained below participants' estimates of its longer-run normal level"
- "Other indicators suggested that labor market conditions had continued to tighten... job openings rate, the quits rate... had returned to pre-recession levels"
- "increases in most aggregate measures of hourly wages and labor compensation remained subdued"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market tight -- unemployment below NAIRU, multiple indicators at pre-recession levels. But the puzzle of subdued wages tempers the tightness signal. Tight conditions, moderate wage pressure.

**Minority views**: "Some" saw room for further utilization increases (pushing lower); "a couple" cautioned that wage acceleration may have already begun (pushing higher).

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 52 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending had been expanding at a moderate rate"
- "equipment investment had picked up during 2017 after having been weak during much of 2016"
- "Shipments and orders of nondefense capital goods had been on a steady uptrend over the first eight months of 2017"

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumption at a moderate pace (at trend) with improving business investment providing a modest above-trend tilt. Net assessment: slightly above trend.

**Minority views**: Limited disagreement; auto sales softening noted but offset by investment pickup. Narrow band.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Many participants viewed accommodative financial conditions, which had prevailed even as the Committee raised the federal funds rate, as likely to provide support for the economic expansion."
- "longer-term interest rates and the foreign exchange value of the dollar declined over the intermeeting period, and equity prices increased"
- "a couple of those participants expressed concern that the persistence of highly accommodative financial conditions could, over time, pose risks to financial stability"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly accommodative/loose despite ongoing rate normalization. Rates fell, dollar weakened, equities rose. This is well below neutral tightness.

**Minority views**: Minimal disagreement on the characterization of conditions as accommodative; debate is about whether this is beneficial or risky.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 60 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all participants thought it would be appropriate... to maintain the current target range" and announce balance sheet normalization
- "many participants thought that another increase in the target range later this year was likely to be warranted"
- "many participants expressed concern that the low inflation readings this year might reflect... developments that could prove more persistent"
- "A few of these participants thought that no further increases in the federal funds rate were called for in the near term"

**Center-of-gravity**: Lean hawkish -- balance sheet normalization initiated, majority expects another hike soon. But inflation concerns create meaningful caution. Not strongly hawkish because of the inflation-related hesitation.

**Minority views**: Dovish wing ("a few" want no more hikes) and hawkish wing ("some" worried about being too slow) create a wider band.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all participants agreed" on maintaining rates and balance sheet normalization
- Range from "a few" wanting no hikes to "some" worried about overshooting -- notable but manageable spread
- "nearly all supported again indicating... a gradual approach"

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on the action with moderate differences about the forward path and inflation interpretation. No formal dissents.

**Minority views**: The spread is real but contained. "Nearly all" vs "all" suggests perhaps one dissent on the statement language.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 28 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "a couple of those participants expressed concern that the persistence of highly accommodative financial conditions could, over time, pose risks to financial stability"
- "Some other participants... cautioned that an unduly slow pace in removing policy accommodation could... lead to an intensification of financial stability risks"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability is mentioned by a small number of participants. It is not a central or prominent theme but surfaces as a secondary consideration in the policy discussion.

**Minority views**: Limited to "a couple" and "some" on the hawkish side. Not broadly discussed.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 65 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "many participants thought that another increase in the target range later this year was likely to be warranted if the medium-term outlook remained broadly unchanged"
- "nearly all supported again indicating... that a gradual approach to increasing the federal funds rate will likely be warranted"
- "A few participants thought that additional increases in the federal funds rate should be deferred"
- Balance sheet normalization announced at this meeting

**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal is mildly hawkish. The dominant signal is continued gradual tightening with another hike likely this year, plus balance sheet normalization underway. Tempered by data-dependence and inflation uncertainty.

**Minority views**: "A few" want to defer creates downside; "some" want faster pace creates upside.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 35 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Near-term risks to the economic outlook appeared roughly balanced"
- "several noted that interpreting the next few inflation reports would likely be complicated by the temporary run-up in energy costs"
- "Several others noted that, in light of the uncertainty around their outlook for inflation, their decision on whether to take such a policy action would depend importantly on whether the economic data in coming months increased their confidence"

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty somewhat elevated, focused primarily on inflation dynamics (transitory vs persistent softness). The broader economic outlook is relatively clear. Hurricane effects add near-term noise but are judged as temporary.

**Minority views**: Limited; most agree on the source of uncertainty (inflation) even if they differ on implications.
