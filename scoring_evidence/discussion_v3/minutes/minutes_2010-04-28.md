**Era context**: April 2010. Early recovery from the Great Recession. The fed funds rate is at 0-0.25%. "Extended period" language is in effect. Economy is recovering but slowly; unemployment remains elevated. QE1 had largely wound down.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all" (economic activity strengthening), "most" (moderate growth, slow recovery, resource slack keeping inflation subdued, economic slack quite elevated), "many" (employment increases, bank lending still contracting), "some" (downside housing risk, concern about Greece, inflation risks tilted downward), "a number" (equipment/software strengthening, recovery could lose traction without job creation), "a few" (risks of early tightening exceed risks of being late), "one" (core inflation held down by shelter prices). The center of gravity clearly tilts toward "recovery continuing but slow with substantial slack."

**Dimension-by-dimension reasoning**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target. "Underlying inflation remained subdued," core readings "generally lower than they had anticipated," "most participants anticipated that substantial resource slack and stable longer-term inflation expectations would likely keep inflation subdued for some time," inflation "somewhat below rates... consistent... with the Federal Reserve's dual mandate." Some saw downside risks to inflation, others saw upside from commodities/expectations. Direction: clearly below target. The "subdued" / "below mandate-consistent rates" language maps to 26-40 range. The fact that readings were even lower than expected and there's mention of possible deflation risk from "some" pushes toward the lower end. Score: ~28.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: "Most members projected that economic slack would continue to be quite elevated for some time." "Substantial resource slack" mentioned repeatedly. Recovery is moderate. "Slow relative to past recoveries from deep recessions." The bright-line rule: "slack remains" language means score MUST be < 50. "Quite elevated" slack in April 2010 = still well below potential. Anchor: 35-42 for 2010-14 recovery. Given "quite elevated" slack, this is the early recovery phase. Score: ~33.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Many participants noted that employment had increased in recent months" but "participants remained concerned about elevated unemployment, including high levels of long-term unemployment and permanent separations." Labor market "beginning to improve" but from very weak levels. "Modest improvement in the labor market this year, with the unemployment rate declining gradually." Unemployment was ~9.7% in April 2010. Score the LEVEL, not the improvement. Still very slack. Anchor: 35-40 for 2011-14. In April 2010 it's even earlier. Score: ~28.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. "Significant gains in retail sales," "business spending on equipment and software had picked up substantially," "production was increasing briskly in many sectors." But: consumer spending "supported importantly by pent-up demands and possibly by other temporary factors," "housing market appeared to have stalled," "nonresidential construction... remained weak," consumer spending unlikely to be "the major factor driving growth." Net: recovering from deep trough, gaining some momentum, but still below trend with many headwinds. Score: ~40.

5. **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Two-sided. "Large firms with access to capital markets appeared to be having little difficulty in obtaining credit." "Narrowing credit spreads" and "rising stock prices." But "bank lending was still contracting and credit remained tight for many borrowers," "smaller firms... continued to face substantial difficulty in obtaining bank loans," "commercial real estate... tight credit conditions." Net: improving but still tight for many. Financial conditions are somewhat tight overall. Score: ~58.

6. **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Unanimous agreement to maintain 0-0.25% target. "Nearly all members judged that it was appropriate to reiterate the expectation... exceptionally low levels of the federal funds rate for an extended period." "A few members noted that at the current juncture, the risks of an early start to policy tightening exceeded those associated with..." (cut off, but clearly dovish). No one is pushing for tightening. Firmly in the dovish/accommodation camp. Anchor: 15-25 for QE era. This is maintaining ZIRP with extended period language, not actively expanding QE. Score: ~22.

7. **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Broad agreement on holding rates and "extended period" language. "Nearly all" supports the guidance — implies one or perhaps two with reservations. "A few" noted risks of early tightening — these are dovish-leaning members, not dissenters from the hold decision. Inflation risk assessment was "mixed" with some seeing downside and others upside — this provides some disagreement on the outlook. Overall: broad agreement on action, modest spread on inflation outlook. Score: ~25.

8. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Several mentions of financial conditions: Greece/European fiscal strains, bank lending contraction, credit conditions for small businesses, narrowing credit spreads, stock prices, commercial real estate vulnerabilities. Financial conditions are discussed meaningfully by multiple participants but as context for the recovery rather than as the dominant concern. Score: ~40.

9. **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Nearly all members judged that it was appropriate to reiterate the expectation that economic conditions... were likely to warrant exceptionally low levels of the federal funds rate for an extended period." "A few members noted that... the risks of an early start to policy tightening exceeded those associated with..." This is clearly a dovish forward signal — extended period language maintained. Anchor: 8-15 for 2014 taper era. This is 2010, still firmly in the "low for long" period. Score: ~15.

10. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Uncertainty about the economic outlook and future government policies" mentioned explicitly. Mixed inflation risk assessments. Greece/European concerns as downside risk. Housing market stalling. Recovery sustainability uncertain. Multiple sources of uncertainty discussed but not at panic-level. Several headwinds cited as "risks." Score: ~42.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 28 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants cited a wide array of evidence as indications that underlying inflation remained subdued."
- "Most participants anticipated that substantial resource slack and stable longer-term inflation expectations would likely keep inflation subdued for some time."
- "The latest readings on core inflation... were generally lower than they had anticipated."
- "Inflation remaining below rates that would be consistent in the longer run with the Federal Reserve's dual objectives."

**Center of gravity**: Participants perceive inflation as subdued and below the longer-run objective, with the latest readings even lower than expected. Substantial slack expected to keep inflation low.

**Minority views**: "Some" saw downside risks (deflation concern); "others" saw upside risks from commodity prices and inflation expectations — this spread informs the ±8 band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 33 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Most members projected that economic slack would continue to be quite elevated for some time."
- "Most anticipated that the pickup in output would be rather slow relative to past recoveries from deep recessions."
- "Substantial resource slack" cited multiple times.

**Center of gravity**: Economy is recovering but far below potential, with "quite elevated" slack expected to persist. Moderate growth pace insufficient to close the gap quickly.

**Minority views**: "Some of the recent data on economic activity had been better than anticipated" — modest upside but not enough to change the picture.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 28 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants remained concerned about elevated unemployment, including high levels of long-term unemployment and permanent separations."
- "A moderate pace of expansion... would imply only a modest improvement in the labor market this year, with the unemployment rate declining gradually."
- "Many participants noted that employment had increased in recent months" — but level still very weak.

**Center of gravity**: Labor market beginning to improve from deeply depressed levels, but unemployment remains high with structural concerns about long-term unemployed.

**Minority views**: Broad agreement on this assessment; narrow uncertainty band.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 40 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Significant gains in retail sales, business spending on equipment and software had picked up substantially."
- "Recent increase in consumer spending appeared to be supported importantly by pent-up demands and possibly by other temporary factors."
- "Housing market appeared to have stalled... at depressed levels." / "Nonresidential construction... remained weak."

**Center of gravity**: Spending is recovering with some bright spots in equipment/software and retail, but durability is questioned and housing/commercial real estate remain very weak. Below trend overall.

**Minority views**: Equipment/software investment is a genuine bright spot noted by several, but housing drag and sustainability concerns temper the assessment.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

- "Smaller firms in particular reportedly continued to face substantial difficulty in obtaining bank loans."
- "Large firms with access to capital markets appeared to be having little difficulty in obtaining credit."
- "Narrowing credit spreads in private debt markets."

**Center of gravity**: Dual-track financial conditions — improving for large firms and capital markets, but still tight for small firms and bank-dependent borrowers. Net somewhat tight.

**Minority views**: The divergence between large and small firm access creates the wider uncertainty band.

**Minority views**: No hawkish voices evident. The mild spread is between the center (hold + extended period) and a few who emphasize even more caution about premature tightening.
