# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2021-03-17

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented format with clear "Participants' Views" section beginning at the relevant portion.

**Era context**: March 2021 — roughly one year after the COVID pandemic onset. The economy is recovering from the pandemic recession, with massive fiscal stimulus (ARP just enacted), ongoing QE at $120B/month, and near-zero rates. Vaccinations are accelerating. Inflation is still below 2% but participants are beginning to discuss transitory supply-driven price increases ahead. Employment is still about 9.5 million jobs below pre-pandemic levels.

**Quantifier distribution**: The text uses "participants" (broad consensus) extensively, "most" several times, "many" a few times, "several" and "a number of" for moderate-sized groups, "a few" and "a couple" for minority views.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants observe that "headline PCE inflation continued to run below 2 percent." They expect the 12-month change to move above 2% due to base effects and supply constraints, but "after the transitory effects of these factors fade... participants generally anticipated that annual inflation readings would edge down next year." Most viewed inflation risks as "broadly balanced." The center of gravity sees current inflation below target with a transitory bump ahead. Per the rubric gotcha, the expected near-term move above 2% due to base effects and supply constraints does indicate some perceived upward pressure, but the current reading is 1.5% and participants describe it as below 2%. Direction: below target but with recognized upward pressures ahead. Score should be in the 38-45 range — below target but not deeply subdued given the anticipated near-term rise. I'll score 42.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Inflation is below target now but with transitory upward pressures recognized. 42 (slightly below midpoint) fits.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants state clearly that "economic activity and employment were currently well below levels consistent with maximum employment" and "the economy was far from achieving the Committee's broad-based and inclusive goal of maximum employment." Payroll employment was "about 9.5 million jobs below its pre-pandemic level." However, the recovery pace had picked up, and participants "significantly revised up their projections for real GDP growth this year." The economy is clearly below potential but recovering. Direction: below potential. Score should be in the 31-40 range given the language "well below" and "far from." I'll score 34.
- Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Economy is well below potential, so low score is correct.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 6.2%, employment 9.5 million below pre-pandemic level, "economy was far from achieving... maximum employment." Labor force participation held down by health concerns and childcare. Some mentions of labor shortages in specific sectors ("shortages in materials, key intermediate inputs, and labor"), but the dominant signal is substantial slack. Direction: well below full employment. Score 28-32. I'll score 30.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Labor market has substantial slack with 6.2% unemployment and 9.5M job shortfall. 30 is appropriate.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Household spending had risen notably so far this year." "Further gains in consumer spending would contribute significantly to the economic recovery." "Many participants also pointed to the elevated level of household savings and judged that the release of pent-up demand could boost consumption growth further." Business equipment investment "continued to increase" with "strong" readings. Residential construction "continued to be solid" and home sales "remained robust." However, services spending requiring close contact "remained weak." The net picture is above-trend momentum driven by fiscal stimulus and pent-up demand, though from a depressed base. Direction: above trend and strengthening. Score 55-60. I'll score 57.
- Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Spending is rising notably with strong momentum. 57 fits.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Overall financial conditions remained highly accommodative, in part reflecting the stance of monetary policy." Longer-term Treasury yields rose "notably" but this was viewed as reflecting improved outlook, not tightening. "While overall financial conditions were still seen as accommodative, a number of participants remarked that financing conditions remained challenging for many small businesses." A couple of participants worried about excessive risk-taking from accommodation. Direction: loose/accommodative overall. Score 20-28. I'll score 24.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Conditions are "highly accommodative," so low score is correct. Small business tightness is a minority note.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Participants "reaffirmed the Federal Reserve's commitment to using its full range of tools to support the U.S. economy." All agreed to maintain 0-0.25% target range. Asset purchases continue at $120B/month. Guidance is outcome-based, requiring "substantial further progress." The economy is "far from" goals. This is clearly accommodative/dovish. The committee is maintaining maximum accommodation with no discussion of tapering timing. Direction: dovish. Score 15-20. I'll score 18.
- Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Committee is maintaining full accommodation with QE ongoing. 18 is appropriate for the QE-maintaining era.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "By unanimous vote" on the policy action. The discussion shows broad agreement on the outlook and policy. Minor differences: "a couple of participants expressed concern that highly accommodative financial conditions could lead to excessive risk-taking." "A few participants" had various minority views on risks. But overall, strong consensus. Direction: low disagreement. Score 15-22. I'll score 18.
- Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Unanimous vote with minor emphasis differences. 18 fits.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Notable rise in Treasury yields was discussed by participants. "A couple of participants expressed concern that highly accommodative financial conditions could lead to excessive risk-taking and the buildup of financial imbalances." "Disorderly conditions in Treasury markets or a persistent rise in yields that could jeopardize progress toward the Committee's goals were seen as cause for concern." There is moderate but not dominant discussion of financial conditions. The ON RRP facility discussion was prominent but that is more about policy implementation. Direction: moderate attention. Score 30-40. I'll score 35.
- Polarity check: Higher = more attention. Moderate discussion of financial conditions including Treasury market moves and risk-taking concerns. 35 fits.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Guidance is strongly dovish — maintain near-zero rates "until labor market conditions had reached levels consistent with the Committee's assessments of maximum employment and inflation had risen to 2 percent and was on track to moderately exceed 2 percent for some time." Asset purchases continue until "substantial further progress." "Changes in the path of policy should be based primarily on observed outcomes rather than forecasts." A number of participants emphasized communicating progress "well in advance" of any change. This is strong forward easing commitment. Direction: dovish forward guidance. Score 8-15. I'll score 12.
- Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. The forward guidance is strongly accommodative with threshold-based conditions. 12 fits.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants continued to see the uncertainty surrounding that outlook as elevated." I'll score 60.
- Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. "Elevated" and "highly uncertain" language. 60 fits well for "elevated" uncertainty.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 42 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants observed that headline PCE inflation continued to run below 2 percent."
- "Most participants also pointed to supply constraints that could contribute to price increases for some goods in coming months as the economy continued to reopen."
- "After the transitory effects of these factors fade, however, participants generally anticipated that annual inflation readings would edge down next year."
**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation is currently below target but participants recognize near-term upward pressure from base effects and supply constraints, yielding a score slightly below midpoint.
**Minority views**: Several noted supply disruptions could push inflation higher than anticipated; several noted factors from the prior expansion could again exert downward pressure. These inform the ±8 band.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 34 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity and employment were currently well below levels consistent with maximum employment."
- "The economy was far from achieving the Committee's broad-based and inclusive goal of maximum employment."
- "Participants significantly revised up their projections for real GDP growth this year."
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is clearly below potential but recovering at an accelerating pace. "Well below" and "far from" language places this firmly in the below-potential range.
**Minority views**: Some noted upside risks from fiscal policy and pent-up demand that could close the gap faster.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 30 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Payroll employment was about 9.5 million jobs below its pre-pandemic level."
- "The economy was far from achieving the Committee's broad-based and inclusive goal of maximum employment."
- "Labor force participation continued to be held down by workers' health concerns and additional childcare responsibilities."
**Center-of-gravity**: Substantial labor market slack with 6.2% unemployment and large job shortfall. Some sector-specific shortages noted but the dominant signal is significant slack.
**Minority views**: Reports of labor shortages in some industries provide slight upward pull.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 57 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Household spending had risen notably so far this year and anticipated that further gains in consumer spending would contribute significantly to the economic recovery."
- "Many participants also pointed to the elevated level of household savings and judged that the release of pent-up demand could boost consumption growth further."
- "Business equipment investment had continued to increase and... readings on new orders and shipments of capital goods remained strong."
**Center-of-gravity**: Spending and investment are rising strongly, boosted by fiscal stimulus, pent-up demand, and accommodative policy. Above trend momentum though some services sectors still depressed.
**Minority views**: Contact-intensive services remain weak, providing some downside offset.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 24 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Overall financial conditions remained highly accommodative, in part reflecting the stance of monetary policy."
- "A number of participants remarked that financing conditions remained challenging for many small businesses."
- "A couple of participants expressed concern that highly accommodative financial conditions could lead to excessive risk-taking."
**Center-of-gravity**: Conditions are broadly very loose/accommodative, with small business financing as a notable exception.
**Minority views**: Small business tightness and risk-taking concerns widen the band slightly.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 18 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants reaffirmed the Federal Reserve's commitment to using its full range of tools to support the U.S. economy."
- "All members agreed to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 0 to 1/4 percent."
- "Asset purchases would continue at least at the current pace" ($120B/month) until "substantial further progress."
**Center-of-gravity**: Strongly dovish — maintaining maximum accommodation with QE and near-zero rates, with no discussion of beginning to taper.
**Minority views**: None expressed hawkish preferences; disagreement was minimal.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 18 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "By unanimous vote, the Committee ratified" the policy action.
- Broad agreement across the discussion with only "a couple" or "a few" expressing minority concerns on specific topics (financial stability risks, various risk scenarios).
**Center-of-gravity**: Strong consensus on both the economic assessment and policy stance. Minor emphasis differences only.
**Minority views**: A couple concerned about risk-taking; a few pointed to CRE/forbearance risks.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 35 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants commented on the notable rise in longer-term Treasury yields... and generally viewed it as reflecting the improved economic outlook."
- "Disorderly conditions in Treasury markets or a persistent rise in yields... were seen as cause for concern."
- "A couple of participants expressed concern that highly accommodative financial conditions could lead to excessive risk-taking and the buildup of financial imbalances."
**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate attention to financial conditions — Treasury yield rise was discussed at length, and financial stability concerns were raised by a minority. Not a dominant topic but more than passing mention.
**Minority views**: Most viewed yield rise benignly; only a couple flagged excessive risk-taking.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 12 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "It would be appropriate to maintain this target range until labor market conditions had reached levels consistent with the Committee's assessments of maximum employment and inflation had risen to 2 percent and was on track to moderately exceed 2 percent for some time."
- "Various participants noted that changes in the path of policy should be based primarily on observed outcomes rather than forecasts."
- "A number of participants highlighted the importance of the Committee clearly communicating its assessment of progress toward its longer-run goals well in advance of the time when it could be judged substantial enough to warrant a change in the pace of asset purchases."
**Center-of-gravity**: Strongly dovish forward guidance with explicit outcome-based thresholds. The forward signal is firmly accommodative with no hint of near-term tightening.
**Minority views**: None advocated for less accommodative forward guidance.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 60 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants continued to see the uncertainty surrounding that outlook as elevated."
- "The path ahead remained highly uncertain, with the pandemic continuing to pose considerable risks to the outlook."
- "Most participants indicated that the pandemic continued to pose considerable risks to the economic outlook, including risks associated with new more-contagious virus strains."
**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is elevated due to the pandemic, though somewhat improved from previous meetings given vaccination progress and fiscal support. "Elevated" and "highly uncertain" language places this above the midpoint.
**Minority views**: Some pointed to upside risks (fiscal more expansionary, pent-up demand), widening the range of possible outcomes.
