# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1999-03-30

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format — staff and participant content potentially mixed, but this text is clearly participant-focused with language cues like "members commented," "most members viewed," "many of the members," etc.

**Era context**: Late 1990s boom. The U.S. economy was experiencing sustained rapid growth, very tight labor markets, and remarkably low inflation — the "New Economy" productivity miracle period. The Asian financial crisis had eased, and the Fed had cut rates in fall 1998 (LTCM/Russia). By March 1999, conditions had stabilized and the question was whether to unwind those cuts.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all" (2x — favored unchanged, endorsed symmetric directive), "most" (2x — projecting slower growth, forecasters wrong), "many" (3x — risks tilted to inflation, absence of inflation warning, next move likely tightening), "several" (1x — no significant changes), "some" (6x — various views on housing, investment, risks), "a few" (1x — overbuilding). This is a meeting with strong consensus on the hold but with a meaningful hawkish undercurrent among "many."

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint — currently no evidence of acceleration, prices stable, unit labor costs very low. BUT "many" see risks tilted toward "eventual emergence of somewhat greater inflation pressures." The current assessment is clearly benign: "no evidence of any acceleration in price inflation," "difficulty or inability of most business firms to raise prices." However, forward-looking risks of inflation are noted. Scoring current perception: below midpoint but not dramatically so — the risk discussion pulls it slightly upward. ~40-43.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Economy running above potential. "Robust growth" that has persisted, "above trend" implicitly — forecasters keep expecting slowdown that doesn't materialize. "Most members viewed a slowing to a rate closer to most estimates of the growth of the economy's potential as a reasonable expectation" — meaning current growth is ABOVE potential. Bright-line rule: above potential language → must be > 50. The expansion is "extraordinary," growth is "robust," demand is strong across sectors. Score ~58-62.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Very tight labor markets across the nation," "persistence of very tight labor markets," "tight—and perhaps tightening—labor markets." Very clear signal of high tightness. Workers with skills in short supply, limited supply of qualified construction workers. Anchors suggest 65-75 for 1999-2000. Score ~68-70.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending with "considerable upward momentum," "favorable fundamentals," business investment in "extraordinary expansion" though expected to moderate. Housing "very strong." Overall spending is clearly above trend. "Recent exceptional pace" of consumer spending. Score ~63-67.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Ready access to consumer credit," "recovery in financial markets," stock market running up, favorable home affordability. Financial conditions are loose/accommodative. Members worried that announcing a tightening bias would move markets too much. Score ~25-30 (loose conditions).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All members favored unchanged policy." BUT "many" thought next move likely tightening; "some" saw next action more likely firming than easing; "a number of members noted a case might be made for unwinding" fall 1998 easing. "Many emphasized the Committee might need to act promptly to forestall buildup of inflationary forces." This is a hold with a clear lean toward tightening — 53-57. Not yet at "conditions warrant increase" (61-70) because all favor holding and timing is "highly uncertain."

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All" favored unchanged. "All" endorsed symmetric directive. Very broad consensus on action. Some variation in reasoning and risk assessment but no dissents, no reservations about the decision itself. Score ~15-20.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Stock market wealth effects discussed significantly (multiple references to equity prices, their effect on spending). Financial market conditions in emerging markets discussed. The concern about market reaction to a directive change is notable. But this is not a dominant topic — it's woven into the economic discussion. Score ~25-30.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Symmetric directive retained. "Many believed next policy move likely would be in direction of some tightening" but "not a foregone conclusion" and "timing highly uncertain." They explicitly chose NOT to signal tightening to avoid market overreaction. Lean is mildly hawkish forward but deliberately muted. Score ~55-58.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Wide range of uncertainty" about timing of moderation. "Substantial degree of uncertainty" about forecasts. "Great deal of uncertainty" about productivity/inflation dynamics. "Timing of next policy action was highly uncertain." Multiple explicit statements of genuine uncertainty. This is notably elevated. Score ~50-55.

### Cross-dimension coherence check:
- OUTPUT_GAP (~60) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (~69): Both above 50, labor tighter — consistent with late-cycle boom where labor tightness exceeds output gap.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (~55) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (~56): Both lean mildly hawkish. Consistent — hold with tightening bias that they chose not to formalize.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (~42) seems low relative to OUTPUT_GAP/LABOR — but this is the whole point of the meeting: the economy is running hot but inflation remarkably hasn't appeared. The text explicitly discusses this puzzle.

### Polarity verification:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says low current pressure, moderate risk. 42 = below midpoint. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says loose conditions. 27 = below midpoint. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 42 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "members commented that they saw no evidence of any acceleration in price inflation despite the continuing strength of the economic expansion and the tightness of labor markets"
- "increases in unit labor costs...had declined to a very low rate over the past year"
- "many of the members commented that the risks to their forecasts were tilted toward the eventual emergence of somewhat greater inflation pressures"
**Center of gravity**: Current inflation is quiescent — no acceleration, firms unable to raise prices — but forward risks of pressure are recognized by many. Slightly below midpoint reflects current benign readings with acknowledged upside risk.
**Minority views**: "Some" concerned about risk that sustained rapid growth would stretch markets; others believed productivity gains could continue offsetting pressures. Uncertainty band reflects this debate.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 60 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "current indicators provided little evidence of any moderation in the pace of the expansion from the robust growth experienced on average over the last few years"
- "most members viewed a slowing to a rate closer to most estimates of the growth of the economy's potential as a reasonable expectation" (implying current growth exceeds potential)
- "extraordinary expansion in business fixed investment"; consumer spending at "exceptional pace"
**Center of gravity**: Economy clearly running above potential with robust growth across sectors. Members expect moderation but it hasn't arrived yet.
**Minority views**: Some sectors (manufacturing, agriculture, energy) experiencing softness from foreign developments, narrowing the band slightly.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 69 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "persistence of very tight labor markets across the nation"
- "limited supply of qualified construction workers"; "workers with skills in especially short supply"
- "tight--and perhaps tightening--labor markets would add to price pressures"
**Center of gravity**: Labor markets are very tight nationally with specific shortages noted. The "very tight" language and discussion of potential further tightening place this firmly in the 65-75 anchor range.
**Minority views**: Narrow band — essentially universal agreement on tightness.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 65 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "growth in consumer spending would retain considerable upward momentum" with "favorable fundamentals"
- "extraordinary expansion in business fixed investment in recent years"
- Housing "very strong in many parts of the country"
**Center of gravity**: All three major spending categories — consumption, business investment, and housing — described as strong to extraordinary, clearly above trend. Expected to moderate but hasn't yet.
**Minority views**: Some signs of deceleration in business investment "from extremely rapid rates"; housing "appeared to have peaked in some areas." Band reflects expected moderation vs. current strength.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 27 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "ready access to consumer credit"; "rise in financial wealth that had continued through the first quarter"
- "recovery in financial markets and the improvement in the economic outlook"
- Members worried a tightening bias announcement would have "a relatively pronounced and undesired effect on financial markets"
**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly accommodative — easy credit, rising equity wealth, recovered markets. The concern about market sensitivity to signals suggests conditions are loose enough that even a hint of tightening would be disruptive.
**Minority views**: Limited direct discussion of financial conditions as a standalone topic.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 55 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all the members indicated that they favored an unchanged policy stance"
- "in the view of some, however, the next policy action was more likely to be a firming than an easing"
- "many of the members emphasized...the Committee might need to act promptly to forestall a buildup of inflationary forces"
- "a number of members noted that a case might be made for unwinding part of the Committee's easing actions during the fall of last year"
**Center of gravity**: Unanimous hold, but with a clear tightening lean among many members. They chose restraint due to uncertainty and absence of current inflation signals, but the bias toward tightening as the next move is clear.
**Minority views**: Some members more confident about productivity offsets; the hold reflects genuine uncertainty not just dovishness.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 15 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all the members indicated that they favored an unchanged policy stance"
- "all the members endorsed a proposal to retain the existing symmetry of the directive"
**Center of gravity**: Near-complete consensus on both the rate decision and the directive. Differences are about risk outlook and reasoning, not about the action.
**Minority views**: Some variation in inflation risk assessment but no operational disagreement.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 28 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Extended discussion of stock market wealth effects on consumer spending
- "the market's stimulative effect on spending was likely to wane over time"
- Concern about market reaction to directive change; emerging market financial conditions discussed
**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are discussed primarily through the lens of their effect on spending and the economy, not as a stability concern per se. Stock market valuation is discussed instrumentally. Moderate but not dominant.
**Minority views**: N/A — fairly uniform treatment.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 56 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all the members endorsed a proposal to retain the existing symmetry of the directive"
- "many believed that the next policy move likely would be in the direction of some tightening"
- "a biased directive would not be consistent with the members' view that a policy adjustment was unlikely in the period just ahead"
- Chose symmetric directive explicitly to avoid signaling tightening prematurely
**Center of gravity**: The forward signal is deliberately neutral in formal terms (symmetric directive) but the underlying lean is mildly hawkish. The Committee explicitly suppressed a stronger signal to avoid market overreaction.
**Minority views**: Tightening "not a foregone conclusion" — some residual two-way uncertainty.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "timing and extent of such moderation were subject to a wide range of uncertainty"
- "such forecasts were subject to a substantial degree of uncertainty"
- "a great deal of uncertainty surrounded the extent to which productivity gains and other factors...might continue to hold down inflation"
- "the timing of the next policy action was highly uncertain"
**Center of gravity**: Multiple explicit statements of genuine uncertainty about the productivity puzzle, the inflation outlook, and policy timing. This goes beyond standard hedging — it reflects a genuinely puzzled Committee facing a historically anomalous economy. Elevated but not extreme.
**Minority views**: The uncertainty is shared broadly; the band reflects variation in how much weight members give it.

### Optional Dimensions:

**INFLATION_GOODS**: N/A — No specific goods inflation discussion by participants (oil prices mentioned but briefly and in context of overall inflation).

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: N/A — Not specifically discussed as a sub-component.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: N/A — Housing discussed in terms of activity, not shelter/rent inflation.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: 47 ±8 [Low confidence]
- "dissipation or reversal of favorable supply factors...could contribute to higher inflation expectations" — risk of increase noted but expectations currently appear anchored.
- Limited direct discussion; scored based on inference from the broader inflation discussion.

**CREDIT_STRESS**: N/A — No specific discussion of credit spreads or stress.

**EQUITY_STRESS**: N/A — Stock market discussed as wealth effect driver, not in terms of valuation stress or bubble concern per se. Close to the line but insufficient direct concern language.

**BANKING_STRESS**: N/A — Not discussed.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: N/A — Not discussed.

**DOLLAR_CONCERN**: N/A — Dollar appreciation mentioned only in passing ("waning effects of the dollar's earlier appreciation") as a supply-side factor for inflation, not as a concern.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: 70 ±10 [Moderate confidence]
- "accelerating productivity helped to account for the economy's ability to sustain not only higher rates of growth of output but also relatively low levels of unemployment"
- "possible further uptick in productivity growth"; "optimism about improving productivity was evident in projections of business profits"
- Members perceive above-trend productivity growth as a significant factor in the economic puzzle.
