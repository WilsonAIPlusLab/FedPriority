# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2009-09-23 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). This is a post-2009 segmented document, so the participant discussion section is clearly delineated.

**Era context**: September 2009 -- six months after the GFC trough. The economy was beginning to recover from the worst recession since the Great Depression. The fed funds rate was at the zero lower bound and the Fed was conducting large-scale asset purchases. Unemployment was near 10%.

**Quantifier distribution**: "most" (recovery under way), "many" (revised up projections; recovery restrained; inflation risks balanced; marked up housing forecasts), "some" (skeptical of slack measures; improvement tentative; consumer spending pickup from special factors), "a few" (disinflation risk; upside inflation risk over longer horizon), "all" (inflation expectations key; importance of communication about withdrawal tools), "several" (foreign growth supporting exports).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target. "Most participants anticipated that slack in both labor and product markets would be substantial over the next few years, leading to subdued and potentially declining wage and price inflation." "A few continued to see some risk of substantial further disinflation, but that risk had eased somewhat further." "Many participants viewed the risks to their inflation outlook over the next few quarters as being roughly balanced." The center of gravity is subdued/muted inflation with diminishing deflation concern. This fits the 20-28 range. The deflation fears are fading ("eased somewhat further") but inflation is clearly well below target with substantial slack expected.

Direction: Below midpoint. Higher = more pressure. Evidence says less pressure. Score below 50. CHECK: Matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy far below potential. "The economy was projected to expand...but at a pace that was unlikely to reduce the unemployment rate appreciably." "Most participants anticipated that slack in both labor and product markets would be substantial over the next few years." Recovery is just beginning from severe downturn. "Economic activity had picked up following its severe downturn; most thought an economic recovery was under way." But recovery is "quite restrained." The gap is large but narrowing from the worst.

Direction: Well below potential. Score must be < 50. The language "substantial slack" for "the next few years" is very strong. The economy is just beginning to recover but slack is enormous. This fits 20-28 range.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor market conditions remained weak." "Job losses remained sizable and the unemployment rate was high." "Unusually large fraction of those who were working part time for economic reasons, the unusually low level of the average workweek." "Only a slow decline in the unemployment rate as the economic recovery proceeds." This is clearly very loose labor market, substantial slack. Fits 18-22.

Direction: Very loose. Higher = tighter. Score well below 50. CHECK: Matches.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumer spending had picked up more than expected" but "partly reflecting special factors like the cash-for-clunkers program." "Participants generally expected no more than moderate growth in consumer spending over the near term." Households face "considerable headwinds." Business investment: "reducing inventories and fixed investment at a slower pace than earlier" -- still contracting but less so. "Business contacts...remained cautious about the recovery." "Low utilization rates and substantial excess capacity could hold back...new capital spending." This is still below trend but improving from freefall. The pickup is modest and partly temporary. Fits 30-35.

Direction: Below trend. Higher = stronger. Score below 50. CHECK: Matches.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Mixed picture. "Financial market conditions had continued to improve over the past several months." "Cumulative improvement in market functioning and pricing since the spring as substantial." "Corporate debt continued to improve, private credit spreads narrowed." BUT: "Credit from banks remained difficult to obtain and costly for many borrowers." "Credit remained quite tight for many businesses and households dependent on banks." "Many regional and small banks were vulnerable." "All categories of bank lending continued to decline." The improvement in capital markets is significant but bank credit remains very tight. Net: still tight but improving from crisis levels. Fits 62-68.

Direction: Above neutral (tight). Higher = tighter. Evidence says tight credit conditions persisting. Score above 50. CHECK: Matches.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The meeting is about maintaining accommodation. "All agreed on the importance of the Federal Reserve continuing to communicate that it has the tools and willingness to begin withdrawing monetary policy accommodation at the appropriate time." Discussion of balance sheet effects on inflation expectations. No discussion of imminent tightening. The focus is on maintaining support with emphasis on exit strategy communication. This is strongly dovish but with attention to eventual normalization communication. Fits 18-24. The discussion centers on QE operations and maintaining accommodation, which per the rubric is a dovish signal.

Direction: Dovish. Higher = more hawkish. Score below 50. CHECK: Matches.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: The document shows broad agreement on the basic picture (recovery under way but restrained, substantial slack). Some variation: "some participants were skeptical of the usefulness of measures of resource utilization" vs. majority view that slack constrains inflation. "A few" saw disinflation risk, "a few" felt risks tilted upside over longer horizon. On financial conditions, "some" vs. "others" on whether improvement is sustainable. This is moderate disagreement on nuances but broad agreement on direction. No formal dissents mentioned. Fits 25-32.

Direction: Moderate. Some variation but broad agreement. CHECK: Reasonable.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Substantial discussion of financial conditions -- an entire paragraph devoted to financial markets (improvement, corporate debt, equity prices, Treasury yields, feedback loops), plus discussion of bank credit tightness, bank vulnerability, commercial real estate. Financial conditions are a significant topic but not the dominant one. Fits 50-58.

Direction: Moderate-to-prominent attention. CHECK: Matches the era.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "All agreed on the importance of the Federal Reserve continuing to communicate that it has the tools and willingness to begin withdrawing monetary policy accommodation at the appropriate time and pace to prevent any persistent increase in inflation." The language "extended period" was the guidance language at this time. The discussion of balance sheet effects and exit communication suggests a mild shift toward thinking about future normalization, but the current stance is firmly accommodative. The forward signal is still easing/maintaining accommodation. Fits 14-20.

Direction: Dovish forward lean. Higher = more hawkish. Score well below 50. CHECK: Matches. But the exit communication emphasis nudges it slightly higher than pure easing commitment.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants expressed considerable uncertainty about the likely strength of the upturn once those supports were withdrawn." "Households' saving behavior remained an important source of uncertainty in the outlook." Multiple sources of uncertainty cited. But there is also rising confidence that recovery is under way. "Considerable uncertainty" language = 60-70 per rubric. However, the overall tone is more confident than peak uncertainty -- recovery is confirmed. Fits 55-62.

Direction: Elevated. Higher = more uncertainty. Score above 50. CHECK: Matches.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (24) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (20): Both well below 50, consistent. Labor slightly lower because of explicit "weak" language.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (20) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (17): Both dovish, consistent. Forward guidance slightly more dovish because it reflects the "extended period" posture.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 24 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Most participants anticipated that slack in both labor and product markets would be substantial over the next few years, leading to subdued and potentially declining wage and price inflation." "A few continued to see some risk of substantial further disinflation, but that risk had eased somewhat further over the intermeeting period." "Many participants viewed the risks to their inflation outlook over the next few quarters as being roughly balanced."
- Center of gravity: Inflation well below target with substantial slack expected to keep prices subdued, but active deflation fears have faded somewhat.
- Minority views: A few see further disinflation risk (would push lower); a few see upside risk over longer horizon (would push higher). These roughly offset.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 24 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Most participants anticipated that slack in both labor and product markets would be substantial over the next few years." "The economy was projected to expand over the remainder of 2009 and during 2010, but at a pace that was unlikely to reduce the unemployment rate appreciably."
- Center of gravity: Economy far below potential with substantial slack expected to persist for years. Recovery has begun but is "quite restrained."
- Minority views: Relatively narrow disagreement on the basic assessment; variation is on how fast the gap closes.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 20 +/-4 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Labor market conditions remained weak." "Job losses remained sizable and the unemployment rate was high." "The unusually large fraction of those who were working part time for economic reasons, the unusually low level of the average workweek...all pointed to a period of modest job gains, and thus only a slow decline in the unemployment rate."
- Center of gravity: Very weak labor market with high unemployment, high underemployment, and only a slow improvement expected. Firms will be slow to hire.
- Minority views: Little disagreement on the weak state of labor markets.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 32 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Consumer spending had picked up more than expected over the intermeeting period, but participants saw that increase as partly reflecting special factors like the cash-for-clunkers program." "Participants generally expected no more than moderate growth in consumer spending over the near term. Households still faced considerable headwinds." "Firms appeared to be reducing inventories and fixed investment at a slower pace than earlier in the year." Business contacts "remained cautious about the recovery."
- Center of gravity: Spending and investment still below trend. Consumer spending picking up but driven by temporary factors; business investment still contracting, though more slowly. Significant headwinds persist.
- Minority views: Some participants noted risk of even higher saving rates, which would push consumption lower.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 64 +/-8 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Credit from banks remained difficult to obtain and costly for many borrowers." "Credit remained quite tight for many businesses and households dependent on banks, and many regional and small banks were vulnerable." BUT also: "Financial market conditions had continued to improve over the past several months." "Markets for corporate debt continued to improve, private credit spreads narrowed further."
- Center of gravity: Financial conditions still tight overall, especially for bank-dependent borrowers, but improving significantly from crisis peaks. Capital markets recovery contrasts with persistent bank credit tightness.
- Minority views: Some see a positive feedback loop of improving conditions; others emphasize remaining strains and risks of deterioration as government programs wind down.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 20 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "All agreed on the importance of the Federal Reserve continuing to communicate that it has the tools and willingness to begin withdrawing monetary policy accommodation at the appropriate time." Discussion of balance sheet size and inflation expectations. Maintained accommodation while discussing exit strategy communication.
- Center of gravity: Firmly dovish -- maintaining maximum accommodation with no near-term tightening considered. Exit strategy discussion is about communication/credibility, not imminent action.
- Minority views: Some emphasis on inflation expectations and balance sheet risks suggests a minority attentive to upside inflation risks, but no one advocates near-term tightening.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 28 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Broad agreement on recovery being under way but restrained. Variation on inflation: "some participants were skeptical of the usefulness of measures of resource utilization in gauging inflation pressures." On financial conditions: divergent views between those seeing a positive feedback loop and those emphasizing remaining strains. On housing: "many" marked up forecasts while "some viewed the improvement as quite tentative."
- Center of gravity: Broad agreement on the overall picture with moderate differences on risk emphasis and nuances. No formal dissents noted.
- Minority views: Disagreement is more about degree and risk weighting than fundamental direction.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 55 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: Extensive discussion of financial market improvement (corporate debt, spreads, equities, Treasury yields), debate about positive feedback loops vs. remaining strains, bank lending declining, commercial real estate deteriorating, bank vulnerability. An entire paragraph devoted to financial developments.
- Center of gravity: Financial stability/conditions are a moderately prominent topic. Multiple participants engage substantively with financial market conditions, but economic conditions (output, labor, spending) receive comparable or greater emphasis.
- Minority views: N/A for this attention-based measure.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 17 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "All agreed on the importance of the Federal Reserve continuing to communicate that it has the tools and willingness to begin withdrawing monetary policy accommodation at the appropriate time and pace." Discussion of how balance sheet size affects inflation expectations. The emphasis on "extended period" language and maintaining accommodation.
- Center of gravity: Forward signal is dovish -- continued accommodation expected for an extended period. The exit strategy discussion is about credibility and communication, not signaling near-term tightening.
- Minority views: The emphasis on exit communication suggests some members are thinking ahead about normalization, but this is about framing, not timing.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 58 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Participants expressed considerable uncertainty about the likely strength of the upturn once those supports were withdrawn or their effects waned." "Households' saving behavior remained an important source of uncertainty in the outlook." Multiple factors cited as uncertain: strength of recovery, housing sustainability, financial market improvement durability.
- Center of gravity: Elevated uncertainty. Participants are more confident a recovery is under way than at earlier meetings, but "considerable uncertainty" remains about its strength and sustainability.
- Minority views: N/A -- broadly shared sense of elevated uncertainty.

### Optional Dimensions

- Evidence: "All participants recognized that inflation expectations are a key determinant of inflation, and that various measures of inflation expectations, although imperfect, needed to be carefully monitored." "To keep inflation expectations well anchored, all agreed on the importance of the Federal Reserve continuing to communicate that it has the tools and willingness to begin withdrawing monetary policy accommodation."
- Center of gravity: Expectations appear anchored but there is active concern about keeping them anchored given the large balance sheet. Slightly below midpoint reflecting the subdued inflation environment.

**CREDIT_STRESS: 66 +/-8 [High confidence]**

- Center of gravity: Bank credit severely stressed; capital market credit improving. Net: above-normal stress, concentrated in bank lending.

**BANKING_STRESS: 68 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Many regional and small banks were vulnerable to the deteriorating performance of commercial real estate loans." "All categories of bank lending continued to decline."
- Center of gravity: Banking system still under significant stress, especially smaller/regional banks exposed to CRE.
