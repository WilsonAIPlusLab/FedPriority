Fed had cut rates in fall 1998 (LTCM/global turmoil) and began reversing with a 25bp hike in June 1999. This meeting is the second hike in the tightening cycle.

"No persuasive evidence that price inflation was currently picking up" BUT "members nonetheless expressed concern about the risks of some acceleration." Current inflation is subdued but risks are tilted upward. This is slightly below the midpoint for current readings but with forward-looking concern — score in the 48-55 range. The fact that they raised rates partly on inflation risk suggests the center of gravity leans above neutral. Score: 52.

Score: 58.

Score: 48 — roughly neutral, having moved from loose to near-neutral.

This is a mildly hawkish forward lean — they're not committing to more tightening but the risks are tilted that way. Score: 58.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 52 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "there was no persuasive evidence in recent statistical measures that price inflation was currently picking up or that inflation expectations were rising"
- "members nonetheless expressed concern about the risks of some acceleration under foreseeable economic circumstances"
- "upturn in commodity prices, notably that of oil... direct and indirect effects of the dollar's depreciation"

**Center-of-gravity**: Current inflation is contained but participants see meaningful upside risks from tight labor, commodities, and dollar depreciation. Score slightly above neutral reflecting forward risk tilt.

**Minority views**: None explicitly opposing the inflation risk view; one member thought low inflation argued against tightening, suggesting lower perceived pressure.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 58 ±7 [High confidence]

- "many members saw the risks to this outlook as tilted to the upside"

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy running above potential with strong domestic demand; members hope for deceleration to potential but see upside risks.

**Minority views**: None — broad agreement on the assessment.

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor markets are clearly tight with shortages reported, and members worry about further tightening. Consistent with late-1990s anchor of 65-75.

**Minority views**: None — universal agreement on tightness.

**Center-of-gravity**: Near-consensus for 25bp hike, motivated by preemptive inflation risk management. Moderate hawkish — they acted but adopted symmetric directive.
