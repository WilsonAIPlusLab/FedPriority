# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2007-05-09

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format, so I must focus only on participant-attributed language ("participants noted," "members favored," "most expected," etc.) and ignore staff content.

**Era context**: May 2007 — the economy is decelerating from the mid-2000s expansion. Housing correction is deepening. Subprime turmoil is emerging but seen as contained. Fed funds rate at 5.25% (the plateau after the 2004-06 hiking cycle). Core inflation remains "uncomfortably high." This is the pre-crisis period where the Fed is on hold, watching inflation while growth slows.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all" (3 uses — all favored hold, all agreed upside inflation risk, all agreed predominant concern is inflation), "nearly all" (core inflation uncomfortably high), "most" (expected core inflation to slow gradually; labor market tightness as upside risk to inflation; continued to expect housing correction), "many" (potential output growth more optimistic than staff; business contacts reported difficulties recruiting), "several" (dollar decline could reinforce import price pressure; labor compensation only modestly increased; moderation in construction employment), "some" (inflation expectations drift risk; resource utilization effects on inflation; business contacts resistant to margin squeeze).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as ABOVE target. "Nearly all participants viewed core inflation as remaining uncomfortably high." "All participants agreed that the risks around the anticipated moderation in inflation were to the upside." March readings were more favorable but followed "several months of elevated inflation data" and "price pressures were not yet viewed as convincingly on a downward trend." Global demand contributing to price pressures. This is clearly above target but not dramatically so — "somewhat elevated" with upside risks. Score range: 61-68. The word "uncomfortably high" and universal upside risk tilts this toward the upper end.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: The economy has slowed — "sub-par performance" in first part of year. But participants expect growth "a little below the economy's trend rate" through remainder of 2007, then at trend in 2008. Labor markets "relatively tight." Many participants more optimistic about potential output than staff. The economy is near potential but slightly below trend growth, suggesting a small negative output gap developing. Housing is a drag. Score should be near 48-50 — slightly below potential. The labor market tightness pulls it back toward potential. Score: 48.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor markets appeared to remain relatively tight." "Unemployment continued around the low levels seen since last fall." "Many business contacts reported difficulties in recruiting suitably qualified workers." However, "aggregate measures of labor compensation had so far increased only modestly, perhaps suggesting that the labor market might be less stretched than it appeared." This is tight but with some ambiguity. Score: 57-60. Center of gravity is tight but not extremely so.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "appeared to have slowed" — real spending on goods "flattened out," contacts report "softening." Expected to grow at "more moderate pace." Business investment "seemed most likely to move higher" supported by strong balance sheets, but "current sluggishness of equipment outlays could persist." Net: below-trend consumer spending partially offset by improving-but-still-sluggish business investment. Score: 42-45, slightly below trend.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Mortgage rates available to prime borrowers remained well below their levels of last summer." "Favorable financial conditions" supporting business investment. Subprime turmoil "remained relatively well contained." "Strong corporate balance sheets." Conditions are loose to neutral — credit is available, conditions are "favorable." Score: 28-32.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All members favored keeping the rate at 5.25%. "Predominant concern remained the risk that inflation would fail to moderate." Downside risks to growth "diminished slightly." The stance is hold with a hawkish lean — inflation is the predominant concern, no hint of easing, but no strong push for hiking either. Score: 58-62.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All members favored keeping the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent." Unanimous. Views are fairly aligned — "all" agreed on inflation upside risks, though there's some variation on labor market interpretation and resource utilization effects on inflation. This is broad agreement with minor differences. Score: 18-22.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Housing/subprime mentioned but described as "contained." Financial conditions discussed briefly as favorable. Subprime turmoil noted but not dwelled on. This is moderate attention — housing market correction is a significant topic but not framed primarily as financial stability. Score: 40-45.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Future policy adjustments would depend on the evolution of the outlook for both inflation and economic growth." The predominant concern is inflation failing to moderate. No signal of rate changes in either direction. This is a hold with mild tightening bias (inflation is the predominant concern). Score: 58-62.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Considerable uncertainty surrounded" the inflation judgment. "Considerable uncertainty attended the prospects for inflation." Housing adjustment duration uncertain. Productivity trends uncertain. Multiple sources of uncertainty highlighted. Score: 48-55.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (48) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (58): Divergent but explained — participants themselves note the tension between slowing output growth and tight labor markets, discussing measurement issues and lagged construction adjustment.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (60) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (60): Aligned — hold with mild hawkish lean, inflation predominant concern.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says "uncomfortably high," upside risks. Score 65 = correct direction.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Economy slightly below trend. Score 48 = correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Conditions are "favorable," loose. Score 30 = correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 65 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Nearly all participants viewed core inflation as remaining uncomfortably high and stressed the importance of further moderation."
- "All participants agreed that the risks around the anticipated moderation in inflation were to the upside."
- "Price pressures were not yet viewed as convincingly on a downward trend."
- "The strength of global demand could contribute to price pressures at home."

**Center-of-gravity**: Near-unanimity that inflation is uncomfortably above target with upside risks, though most expect gradual moderation. This places it clearly above target but not at alarming levels.

**Minority views**: Some noted March readings were more favorable, which slightly tempers the upside.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 48 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The pace of economic expansion had slowed in the first part of this year, but the recent sub-par performance probably exaggerated the weakness of underlying demand."
- "Real GDP would advance at a pace a little below the economy's trend rate of growth through the remainder of this year and then pick up to a rate broadly in line with the economy's trend rate in 2008."
- "Many participants commented that their view of potential output growth was somewhat more optimistic than that of the staff."

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy near potential but growing slightly below trend, implying a small and slowly widening output gap. Not yet materially below potential.

**Minority views**: Labor market tightness data suggests the gap may be negligible; housing drag suggests it could widen further.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 58 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Labor markets appeared to remain relatively tight. Unemployment continued around the low levels seen since last fall."
- "Many business contacts reported difficulties in recruiting suitably qualified workers, especially for certain types of professional and skilled positions."
- "Aggregate measures of labor compensation had so far increased only modestly, perhaps suggesting that the labor market might be less stretched than it appeared."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is tight but not severely so — tight conditions evidenced by low unemployment and recruitment difficulties, partially offset by modest wage growth.

**Minority views**: Several participants see compensation data as suggesting less tightness than headline numbers imply.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 43 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Growth in consumer spending appeared to have slowed over the past few months. Real spending on goods had flattened out."
- "Meeting participants expected household expenditure to grow at a more moderate pace in coming quarters."
- "The growth of business fixed investment seemed most likely to move higher in coming quarters, supported by strong corporate balance sheets and profits."

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending below trend and decelerating, partially offset by improving business investment outlook. Net assessment is slightly below trend.

**Minority views**: Downside risk from housing impact on consumer spending; upside from capital goods upturn.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 30 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Mortgage rates available to prime borrowers remained well below their levels of last summer."
- "Favorable financial conditions" cited as supporting business investment.
- "The turmoil in the subprime market evidently had not spread to the rest of the mortgage market."
- "Strong corporate balance sheets and profits."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are loose — credit is available, conditions are favorable, subprime stress is contained and not spreading. Somewhat loose to neutral.

**Minority views**: Some concern that conditions could become "less supportive" going forward.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 60 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All members favored keeping the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent."
- "The risk that inflation would fail to moderate as desired remained the Committee's predominant concern."
- "Downside risks were judged to have diminished slightly."

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous hold with a clear hawkish lean — inflation is the dominant concern, growth risks have diminished, no discussion of easing. But no active push for further tightening either.

**Minority views**: Minimal — very narrow range of views on this occasion.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 20 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All members favored keeping the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent."
- "All participants agreed that the risks around the anticipated moderation in inflation were to the upside."
- Some variation on labor market interpretation and resource utilization effects, but no fundamental disagreements.

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement with only minor emphasis differences. No dissents.

**Minority views**: Differences exist on labor market interpretation and whether resource utilization affects inflation, but these don't rise to meaningful disagreement.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 42 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Extensive discussion of housing correction, subprime turmoil (contained), and potential spillovers to consumer spending.
- "The turmoil in the subprime market evidently had not spread to the rest of the mortgage market."
- Financial conditions discussed as favorable for business investment.

**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate attention to financial conditions/stability. Housing and subprime are discussed at meaningful length but framed as contained. Financial conditions are a context factor, not the dominant concern.

**Minority views**: None notable — participants broadly agree subprime is contained.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 60 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Future policy adjustments would depend on the evolution of the outlook for both inflation and economic growth."
- "The Committee agreed that the statement should continue to note that their predominant policy concern was the risk that inflation would fail to moderate as expected."
- No signal of rate cuts; inflation concern is predominant.

**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal is mildly hawkish — data-dependent but with inflation as the predominant concern. No easing bias despite growth slowdown.

**Minority views**: None expressed on forward guidance direction.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Considerable uncertainty surrounded that judgment" (re: core inflation moderating).
- "Considerable uncertainty attended the prospects for inflation."
- Uncertainty about housing inventory correction duration, productivity trends, and whether labor market tightness will generate wage pressures.

**Center-of-gravity**: Elevated uncertainty — "considerable uncertainty" is used twice, and multiple sources of genuine uncertainty are identified (inflation path, housing, productivity, labor market dynamics).

**Minority views**: N/A — uncertainty itself is broadly shared.
