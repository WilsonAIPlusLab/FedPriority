# FOMC Meeting Scoring — 2001-05-15 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Unsegmented pre-2009 format — staff and participant content are mixed, but participant language cues are clear ("members commented," "members noted," "members saw," etc.). I will score only participant-attributed content.

**Era context**: May 2001 — the U.S. is in the early stages of what would become the 2001 recession (officially dated March–November 2001). The Fed had been cutting rates aggressively since January 2001 (from 6.5% down), and this meeting cuts another 50bp to 4.0%. The tech bubble had burst, manufacturing was weak, and inventories were being liquidated. Inflation was not the primary concern but some members noted an updrift in core measures.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all" (support further easing, accept risk-tilted statement), "most" (don't foresee significant rise in inflation), "many" (weak upturn possible, inventory adjustment may last), "a number of" (consumer spending held up, inflation updrift worrisome), "some" (pessimism limited to near term, policy only marginally accommodative), "one" (preferred 25bp), "two others" (could have accepted 25bp).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Members are mixed. "A number of members commented that the persisting updrift in some key measures of core inflation had become increasingly worrisome." But "most members did not foresee a significant rise in inflation." They cite competitive conditions making it hard to raise prices, expect easing labor pressures to damp inflation. Some rise in bond yields may reflect inflation expectations. Net: slightly above neutral — there IS some observed updrift that is "worrisome" to several, but the majority expects it to be contained. Direction: modestly above midpoint. Score ~52-55.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Slowdown in the expansion to a now quite sluggish pace"; "subpar economic growth"; "continuing weakness in the economy"; economy "displaying some signs of fragility." Labor markets still "tight by historical standards" but loosening. The economy is clearly below potential and weakening. Not a deep recession yet — members still expect upturn later in the year. Score ~35-38.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Widespread evidence of some lessening of pressures in most labor markets" but "labor markets were still tight by historical standards." Wage inflation hasn't declined yet. This is a market that WAS very tight and is loosening but hasn't reached slack. Score ~48-52. The language "still tight by historical standards" keeps it near or just below 50, while "lessening pressures" pulls it down. Net: ~48.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "held up reasonably well" despite adverse factors. Housing "robust." But business capital spending is falling sharply — "further, and possibly larger, declines"; firms "canceling, cutting back, or stretching out" capex. Inventory liquidation is a major drag. Overall spending picture is clearly below trend, dragged down by investment collapse, partially offset by resilient consumption. Score ~33-37.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Stock market declines noted as negative wealth effects. Bond yields rising somewhat. But mortgage rates are "very attractive," housing is strong. "Buildup in liquidity" noted. Policy has eased substantially. Financial conditions seem mixed — some tightening from equity losses but monetary accommodation providing offset. Limited direct discussion of credit conditions/spreads. Score ~48-52 (roughly neutral).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All but one support 50bp cut. Risk assessment tilted toward weakness. "Some members noted that the end of the easing process might be near." But still cutting 50bp and maintaining a dovish risk tilt. Clearly dovish — but with awareness that easing may be nearly done. Score ~28-33. This is moderate dovish: majority favors easing, implementing a 50bp cut.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Risk assessment tilted toward weakness (dovish). BUT "members anticipated that a neutral balance of risks statement could be appropriate before long" and "some members noted that the end of the easing process might be near." This is an easing lean with signals that the easing cycle is approaching its end. Score ~30-35 — still dovish forward signal but with data-dependent hedging toward neutral.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One member preferred 25bp, two others could have accepted 25bp. But all supported further easing in principle. No formal dissent on the action itself (all voted for it or could support it). The disagreement is about pace (50 vs 25bp), not direction. This is moderate — some differences about magnitude. Score ~30-35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Stock market declines mentioned as wealth effect on consumption. Bond yields discussed in inflation expectations context. No dedicated discussion of financial stability risks, systemic concerns, or market functioning. Brief/contextual mentions only. Score ~15-20.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Members were uncertain as to the degree and timing of the strengthening in final demand"; "many saw a variety of factors that pointed to the possibility that the upturn could be weaker or more delayed"; "How long inventory cutbacks would continue... remained a key uncertainty"; "unknown persistence and dimension"; "prevailing uncertainties." Multiple expressions of genuine uncertainty about the outlook. This is clearly elevated. Score ~55-60.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (~36) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (~48): These diverge somewhat. Justified because the labor market is a lagging indicator — the economy has slowed sharply but labor markets haven't fully reflected that yet. Members explicitly note markets are "still tight by historical standards" even as growth is subpar.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (~30) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (~32): Aligned — both dovish but with hints the cycle is nearing its end.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says modest updrift but expected to be contained. Score 53 — just above neutral. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Economy is below potential. Score 36 — below 50. Matches.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Markets loosening but still historically tight. Score 48 — just below midpoint. Matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: **53** ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "A number of members commented that the persisting updrift in some key measures of core inflation had become increasingly worrisome."
- "Most members did not foresee a significant rise in inflation as a likely prospect" citing competitive conditions and expected easing of labor pressures.
- "Not all measures of core inflation had accelerated; in particular, core PCE price inflation had been quite stable."

**Center of gravity**: Members acknowledge a modest updrift in some inflation measures, but the majority expects it to be contained by slowing growth and easing labor pressures. Slightly above neutral.
**Minority views**: "A number of members" were genuinely worried about inflation updrift; risk of stronger rebound adding price pressure noted.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: **36** ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The slowdown in the expansion to a now quite sluggish pace was likely to be more prolonged than they had anticipated earlier."
- "It was not entirely clear that the slowing in the growth of the economy had bottomed out."
- "Continuing subpar economic growth" and "economy perhaps still in the midst of a process of weakening growth in aggregate demand."

**Center of gravity**: The economy is clearly operating below potential with sluggish and possibly still-weakening growth. Not a deep recession — members expect eventual upturn — but significant slack is developing.
**Minority views**: "A relatively prompt and strong rebound could not be ruled out" — keeps uncertainty band from being too narrow.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: **48** ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Widespread evidence of some lessening of pressures in most labor markets across the nation had not yet resulted in lower wage inflation."
- "Labor markets were still tight by historical standards."
- "Further increases in unemployment in the period just ahead."

**Center of gravity**: Labor markets are loosening from previously tight levels but remain historically tight. The transition is underway but not complete. Just below the neutral/full-employment midpoint.
**Minority views**: Some members emphasize residual tightness; others emphasize ongoing softening and expected further unemployment increases.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: **34** ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending had held up reasonably well in recent months despite a variety of adverse developments."
- "A softening in business demand for capital equipment had accounted for much of the slowdown"; "the latest available data on new orders pointed to further, and possibly larger, declines in business spending."
- "Ongoing efforts to reduce excess inventories were continuing to curb output" — inventory liquidation a major drag.

**Center of gravity**: Business investment is contracting sharply, inventory liquidation is significant, and consumer spending is merely holding steady rather than growing. Net spending picture is clearly below trend.
**Minority views**: Housing described as "robust" and energy investment supportive, preventing a lower score.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: **50** ±10 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Negative wealth effects of stock market declines" mentioned as headwind.
- "Very attractive mortgage interest rates" supporting housing — indicating loose conditions in that segment.
- "Buildup in liquidity" from easing; "policy might still be considered to be only marginally accommodative."

**Center of gravity**: Mixed signals — equity market losses tighten conditions while aggressive rate cuts and mortgage rate declines loosen them. Limited direct discussion of credit spreads or lending standards. Roughly neutral.
**Minority views**: Some members see policy as only "marginally accommodative" despite substantial cuts, suggesting conditions may be less easy than the rate cuts imply.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: **30** ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All but one of the members indicated that they could support a proposal calling for further easing of reserve conditions consistent with a 50 basis point reduction."
- "All the members agreed that further easing was desirable in light of what they viewed as the continuing weakness in the economy."
- "Some members noted that the end of the easing process might be near" — tempering the dovishness somewhat.

**Center of gravity**: Clear dovish consensus — all favor easing, most support 50bp. The dovishness is moderated by awareness that the easing cycle may be approaching its end and by inflation concerns.
**Minority views**: One member preferred 25bp; two others could accept 25bp. These members emphasized caution given cumulative easing and inflation risks.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: **32** ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "One member expressed a strong preference for a 25 basis point reduction and two others indicated that they could have accepted that more limited easing move."
- "Despite their somewhat differing preferences, all the members agreed that further easing was desirable."
- Members who preferred 25bp emphasized different risk weighting (inflation vs. growth) but shared the same directional assessment.

**Center of gravity**: Moderate differences about pace of easing (50 vs. 25bp) but agreement on direction. No fundamental disagreement about the economic assessment or need for easing. Classic pace-disagreement pattern.
**Minority views**: The 25bp camp represents a meaningful minority but not a sharp division.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: **18** ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Stock market declines discussed as wealth effect on consumers, not as financial stability concern per se.
- Bond yields discussed in context of inflation expectations.
- No discussion of systemic risk, credit market dysfunction, or financial stability frameworks.

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are mentioned incidentally in the context of their economic effects (wealth, mortgage rates) but financial stability as a topic receives essentially no dedicated attention.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: **32** ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The Committee continued to regard the risks to the economic outlook as being tilted toward weakness even after today's easing action."
- "Members anticipated that a neutral balance of risks statement could be appropriate before long."
- "It was not clear how much more the federal funds rate might have to be reduced after today... some members noted that the end of the easing process might be near."

**Center of gravity**: Forward signal is still dovish (risks tilted toward weakness, more easing possible) but the committee is signaling that the easing cycle is approaching its conclusion. An easing lean with data dependence and a clear nod toward future neutrality.
**Minority views**: Some members more firmly signal the end of easing is near, pulling forward guidance toward neutral.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: **57** ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The members were uncertain as to the degree and timing of the strengthening in final demand."
- "How long inventory cutbacks would continue to exert a significant drag on the economic expansion remained a key uncertainty."
- "Economy perhaps still in the midst of a process of weakening growth in aggregate demand of unknown persistence and dimension."
- "Given prevailing uncertainties, it would be premature for the Committee to shift its balance of risks statement."

**Center of gravity**: Multiple expressions of genuine uncertainty about the timing and strength of recovery, inventory adjustment duration, and the persistence of weakness. Clearly elevated above normal levels.
