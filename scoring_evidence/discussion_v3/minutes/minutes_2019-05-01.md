# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2019-05-01 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented format with clearly labeled participant discussion sections beginning at "Participants' Views on Current Conditions and the Economic Outlook." Staff sections are clearly delineated and will be excluded from scoring.

**Era context**: May 2019 -- mid-cycle pause. The Fed had hiked four times in 2018 and paused in January 2019, adopting a "patient" approach. The economy was growing solidly, the labor market was tight, but inflation had softened below 2%. Trade tensions with China were ongoing but appeared to be easing at this point. Financial conditions had recovered from the Q4 2018 sell-off.

**Quantifier distribution**: The text uses "participants agreed" (broad consensus), "many" (majority-minus), "several" (notable minority), "some" (smaller subset), "a number of" (moderate group), "a few" (small minority), and "a couple" (very small). There is broad agreement on the hold decision and patience, with the range of views primarily around inflation trajectory and whether future moves should be up or down.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target. "Both overall inflation and inflation for items other than food and energy had declined and were running below 2 percent." "Inflation pressures remained muted." Many viewed the dip as "transitory" linked to "idiosyncratic factors," but the actual readings are below target. "Some viewed the downside risks to inflation as having increased." The trimmed mean was "stable at or close to 2 percent" -- providing some offset. Direction: below target. Magnitude: "muted," "declined," "running below 2 percent" -- this is moderate below-target language. Anchor: 2013-19 below target = 38-42. The 2019 readings were slightly below target but not alarming, with idiosyncratic factors partly blamed. Score around 38-40.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants describe the economy as growing at a "solid rate" with strong labor markets. The staff projects GDP "above potential output growth in 2019 and 2020." Resource utilization "appeared to be high." "Tight resource utilization" is mentioned. The bright-line rule: "above potential" language means score MUST be >50. "Solid" growth, "above potential" -- this is modestly above potential. Score around 53-55.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor market conditions remained strong." "Unemployment rate had stayed low." "Contacts in a number of Districts continued to report shortages of qualified workers." "Tight labor markets." "Few reports of a broad-based pickup in wage growth" -- so wages not surging despite tightness. This is clearly tight. Anchor: 2016-17 = 50. This is tighter than that. Score around 58-62.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: First-quarter household spending and business fixed investment "slowed." But "first-quarter softness in household spending was likely to prove temporary." "Growth in PCE in coming months was expected to be solid." Business investment growth "moderated" but "continued growth in capital expenditures this year, albeit probably at a slower pace." Net: moderate growth, temporary soft patch. "Solid" going forward. Anchor: 2014-19 "solid pace" = 50-55. Score around 50-52 given the acknowledged softening.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Financial market conditions had improved." "Financial conditions had improved since the turn of the year." "Improvement in financial conditions was regarded by many participants as providing support for the outlook." Corporate bond spreads narrowed, equity prices rose, financing conditions described as "accommodative." This is clearly on the loose side. Anchor: 2017-19 normal = 45-55. But the text emphasizes improvement and accommodation. Score around 35-38 (somewhat loose to neutral, leaning loose).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Unanimous hold. "A patient approach to determining future adjustments to the target range... remained appropriate." "Patient approach would likely remain warranted, especially in an environment of continued moderate economic growth and muted inflation pressures." A few thought the Committee "would likely need to firm the stance" eventually. A few others thought subdued inflation might mean resource utilization not as high as unemployment suggests (dovish). Several worried inflation expectations could become anchored below target. Center of gravity: hold with patience, muted inflation keeping them from hiking. This is balanced to slightly dovish lean. Anchor: 2014-15 pre-liftoff = 40-50. Score around 46-48 -- hold-steady with mild patience/easing lean.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Unanimous vote. Broad agreement on the hold and patience. Some divergence on inflation outlook (transitory vs. concerning) and whether future moves should be tightening or easing. "A few" on each side. Modest disagreement. Score around 22-28.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: "Among those participants who commented on financial stability, most highlighted recent developments related to leveraged loans and corporate bonds as well as the current high level of nonfinancial corporate indebtedness." "A few participants suggested that heightened leverage... could render the business sector more sensitive to economic downturns." "A couple of participants observed that asset valuations in some markets appeared high." This is a dedicated but limited discussion. Anchor: 2013-19 typical = 20-35. Score around 30-35.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Patient approach... remained appropriate." "Even if global economic and financial conditions continued to improve, a patient approach would likely remain warranted." No signals of imminent rate changes. A few mentioned eventual firming might be needed; several worried about inflation expectations drifting down. The forward signal is neutral-to-slightly-dovish -- patience with no directional commitment. Anchor: 2019 mid-cycle = 35-45. Score around 42-45.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Some of the risks and uncertainties... had moderated." "Many uncertainties affecting the U.S. and global economic outlooks had receded, though some risks remained." "Downside risks to the outlook for growth remain" (most). Trade uncertainty still present. This is moderate uncertainty -- somewhat reduced from earlier in the year but not gone. Anchor: standard = 25-35. Score around 32-36.

### Cross-dimension coherence check:
- OUTPUT_GAP (54) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (60): Both above 50, labor tighter than output gap -- consistent with an economy where the labor market tightened more visibly than output.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (47) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (43): Both near the balanced-to-slightly-dovish range. Consistent -- patient hold with no directional commitment, mild dovish lean from below-target inflation.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 39 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Inflation pressures remained muted and that the most recent data on overall inflation, and inflation for items other than food and energy, had come in lower than expected."
- "Some viewed the downside risks to inflation as having increased."
- "The trimmed mean measure had been stable at or close to 2 percent over recent months."

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants collectively see inflation as running below 2% target, with recent readings softer than expected. Idiosyncratic factors partially explain the dip, and the trimmed mean is near 2%, preventing a lower score. The center view is below-target but not alarmingly so.

**Minority views**: "Some" concerned about downside risks to inflation; "a few" worried inflation expectations could anchor below target. "A few" on the other side thought eventual firming might be needed, implying inflation could pick up.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 54 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity had risen at a solid rate."
- Staff projected GDP "to expand at a rate above the staff's estimate of potential output growth in 2019 and 2020."
- "Resource utilization appeared to be high" and "tight resource utilization."

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is operating modestly above potential, with strong labor markets and solid growth. The first-quarter composition was seen as unsustainable (inventories, net exports), but underlying growth remained above trend.

**Minority views**: "Some participants observed that... they expected real GDP growth to slow over the medium term, moving back toward their estimates of trend output growth."

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants agreed that labor market conditions remained strong."
- "Contacts in a number of Districts continued to report shortages of qualified workers, in some cases inducing businesses to find novel ways to attract new workers."
- "Few reports of a broad-based pickup in wage growth."

**Center-of-gravity**: The labor market is clearly tight -- low unemployment, worker shortages reported, strong job gains. However, wage growth has not broadly accelerated, which tempers the score somewhat relative to extreme tightness.

**Minority views**: "A few participants commented that labor market conditions in their Districts were putting upward pressure on compensation levels for lower-wage jobs" (suggesting even tighter conditions in some areas). "Several participants noted that business contacts expressed optimism that despite tight labor markets they would be able to find workers" (suggesting tightness manageable).

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 51 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Growth in household spending and business fixed investment had slowed in the first quarter."
- "First-quarter softness in household spending was likely to prove temporary. With the strong jobs market, rising incomes, and upbeat consumer sentiment, growth in PCE in coming months was expected to be solid."
- "Growth of business fixed investment had moderated... continued growth in capital expenditures this year, albeit probably at a slower pace than in 2018."

**Center-of-gravity**: Q1 showed a temporary soft patch in both consumption and investment, but participants expected a rebound. The net assessment is at-trend to modestly above, with forward momentum expected to be solid but not exceptional.

**Minority views**: "A few others reported less buoyant business sentiment." "A few others were less optimistic" about housing contributions.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 36 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Financial market conditions had improved following the period of stress observed over the fourth quarter of last year and that the volatility in prices and financial conditions had subsided."
- "The improvement in financial conditions was regarded by many participants as providing support for the outlook for economic growth and employment."
- Financing conditions described as "accommodative" across business, CRE, and residential mortgage markets.

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions had eased materially since Q4 2018 and were regarded as supportive/accommodative. Equity prices recovered, spreads narrowed, lending standards eased. This places conditions on the loose side of neutral.

**Minority views**: None noted tighter conditions; the concern was more about whether conditions were too easy (leveraged loans, corporate debt).

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 47 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants generally agreed that a patient approach to determining future adjustments to the target range for the federal funds rate remained appropriate."
- "A few participants noted that if the economy evolved as they expected, the Committee would likely need to firm the stance of monetary policy."
- "Several participants commented that if inflation did not show signs of moving up over coming quarters, there was a risk that inflation expectations could become anchored at levels below those consistent with the Committee's symmetric 2 percent objective."

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous hold with strong consensus on patience. The lean is very slightly dovish because the dominant concern is below-target inflation rather than overheating. However, the hold reflects balanced assessment, not an easing bias per se.

**Minority views**: "A few" lean hawkish (eventual firming needed). "A few" lean dovish (resource utilization may not be as high; inflation concerns). This creates the +-6 band.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 22 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Unanimous vote to maintain the target range.
- Broad agreement on patience and the economic assessment.
- Some divergence on inflation outlook and future policy direction: "a few" favored eventual tightening, "a few" others more concerned about low inflation/slack.

**Center-of-gravity**: Low disagreement. Unanimous vote with broad alignment on the patient approach. Minor differences about inflation interpretation and future direction, but these are modest "a few" vs. "a few" divergences, not sharp camps.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 32 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Among those participants who commented on financial stability, most highlighted recent developments related to leveraged loans and corporate bonds as well as the current high level of nonfinancial corporate indebtedness."
- "A few participants suggested that heightened leverage and associated debt burdens could render the business sector more sensitive to economic downturns."
- "A couple of participants observed that asset valuations in some markets appeared high, relative to fundamentals."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability received a dedicated discussion but from a subset of participants ("among those participants who commented"). The concerns were specific (leveraged loans, corporate debt, asset valuations) but did not dominate the meeting. This is limited-to-moderate attention.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 43 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "A patient approach to determining future adjustments to the target range for the federal funds rate remained appropriate."
- "Even if global economic and financial conditions continued to improve, a patient approach would likely remain warranted."
- "A few participants noted that if the economy evolved as they expected, the Committee would likely need to firm the stance of monetary policy."

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is essentially neutral -- patient, data-dependent, no directional commitment. The slight tilt is dovish given below-target inflation dominates concerns, but a few see eventual firming as appropriate. Net: neutral to slightly dovish forward lean.

**Minority views**: "A few" pointing toward eventual firming pulls the score slightly above a pure easing lean. "Several" worried about inflation expectations drifting lower pulls it slightly dovish.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 34 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Some of the risks and uncertainties that had surrounded their outlooks earlier in the year had moderated."
- "Many uncertainties affecting the U.S. and global economic outlooks had receded, though some risks remained."
- "Most participants observed that downside risks to the outlook for growth remain."

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty had receded from earlier in 2019 but had not disappeared. Trade policy, global growth, and inflation trajectory remained uncertain. This is somewhat elevated but trending toward normal -- consistent with standard caveats plus some remaining specific risks.
