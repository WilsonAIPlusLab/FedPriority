# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1995-09-26

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format -- staff and participant content are mixed. I will score only portions with clear participant attribution ("members commented," "members generally believed," "several members expressed," etc.).

**Era context**: September 1995. The Fed had been tightening through 1994-early 1995 (fed funds rate raised from 3% to 6%). By mid-1995 the economy had slowed, and the Fed had already cut once in July 1995. This meeting occurs during a period of moderate growth, with inflation having moderated but still a concern for some members.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all the members" (hold policy, no bias), "many members" (downside risks slightly greater; concern about no further progress on inflation), "several members" (policy might need easing), "some members" (direction of next move unclear), "a few members" (risk of inflation intensification; reservations about proposed legislation), "a number of members" (fiscal uncertainty; inflation likely contained).

**Core dimension directional assessments**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as having moderated but still present. "Slower increases in key measures of consumer and producer prices" are "welcome," but "many expressed concern...that significant further progress toward achieving stable prices might not be made." The labor market "remained tight" and "capacity utilization was still above its historical average." A few see risk of "intensification of inflationary pressures." Center of gravity: inflation is somewhat contained but not declining further -- slightly below or near the uncomfortable zone. Not alarming but not at target. This is a mid-1990s context where inflation was around 2.5-3%. Score should be in the 45-55 range -- modestly above comfortable levels, with inflation having moderated but persistent.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: "Growth at a pace averaging close to, or perhaps slightly below, the economy's potential." The economy has "considerable and desirable expansionary momentum." This is essentially at-potential language. Members see it near potential, maybe a touch below. Score: 48-52 range.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "The labor market remained tight." Workers had concerns about job security despite strong demand. "The rise in worker compensation had been unusually restrained in recent years in relation to the strong demand for workers." This is a tight labor market with some unusual restraint on wages. Score should be above 50 -- tight but with mitigating factors. Around 55-58.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "comparatively moderate growth" as the likely prospect. Business fixed investment "remained a strongly positive factor." Housing responding to lower mortgage rates. Inventory drag completing. Mixed but moderately positive overall. Score: 52-56.

5. **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Very limited direct participant discussion of financial conditions. Market interest rates had fallen. Mortgage rates more attractive. "Several members expressed the opinion that...a current policy stance that they viewed as slightly restrictive." This suggests conditions are somewhat tight from the policy side, but rates are declining. Minimal direct discussion of financial conditions by participants. Score around 50-55 -- slightly tight given the "slightly restrictive" characterization.

6. **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All the members supported a proposal to maintain an unchanged degree of pressure on reserve positions." No bias in the directive. Several members thought "policy might have to be eased eventually" due to downside risks. Other members wanted to wait for more assurance on inflation. The center of gravity is hold with a mild lean toward easing bias -- but not enough to act. Score: 45-48 (lean dovish to balanced).

7. **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Unanimous vote (11-0) with no bias. However, there are distinct camps -- several favoring eventual easing vs. others emphasizing inflation risks. "The direction of the next policy move was not clear in the view of some members." Moderate internal disagreement despite unanimous vote. Score: 30-35.

8. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Almost no discussion of financial stability by participants. Brief mentions of financial market reactions to fiscal policy, financial asset values supporting consumer spending. Very low emphasis. Score: 8-12.

9. **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: No bias in the directive. Several members lean toward easing eventually. Others see direction as unclear. The forward signal is essentially neutral with a very mild easing tilt. Score: 43-47.

10. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The outlook for economic activity remained subject to a variety of uncertainties, including the still unsettled course of the federal budget." Fiscal policy is "a major uncertainty." Inventory projections "subject to a high degree of uncertainty." Many members saw downside risks as slightly greater. This is somewhat elevated uncertainty. Score: 40-48.

**Cross-dimension coherence checks**:
- OUTPUT_GAP (~50) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (~56): Slight divergence is explainable -- the economy is near potential but the labor market is described as "tight," which is consistent with 1990s structural features (NAIRU debate).
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (~46) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (~45): Aligned -- both lean mildly dovish/neutral.

**Polarity verification**:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says moderate but persistent inflation, tight labor market, some risk of intensification. Score ~52 is correct (slightly above midpoint).
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says near potential, slightly below. Score ~49 is correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says "tight." Score ~56 is correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter conditions. "Slightly restrictive" policy stance. Score ~53 checks out.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 52 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the slower increases in key measures of consumer and producer prices since earlier in the year were a welcome development"
- "many expressed concern, however, that significant further progress toward achieving stable prices might not be made over the next year or two"
- "the labor market remained tight and capacity utilization was still above its historical average"
- "a few members commented that current forecasts were subject to a range of error that included a risk of some intensification of inflationary pressures"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Inflation has moderated from earlier in the year but remains at a level that most members do not expect to decline further; the labor market and capacity utilization keep latent pressure alive. This is slightly above the comfortable midpoint -- inflation is contained but not improving.

**Minority views**: A few members see upside risk to inflation (pushing score higher); a number see inflation as likely to remain contained (pulling lower). The band reflects this range.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 50 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Growth at a pace averaging close to, or perhaps slightly below, the economy's potential was viewed as the most likely prospect for the year ahead"
- "the economy...appeared on balance to have considerable and desirable expansionary momentum"
- "many members saw the risks of a shortfall from expectations as slightly greater than those of significantly faster growth"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Members explicitly describe the economy as growing near potential -- the quintessential at-potential characterization. The slight downside risk bias keeps the score right at or marginally below 50.

**Minority views**: Downside risks acknowledged by many but no one describes the economy as clearly below potential.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 56 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the labor market remained tight"
- "the rise in worker compensation had been unusually restrained in recent years in relation to the strong demand for workers"
- "continued strength in the demand for labor might be expected to induce more rapid increases in labor compensation over time"
- "still widespread concerns about job security associated with ongoing business restructuring and downsizing activities"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The labor market is described as tight with strong demand for workers, but wage pressures are unusually restrained due to worker job-security fears and restructuring. This is a moderately tight market -- above the midpoint but not severely so.

**Minority views**: Some members note that underlying employment cost factors were "not likely to change greatly," suggesting stable tightness rather than intensifying.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 54 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "members generally viewed comparatively moderate growth in consumer spending as a likely prospect"
- "Business fixed investment remained a strongly positive factor in the economy"
- "Housing demand was continuing to respond to more attractive mortgage rates"
- "Diminished growth in inventories still seemed to be retarding the expansion" but adjustments "largely completed"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumer spending is moderate, business investment is strong, housing is picking up, and the inventory drag is fading. Net assessment is slightly above trend -- solid but not booming.

**Minority views**: Some restraining factors noted (consumer debt loads, waning pent-up demand), but the strong business investment and housing recovery tilt the balance modestly positive.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 53 +/-8 [Low confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "several members expressed the opinion that...a current policy stance that they viewed as slightly restrictive"
- "increases that had occurred in the value of financial assets" (suggesting some looseness)
- "Housing demand was continuing to respond to more attractive mortgage rates" (rates had fallen)

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Very little direct participant discussion of financial conditions. The characterization of policy as "slightly restrictive" by several members, combined with falling market rates and rising asset values, suggests roughly neutral to slightly tight conditions. Low confidence due to minimal direct evidence.

**Minority views**: The description of policy as "slightly restrictive" is held by only "several members," not the majority.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 46 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "all the members supported a proposal to maintain an unchanged degree of pressure on reserve positions"
- "several members expressed the opinion that policy might have to be eased eventually in light of the downside risks"
- "Other members...placed more emphasis on current forecasts of little or no progress in reducing inflation...thought it would be premature to ease"
- "the direction of the next policy move was not clear in the view of some members"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Unanimous hold with no directive bias. The committee is split between those leaning toward eventual easing and those wanting to hold for inflation reasons. The center of gravity is a balanced-to-mildly-dovish hold, reflecting the acknowledgment of downside risks but no urgency to act.

**Minority views**: Several lean toward easing; others see the next move as unclear or possibly tightening. This split widens the uncertainty band.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Votes against this action: None" -- unanimous 11-0 vote
- Two distinct camps: several favoring eventual easing vs. others emphasizing inflation risks and premature easing concerns
- "the direction of the next policy move was not clear in the view of some members"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Unanimous vote with symmetric directive, but underlying disagreement about the future direction of policy. This is broad agreement on the current action with notable differences about future direction -- consistent with the 16-30 range.

**Minority views**: The disagreement is about future direction, not the current action, which limits dissent intensity.

- Passing references to financial asset values, financial market reactions to fiscal policy
- No substantive participant discussion of financial stability risks, credit conditions, or market stress

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial stability is essentially not a topic of participant discussion at this meeting. Only incidental references to financial markets in the context of other topics. Consistent with the 1993-96 anchor of 1-10.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 45 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "did not include a presumption about the likely direction of any adjustments to policy during the intermeeting period"
- "several members expressed the opinion that policy might have to be eased eventually"
- "Other members...thought it would be premature to ease policy without greater assurance that inflation had been contained"
- "the direction of the next policy move was not clear in the view of some members"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal is essentially neutral with a very mild easing tilt. Several members lean toward eventual easing, but no consensus on direction. The directive is explicitly symmetric. Score is just below 50, reflecting the mild dovish lean without commitment.

**Minority views**: The hawkish contingent that sees easing as premature pulls the forward guidance back toward neutral.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 45 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The outlook for economic activity remained subject to a variety of uncertainties, including the still unsettled course of the federal budget"
- "fiscal policy developments constituted a major uncertainty in the outlook for economic activity"
- "projections of inventory behavior were subject to a high degree of uncertainty"
- "many members saw the risks of a shortfall from expectations as slightly greater than those of significantly faster growth"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Uncertainty is somewhat elevated, driven primarily by the fiscal policy debate, inventory dynamics, and general economic trajectory. Multiple sources of uncertainty are cited with language like "major uncertainty" and "high degree of uncertainty." This exceeds normal caveats but falls short of deep concern.

**Minority views**: None on the uncertainty dimension itself -- the elevated uncertainty appears broadly shared.
