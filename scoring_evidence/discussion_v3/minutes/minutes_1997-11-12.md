# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1997-11-12

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format — staff and participant content are mixed, so I focus only on participant-attributed language ("members focused," "members agreed," "members cited," etc.).

**Era context**: November 1997. Late 1990s expansion — strong growth, tight labor markets, subdued inflation (the "Goldilocks" economy). Asian financial crisis is unfolding. Productivity acceleration emerging. Fed funds rate at 5.5%.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all but one" (twice — on hold decision and tightening bias), "members agreed," "some members," "a number of members," "other members." One formal dissent (Broaddus, hawkish).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Members note "persistence of subdued increases in prices," inflation "remained relatively subdued in comparison with past experience under broadly similar economic conditions." However, "members felt that the risks remained in the direction of rising price inflation." Special factors (dollar, productivity, health costs) keeping inflation low, but members expect favorable influences to "erode over the year ahead." Current inflation subdued but upside risks acknowledged. Direction: below target currently but risks tilted up. Score should be in the 38-45 range — subdued now, but members see upside risk. I'll score 42.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Continued solid advance in economic activity, spurred by strength in all major sectors." "Unsustainable pace of domestic demand and rising resource utilization." "Clear risk of additional pressures on already tight resources." "Aggregate final demand was so strong that... economic activity and the associated demand for labor having expanded at an unsustainable pace." This is clearly above potential. Anchor for late 90s boom: 57-63. Score 60.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Considerable emphasis to recent developments in labor markets." "Low and falling rates of unemployment, and a diminishing supply of new workers." "Tight labor markets throughout the nation." "Demand for many types of workers exceeded the supply in many regions." "Growth of economic activity in various parts of the country was being held back by the scarcity of labor." This is very tight. Anchor for 1999-2000: 65-75. This is 1997 and already very tight. Score 70.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Strength in consumer spending had provided an important underpinning for robust economic expansion." "Substantial growth was likely to persist." "Outlook for capital spending also remained quite favorable" with "ongoing surge in such spending." "Private domestic demand most likely would continue to display considerable strength." This is clearly above trend — robust/surging. Score 65.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Broadly supportive financial conditions." "Credit from a wide variety of lenders appeared to be amply available on favorable terms, perhaps overly so." "Ready availability of finance on attractive terms." Despite Asian turmoil, conditions remain loose. "Overall financial conditions remained quite supportive despite the recent market turmoil." Score around 25 — loose conditions.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All but one hold steady, but with asymmetric directive toward tightening retained. "Members continued to view the next policy move as more likely to be in the direction of some firming." One dissent for tightening (Broaddus). "Some members" stressed unsustainable pace called for near-term tightening. Others emphasized productivity trends raising questions about inflation pickup. This is a hold with a clear tightening bias. Score 57 — lean hawkish, hold with tightening bias.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal dissent (Broaddus, hawkish). Within the majority, there were differences — "some members" wanted near-term tightening, others focused on uncertainties. But all but one endorsed the hold. Score 30 — broad agreement with one dissent and some internal differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Asian turmoil discussed prominently. "Turmoil in Asian financial markets and economies" is one of two key uncertainties. Equity market volatility mentioned. "Markets still skittish." "Increased uncertainty about asset values." Dollar appreciation concerns. This gets moderate attention — several members address it meaningfully. Score 45.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Asymmetric directive toward tightening retained. "Members continued to view the next policy move as more likely to be in the direction of some firming than toward easing." But current action is hold, and Asian uncertainty injects caution. Mild tightening bias forward. Score 62.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Two important influences that were injecting new uncertainties into this outlook." "Ultimate extent of the adjustment in Asia was unknown." "Too early to judge the persistence of the uptrend in productivity growth." "Considerable debate" on extent/timing of inflation pickup. "Uncertainties about the economic outlook." This is somewhat elevated — specific new uncertainties acknowledged. Score 42.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (60) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70): Both above 50, consistent with late-90s boom. Labor tighter than output gap reflects explicit emphasis on labor scarcity.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (57) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (62): Aligned — hold with tightening bias. Forward guidance slightly more hawkish reflecting the retained asymmetric directive.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says subdued but risks tilted up. 42 (slightly below midpoint) matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says above potential/unsustainable. 60 matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says loose/supportive. 25 matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 42 ±8 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "persistence of subdued increases in prices"; "inflation had remained relatively subdued in comparison with past experience under broadly similar economic conditions"; "members felt that the risks remained in the direction of rising price inflation though the extent and timing of that outcome were subject to considerable debate"
- **Center of gravity**: Inflation currently subdued, but members see clear upside risk from tight labor markets, with favorable factors (productivity, dollar) expected to erode.
- **Minority views**: Some members stressed that productivity/cost trends raised questions about whether inflation would pick up at all.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 60 ±7 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "continued solid advance in economic activity, spurred by strength in all major sectors"; "clear risk of additional pressures on already tight resources"; "unsustainable pace of domestic demand and rising resource utilization"
- **Center of gravity**: Economy clearly above potential with all major sectors strong; demand growth described as unsustainable.
- **Minority views**: Asian spillovers could moderate growth, but this is forward-looking uncertainty, not current assessment.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 70 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "low and falling rates of unemployment, and a diminishing supply of new workers"; "tight labor markets throughout the nation"; "demand for many types of workers exceeded the supply in many regions"; "growth of economic activity in various parts of the country was being held back by the scarcity of labor"
- **Center of gravity**: Extremely tight labor markets with widespread shortages constraining growth; wage pressures moderate but supplemented by off-measure incentives.
- **Minority views**: None noted — broad agreement on tightness.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 65 ±7 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Strength in consumer spending had provided an important underpinning for robust economic expansion, and substantial growth was likely to persist"; "ongoing surge in such spending" (capital investment); "private domestic demand most likely would continue to display considerable strength"
- **Center of gravity**: Both consumption and investment well above trend, described as robust and surging.
- **Minority views**: Some vulnerability to Asian-related financial developments noted.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 25 ±8 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "broadly supportive financial conditions"; "credit from a wide variety of lenders appeared to be amply available on favorable terms, perhaps overly so in present circumstances"; "ready availability of finance on attractive terms"
- **Center of gravity**: Financial conditions loose and supportive despite Asian turmoil. Credit amply available.
- **Minority views**: Markets described as "skittish" due to Asia, but this had not translated into tighter financial conditions domestically.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 57 ±8 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "all but one member endorsed a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance"; "members continued to view the next policy move as more likely to be in the direction of some firming"; Mr. Broaddus dissented favoring "modest tightening"; "other members stressed that the unsustainable pace of domestic demand and rising resource utilization seemed to call for a near-term tightening"
- **Center of gravity**: Hold with clear tightening bias. Majority holds due to Asian uncertainty and productivity questions, but several wanted to tighten now.
- **Minority views**: One dissent for tightening; "some" members within majority also leaned toward near-term tightening.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 ±7 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: One formal dissent (Broaddus). "All but one member" supported the directive. Within the majority, differences between those emphasizing productivity/uncertainty and those stressing unsustainable demand.
- **Center of gravity**: Broad agreement on hold with tightening bias, one dissent, moderate internal debate about timing.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 45 ±8 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Turmoil in Asian financial markets and economies" identified as one of two key uncertainties; "markets still skittish, a tightening of U.S. monetary policy risked an oversized reaction"; "increased uncertainty about asset values could engender greater caution on spending"; "substantial decline in equity values would reduce household wealth"
- **Center of gravity**: Asian financial turmoil is a prominent topic with meaningful discussion, but it is framed primarily through the lens of economic spillover rather than systemic financial stability concern.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 62 ±7 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "retain in the operating paragraph of the directive the existing asymmetry toward restraint that was initially adopted at the May meeting"; "a somewhat higher federal funds rate would be acceptable or a slightly lower federal funds rate might be acceptable"; "members continued to view the next policy move as more likely to be in the direction of some firming"
- **Center of gravity**: Mild tightening bias in forward guidance. Asymmetric directive toward restraint maintained, but no urgency to act imminently.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 42 ±8 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "two important influences that were injecting new uncertainties into this outlook"; "ultimate extent of the adjustment in Asia was unknown"; "too early to judge the persistence of the uptrend in productivity growth"; "the extent and timing of that outcome were subject to considerable debate"
- **Center of gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty driven by two specific new factors (Asia and productivity), but overall economic picture still relatively clear.
