# FOMC Scoring: 2013-07-31 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented -- participant discussion section is clearly demarcated from staff content.

**Era context**: Mid-2013, post-GFC recovery. QE3 is underway ($85B/month). The "taper tantrum" had just occurred following the June 2013 meeting and press conference. Economy growing modestly; unemployment still elevated around 7.6%; inflation running below 2% target. Key debate is when to begin tapering asset purchases.

**Quantifier distribution**: "almost all" (comfortable with contingent outlook for purchases; agreed no change yet appropriate), "most" (saw additional info in statement as potentially useful), "many" (saw difficulties with additional info), "a number of" (less confident about near-term pickup; viewed low inflation as reflecting deficient demand), "several" (confident housing resilient; favored including additional info; willing to contemplate lowering threshold), "some" (felt financial conditions tightened significantly; felt market expectations well aligned), "a few" (concerned wealth wouldn't translate to spending; suggested inflation readings temporary; stressed need for patience; others pointed to contingent plan suggesting tapering soon; concern about lowering unemployment threshold).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW target. "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's longer-run objective." "Recent readings on inflation were below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent." A range of views on return to target -- "a few" thought it temporary and would return "reasonably quickly," while "a number of others" viewed low inflation as "largely reflecting persistently deficient aggregate demand" suggesting it "could remain below 2 percent for a protracted period." Center of gravity: clearly below target, with debate on persistence. Score should be in the 26-35 range. Anchor guidance: 38-42 for 2013-19 below target, but this meeting shows more concern than typical -- inflation is specifically described as below objective with risk of persistence. I'll score 30, as this is actively below target with genuine concern expressed by a meaningful number of participants.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as BELOW potential. "growth in economic activity during the first half of this year was somewhat below their earlier expectations." "economic activity expanded at a modest pace." Slack is explicitly referenced: "slow reduction in the slack in labor and product markets." Fiscal restraint is a drag. Growth is "modest" and expected to "pick up somewhat." This is clearly below potential but in recovery. Anchor: 35-42 for 2010-14 recovery. Score: 38.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see the labor market as WEAK/slack remains. "the unemployment rate remained elevated." "the continuing low readings on the participation rate and the employment-to-population ratio, together with a high incidence of workers being employed part time for economic reasons, were generally seen as indicating that overall labor market conditions remained weak." This is clearly below full employment with multiple indicators of slack. Anchor: 35-40 for 2011-14. Score: 35.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumer spending continued to advance, but spending on items other than motor vehicles was relatively soft." Housing "continued to pick up." Business outlook "still appeared to be mixed." "Household spending and business fixed investment continued to advance." Net position: advancing but at a below-trend pace with mixed signals. Score: 40.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: This is a major topic at this meeting due to the taper tantrum. "some participants felt that, as a result of recent financial market developments, overall financial market conditions had tightened significantly." "Several others, however, judged that the rise in rates was likely to exert relatively little restraint, or that the increase in equity prices and easing in bank lending standards would largely offset the effects." "Some participants also stated that financial developments...might have helped put the financial system on a more sustainable footing." The center of gravity shows conditions have tightened from very loose levels, but debate exists about how much. Rates rose, mortgage rates rose, but equity prices up and bank lending easing. Net: somewhat tighter than before but from very accommodative levels. Score: 42 (somewhat loose to neutral side, having moved up from very loose).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "almost all Committee members agreed that a change in the purchase program was not yet appropriate." Still purchasing $85B/month. "highly accommodative stance of monetary policy was warranted." But the contingent taper plan is on the table -- "almost all participants confirmed that they were broadly comfortable with the characterization of the contingent outlook" for tapering later in 2013. One dissent wanting more explicit tapering signal. "a few others pointed to the contingent plan...and suggested that it might soon be time to slow somewhat the pace of purchases." This is clearly still in QE territory, but with tapering on the near horizon. During QE debates, participants arguing "how much more to buy" are in 15-25 range. Here they are debating when to begin reducing purchases, which is a step more hawkish than peak QE. Score: 32 (moderate dovish but with clear lean toward beginning normalization).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal dissent (wanting more explicit tapering statement). Views ranged from "a few emphasized importance of being patient" to "a few others suggested it might soon be time to slow purchases." Debate on whether to include additional info in statement, with "several" on each side. Debate on forward guidance thresholds. This is moderate disagreement -- one dissent, views ranging from patient accommodation to near-term tapering. Score: 38.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Substantial discussion of financial market developments -- the taper tantrum aftermath is discussed at length. Market volatility, interest rate increases, term premiums, speculative positions unwinding, equity prices, bank lending standards. "Some participants also stated that financial developments during the intermeeting period might have helped put the financial system on a more sustainable footing, insofar as those developments were associated with an unwinding of unsustainable speculative positions." This is a prominent topic. Score: 48.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is toward eventual tapering. "if economic conditions improved broadly as expected, the Committee would moderate the pace of its securities purchases later this year" and "conclude the purchase program around the middle of 2014." Discussion of maintaining the 6.5% unemployment threshold for federal funds rate. "several participants were willing to contemplate lowering the unemployment threshold if additional accommodation were to become necessary." Discussion of reinforcing that rate hikes are distant even after taper. The forward signal is still accommodative overall -- tapering is reducing accommodation but rates staying at zero for a long time, with thresholds in place. Score: 18 (mild easing bias/low for long, with taper approaching but rates guidance still strongly dovish). Anchor: 8-15 for 2014 taper; this is just before taper begins, so slightly above that range.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "a number of participants indicated that they were somewhat less confident about a near-term pickup in economic growth." "heightened financial market uncertainty about the path of monetary policy." "uncertainty about the effects of the federal spending sequestration and related furloughs clouded the outlook." Fiscal policy effects uncertain, market reactions uncertain, inflation persistence uncertain. This is somewhat elevated uncertainty. Score: 40.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (38) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (35): Both below 50, consistent -- economy below potential with weak labor market. Labor slightly lower reflecting emphasis on multiple slack indicators. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (32) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (18): Both dovish but diverge somewhat. Current lean is moderately dovish (still doing QE but talking about tapering). Forward guidance is more dovish (rates at zero with thresholds, contemplating even lower thresholds). This divergence makes sense: tapering is about reducing the pace of NEW accommodation, while rate guidance signals accommodation lasting a long time.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (30): Higher = more pressure. Evidence says below target. 30 < 50. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP (38): Higher = more above potential. Evidence says below potential with slack. 38 < 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (42): Higher = tighter. Conditions loosened from very accommodative but have tightened from the post-June market moves. 42 = somewhat loose to neutral. Makes sense -- still accommodative overall but having tightened from very loose.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (32): Higher = more hawkish. 32 = moderate dovish. QE still running but taper approaching. Correct.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (18): Higher = more hawkish. 18 = strong easing bias. Threshold-based guidance with zero rates. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 30 +-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's longer-run objective."
- "A number of others...viewed the low inflation readings as largely reflecting persistently deficient aggregate demand, implying that inflation could remain below 2 percent for a protracted period."
- "A few participants, who felt that the recent low inflation rates were unlikely to persist...suggested that...inflation could be expected to return to 2 percent reasonably quickly."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Inflation is clearly running below the 2% target, with a meaningful contingent of participants concerned it could remain below target for a protracted period due to persistent demand weakness, while only a few see the shortfall as temporary.

**Minority views**: "A few" saw the below-target readings as transitory and expected a reasonably quick return to 2%, pulling the uncertainty band upward.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 38 +-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "economic activity expanded at a modest pace during the first half of the year"
- "a number of participants indicated that growth in economic activity during the first half of this year was somewhat below their earlier expectations"
- "Participants generally continued to anticipate that the growth of real GDP would pick up somewhat in the second half of 2013 and strengthen further thereafter"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is operating below potential with a modest pace of expansion, slack remaining in labor and product markets, and fiscal restraint serving as a headwind, though participants expect gradual improvement.

**Minority views**: None sharply divergent; the range is around the degree of shortfall rather than the direction.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 35 +-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the unemployment rate remained elevated, and the continuing low readings on the participation rate and the employment-to-population ratio, together with a high incidence of workers being employed part time for economic reasons, were generally seen as indicating that overall labor market conditions remained weak"
- "The June employment report showed continued solid gains in payrolls"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Despite solid payroll gains, the labor market remains clearly weak with elevated unemployment, low participation, and high involuntary part-time employment. Multiple indicators point to substantial remaining slack.

**Minority views**: Some nuance around whether employment gains had been stronger than expected given output growth, but no one characterized the labor market as tight.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 40 +-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending continued to advance, but spending on items other than motor vehicles was relatively soft."
- "In the business sector, the outlook still appeared to be mixed."
- "the housing sector was strengthening" but "recent mortgage rate increases might serve to restrain housing activity"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Spending and investment are advancing but at a below-trend pace, with consumer spending soft outside autos, business investment mixed, and housing strengthening but facing headwinds from rising mortgage rates.

**Minority views**: "A few participants expressed concern that higher household wealth might not translate into greater consumer spending," pulling somewhat lower, while housing optimists (several) pull modestly higher.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 42 +-10 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "some participants felt that, as a result of recent financial market developments, overall financial market conditions had tightened significantly, importantly reflecting larger term premiums"
- "Several others, however, judged that the rise in rates was likely to exert relatively little restraint, or that the increase in equity prices and easing in bank lending standards would largely offset the effects"
- "Some participants also stated that financial developments...might have helped put the financial system on a more sustainable footing, insofar as those developments were associated with an unwinding of unsustainable speculative positions"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions have tightened from very accommodative levels due to the post-June rate rise, but participants are divided on the significance, with some seeing meaningful tightening and others noting offsets from equity gains and easier bank lending.

**Minority views**: Wide spread -- "some" see significant tightening (pushing toward 50+), while "several others" see offsets keeping conditions supportive (pushing toward 35). This justifies the wide +-10 band.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 32 +-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "almost all Committee members agreed that a change in the purchase program was not yet appropriate"
- "members judged that a highly accommodative stance of monetary policy was warranted"
- "a few others pointed to the contingent plan...and suggested that it might soon be time to slow somewhat the pace of purchases"
- One member dissented wanting "a more explicit statement...that asset purchases would be reduced in the near future"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The committee maintains its strongly accommodative stance with $85B/month in purchases, but the lean is clearly toward beginning to taper in the near term, making this moderately dovish with a normalization trajectory emerging.

**Minority views**: One dissent for more explicit tapering signal pulls hawkish; "a few" emphasizing patience pull dovish.

- Debate between "a few" urging patience vs. "a few others" suggesting it might be time to taper soon

**Center-of-gravity summary**: One formal dissent plus a meaningful spread of views on the timing and pace of tapering, with camps ranging from patient accommodation to near-term reduction, indicates moderate disagreement.

**Minority views**: N/A (this dimension captures the spread itself).

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 48 +-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Extensive discussion of the taper tantrum aftermath: "Both domestic and foreign asset markets were volatile at times during the intermeeting period"
- "heightened financial market uncertainty about the path of monetary policy and a shift of market expectations toward less policy accommodation"
- Discussion of "unwinding of unsustainable speculative positions or an increase in term premiums from extraordinarily low levels"
- Discussion of mortgage rate effects on housing, equity price effects on wealth

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions and stability are a prominent topic at this meeting, with several participants discussing market volatility, rate movements, speculative positioning, and the interaction between policy communications and financial conditions at meaningful length.

**Minority views**: N/A.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 18 +-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the Committee reaffirmed its intention to keep the target federal funds rate at" near-zero with 6.5% unemployment threshold
- "there was support for maintaining the current numerical thresholds in the forward guidance"
- "several participants were willing to contemplate lowering the unemployment threshold if additional accommodation were to become necessary"
- Discussion of reinforcing that rates would stay low even after 6.5% threshold reached

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal remains firmly accommodative -- threshold-based guidance keeps rates at zero, with discussion of potentially lowering thresholds or adding reinforcement that rate hikes would be distant even past thresholds. The taper discussion adds some hawkish lean to the purchase program outlook but the rate guidance is strongly dovish.

**Minority views**: The contingent taper plan (begin reducing later in 2013, conclude mid-2014) pulls the score somewhat higher from pure threshold-based guidance levels.

---

**Score: 40 +-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "a number of participants indicated that they were somewhat less confident about a near-term pickup in economic growth"
- "heightened financial market uncertainty about the path of monetary policy"
- "uncertainty about the effects of the federal spending sequestration and related furloughs clouded the outlook"
- Uncertainty about whether wealth would translate to spending, whether housing would be resilient to higher rates, and how long low inflation would persist

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Uncertainty is somewhat elevated, with participants expressing reduced confidence about the near-term growth pickup, uncertainty about fiscal effects, market reactions to policy communications, and the persistence of below-target inflation.

**Minority views**: None particularly divergent.
