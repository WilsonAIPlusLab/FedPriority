# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2011-04-27

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented format with clear separation of staff review and participant discussion sections.

**Era context**: April 2011 — roughly two years into the recovery from the Great Recession. QE2 ($600 billion Treasury purchases) nearing completion. Unemployment still elevated (~8.8%). Headline inflation rising due to commodity/energy price surge, but core inflation subdued. Fed funds rate at 0-0.25%. The meeting featured extensive discussion of normalization strategy (exit principles) but no policy changes.

**Quantifier distribution**: "participants agreed" (several times), "most participants," "many participants," "several participants," "some participants," "a few participants," "one participant" — full range used, indicating differentiated views.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as above the midpoint because headline inflation driven by energy/commodity prices is clearly elevated, and "many participants had become more concerned about the upside risks to the inflation outlook." However, "core inflation and other indicators of underlying inflation over the medium term had increased modestly in recent months, but their levels remained subdued." "Participants generally anticipated that the higher level of overall inflation would be transitory." The center of gravity: headline elevated, core still subdued, transitory expectation BUT rising concern about pass-through. Per the rubric gotcha, score the perceived-now elevated part. This puts it in the 55-62 range — modestly to clearly above target on headline, but core still subdued tempers this. Score: 58.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic recovery was proceeding at a moderate pace" — still recovering, implying below potential. Unemployment at 8.8%, "remained elevated." "Low rates of resource utilization" explicitly stated. Housing "remained very weak." This is clearly below potential with slack remaining. Score should be < 50. The "moderate" recovery language and continued slack point to 35-40 range. Score: 37.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 8.8%, "remained elevated." "Overall conditions in labor markets had continued to improve, albeit gradually." "Increases in wages have been subdued." "Signs of rising wage pressures were reportedly limited to a few skilled job categories." Participants discussed whether the unemployment rate drop "might be overstating the degree of improvement." This is clearly slack labor market — improving but far from tight. Anchor: 35-40 for 2011-14. Score: 35.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "remained moderate despite the effects of higher gasoline and food prices." Investment in E&S "fairly robust." But housing "remained distressed," commercial real estate "continued to be weak." Consumer sentiment "declined noticeably." Net picture: moderate consumer spending, robust business investment, very weak housing. The mix is below trend overall but not contracting. Score: 40.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Financial market conditions continued to improve over the intermeeting period." Equity prices rose. Banks reported easing of lending terms. Consumer credit conditions "eased somewhat." But "loan demand remained weak" and "commercial real estate lending" had "tight credit conditions." Municipal bond market "remained somewhat strained." Overall conditions moving from tight toward neutral but still somewhat restrictive in some areas. Score: 42.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Committee maintained QE2, kept rates at 0-0.25%, kept "extended period" language. "No changes to the Committee's asset purchase program or to its target range for the federal funds rate were warranted." However, extensive normalization strategy discussion signals thinking about exit. "Some participants expressed the view that...the Committee would need to be prepared to begin taking steps toward less-accommodative policy." "A few members viewed the increase in inflation risks as suggesting that economic conditions might well evolve in a way that would warrant...less-accommodative policy sooner than currently anticipated." But other participants warned against early exit. The center of gravity is hold steady with mild lean toward eventually tightening. QE2 completing as planned (not extended). This is in the 35-42 range — late-QE era, preparing to end purchases but not yet tightening. Score: 38.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissents mentioned. But "a few members remained uncertain about the benefits of the asset purchase program." Views on normalization showed "a range of views" — from those wanting to sell assets before rate hikes to those wanting sales after. "Some participants" wanted less accommodation soon, others warned against early exit. "Some members pointed out...another program of asset purchases would be unlikely." This is moderate disagreement without formal dissents — different camps on timing/sequencing. Score: 38.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Brief discussion: "a few participants expressed concern that the easing of credit conditions was creating incentives for increased leverage and risk-taking in some areas." European sovereign debt mentioned. But financial stability was not a major focus — most discussion was about real economy and inflation. Score: 25.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate for an extended period" maintained. But extensive normalization discussion, and "a few members" thought conditions might warrant tightening "sooner than currently anticipated." QE2 completing, not being extended. The forward signal is still accommodative but with the first hints of eventual normalization. Score: 25.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "A number now judged those risks to be tilted to the downside." Multiple downside risks enumerated: energy prices, European fiscal strains, Japan supply disruptions, fiscal adjustments, debt limit. "Most participants continued to see the risks to their outlooks for economic growth as being broadly balanced" but with notable exceptions. Upside inflation risks also discussed. This is somewhat elevated uncertainty. Score: 42.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (37) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (35): Both below 50, consistent — economy below potential with slack labor market. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (38) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (25): Both dovish side but FG more dovish because "extended period" language maintained. The gap reflects that current action is accommodative while some members are starting to think about normalization. This is reasonable — the forward guidance language is still firmly accommodative even as the discussion turns toward exit.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (58): Higher = more pressure. Evidence says headline elevated, core subdued but rising concern. 58 > 50 matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP (37): Higher = more above potential. Evidence says below potential with slack. 37 < 50 matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (42): Higher = tighter. Conditions improving/easing toward neutral. 42 < 50 matches — somewhat loose side of neutral.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 58 ±8 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Participants discussed the recent rise in inflation, which had been driven largely by significant increases in energy and, to a somewhat lesser extent, other commodity prices." "Many participants had become more concerned about the upside risks to the inflation outlook." "Core inflation and other indicators of underlying inflation over the medium term had increased modestly in recent months, but their levels remained subdued."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Headline inflation clearly elevated from commodity prices with rising concern about pass-through, but core still subdued and most view the rise as transitory.
- **Minority views**: "One participant suggested that excess liquidity might be leading to speculation in commodity markets." A few saw inflation risks as warranting sooner tightening.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 37 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The economic recovery was proceeding at a moderate pace, albeit somewhat slower than had been anticipated." "Low rates of resource utilization" cited as reason for extended period language. Unemployment at 8.8%, "remained elevated."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Economy clearly below potential — moderate recovery with substantial remaining slack in labor and product markets.
- **Minority views**: Some noted the rapid decline in unemployment "suggested the possibility of stronger-than-anticipated economic growth."

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 35 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Overall conditions in labor markets had continued to improve, albeit gradually." "Increases in wages have been subdued." "Signs of rising wage pressures were reportedly limited to a few skilled job categories." Participants discussed whether the unemployment rate drop "might be overstating the degree of improvement."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Labor market improving but still quite slack — unemployment elevated, wage growth muted, considerable hidden slack.
- **Minority views**: "A few participants noted that firms may be poised to accelerate their pace of hiring because they have exhausted potential productivity gains."

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 40 ±6 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Growth in consumer spending remained moderate despite the effects of higher gasoline and food prices." "Investment in equipment and software was fairly robust." "The housing sector remained distressed...commercial real estate sector continued to be weak."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Mixed picture — moderate consumer spending, robust business investment, but very weak housing and commercial real estate drag the overall assessment below trend.
- **Minority views**: None significant; broad agreement on the mixed picture.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 42 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Financial market conditions continued to improve over the intermeeting period." "Banks reported an easing of lending terms on C&I loans." "Consumer credit conditions also eased somewhat from the tight conditions seen during the recession." But "tight credit conditions for commercial real estate lending."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Conditions easing from post-crisis tightness toward neutral, but pockets of tightness remain (CRE, municipal bonds).
- **Minority views**: "A few participants expressed concern that the easing of credit conditions was creating incentives for increased leverage and risk-taking."

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 38 ±10 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: Committee maintained QE2 completion, 0-0.25% rate, and "extended period" language. "Some participants expressed the view that...the Committee would need to be prepared to begin taking steps toward less-accommodative policy." But others "indicated that underlying inflation remained subdued...an early exit could unnecessarily damp the ongoing economic recovery."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Still firmly accommodative but approaching the end of QE2 with emerging discussion about eventual normalization. No eagerness to tighten soon.
- **Minority views**: "A few of these participants thought that economic conditions might warrant action to raise the federal funds rate target or to sell assets...later this year." Others warned against early exit.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 38 ±7 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: No formal dissents. But "participants expressed a range of views on some aspects of a normalization strategy." "A few members remained uncertain about the benefits of the asset purchase program." "Some members pointed out that...another program of asset purchases would be unlikely to outweigh the costs."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Moderate disagreement on normalization timing/sequencing and inflation risks, but consensus on current policy action (hold). No formal dissents.
- **Minority views**: Range from those wanting earlier tightening to those cautioning against premature exit.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 25 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "A few participants expressed concern that the easing of credit conditions was creating incentives for increased leverage and risk-taking in some areas, such as leveraged syndicated loans and loans to finance land acquisition." Brief mention of European sovereign stress (Greece, Portugal).
- **Center-of-gravity**: Limited discussion — a few passing mentions of financial stability concerns, not a major focus of the meeting.
- **Minority views**: The few who raised leverage/risk-taking concerns were outliers in emphasis.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 25 ±8 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The Committee continued to anticipate that economic conditions...were likely to warrant exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate for an extended period." "A few members viewed the increase in inflation risks as suggesting that economic conditions might well evolve in a way that would warrant the Committee taking steps toward less-accommodative policy sooner than currently anticipated."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Forward guidance firmly accommodative ("extended period"), but the normalization discussion and a few members' hawkish signals introduce some upward pull.
- **Minority views**: A few members signaling possible earlier tightening.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 42 ±7 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Most participants continued to see the risks to their outlooks for economic growth as being broadly balanced, a number now judged those risks to be tilted to the downside." Downside risks enumerated: energy prices, Europe, Japan, fiscal issues, debt limit. "They all agreed on the importance of closely monitoring developments regarding inflation and inflation expectations."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty — multiple risk factors identified, some shift toward downside risks, but not deep uncertainty. Standard caveats elevated by Japan disaster, European crisis, and commodity price volatility.
- **Minority views**: Split between balanced and downside risk assessment.
