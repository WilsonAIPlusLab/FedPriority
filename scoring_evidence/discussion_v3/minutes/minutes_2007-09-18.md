# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2007-09-18

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format -- staff and participant content mixed. I will score only portions with participant language cues ("participants noted," "members judged," "several cited").

**Era context**: September 2007 -- early stages of the financial crisis. Subprime mortgage problems spreading to broader credit markets. The Committee cut the fed funds rate by 50 basis points at this meeting. Housing downturn deepening; credit markets under significant strain but not yet in full crisis mode.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all members" (favored easing, agreed on 50bp cut); "participants" broadly (used throughout for general views); "some participants" (weakening consumer spending, concerns about weaker economy leading to further financial tightening); "several participants" (reports of labor shortages); "some" (nascent slowdown); no strong "most" vs "a few" splits on most topics -- views are broadly aligned with downside concern.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as slightly below the midpoint concern-wise. Key evidence: "incoming data on core inflation continued to be favorable," participants were "a little more confident that the decline in inflation earlier this year would be sustained," and "inflation expectations seemed to be contained." However, they "remained concerned about possible upside risks" citing higher benefit costs, rising unit labor costs, resource utilization, and dollar depreciation. The center of gravity is that inflation has improved but upside risks remain. Direction: modestly below elevated, roughly near target with some residual concern. Score around 48-52. The favorable data and contained expectations pull toward below 50, but the cited upside risk factors (unit labor costs, resource utilization, dollar) keep it near 50. I'll score 50.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says improving but with upside risks. 50 = balanced. Matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as likely to run below potential going forward. "economic growth likely to run below its potential for a while." However, the economy hasn't collapsed -- it's the housing sector dragging, with other sectors still holding. The current state is near potential but heading below. Current assessment: slightly below potential. Score around 43-45.

Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. "Below potential" language requires score < 50. 44 matches.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Mixed signals. "trend growth in jobs had fallen off," financial services layoffs announced, temp workers softening, construction employment weakening. BUT "labor markets across the country generally remained fairly tight, with several participants citing continued reports of shortages of labor." Initial claims "did not indicate a substantial and widespread weakening." Center of gravity: still fairly tight but softening. Score around 48-50. The "generally remained fairly tight" with "shortages" suggests near NAIRU or slightly above, but with clear signs of weakening. I'll score 48.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. "Fairly tight" with "shortages" but weakening trend. 48 = near NAIRU, slightly loosening. Matches.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending expected to "continue to expand at a moderate pace" but with downside risks (auto sales flagged, confidence down, home equity credit tightening). Business investment "likely to remain healthy" -- investment-grade credit unimpeded, many firms internally financed. But lower-rated firm financing "slowed sharply," commercial real estate credit tightened, some business executives "more cautious." Net: moderate but with clear downside risks emerging. Score around 42-45. Below trend but not contracting.

Polarity check: Higher = stronger. "Moderate pace" with downside risks = slightly below trend. 43 matches.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: This is a dominant theme. Credit markets tightening significantly. "banks might need to take a large volume of assets onto their balance sheets," "banks to tighten the availability of credit," "risk spreads probably would be wider and credit terms tighter," subprime mortgages "essentially unavailable," jumbo borrowers facing "higher rates and tighter lending standards," leveraged loans and spec-grade issuance "slowed sharply." But: conforming mortgages still available, investment-grade credit "unimpeded," conditions "seemed to have improved somewhat in the most recent days." This is clearly tighter than neutral but not yet a full crisis. Score around 65-68.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter conditions. Significant tightening described across multiple credit channels. 67 matches.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All members favored an easing" -- unanimous 50bp cut. Rationale was to "help offset the effects of tighter financial conditions." "Without such policy action, members saw a risk that tightening credit conditions and an intensifying housing correction would lead to significant broader weakness." This is clearly dovish action. All members agreed. But it's a responsive cut to financial conditions, not panic-mode emergency easing. Score around 25-30. A 50bp cut with unanimous support is a strong dovish signal.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Unanimous 50bp cut = strongly dovish. 27 matches.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All members favored an easing" and "all members agreed that a rate cut of 50 basis points at this meeting was the most prudent course of action." No dissents mentioned, broad agreement. Very low dissent. Score around 10-12.

Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Unanimous agreement. 10 matches.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial conditions and credit market turmoil dominate this meeting discussion. The first two full paragraphs are entirely about credit market developments. Housing discussed primarily through financial lens. Even the policy discussion centers on financial conditions. This is a very prominent focus. Score around 72-78.

Polarity check: Higher = more attention. Financial stability/conditions dominate the meeting. 75 matches.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The Committee "decided to refrain from providing an explicit assessment of the balance of risks" due to uncertainty. "Future actions would depend on how economic prospects were affected by evolving market developments." No explicit forward signal -- data-dependent and deliberately vague. But the context of a 50bp cut with growth "likely to run below its potential for a while" implies an easing lean. Score around 30-35 -- easing lean with strong data dependence.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Deliberate ambiguity after a big cut, with easing lean. 32 matches.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Extremely prominent. "particularly high uncertainty" about the outlook. "the unusual nature of the current financial shock." "incoming data in many cases were of limited value in assessing the likely evolution." The Committee refused to provide a balance-of-risks statement because it "could give the mistaken impression that the Committee was more certain about the economic outlook than was in fact the case." "heightened uncertainty about the economic outlook." Score around 72-78.

Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty expressed. "Particularly high uncertainty," refusal to state balance of risks. 75 matches.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (44) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (48): Close together, both near/slightly below midpoint. Labor is slightly higher because markets are still "fairly tight" even as growth slows. Coherent -- economy transitioning from near-potential to below-potential.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (27) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (32): Both dovish, with forward guidance slightly less dovish (deliberate ambiguity vs. the clear dovish action). Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 50 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "incoming data on core inflation continued to be favorable, and they generally were a little more confident that the decline in inflation earlier this year would be sustained"
- "Participants nonetheless remained concerned about possible upside risks to inflation. Higher benefit costs, rising unit labor costs more generally, reduced markups, and levels of resource utilization... that remained relatively high were all cited as factors"
- "Inflation expectations seemed to be contained"

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation recently favorable and expectations contained, but participants see meaningful upside risks from costs and resource utilization. Balanced assessment.

**Minority views**: No distinct minority -- all seem to share this balanced-with-upside-risk view.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 44 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "economic growth likely to run below its potential for a while"
- "recent data and anecdotal information pointed to a possible nascent slowdown in the pace of expansion"
- "risks to growth skewed to the downside"

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy near but dipping below potential, with forward expectation clearly below potential. Not yet in contraction.

**Minority views**: Some noted "resilience of the economy" leaving open possibility that effects prove "limited," pulling slightly toward 50.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 48 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "labor markets across the country generally remained fairly tight, with several participants citing continued reports of shortages of labor from their contacts in some sectors"
- "trend growth in jobs had fallen off even prior to the recent financial market strains, and participants judged that some further slowing of employment growth was likely"
- "initial claims for unemployment insurance did not indicate a substantial and widespread weakening in labor demand"

**Center-of-gravity**: Currently still fairly tight but with clear softening trajectory. Near NAIRU but loosening at the margin.

**Minority views**: Several citing shortages vs. evidence of layoffs in financial services and construction create a moderate spread.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 43 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "consumer expenditures to continue to expand at a moderate pace on average over coming quarters"
- "business investment was likely to remain healthy in coming quarters"
- "some participants saw indications of a possible weakening of consumer spending" and "the pace of financing for lower-rated firms... had slowed sharply"

**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate but below-trend, with significant downside risks from credit tightening weighing on both consumer and lower-rated business spending.

**Minority views**: Business investment seen as relatively resilient (internally financed) vs. consumer spending facing more headwinds.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 67 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "banks might need to take a large volume of assets onto their balance sheets... might well lead banks to tighten the availability of credit to households and firms"
- "subprime mortgages remained essentially unavailable, little activity was evident in the markets for other nonprime mortgages, and prime jumbo mortgage borrowers faced higher rates and tighter lending standards"
- "risk spreads probably would be wider and credit terms tighter than they had been a few months ago"

**Center-of-gravity**: Significant tightening in credit conditions across multiple channels, though not yet a full credit freeze. Conforming mortgages and investment-grade corporate credit still functioning.

**Minority views**: Conditions "seemed to have improved somewhat in the most recent days" -- some participants may see less severe tightening.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 27 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all members favored an easing of the stance of monetary policy"
- "all members agreed that a rate cut of 50 basis points at this meeting was the most prudent course of action"
- "Without such policy action, members saw a risk that tightening credit conditions and an intensifying housing correction would lead to significant broader weakness in output and employment"

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimously dovish -- a 50bp cut to preemptively counter financial tightening and downside growth risks.

**Minority views**: None -- unanimity on action.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 10 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all members favored an easing" -- unanimity on direction
- "all members agreed that a rate cut of 50 basis points" -- unanimity on magnitude
- No mention of reservations, reluctance, or dissent

**Center-of-gravity**: Near-complete consensus on both direction and magnitude of action.

**Minority views**: None evident in the text.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 75 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- First two paragraphs entirely devoted to credit market disruptions and their economic effects
- "participants focused on the potential for recent credit market developments to restrain aggregate demand"
- Policy discussion framed almost entirely in financial conditions terms: "help offset the effects of tighter financial conditions"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability/conditions are the dominant topic of the meeting, rivaling or exceeding the traditional economic assessment in emphasis.

**Minority views**: None -- all participants engage with this topic prominently.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 32 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the Committee decided to refrain from providing an explicit assessment of the balance of risks"
- "Future actions would depend on how economic prospects were affected by evolving market developments and by other factors"
- Context: 50bp cut with "growth likely to run below its potential for a while" implies easing lean

**Center-of-gravity**: Deliberately ambiguous forward signal with an implicit easing lean. Data-dependent but in a context that suggests further easing is more likely than reversal.

**Minority views**: The deliberate ambiguity itself reflects some uncertainty about the forward path among participants.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 75 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the outlook for economic activity as characterized by particularly high uncertainty, with the risks to growth skewed to the downside"
- "the incoming data in many cases were of limited value in assessing the likely evolution of economic activity and prices"
- Committee refrained from balance-of-risks statement because it "could give the mistaken impression that the Committee was more certain about the economic outlook than was in fact the case"

**Center-of-gravity**: Very high uncertainty explicitly acknowledged. The refusal to provide a balance-of-risks assessment is an unusually strong signal of uncertainty.

**Minority views**: None -- broadly shared view.
