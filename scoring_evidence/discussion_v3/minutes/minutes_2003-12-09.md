# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2003-12-09

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format, but participant language cues are clear throughout ("members," "all the members," "most members," "some members," "a number of members").

**Era context**: December 2003. The fed funds rate is at 1%, the lowest level in a generation. The economy is recovering from the 2001 recession and the slow "jobless recovery" period. Growth has finally accelerated in Q3 2003 (GDP grew ~8% annualized). Deflation fears that dominated earlier in 2003 are receding. The "considerable period" forward guidance language is actively debated.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all" (favored unchanged policy, agreed on risk wording), "most" (upside inflation risks less pressing than downside; endorsed retaining "considerable period"), "a majority" (preferred retaining "considerable period"), "a number of" (argued for deletion of "considerable period"; saw slight further disinflation), "some" (concerned about spending slowdown, financial stress, dollar effects, inflation expectations), "members generally" (expected growth to trim slack).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target and likely to remain subdued. "Persisting, though decreasing, margins of slack...would keep inflation low." "A number of members seeing a small further decline in inflation as a distinct possibility." Deflation risk now "virtually nil" per most members. But there are countervailing upward pressures noted (weaker dollar, commodity prices, benefit costs). "Most members currently considered the upside risks to inflation to be a bit less pressing than those on the downside for the next few quarters." This is clearly below-target inflation with risks tilting slightly to the downside still. Score should be in the 20-30 range. Direction: below midpoint. The text says "very low inflation" and "subdued" -- this fits 11-25 range, but since deflation risk is now "virtually nil" and risks are becoming more balanced, the upper end of that range or low end of 26-40 is appropriate. Score: 25.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Wide margins of unused labor and other resources." "Persisting, though decreasing, margins of slack in labor and goods markets." Members expect growth to "trim slack" over the forecast period. This is clearly below potential. The economy is recovering strongly but significant slack remains. The language "wide margins" is strong. Score should be in the 31-40 range -- below potential, recovering but gap persists. The "wide margins" language pulls it toward the lower end. Score: 35.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Current unemployment rate likely understated the slack in labor markets, since the labor market participation rate had fallen significantly." Employment "had revived" but from weakness. Manufacturing employment "displayed signs of stabilizing." Members expect "further gains in employment." This is clearly a slack labor market with early signs of improvement. Score: 30.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: This is a strong story. "Spending by households had remained strong." Business investment "increased rapidly." Equipment and software spending in Q3 was "even larger than had been thought." Inventory restocking expected to contribute. Consumer spending "remained firm." Holiday sales "somewhat above last year's pace." Residential construction "at a high level." This is above-trend spending and investment. Score should be in the 55-65 range. Score: 58.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Accommodative conditions in financial markets" mentioned explicitly. "Accommodative financial markets" again in the business investment discussion. "Highly stimulative macroeconomic policies." There is no discussion of tight conditions whatsoever. This is clearly loose. Score: 20.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All the members favored an unchanged policy" at 1%. "Currently accommodative policy stance remained appropriate." "For the near term...substantial benefit in maintaining an unchanged policy stance and considerable risk in taking preemptive action." A majority preferred to retain "considerable period" language. But members acknowledge "at some point, a move in the direction of a less accommodative and more neutral policy posture might well be necessary." This is dovish -- holding at emergency-low rates with explicit accommodation commitment. But there's a clear acknowledgment that eventual tightening is coming. This fits "lean dovish to balanced: hold steady with mild easing bias" (41-50) but actually more dovish given the "considerable period" retention. More like 25-35 range -- moderate dovish, maintaining max accommodation. Score: 30.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: All favored unchanged policy. The disagreement is about the forward guidance language ("considerable period"). "A number" wanted to delete it, "a majority" preferred to retain it. "Views differed" on this point. No formal dissents on the rate decision. This is moderate disagreement on communication, not on the fundamental policy action. Score: 30.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial conditions are mentioned as context ("accommodative conditions in financial markets") but not as a stability concern. "Some members pointed to signs of increased financial stress" among households. Some members expressed concern about large deficits' effects on financial markets. Current account deficits mentioned. But these are passing references, not a sustained financial stability discussion. Score: 18.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The big debate is about retaining "considerable period" language. A majority endorsed keeping it. They explicitly tied it to "persistence of quite low inflation and slack in resource use." But the changes in risk assessment (deleting the combined risk statement, noting disinflation risks diminished) represent a slight step toward eventual normalization. The forward signal is still strongly dovish -- accommodation for a "considerable period" -- but with a subtle hawkish shift from the prior meeting. Score: 18.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Still considerable uncertainty about the speed with which those margins would be worked down." Large current account deficits "added to uncertainty about the longer-term prospects." "Assessments by individual members of the likely pace of the expansion going forward differed somewhat." But overall, confidence is growing -- downside risks "had diminished," expansion is "more assured." This is somewhat elevated uncertainty but not extreme. Score: 38.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (35) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (30): Both below 50, consistent with slack economy. Labor slightly lower reflects the emphasis on hidden slack (participation rate decline).
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (30) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (18): Both dovish. Forward guidance is more dovish because "considerable period" is explicitly retained, while the current lean includes acknowledgment of eventual tightening. The divergence is explained by the fact that forward guidance is strongly dovish while current assessment is shifting.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 25: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says low/subdued. 25 < 50. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP 35: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says below potential with slack. 35 < 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS 20: Higher = tighter. Evidence says accommodative/loose. 20 < 50. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 30: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says dovish hold. 30 < 50. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 25 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Persisting, though decreasing, margins of slack in labor and goods markets and further gains in productivity would keep inflation low, with a number of members seeing a small further decline in inflation as a distinct possibility."
- "Most members currently considered the upside risks to inflation to be a bit less pressing than those on the downside for the next few quarters."
- "The risk of a pernicious deflation...was now regarded by most as virtually nil."

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation is very low and expected to remain subdued; deflation risk has receded but a slight further decline remains the most plausible outcome for several members. Countervailing upward pressures from the dollar and commodities are noted but not dominant.

**Minority views**: Some members flagged rising inflation expectations (TIPS breakevens), weaker dollar, and commodity prices as potential upside risks, widening the band.

---

### OUTPUT_GAP: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Wide margins of unused labor and other resources."
- "They generally expected growth to run at a rate that would trim slack in labor and goods markets over the forecast period."
- "Persisting, though decreasing, margins of slack."

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is operating meaningfully below potential with significant slack remaining, but the gap is narrowing as growth has accelerated sharply.

**Minority views**: Little disagreement on the direction; some variation in expected pace of closure.

---

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The current unemployment rate likely understated the slack in labor markets, since the labor market participation rate had fallen significantly of late."
- "Employment had revived" and members expected "further gains in employment."
- Manufacturing employment "displayed signs of stabilizing."

**Center-of-gravity**: The labor market has significant slack, with hidden underemployment beyond what the headline unemployment rate suggests. Early signs of improvement are present but from a weak level.

**Minority views**: Narrow range of views; all agree on significant slack.

---

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 58 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Spending by households had remained strong even as the effects of tax cuts and mortgage refinancing began to wane."
- "Investment outlays had increased rapidly" and "the increase in outlays for equipment and software in the third quarter was even larger than had been thought."
- "Inventory investment could be expected to contribute to growth in aggregate demand in coming quarters."

**Center-of-gravity**: Both consumption and investment are running above trend, with business investment accelerating sharply and consumer spending remaining solid. Multiple sectors contributing.

**Minority views**: "Some members pointed to signs of increased financial stress that could limit the ability of many households to hold spending at recent levels."

---

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 20 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Accommodative conditions in financial markets" (mentioned twice).
- "Highly stimulative macroeconomic policies."
- Business investment gains owed partly to "accommodative financial markets."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly loose and supportive of economic activity. No tightening pressures discussed.

**Minority views**: Some concern about long-term deficit effects on financial markets, but no near-term tightening signals.

---

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 30 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All the members favored an unchanged policy that continued to incorporate a target of 1 percent for the federal funds rate."
- "The currently accommodative policy stance remained appropriate in a period characterized by very low inflation, wide margins of unused labor and other resources."
- "Members saw substantial benefit in maintaining an unchanged policy stance and considerable risk in taking preemptive action."
- "At some point, a move in the direction of a less accommodative and more neutral policy posture might well be necessary."

**Center-of-gravity**: Strongly dovish hold at 1%. All agree the accommodative stance is appropriate given slack and low inflation. However, the seeds of eventual normalization are acknowledged.

**Minority views**: "A number of members argued" for deleting "considerable period" to enhance flexibility -- a modestly more hawkish subgroup, though they still favored the hold.

---

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All the members favored an unchanged policy" -- unanimous on the rate decision.
- "Views differed with regard to the reference in recent statements to maintaining an accommodative monetary policy 'for a considerable period.'"
- "A number of members argued that its deletion would serve to enhance the Committee's flexibility. A majority, however, preferred to retain the phrase."

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous on the rate decision but meaningful disagreement on communication strategy and forward guidance language. No formal dissents.

**Minority views**: The "considerable period" debate shows a notable split but confined to communication, not the fundamental policy action.

---

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 18 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Accommodative conditions in financial markets" mentioned as context for the expansion.
- "Some members pointed to signs of increased financial stress" among households.
- "Some members expressed concern about the possible adverse effects of such deficits on financial markets."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions/stability are mentioned briefly as backdrop context, not as a primary topic of discussion. No sustained or prominent focus.

**Minority views**: None significant.

---

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 18 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "A majority...preferred to retain the phrase ['considerable period'], at least for now."
- "All the members saw merit in associating it more clearly with economic conditions, specifically the persistence of quite low inflation and slack in resource use."
- "Most members endorsed a proposal to delete as no longer necessary the previous summary statement relating to the risks to growth and inflation taken together."

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward guidance is strongly dovish -- "considerable period" of accommodation explicitly retained and tied to economic conditions. However, the deletion of the combined risk statement and the condition-linking represent a subtle shift toward eventual normalization.

**Minority views**: "A number of members" wanted to drop "considerable period" entirely, which would have been a hawkish shift in guidance.

---

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 38 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Still considerable uncertainty about the speed with which those margins would be worked down."
- "Large current account deficits and their potential correction added to uncertainty about the longer-term prospects."
- "Assessments by individual members of the likely pace of the expansion going forward differed somewhat."

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is somewhat elevated, particularly around the pace of slack absorption and the external sector. But confidence is rising -- downside risks "had diminished" and the expansion is seen as "more assured."

**Minority views**: Limited divergence on uncertainty itself.
