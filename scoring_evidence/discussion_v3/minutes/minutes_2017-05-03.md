# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2017-05-03

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). This is a post-2009 segmented document with clear participant vs. staff separation.

**Era context**: May 2017. The Fed was in the early-to-middle stage of its gradual hiking cycle, having raised rates in December 2015, December 2016, and March 2017. The economy was near full employment, inflation was running close to but slightly below 2%, and Q1 GDP came in weak. Balance sheet normalization discussions were approaching.

**Quantifier distribution**: "participants agreed" / "participants generally" (several instances), "most participants" (inflation transitory), "many" (housing/BFI firming, fiscal upside risks, global risks receded), "several" (downside global risks, wage pickups, labor slack, more rapid removal), "some" (financial conditions, core PCE below target), "a few" (uncertainty about weakness, inflation concern, labor slack, undershooting unemployment), "a couple" (rate hike warranted, more gradual, global brightening).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as close to but slightly below target. Headline PCE hit 2% in February but dropped to 1.8% in March; core PCE at 1.6%. "Most participants viewed the recent softer inflation data as primarily reflecting transitory factors." "Several participants emphasized that inflation measured on a 12-month basis had been running very close to the Committee's 2 percent target." Some idiosyncratic factors (wireless telephone services). Market-based inflation compensation remained low. Direction: slightly below target. Score should be in the 41-49 range. The softness is viewed as transitory, but current readings are below target. I'll score 43. Higher = more pressure. My evidence says slightly below target. Score matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "The unemployment rate had reached or fallen below levels that participants judged likely to be normal over the longer run." The economy is projected to "continue operating above its longer-run potential" (staff language, but participants endorsed similar view). GDP growth slowed in Q1 but viewed as transitory. "A few participants continued to anticipate a substantial undershooting of the longer-run normal level of the unemployment rate." Direction: modestly above potential. The bright-line rule says any "above potential" language requires >50. Score: 52. Coherent with late-cycle 2017 context.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "At 4.5 percent, the unemployment rate had reached or fallen below levels that participants judged likely to be normal over the longer run." "Contacts in several Districts reported a pickup in wage increases, shortages of workers in selected occupations." But "several other participants suggested some margins may remain along which labor market utilization could increase further." Direction: tight, at or slightly beyond full employment. Score: 53. Anchor for 2016-17 is ~50; with unemployment now below longer-run level and reports of shortages, slightly above 50 is warranted.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending slowed significantly in Q1 but viewed as transitory. "Fundamentals underpinning the continued growth of consumption remained solid." "Business fixed investment increased at a solid pace in the first quarter." "Many pointed to the recent firming of the housing market and business fixed investment as welcome developments." Several reported planned capital expenditure increases. The NET reading: Q1 spending weak (transitory), BFI strong, housing firming. Direction: roughly at trend with some mixed signals. Score: 48. The Q1 weakness drags it slightly below the "solid pace" anchor, but it's viewed as temporary.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Some participants commented on changes in financial conditions... the decline in longer-term interest rates and the modest depreciation of the dollar over the intermeeting period would provide some stimulus to aggregate demand, that the Committee's recent policy actions had not resulted in a tightening of financial conditions." Direction: conditions are somewhat loose despite rate hikes. Score: 38. Anchor for 2017-19 is 45-55; conditions described as not having tightened and even providing stimulus suggests below neutral.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Participants left rates unchanged but "most participants judged that if economic information came in about in line with their expectations, it would soon be appropriate for the Committee to take another step in removing some policy accommodation." "A couple of participants indicated that increasing the target range for the federal funds rate at the current meeting would be warranted." "Participants generally reiterated their support for a continued gradual approach to raising the federal funds rate." "Several participants, however, pointed to conditions under which the Committee might need to consider a somewhat more rapid removal of monetary accommodation." Direction: moderately hawkish - holding now, but signaling a near-term hike, with gradual tightening broadly supported. Score: 63. Anchor for 2017-18 hiking is 70-75, but this is a hold meeting with no urgency and some dovish voices arguing for even more gradual pace.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissents. "A couple of participants indicated that increasing the target range... at the current meeting would be warranted" but went along. Some differences on pace (several for possibly faster, a couple for slower). "A few expressed concern that progress toward the Committee's objective may have slowed" vs. "most participants viewed the recent softer inflation data as primarily reflecting transitory factors." Moderate range of views but broad consensus on holding and gradual approach. Score: 28. Typical one-or-zero-dissent meeting with some differences in emphasis.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: "Several participants emphasized that higher requirements for capital and liquidity in the banking system and other prudential standards had contributed to increased resilience." Concerns about possible easing of regulatory standards, CRE valuations elevated, housing finance reform implications. This is a substantive paragraph but not a dominant topic. Direction: limited to moderate discussion. Score: 30. A few participants discuss it meaningfully but it's not a major focus.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Most participants judged that if economic information came in about in line with their expectations, it would soon be appropriate for the Committee to take another step in removing some policy accommodation." "Participants generally reiterated their support for a continued gradual approach." Several mentioned conditions for more rapid removal. Direction: mild tightening bias, gradual normalization. Score: 65. Anchor for 2015-18 hiking cycle is 60-75; this is squarely in that range with clear forward tightening signal.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants generally indicated that their assessments of the medium-term economic outlook had changed little." Near-term risks "roughly balanced." Fiscal policy uncertainty noted by many. "A few emphasized the uncertainty with regard to the reasons for the unexpected weakness in consumer spending." "A couple of participants pointed out that uncertainty about both the longer-run normal rate of unemployment and labor force trends made it difficult to assess." Some uncertainty about inflation. Direction: moderate uncertainty, standard for the era. Score: 30. Anchor for mid-2017 is 10-20, but there's meaningful fiscal policy uncertainty and some inflation uncertainty that pushes it a bit higher.

### Cross-dimension coherence check:
- OUTPUT_GAP (52) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (53): Both slightly above neutral, coherent. Economy near or slightly above potential, labor market at or just past full employment.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (63) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (65): Well-aligned. Both reflect gradual tightening posture.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (43) with HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (63): Inflation below target but participants still leaning hawkish due to labor tightness and expectation inflation will return to 2%. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 43 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Core PCE price inflation... moved down to 1.6 percent over the 12 months ending in March."
- "Most participants viewed the recent softer inflation data as primarily reflecting transitory factors."
- "Several participants emphasized that inflation measured on a 12-month basis had been running very close to the Committee's 2 percent target."

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation is slightly below the 2% target; recent readings were soft, but most participants attribute this to transitory factors and expect stabilization around 2%.

**Minority views**: "A few expressed concern that progress toward the Committee's objective may have slowed"; "several others continued to see downside risks to the inflation outlook" -- these widen the uncertainty band downward.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 52 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The unemployment rate had reached or fallen below levels that participants judged likely to be normal over the longer run."
- "Growth in economic activity slowed in the first quarter" but "the slowing as likely to be transitory."
- Participants expected "economic activity would expand at a moderate pace."

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is modestly above potential -- unemployment below longer-run normal -- though Q1 GDP was temporarily weak.

**Minority views**: Narrow disagreement; Q1 weakness was broadly viewed as transitory.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 53 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "At 4.5 percent, the unemployment rate had reached or fallen below levels that participants judged likely to be normal over the longer run."
- "Contacts in several Districts reported a pickup in wage increases, shortages of workers in selected occupations."
- "Several other participants suggested some margins may remain along which labor market utilization could increase further."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is tight, at or slightly past full employment, with some localized shortages but also possible remaining margins of slack.

**Minority views**: Some see remaining slack (labor force participation rising); a few anticipate substantial undershooting of normal unemployment, widening the band.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 48 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Household spending rose only modestly, but the fundamentals underpinning the continued growth of consumption remained solid."
- "Business fixed investment increased at a solid pace in the first quarter."
- "The slowing in growth during the first quarter was likely to be transitory."

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending was temporarily weak in Q1 while business investment firmed; net assessment is roughly at trend with expectations of rebound.

**Minority views**: "A few emphasized the uncertainty with regard to the reasons for the unexpected weakness in consumer spending."

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 38 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The decline in longer-term interest rates and the modest depreciation of the dollar over the intermeeting period would provide some stimulus to aggregate demand."
- "The Committee's recent policy actions had not resulted in a tightening of financial conditions."

**Center-of-gravity**: Despite rate hikes, financial conditions had not tightened and were somewhat supportive -- somewhat loose relative to neutral.

**Minority views**: Limited discussion; only "some participants" addressed this topic.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 63 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Most participants judged that if economic information came in about in line with their expectations, it would soon be appropriate for the Committee to take another step in removing some policy accommodation."
- "Participants generally reiterated their support for a continued gradual approach to raising the federal funds rate."
- "Several participants, however, pointed to conditions under which the Committee might need to consider a somewhat more rapid removal of monetary accommodation."

**Center-of-gravity**: Moderately hawkish. Consensus for gradual tightening with a near-term hike on the horizon, though no urgency at this meeting.

**Minority views**: "A couple of others judged that the Committee could withdraw monetary accommodation even more gradually" (dovish wing); "a couple of participants indicated that increasing the target range... at the current meeting would be warranted" (hawkish wing).

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 28 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- No formal dissents; unanimous hold decision.
- "A couple of participants indicated that increasing the target range for the federal funds rate at the current meeting would be warranted" but accepted the hold.
- Differences on pace: several for possibly faster removal, a couple for slower.

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on holding rates and gradual approach, with minor tactical differences about pace.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 30 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Several participants emphasized that higher requirements for capital and liquidity in the banking system and other prudential standards had contributed to increased resilience in the financial system."
- "They expressed concerns that a possible easing of regulatory standards could increase risks to financial stability."
- "Real estate values were elevated in some sectors of the CRE market."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability received a dedicated paragraph with several participants weighing in, but it was not a dominant topic at this meeting.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 65 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Most participants judged that... it would soon be appropriate for the Committee to take another step in removing some policy accommodation."
- "A continued gradual removal of monetary policy accommodation remained appropriate."
- "Several participants... pointed to conditions under which the Committee might need to consider a somewhat more rapid removal."

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear forward signal toward further gradual tightening, with the next hike likely coming soon.

**Minority views**: A couple favored even more gradual removal; several flagged scenarios for faster removal.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 30 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Near-term risks to the economic outlook appeared roughly balanced."
- "Prospects for enactment of a more expansionary fiscal program, as well as its size, composition, and timing, remained highly uncertain."
- "A couple of participants pointed out that uncertainty about both the longer-run normal rate of unemployment and labor force trends made it difficult to assess the scope for additional sustainable increases in labor utilization."

**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate uncertainty, mainly around fiscal policy prospects and some structural parameters, but overall outlook little changed and risks balanced.
