# FOMC Meeting Scoring — 2016-06-15 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented — participant discussion section is clearly separated from staff review.

**Era context**: Mid-2016. The Fed had lifted off in December 2015 with a single 25bp hike and had been on hold since. The economy was expanding at a moderate pace but the May 2016 employment report was shockingly weak. Brexit referendum was imminent. Inflation running below 2%. This is a "patient hold" meeting with elevated uncertainty about labor market and global risks.

**Quantifier distribution**: "most" appears frequently (expectations for gradual rate path, inflation returning to 2%, Brexit risk). "many" used for neutral rate discussion, labor market transitory noise view. "some" used for both dovish and hawkish camps. "several" used for both sides of the rate debate. "a few" used for cautious consumer outlook views and labor supply constraint interpretation. This is a meeting with notable two-sidedness.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target. "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective." Core PCE at 1.6%. "Most participants expected to see continued progress toward the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective" — but "other participants were less confident that inflation would return to its target level over the medium term." Market-based inflation compensation declined. Direction: below target but with some firming signs. The center of gravity is below-target with modest upward movement expected. Score should be in the 35-42 range (below target, some firming). I'll set 38.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Inflation below target with gradual firming = below midpoint. 38 matches.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**: "Growth in economic activity appeared to have picked up" from earlier weak quarters, but business fixed investment "had been soft" and payroll gains weakened sharply. Some participants saw labor market "at or near those consistent with maximum employment." Others worried about a "possible downshift." The economy is near potential with mixed signals — some indicators suggest near-potential, others suggest slight slack. The anchor for 2016 is 50-55 ("near potential"). With the weak May jobs report clouding the picture, and mixed views, I'll score 48 — slightly below potential.
- Bright-line check: Some say "at or close to" maximum employment, others see weakness. Center of gravity is "approaching but not clearly above" potential. 48 is consistent.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: The May employment report was "considerably weaker" than expected. But "some noted that other indicators did not corroborate a material weakening." Some viewed labor market "at or near those consistent with maximum employment" while others pointed to weak payrolls, declining participation, and part-time work. The center of gravity: near full employment but suddenly uncertain. The unemployment rate had been falling. Anchor: 50 for 2016-17. With the May shock introducing doubt but many still seeing near-full employment, I'll score 48.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Near full employment with a weak payroll report creating doubt = slightly below the full-employment midpoint. 48 fits.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "appeared to have picked up" with "strong gains in April and May" in retail sales. Supported by "ongoing gains in income, robust household balance sheets." But business fixed investment "had been soft" — "softness in business fixed investment since late last year persisted." Housing "continued to improve." The consumer side is above trend; business investment is below trend. Net: consumer strength partly offset by investment weakness. Around trend or slightly below. Score: 47.
- Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Mixed picture with consumer rebound but soft capex = near trend, slightly below. 47 fits.

**5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Several mentioning, in particular, that financial conditions were accommodative." "Global financial conditions had improved since earlier in the year." But participants noted risks from Brexit and China. The center of gravity describes accommodative financial conditions with risk events on the horizon. Score: 35 — somewhat loose to neutral, leaning accommodative.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Accommodative conditions = below 50. 35 fits.

**6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Members agreed to hold rates unchanged. "Most participants judged that... raising the target range for the federal funds rate would be appropriate if incoming information confirmed" growth pickup. "Some" emphasized that "taking another step in removing monetary accommodation should not be delayed too long." "However, some other participants were uncertain whether economic conditions would soon warrant an increase." "A few pointed out that... maintaining an accommodative stance of policy could extend the strengthening." The decision was unanimously to hold, with a tightening bias contingent on data confirmation. This is a hold-with-mild-hawkish-lean meeting. Anchor: 40-50 for 2014-15 pre-liftoff; this is post-liftoff but pausing, so similar range. Score: 50 — balanced, leaning very slightly hawkish. The hold is cautious but most still expect hikes ahead. I'll say 50.
- Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Holding with data-dependent bias, most expecting gradual hikes but wanting confirmation = balanced. 50 fits.

**7. DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissents mentioned. But there are clearly two camps: "some" wanting to hike soon vs. "some other participants" uncertain about near-term hikes. Multiple interpretations of the employment data. "Several" expressed concern about delay; "several" worried about downside risks. This is meaningful disagreement but within a hold consensus. Score: 38 — moderate differences, no formal dissents but notable spread of views.

**8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Brexit referendum is a major discussion topic — "most participants noted that the upcoming British referendum... could generate financial market turbulence." "Some also noted... continued uncertainty regarding the outlook for China's foreign exchange policy." "Several of these participants expressed concern that a delay... would increase the risks to financial stability." Financial stability is discussed by multiple participants but is not the dominant topic — it's one of several factors. Score: 40.

**9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Most still expected that the appropriate target range... would rise gradually in coming years." "Some noted that their forecasts were now consistent with a shallower path than they had expected." "The Committee expected that economic conditions would evolve in a manner that would warrant only gradual increases" and "the federal funds rate was likely to remain, for some time, below levels that were expected to prevail in the longer run." This is a mild tightening bias but very gradual and data-dependent. Anchor: 60-75 for the hiking cycle, but this is a pause with downgraded path. Score: 58 — mild tightening bias, gradual.

**10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Almost all participants judged that the surprisingly weak May employment report increased their uncertainty about the outlook for the labor market." Brexit uncertainty was a major factor in the decision. "Several noted that in the prevailing circumstances of considerable uncertainty about the neutral federal funds rate." Multiple sources of uncertainty: labor market, Brexit, neutral rate, global risks. Score: 55 — elevated uncertainty from multiple sources.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (48) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (48): Both slightly below midpoint, consistent — economy near but not quite at potential, labor market near full employment but May report creates doubt.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (50) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (58): Slight divergence — current action is balanced/hold but forward signal tilts mildly hawkish (gradual hikes expected). This makes sense: they held this meeting but signal future tightening.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 38 ±7 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective, partly reflecting earlier declines in energy prices and in prices of non-energy imports." "Core PCE price inflation registered an increase of 1.6 percent for the 12 months ending in April." "Most participants expected to see continued progress toward the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective" but "other participants were less confident that inflation would return to its target level over the medium term."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Inflation running meaningfully below target with some firming in core measures but persistent downside risks from global factors and declining inflation compensation measures.
- **Minority views**: Some saw core inflation firming and wage growth as signs of approaching target; others saw "persistent disinflationary pressures" and risk of very slow progress. Band reflects this spread.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 48 ±6 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Growth in economic activity appeared to have picked up" but "business fixed investment had been soft." "Some of them judged that labor market conditions were now at or close to the Committee's objectives." Others saw "the recent soft readings on payroll jobs... suggested a possible downshift in the pace of improvement."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Economy near potential but with downside uncertainty from the weak May employment report and soft business investment.
- **Minority views**: Some saw the economy at potential (would push score to 50+); others worried about a broader slowdown (would pull to 42-44).

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 48 ±8 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The employment report for May showed considerably weaker growth in payrolls than had been expected." "Some noted that other indicators did not corroborate a material weakening of labor market conditions." "Some of them judged that labor market conditions were now at or close to the Committee's objectives."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Near full employment by most measures, but the May report introduced significant uncertainty about the underlying pace of improvement. Multiple interpretations of the data coexist.
- **Minority views**: "A few participants suggested that the weak employment growth may instead reflect supply constraints associated with a general tightening of labor market conditions" (would imply 52-55). Others pointed to broad weakness indicators (would imply 42-44). Wide band reflects the disagreement.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 47 ±6 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Growth of consumer spending appeared to have picked up... Retail sales posted strong gains in April and May." But "the softness in business fixed investment since late last year persisted early in the second quarter." "Some participants mentioned that the sluggishness in business investment could portend a broader economic slowdown."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Strong consumer rebound offset by persistently soft business investment, netting to near-trend overall spending.
- **Minority views**: A few were cautious about consumer spending sustainability; some saw business investment picking up ahead.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 35 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Several mentioning, in particular, that financial conditions were accommodative." "Global financial conditions had improved since earlier in the year." "Most participants noted that the upcoming British referendum... could generate financial market turbulence."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Conditions described as accommodative overall, though with downside risks from upcoming global events.
- **Minority views**: "A couple of participants observed that financial strains caused by previous declines in energy prices had continued for firms or financial institutions in their Districts."

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 50 ±8 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Members agreed to leave the target range for the federal funds rate unchanged." "Some of them emphasized that... taking another step in removing monetary accommodation should not be delayed too long." "However, some other participants were uncertain whether economic conditions would soon warrant an increase." "A few pointed out that... maintaining an accommodative stance of policy could extend the strengthening of the labor market."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Balanced — unanimous hold with genuine two-sidedness. Hawks want to resume hiking soon; doves want more evidence and see asymmetric risks from premature tightening.
- **Minority views**: Hawkish camp (several wanting imminent hike) vs. dovish camp (several wanting extended patience). Roughly balanced in the text.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 38 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: No formal dissents reported. But clear camps: "some" urged not delaying further; "some other participants were uncertain" about near-term hikes. Multiple competing interpretations of the May employment report. "Several expressed concern about delay... Several of them noted downside risks."
- **Center-of-gravity**: No dissents but meaningful disagreement about both the economic assessment and the appropriate timing of the next move.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 40 ±7 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Most participants noted that the upcoming British referendum on membership in the European Union could generate financial market turbulence." "Some also noted that continued uncertainty regarding the outlook for China's foreign exchange policy... represented appreciable risks to global financial stability." "Several of these participants expressed concern that a delay... would increase the risks to financial stability."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability received moderate attention — Brexit risk, China risks, and reach-for-yield concerns all discussed, but economic assessment still dominated.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 58 ±7 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Most still expected that the appropriate target range for the federal funds rate... would rise gradually in coming years." "Some noted that their forecasts were now consistent with a shallower path than they had expected at the time of the March meeting." "The Committee expected that economic conditions would evolve in a manner that would warrant only gradual increases."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal is mildly hawkish — gradual hikes expected ahead, but the path has been downgraded and is highly data-dependent.
- **Minority views**: Several saw downside risks requiring extended accommodation; some wanted near-term action.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 55 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Almost all participants judged that the surprisingly weak May employment report increased their uncertainty about the outlook for the labor market." "Several noted that in the prevailing circumstances of considerable uncertainty about the neutral federal funds rate." Brexit referendum added further uncertainty to deliberations.
- **Center-of-gravity**: Elevated uncertainty from multiple simultaneous sources — weak employment data, Brexit, neutral rate uncertainty, and global risks — leading participants to delay action pending further information.
