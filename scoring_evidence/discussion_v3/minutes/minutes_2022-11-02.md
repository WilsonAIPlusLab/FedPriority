# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2022-11-02 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented -- participant discussion section is clearly separated from staff content.

**Era context**: November 2022. The Fed had been aggressively hiking rates with 75bp moves since June 2022 to combat inflation running at multi-decade highs. CPI was above 7%, PCE well above target. The labor market remained historically tight. This meeting resulted in another 75bp hike but began signaling a possible slowdown in the pace of future increases.

**Quantifier distribution**: The text uses "all/unanimous" for the 75bp decision; "substantial majority" for slowing the pace soon; "many" for services price persistence, signs of labor rebalancing, uncertainty about terminal rate; "several" for wage moderation, rent slowing, financial system risks; "some" for lower-income strain, wage-price inconsistency, easing supply constraints, risk of overtightening; "a few" for wage-price spiral risk, Treasury market preparedness, wanting to wait before slowing pace.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants "agreed that inflation was unacceptably high and was well above the Committee's longer-run goal of 2 percent." Inflation "remained elevated" with "very few signs that inflation pressures were abating." Services price pressures increasing and "historically more persistent." However, some noted easing supply constraints and lower commodity prices as medium-term offsets. Near-term assessment is clearly high. Direction: well above midpoint. The language "unacceptably high," "well above," "far too high" is very strong. Anchor: 2022 Q2-Q3 peak is 82-88. By November 2022, there were some early signs of goods disinflation and rent moderation noted, so slightly below peak. Score ~80-82.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic activity in the near term appeared likely to expand at a pace below its trend growth rate." "Softening in consumer and business spending growth." "Notable slowing in interest rate-sensitive sectors." However, the economy is still growing (GDP "rebounded in the third quarter"), labor market "remained extremely tight." Participants welcomed below-trend growth as helpful for rebalancing. The economy is decelerating from above-potential but language suggests it is still near or modestly above potential given the tight labor market. The bright-line rule: "below its trend growth rate" suggests activity is slowing but participants note the labor market is "extremely tight" -- implying demand still exceeds supply capacity. Score slightly above 50 to reflect the tension between slowing growth and still-tight conditions. ~52-54.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor market had remained very tight, with the unemployment rate near a historically low level, the number of job vacancies very high, a low pace of layoffs, robust employment gains, and elevated nominal wage growth." "Extremely tight." "Acute labor shortages" in certain sectors. "Many participants noted tentative signs that the labor market might be moving slowly toward a better balance" -- but these are "tentative." Center of gravity: very tight with early signs of easing. Anchor: 65-75 for 2022-23. Score ~70-72.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Growth in consumer spending had softened recently." "Reduction in discretionary expenditures." "Growth in investment spending was modest" and "weighed down by tighter financial conditions." Housing "notably restrained." But household balance sheets "still strong" supporting spending. Overall: below trend and softening. The language "softened," "modest," "weighed down" suggests below-trend activity. Score ~38-42.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Tightening of financial conditions associated with the Committee's policy actions." "Financial conditions had tightened significantly." "Interest rate-sensitive sectors" clearly impacted. "Strained liquidity conditions" in Treasury market. Global financial conditions tightening. Several participants noted "risk of instability or dislocations in the financial system." This is clearly tight. Anchor: 60-70 for 2022 rate hikes. Score ~67-70.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All participants agreed" on 75bp hike. "Ongoing increases" anticipated. Terminal rate assessment "somewhat higher than they had previously expected." But "a substantial majority judged that a slowing in the pace of increase would likely soon be appropriate." Level of terminal rate more important than pace. Some noted risk of overtightening. This is strongly hawkish (75bp hike, ongoing increases, higher terminal) but with a meaningful signal toward moderation. Anchor: 80-85 for late 2022 75bp hikes. The slowing-pace signal tempers this somewhat. Score ~78.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: All agreed on 75bp -- unanimous. But there is a spread between those wanting to slow pace soon ("substantial majority") vs. "a few other participants" wanting to wait. Some noted overtightening risk while others emphasized inflation persistence. This is relatively contained disagreement -- mainly about pace going forward, not about the current action. Score ~30-33.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: A full paragraph on Treasury market functioning, gilt market disruptions in UK, nonbank financial institution risks, potential interactions of regulations with market activity. "A number of participants discussed a range of issues" regarding market resilience. Several noted risks from nonbank financial institutions. A few noted risk of instability from rapid tightening. This is meaningful attention. Score ~50-55.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Ongoing increases in the target range would be appropriate." Terminal rate "somewhat higher than previously expected." But "substantial majority judged that a slowing in the pace of increase would likely soon be appropriate." The forward signal is still hawkish (more hikes coming, higher terminal) but clearly moderating pace. Anchor: 80-95 for 2022 aggressive hiking. The slowing signal brings this down somewhat. Score ~80-82.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants generally noted that the uncertainty associated with their economic outlooks was high." "Significant uncertainty about the ultimate level of the federal funds rate." Extensive discussion of lags and their uncertainty. "High degree of uncertainty involved in applying the evidence on lags." "Heightened uncertainty regarding the outlooks for both inflation and real activity." Direction: clearly elevated. Score ~62-67.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (53) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (71): These diverge because the economy is in a transitional phase -- growth is slowing toward/below trend but the labor market hasn't yet loosened. This is explicitly noted by participants ("labor market having remained strong to date, even alongside the slowing in economic activity"). The divergence is justified.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (78) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (81): These are aligned -- both strongly hawkish with slight moderation signal. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE is slightly higher because the forward signal still points to more hikes and higher terminal, even as pace slows.

### Polarity verifications:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. "Unacceptably high" = high score. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Economy slowing but labor still very tight. Score slightly above 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. "Tightened significantly." High score. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 80 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants agreed that inflation was unacceptably high and was well above the Committee's longer-run goal of 2 percent"
- "inflation remained well above the Committee's longer-run goal of 2 percent and the recent data on inflation provided very few signs that inflation pressures were abating"
- "Many participants observed that price pressures had increased in the services sector and that, historically, price pressures in this sector had been more persistent"

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous view that inflation is far too high with few signs of abating, though some acknowledged early signs of goods disinflation and rent moderation on the horizon.

**Minority views**: Some noted lower commodity prices and easing supply constraints "should contribute to lower inflation in the medium term," which introduces modest downside to the uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 53 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "economic activity in the near term appeared likely to expand at a pace below its trend growth rate"
- "softening in consumer and business spending growth" and "notable slowing in interest rate-sensitive sectors"
- But "the labor market remained extremely tight" and "nominal wage growth remained elevated"

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is in transition -- growth has slowed below trend but demand-supply imbalances persist given the extremely tight labor market. The output gap is closing but still modestly positive.

**Minority views**: Some emphasized resilient consumer balance sheets; others highlighted more pronounced slowing. Wider uncertainty band reflects this tension.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 71 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the labor market had remained very tight, with the unemployment rate near a historically low level, the number of job vacancies very high, a low pace of layoffs, robust employment gains, and elevated nominal wage growth"
- "employers in certain sectors... faced particularly acute labor shortages"
- "many participants noted tentative signs that the labor market might be moving slowly toward a better balance of supply and demand"

**Center-of-gravity**: Very tight labor market with only tentative, early signs of rebalancing. Unemployment near historic lows, vacancies very high.

**Minority views**: Many noted early signs of rebalancing (lower turnover, wage moderation) which prevents scoring at the very top of the range.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 39 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "growth in consumer spending had softened recently" with "a reduction in discretionary expenditures"
- "growth in investment spending was modest" and "business investment was being weighed down by tighter financial conditions"
- "higher mortgage interest rates had notably restrained housing activity"

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer and business spending both softening, housing notably weak. Below trend but not contracting -- household balance sheets still provide support.

**Minority views**: "A few participants reported that some business contacts indicated that their investment spending had been resilient," preventing a lower score.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 68 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Financial conditions had tightened significantly in response to the Committee's policy actions, and their effects were clearly evident in the most interest rate-sensitive sectors"
- "elevated interest rate volatility and indications of strained liquidity conditions"
- "Several participants commented that continued rapid policy tightening increased the risk of instability or dislocations in the financial system"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly tight and actively restraining the economy, with some stress indicators in Treasury markets, though not at crisis levels.

**Minority views**: Narrow -- participants broadly agree conditions have tightened significantly.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 78 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all participants agreed that it was appropriate to raise the target range for the federal funds rate 75 basis points at this meeting"
- "their assessment of the ultimate level of the federal funds rate that would be necessary to achieve the Committee's goals was somewhat higher than they had previously expected"
- "a substantial majority of participants judged that a slowing in the pace of increase would likely soon be appropriate"

**Center-of-gravity**: Strongly hawkish -- unanimous 75bp hike with expectations for continued increases and a higher terminal rate. The slowing-pace signal moderates the hawkishness slightly from peak levels, as the Committee pivots from pace to level.

**Minority views**: "Some participants observed that there had been an increase in the risk that the cumulative policy restraint would exceed what was required" -- a mild dovish voice. "A few other participants noted that, before slowing the pace... it could be advantageous to wait" -- a more hawkish voice.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- All agreed on 75bp hike (unanimous action)
- "a substantial majority" for slowing pace soon vs. "a few other participants" wanting to wait
- "Some participants observed... risk that cumulative policy restraint would exceed what was required" vs. others seeing upside inflation risks

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on the current action with moderate differences about pace and risk balance going forward. No formal dissents mentioned.

**Minority views**: The tension between those wanting to slow pace and those wanting to wait creates some disagreement, but it is about tactics rather than fundamental direction.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 52 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Full paragraph on Treasury market functioning: "participants noted the importance of orderly functioning of the market for U.S. Treasury securities"
- "a number of participants discussed a range of issues that could be considered by the appropriate authorities regarding market resilience"
- "Several participants noted the risks posed by nonbank financial institutions amid the rapid global tightening of monetary policy and the potential for hidden leverage"

**Center-of-gravity**: Meaningful discussion of financial stability -- Treasury market functioning, UK gilt market disruptions as cautionary tale, nonbank risks, potential for financial system instability. More than passing mentions but not the dominant topic.

**Minority views**: Discussion was broad-based rather than contentious.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 81 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "they continued to anticipate that ongoing increases in the target range for the federal funds rate would be appropriate in order to attain a sufficiently restrictive stance"
- "their assessment of the ultimate level of the federal funds rate... was somewhat higher than they had previously expected"
- "a substantial majority of participants judged that a slowing in the pace of increase would likely soon be appropriate"

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal remains firmly hawkish -- more rate increases ahead, higher terminal rate -- but with a clear signal that the pace of hikes will moderate. The emphasis shifts from pace to ultimate level.

**Minority views**: A few preferred waiting before slowing pace, suggesting even more hawkish forward lean.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 64 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "participants generally noted that the uncertainty associated with their economic outlooks was high"
- "significant uncertainty about the ultimate level of the federal funds rate needed to achieve the Committee's goals"
- "the high degree of uncertainty involved in applying the evidence on lags to the current situation"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants broadly express elevated uncertainty, particularly about monetary policy lags, the terminal rate, and whether the economy's post-pandemic dynamics differ from historical patterns.

**Minority views**: No one argued for low uncertainty; the discussion is uniformly about elevated uncertainty.
