# FOMC Meeting Scoring — 2014-09-17 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized participant views). Segmented — the participant discussion section is clearly separated from staff content. Quantifier language is used throughout ("most," "several," "some," "a few," "a couple").

**Era context**: September 2014. The economy is in mid-recovery from the GFC. The taper of asset purchases is nearing completion (ended October 2014). The fed funds rate remains at the zero lower bound. Unemployment has declined but remains elevated. Inflation is running below the 2% target. The Committee is debating the forward-guidance language ("considerable time") and the eventual timing of liftoff.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW the Committee's 2% objective. "Inflation was running below the Committee's longer-run objective" and readings were "somewhat softer" over the intermeeting period partly due to declining energy prices. "Most participants anticipated that inflation would move gradually back toward its objective." "Several expressing concern that inflation might persist below the Committee's objective for quite some time." A few saw labor markets as tight enough for wages to move up, but this is a minority. Direction: below midpoint. Magnitude: "running below" is not dramatically below — it's persistent but not alarming. Score ~36. Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says below target. Score below 50. Correct.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic activity was expanding at a moderate rate." "Most participants judged that there remained significant underutilization of labor resources." Participants expected "further gradual decline in the unemployment rate." The word "significant underutilization" clearly signals below potential. Bright-line rule: "significant underutilization" → must be < 50. "Moderate" expansion rate. The economy is recovering but not yet at potential. Score ~40. Anchor: 2010-14 recovery = 35-42. This fits well.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Most agreed that underutilization of labor resources remained significant." Unemployment rate was "little changed." Positive signals: decline in longer-term unemployment, low new claims, rise in job openings, payroll gains averaging 200K+. But: "the level of nonfarm payroll jobs had only recently returned to its pre-recession level," part-time for economic reasons still elevated, LFPR below structural trend. "Most measures of labor compensation showed no broad-based increase in wage inflation." Some specific industries with upward pressure, but that's the minority. Direction: below full employment, with improvement. Score ~38. Anchor: 2011-14 = 35-40. Fits. Polarity: Higher = tighter. Evidence says slack. Below 50. Correct. Coherence with OUTPUT_GAP (~40): both below 50, consistent.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Household spending "rising moderately." "Several participants noting that the recent positive reports on retail sales, motor vehicle purchases, and health-care spending had reduced their concern." Favorable factors: household wealth gains, improved balance sheets, rising confidence. But some heard "mixed reports." Housing recovery "remained slow." Business conditions improving: "rising confidence," "increasing willingness to undertake new investment projects," manufacturing "strong," "elevated levels of new orders and shipments of capital goods." Net assessment: moderate positive. Consumer spending moderate, business investment strengthening, housing weak. Score ~50. Anchor: 2014-19 "solid pace" = 50-55. The housing drag pulls it slightly down. Score 49.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Improvement in business conditions was reflected in reports of increased demand for loans." Borrowers expanding credit lines and obtaining new commitments. Bankers easing terms/conditions in response to competition. "Stretched stock market valuations, and compressed risk spreads." "Relatively low mortgage rates." This all points to accommodative/loose financial conditions. Some noted financial stability concerns about leverage and valuations — this is a loose-conditions concern, not tight. Score ~28. Polarity: Higher = tighter. Evidence: conditions are loose. Below 50. Correct. Anchor: The 2013-19 typical is 45-55, but in 2014 with QE still winding down and very low rates, conditions were notably accommodative. 28 seems right.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The Committee continued the taper (already decided) and maintained the "considerable time" forward guidance. "Several participants thought that the current forward guidance suggested a longer period before liftoff... than they believed was likely to be appropriate" — these are the hawks wanting faster guidance changes. But the majority maintained current guidance. "Some participants saw the current forward guidance as appropriate in light of risk-management considerations, which suggested that it would be prudent to err on the side of patience." The overall lean is still accommodative but clearly transitioning toward normalization discussion. They're finishing the taper, debating when to change forward guidance, but not ready to tighten. Score ~45. Anchor: 2014-15 pre-liftoff = 40-50. Fits. The "several" wanting faster action pushes slightly above pure dovish.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Most" agreed on significant labor underutilization. "Participants differed somewhat in their assessments of how quickly inflation would move up." On forward guidance, there's notable disagreement: "several" wanted stronger/earlier guidance changes, while "some" wanted patience for risk management. "Participants continued to express differing views on the extent of remaining slack." There are clear differences but no heated exchanges or formal dissents mentioned. Score ~35. Anchor: typical one-dissent meeting = 25-35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: There's a meaningful but not dominant paragraph on financial stability: "deterioration in leveraged lending standards, stretched stock market valuations, and compressed risk spreads." But it's tempered: one participant said leveraged loan market "moving into better balance," and "a couple" said conditions "did not suggest the types of financial stability considerations that would impede" macro objectives. Also the discussion of market expectations for the fed funds rate path is about financial conditions broadly. Moderate attention. Score ~32. Anchor: 2013-19 typical = 20-35.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The "considerable time" language was maintained. Discussion focused on whether/how to evolve it. "Several participants thought that the current forward guidance... suggested a longer period before liftoff... than they believed was likely to be appropriate." Others emphasized data dependence and risk management. The overall signal: still accommodative but increasingly pointing toward eventual normalization. "If employment and inflation converged more rapidly... the date of liftoff could be earlier." The forward signal is mildly hawkish relative to the previous pure accommodation, but still in the easing/patient camp. Score ~38. Anchor: 2014 taper = 8-15 (but taper is ending); 2015 pre-liftoff = 25-40. This is the late-taper/early pre-liftoff transition. 38 fits.

Coherence check: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (~45) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (~38). The current lean is slightly more hawkish than the forward signal because the Committee is maintaining "considerable time" language (keeping the forward signal patient) while internally debating moving sooner. This slight divergence makes sense.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Most viewed the risks to the outlook for economic activity and the labor market as broadly balanced." But "a number of participants noted that economic growth over the medium term might be slower than they expected" due to foreign growth, productivity, or housing. Concerns about euro area, China, Japan, Middle East, Ukraine noted. "Participants differed somewhat in their assessments." The forward guidance discussion itself reflects uncertainty about timing. Overall: somewhat elevated but not extreme. Score ~35. Anchor: 2013-14 stable expansion = 25-35.

### Quantifier Distribution
- "most": labor underutilization significant (×2), inflation would gradually return, risks broadly balanced, preferred data-dependent forward guidance
- "several": concern inflation below target, manufacturing activity strong, new orders/investment signs, subdued wage growth as signal of slack, forward guidance too dovish
- "some": mixed consumer reports, dollar appreciation concern, forward guidance appropriate for risk management, reluctant to weight one monthly report
- "a few": labor markets tight enough for wages to rise, difficulties with numerical thresholds, relative strength in multifamily
- "a couple": construction held back by shortages, borrowers expanding credit lines, dollar appreciation could slow inflation, stability conditions not impeding objectives

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 36 ±6 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Inflation was running below the Committee's longer-run objective" and "readings on consumer prices over the intermeeting period were somewhat softer than during the preceding four months, in part because of declining energy prices." "Several expressing concern that inflation might persist below the Committee's objective for quite some time." "Most participants anticipated that inflation would move gradually back toward its objective."
- **Center of gravity**: Inflation clearly below target with most expecting gradual return; no urgency about deflation but persistent below-target concern.
- **Minority views**: "A few seeing labor markets as sufficiently tight that wages and prices would soon begin to move up noticeably" — these push the upper band slightly.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 40 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Economic activity was expanding at a moderate rate." "Most participants judged that there remained significant underutilization of labor resources." "The level of nonfarm payroll jobs had only recently returned to its pre-recession level."
- **Center of gravity**: Economy below potential with significant slack remaining, but recovering at a moderate pace. Clearly below potential per bright-line rule.
- **Minority views**: Minor — "most" agreed on significant underutilization; a few saw slack as more limited.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 38 ±6 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Most agreed that underutilization of labor resources remained significant." "Most measures of labor compensation showed no broad-based increase in wage inflation." Positive signals: "payroll gains averaged more than 200,000," "decline in longer-term unemployment, the low level of new claims for unemployment insurance, the rise in job openings."
- **Center of gravity**: Labor market improving but still well below full employment. Substantial slack in levels despite strong flow improvements.
- **Minority views**: "A few seeing labor markets as sufficiently tight" for wages to rise; "businesses in several Districts continued to report upward pressure on wages in specific industries."

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 49 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Household spending appeared to be rising moderately." Business contacts indicated "improvements in business conditions, rising confidence about the economic outlook, and increasing willingness to undertake new investment projects." "Manufacturing activity was strong." But "the recovery in housing activity remained slow."
- **Center of gravity**: Consumer spending moderate, business investment strengthening with strong signals in manufacturing and capital goods, but housing weakness drags the net position to roughly at trend.
- **Minority views**: "Other participants said they heard mixed reports from business contacts regarding consumer spending."

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 28 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Increased demand for loans at banks in several Districts." "Bankers... had eased the terms and conditions on loans in response to competition." "Stretched stock market valuations, and compressed risk spreads" — these are symptoms of loose, not tight, conditions. "Relatively low mortgage rates."
- **Center of gravity**: Financial conditions clearly accommodative/loose, with easing lending standards and compressed spreads.
- **Minority views**: Limited — the concerns raised were about conditions being too loose, not about tightness.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 45 ±7 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: Maintained "considerable time" guidance. "Several participants thought that the current forward guidance... suggested a longer period before liftoff... than they believed was likely to be appropriate." "Some participants saw the current forward guidance as appropriate in light of risk-management considerations, which suggested that it would be prudent to err on the side of patience."
- **Center of gravity**: Lean dovish to balanced — still maintaining accommodation but with growing discussion of normalization timing. The majority held current stance but acknowledged the approaching transition.
- **Minority views**: "Several" wanted more hawkish guidance; "some" emphasized patience/risk management favoring continued accommodation.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 35 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Participants continued to express differing views on the extent of remaining slack in labor markets." "Participants differed somewhat in their assessments of how quickly inflation would move up." Forward guidance debate showed clear camps: "several" vs. "some" on timing.
- **Center of gravity**: Moderate differences on multiple dimensions (slack assessment, inflation dynamics, forward guidance timing) but no formal dissents mentioned and no sharp exchanges.
- **Minority views**: N/A (this dimension captures the spread itself).

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 32 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Some financial developments that could undermine financial stability over time were noted, including a deterioration in leveraged lending standards, stretched stock market valuations, and compressed risk spreads." "A couple of participants, while stressing the importance of remaining vigilant about potential risks to financial stability, observed that conditions in financial markets at present did not suggest the types of financial stability considerations that would impede the achievement of the Committee's macroeconomic objectives."
- **Center of gravity**: Meaningful but not dominant discussion; concerns raised and then partially tempered.
- **Minority views**: One participant suggested leveraged loan market "moving into better balance."

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 38 ±7 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: Maintained "considerable time" language. "Most participants indicated a preference for clarifying the dependence of the current forward guidance on economic data." "It was emphasized that the current forward guidance for the federal funds rate was data dependent." "If employment and inflation converged more rapidly... the date of liftoff could be earlier."
- **Center of gravity**: Forward signal still in patient/accommodative territory but increasingly data-dependent and pointing toward eventual normalization. Not yet a tightening signal.
- **Minority views**: "Several" thought guidance implied too-long a wait; debate about how to evolve the language.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 35 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Most viewed the risks to the outlook for economic activity and the labor market as broadly balanced." "A number of participants noted that economic growth over the medium term might be slower than they expected if foreign economic growth came in weaker than anticipated." Concerns about euro area, China, Japan, Middle East, Ukraine.
- **Center of gravity**: Risks seen as broadly balanced but with notable downside risks from international developments and structural factors. Somewhat elevated but not unusual.
- **Minority views**: International risk concerns from "a number of" participants add to the uncertainty band.
