# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2012-03-13

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented -- participant views section is clearly separated from staff review.

**Era context**: March 2012 -- post-GFC recovery. The economy is in a slow recovery with elevated unemployment, QE programs active (Operation Twist ongoing), and the fed funds rate at 0-0.25%. Forward guidance extends through late 2014. This is squarely in the QE/recovery era.

**Quantifier distribution**: "most" appears frequently (inflation expectations, housing depressed, no material revision to outlook). "Many" used for financial strains easing. "Several" used for factors restraining consumption and forward employment concern. "A few" used for higher GDP expectations, below-mandate inflation concern. "One" used for dissenting hawkish member and for optimistic housing view. "A number of" used for restraining factors, labor slack, fiscal cliff risk, euro-area longer-term risks.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW target. "Inflation had been subdued in recent months." "Most participants saw little evidence of cost pressures." "Most participants anticipated that after the temporary effect of the rise in oil and gasoline prices had run its course, inflation would be at or below the 2 percent rate." "A few participants were concerned that...inflation might be below the mandate-consistent rate for some time." The oil price increase is acknowledged as temporary. A minority ("other participants") worried about inflation pressures increasing. Center of gravity: clearly below target, subdued, with temporary oil bump. Direction: below midpoint. Score ~35. The "subdued" language plus "at or below 2 percent" expectations, with a few worried about persistent below-target inflation, places this in the 26-40 range ("below target; slack keeping prices in check").

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as BELOW potential. "Economy had been expanding moderately." "Wide margin of slack in product and labor markets." "A number of participants judged that the labor market currently featured substantial slack." One participant thought output "may be much closer to potential than had been thought," but this is clearly a minority view. "Number of factors continued to be seen as likely to restrain the pace of economic expansion." The bright-line rule: "substantial slack" language means score MUST be < 50. Given "expanding moderately" but "wide margin of slack" still present, and "unemployment rate would decline gradually," this is firmly in the 31-40 range (below potential, recovering but gap persists). Score ~36.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see labor market as still quite loose despite improvement. "Labor market conditions had improved further: Payroll employment had continued to expand, and the unemployment rate had declined notably in recent months. Still, unemployment remained elevated." "A number of participants judged that the labor market currently featured substantial slack." Aggregate hours "remained well below the series' pre-recession peak." "High number of persons working part time for economic reasons." The level is still loose despite improvement trend. Score ~33 (below full employment, slack remains, unemployment elevated).

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumer spending had increased moderately of late." "Household spending and business fixed investment had continued to advance." But "growth in real disposable income was still sluggish, and consumer sentiment...remained weak." "Most firms reportedly remained fairly cautious." Housing "remained depressed." Manufacturing, energy, agriculture "continued to advance." Auto sales strengthened. This is a mixed-to-moderate picture. Score ~40 (below trend but advancing; "sluggish" to "moderate").

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Financial conditions had improved notably since the January meeting: Equity prices were higher and risk spreads had declined." "Many participants noted that strains in global financial markets had eased somewhat, and that financial conditions were more supportive of economic growth." "Better conditions in corporate credit markets, especially the markets for high-yield bonds and leveraged loans." "Banking contacts were reporting steady, though modest, growth in C&I loans." Conditions are described as supportive/improving, with risk spreads declining. But euro-area risks remain in background. Overall: conditions somewhat loose to neutral, supportive. Score ~35 (somewhat loose; "generally supportive" of growth, improving credit conditions).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Members agreed that it would be appropriate to maintain the existing highly accommodative stance of monetary policy." They kept the fed funds rate at 0-0.25%, continued Operation Twist, and retained reinvestment policies. "Almost all members again agreed to indicate that the Committee expects to maintain a highly accommodative stance...at least through late 2014." "A number of members perceived a nonnegligible risk that improvements in employment could diminish...and saw this risk as reinforcing the case for leaving the forward guidance unchanged." One member dissented, judging current accommodation "much beyond this year would likely be inappropriate." This is clearly dovish -- maintaining maximum accommodation with extended forward guidance. The presence of ongoing QE-type programs (Operation Twist) puts this in the 15-25 range per rubric ("strong dovish: majority favors...maintaining max accommodation"). The one hawkish dissenter is a minor outlier. Score ~22.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One member dissented (the hawkish member). "A few others cited a weaker path of potential output" (implying less slack than headline numbers). "Other participants...were worried that inflation pressures could increase." There are some differences about the output gap and inflation outlook, but the policy action had broad agreement with only one clear dissent. Score ~30 (broad agreement with minor differences; 0-1 dissents).

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: "Many participants noted that strains in global financial markets had eased somewhat." Euro-area concerns discussed by "many" and "a number of" participants. "Participants saw the possibility of disruptions in global financial markets as continuing to pose a risk to growth." Financial conditions (equity prices, credit markets, banking contacts) discussed at moderate length. This is a meaningful topic but not dominant. Score ~40 (moderate discussion; several participants address it meaningfully).

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Almost all members again agreed to indicate that the Committee...currently anticipates that economic conditions...are likely to warrant exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate at least through late 2014." "Several members continued to anticipate...that the unemployment rate would still be well above their estimates of its longer-term normal level, and inflation would be at or below the Committee's longer-run objective, in late 2014." This is strong calendar-based forward guidance promising low rates through late 2014. Per the anchors, 2012-13 threshold/date-based guidance = 3-8 range. Score ~7 (strong date-based easing commitment through late 2014).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "High levels of uncertainty among households and businesses." "A number of members perceived a nonnegligible risk that improvements in employment could diminish as the year progressed, as had occurred in 2010 and 2011." "Unseasonably warm weather...added one more element of uncertainty to the interpretation of incoming data." "Increased volatility in financial markets remained a possibility." Euro-area risks. Fiscal cliff risk ("abrupt and sharp fiscal tightening would occur at the start of 2013"). Multiple sources of uncertainty mentioned, but not described as extreme or unprecedented. Score ~42 (somewhat elevated; several specific uncertainty factors cited).

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (36) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (33): Both below 50, consistent -- economy below potential with labor slack. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (22) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (7): Both dovish. The forward guidance is even more dovish because it's an explicit date-based commitment (late 2014), while the policy lean includes maintaining accommodation. The divergence is expected: forward guidance is a stronger commitment mechanism. Coherent.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (35): Higher = more pressure. Evidence says "subdued," "at or below 2%." Below midpoint. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP (36): Higher = more above potential. Evidence says "substantial slack." Below midpoint. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (35): Higher = tighter. Evidence says "improved," "more supportive," "eased." Below midpoint = loose. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (22): Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says maintaining max accommodation, QE-era policies. Below midpoint = dovish. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 35 +/-8 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Inflation had been subdued in recent months, although prices of crude oil and gasoline had increased of late." "Most meeting participants saw little evidence of cost pressures." "Most participants anticipated that after the temporary effect of the rise in oil and gasoline prices had run its course, inflation would be at or below the 2 percent rate." "A few participants were concerned that, with the persistence of considerable resource slack, inflation might be below the mandate-consistent rate for some time."
- **Center of gravity**: Participants perceive inflation as subdued and below target, with a temporary oil-price bump that does not alter the medium-term view. The majority expects inflation at or below 2%.
- **Minority views**: "Other participants...were worried that inflation pressures could increase as the expansion continued" and cited risks from oil prices and accommodative policy. A few worried inflation could stay persistently below mandate. These widen the band.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 36 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Economy had been expanding moderately." "A number of participants judged that the labor market currently featured substantial slack." Various indicators cited including aggregate hours "well below the series' pre-recession peak" and "high number of persons working part time for economic reasons." "Number of factors continued to be seen as likely to restrain the pace of economic expansion."
- **Center of gravity**: Economy is recovering but remains well below potential with substantial slack in product and labor markets.
- **Minority views**: "One participant pointed to inflation readings and a high rate of long-duration unemployment as signs that the current level of output may be much closer to potential than had been thought." This is a clear outlier.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 33 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Labor market conditions had improved further...the unemployment rate had declined notably in recent months. Still, unemployment remained elevated." "A number of participants judged that the labor market currently featured substantial slack." Indicators: aggregate hours well below pre-recession peak, high part-time for economic reasons, low ratios of job openings to unemployment.
- **Center of gravity**: Labor market improving from depressed levels but still substantially slack. Unemployment elevated, far from full employment.
- **Minority views**: One participant thought output closer to potential; the majority emphasized substantial remaining slack.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 40 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Consumer spending had increased moderately of late." "Household spending and business fixed investment had continued to advance." But "growth in real disposable income was still sluggish, and consumer sentiment...remained weak." "Most firms reportedly remained fairly cautious -- particularly on hiring decisions." Housing "remained depressed."
- **Center of gravity**: Moderate advances in spending and investment, but below trend given sluggish income growth, weak sentiment, and depressed housing. Manufacturing and autos showing strength.
- **Minority views**: "A few participants suggested that recent improvements...could help lay the groundwork for a strengthening in the pace of household spending."

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 35 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Financial conditions had improved notably since the January meeting: Equity prices were higher and risk spreads had declined." "Financial conditions were more supportive of economic growth." "Better conditions in corporate credit markets, especially the markets for high-yield bonds and leveraged loans." But "a number of participants noted that a longer-term solution to the banking and fiscal problems in the euro area would require substantial further adjustment."
- **Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are loose/supportive and improving, with risk spreads declining and equity prices up. Euro-area tail risk remains.
- **Minority views**: Euro-area disruption risk creates upside uncertainty on tightness.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 22 +/-8 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Members agreed that it would be appropriate to maintain the existing highly accommodative stance of monetary policy." "Almost all members again agreed to indicate...exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate at least through late 2014." "A number of members perceived a nonnegligible risk that improvements in employment could diminish...and saw this risk as reinforcing the case for leaving the forward guidance unchanged."
- **Center of gravity**: Strong dovish consensus -- maintaining maximum accommodation with extended forward guidance and Operation Twist. QE-era accommodation.
- **Minority views**: "One member judged that maintaining the current degree of policy accommodation much beyond this year would likely be inappropriate." This single hawkish dissenter widens the band.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Almost all members again agreed..." with one member dissenting on appropriateness of continued accommodation. Some disagreement on output gap (one vs. a number). Some disagreement on inflation outlook (a few below-target concerned vs. other participants worried about increases).
- **Center of gravity**: Broad agreement on policy with one clear dissent and moderate differences on economic assessment.
- **Minority views**: Differences on slack estimation and inflation trajectory create moderate but not sharp disagreement.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 40 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Many participants noted that strains in global financial markets had eased somewhat." Extensive discussion of euro-area policy actions (Greek debt swap, ECB LTRO). "Participants saw the possibility of disruptions in global financial markets as continuing to pose a risk to growth." Discussion of equity prices, credit markets, banking conditions.
- **Center of gravity**: Financial conditions/stability is a moderately prominent topic -- discussed by "many" participants with specific evidence cited, but not the dominant concern.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 7 +/-4 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Almost all members again agreed to indicate that the Committee expects to maintain a highly accommodative stance for monetary policy and currently anticipates that economic conditions...are likely to warrant exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate at least through late 2014." "Several members continued to anticipate...that the unemployment rate would still be well above their estimates of its longer-term normal level...in late 2014."
- **Center of gravity**: Strong date-based forward guidance committing to near-zero rates through late 2014. This is explicit easing commitment of the type associated with the lowest scores on this dimension.
- **Minority views**: One member found continued accommodation beyond this year inappropriate, but did not shift the forward guidance language.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 42 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "High levels of uncertainty among households and businesses." "A number of members perceived a nonnegligible risk that improvements in employment could diminish as the year progressed, as had occurred in 2010 and 2011." "Unseasonably warm weather of recent months added one more element of uncertainty." Euro-area disruption risk, fiscal cliff risk, China/emerging Asia slowdown risk.
- **Center of gravity**: Multiple specific sources of uncertainty noted by participants, including weather effects on data interpretation, euro-area tail risks, and the possibility that employment gains might not be sustained. Somewhat elevated but not extreme.
