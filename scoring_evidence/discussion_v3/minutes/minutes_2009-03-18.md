# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2009-03-18 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). This is a post-2009 segmented document, though it includes substantial staff review sections (pages 4-7) that must be excluded from scoring. Participant views begin at "Meeting Participants' Views and Committee Policy Action" (line 371) and continue through the policy discussion.

**Era context**: March 2009 -- the GFC trough. The economy is in severe recession, the financial system remains deeply stressed, the fed funds rate is already at the zero lower bound, and the Committee is launching large-scale asset purchases (QE1). This is one of the most extreme downturns in postwar history.

**Quantifier distribution in participant section**: "nearly all" (conditions deteriorated), "all" (inflation pressures subdued), "several" (inflation below desirable, employment cutbacks), "most" (downside risks predominating), "some" (various views on housing, spending), "a few" (deflation risk), "many" (banking system essential), "one member" (various tactical preferences). The language is heavily tilted toward consensus on the dire economic outlook, with differences mostly about tactics and timing of recovery.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as well below the midpoint. "All participants agreed that inflation pressures were likely to remain subdued, and several expressed the view that inflation was likely to persist below desirable levels." "A few pointing to the risk of deflation." Rising unemployment and falling capacity utilization holding down wages and prices. Inflation expectations appeared subdued. This is a clear deflation-risk environment. Direction: significantly below 50. Anchor: 15-20 for 2009 deflation fears. The "all" quantifier on subdued inflation and "a few" on deflation risk supports 15-18. Score: 16.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**: "Nearly all meeting participants said that conditions had deteriorated relative to their expectations." "The slowdown was widespread across sectors." Production and sales declining steeply, business capital spending weakening, foreign economic activity in apparent sharp fall. "Very weak economic outlook." This is deep recession, far below potential. Bright-line rule: massive slack = well below 50. Anchor: 15-22 for GFC trough. Score: 14.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Large declines in equity prices... and mounting job losses." "Participants anticipated further employment cutbacks over coming months." "Rising unemployment and falling capacity utilization were holding down wages and prices." Staff data (for context only): unemployment at 8.1% and climbing, payroll employment dropped considerably. The participant discussion reflects massive labor slack. Anchor: 15-22 for 2009. Score: 14.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Large declines in equity prices, a further drop in house prices, and mounting job losses threatened to further depress consumer spending." "Business capital spending was weakening in an environment of uncertainty and low business confidence." "Expenditures were being cut substantially for a wide range of capital equipment." "Several participants noted the tentative signs of stabilization in consumer spending in January and February" -- but "others suggested that strains on household balance sheets... could well lead to further increases in the saving rate." Net: spending collapsing/at bottom with only tentative stabilization. Anchor: 15-20 for 2008Q4-2009Q1. The tentative stabilization nudges slightly above pure freefall. Score: 17.

**5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Credit conditions remained very tight, and financial markets remained fragile and unsettled, with pressures on financial institutions generally intensifying this year." "For the most part, participants viewed conditions in financial markets as little changed but remaining extraordinarily stressed." "Businesses reported that bank financing was becoming more expensive and more difficult to obtain." This is extreme financial tightness, though slightly less than the September 2008 peak (participants note some indicators worsened but conditions broadly similar, and investment-grade bond issuance was a "bright spot"). Anchor: 85-95 for crisis. This is severe but not the absolute peak of the credit freeze. Score: 82.

**6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Committee members agreed that substantial additional purchases of longer-term assets eligible for open market operations would be appropriate." They agreed to purchase up to $750B in agency MBS, $100B in agency debt, and $300B in Treasuries. This is a massive expansion of QE at the zero lower bound. "Such purchases would provide further monetary stimulus to help address the very weak economic outlook and reduce the risk that inflation could persist for a time below rates." Differences were only about the composition and size of purchases, not direction. Anchor: 2-5 for March 2009. Score: 5.

**7. DISSENT_INTENSITY**: The Committee unanimously supported the general direction (additional purchases). Differences were about composition: "One member preferred to focus additional purchases on longer-term Treasury securities, whereas another member preferred to focus on agency MBS. However, both could support expanded purchases across a range of assets." "Members expressed a range of views as to the preferred size of the increase in purchases." These are tactical differences within a consensus on direction. No dissents. Anchor: 40-50 for March 2009. The range of views on size and composition warrants moderate disagreement. Score: 35.

**8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial conditions/stability are discussed extensively throughout the participant section. "Financial markets remained fragile and unsettled, with pressures on financial institutions generally intensifying." "Many viewed the strengthening of the banking system as essential for a sustained and robust recovery." TALF, asset purchases, swap lines, banking system programs -- all extensively discussed. Financial stability is a dominant topic rivaling the economic assessment. Anchor: 60-75 for 2008 early crisis. This is deeper into the crisis. Score: 78.

**9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The Committee launched massive QE ($1.15 trillion in total additional purchases). The forward signal is maximal easing -- "help address the very weak economic outlook and reduce the risk that inflation could persist for a time below rates that best foster longer-term economic growth and price stability." Purchases extended over 6-9 months. The forward posture is strong easing/accommodation. Anchor: 2-10 for 2020 emergency (similar crisis context). Score: 6.

**10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants expressed concern about downside risks to an outlook for activity that was already weak." "Participants expressed a variety of views about the strength and timing of the recovery." "Some believed that the natural resilience of market forces would become evident later this year. Others, who saw recovery as delayed and potentially weak." "Working across a range of assets and instruments was appropriate when the effects of any one tactic were uncertain." The uncertainty is high but perhaps not at the absolute peak -- participants have a shared view of the current situation (severe downturn) but vary on recovery timing. Anchor: 85-95 for March 2009 trough. Score: 78.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 16 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All participants agreed that inflation pressures were likely to remain subdued, and several expressed the view that inflation was likely to persist below desirable levels."
- "A few pointing to the risk of deflation. Even without a continuation of outright price declines, falling expectations of inflation would raise the real rate of interest."
- "Rising unemployment and falling capacity utilization were holding down wages and prices and inflation expectations appeared subdued."

**Center of gravity**: Unanimous view that inflation is subdued with downside risk toward deflation. This places the score solidly in the "meaningful deflation risk" range.
**Minority views**: A few see outright deflation risk (would push to 10-12), while the majority sees simply subdued/below desirable (15-20). Band reflects this spread.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 14 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Nearly all meeting participants said that conditions had deteriorated relative to their expectations at the time of the January meeting. The slowdown was widespread across sectors."
- "District business contacts indicated that production and sales were declining steeply."
- "Most participants viewed downside risks as predominating in the near term, mainly owing to potential adverse feedback effects."

**Center of gravity**: The economy is in severe, worsening contraction -- among the deepest recessions in postwar history with conditions deteriorating even from already dire expectations.
**Minority views**: Some see tentative stabilization signs (consumer spending), which provides the upper edge of the band.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 14 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Mounting job losses" with "participants anticipated further employment cutbacks over coming months."
- "Rising unemployment and falling capacity utilization were holding down wages and prices."
- Large declines in payroll employment were widespread across industries.

**Center of gravity**: Massive labor slack with conditions still deteriorating. Deep recession labor market.
**Minority views**: Some note "perhaps at a gradually diminishing rate" of cutbacks, suggesting the worst pace may be nearing a peak.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 17 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Business capital spending was weakening in an environment of uncertainty and low business confidence."
- "Expenditures were being cut substantially for a wide range of capital equipment, and spending on nonresidential structures had recently turned down."
- "Several participants noted the tentative signs of stabilization in consumer spending in January and February. However, others suggested that strains on household balance sheets... could well lead to further increases in the saving rate."

**Center of gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment are deeply depressed. Consumer spending shows only tentative stabilization at very low levels; business investment continues to fall. Net assessment: near the bottom of the range with faint signs of leveling.
**Minority views**: Split between those seeing tentative stabilization and those expecting further weakness.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 82 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Credit conditions remained very tight, and financial markets remained fragile and unsettled, with pressures on financial institutions generally intensifying this year."
- "For the most part, participants viewed conditions in financial markets as little changed but remaining extraordinarily stressed."
- "Businesses reported that bank financing was becoming more expensive and more difficult to obtain."

**Center of gravity**: Extraordinarily stressed financial conditions, though not quite at the absolute peak of September-October 2008. Investment-grade bond issuance noted as a "bright spot" prevents scoring at 90+.
**Minority views**: "Some indicators... suggested a worsening of financial market strains since January" (pushes higher) vs. investment-grade issuance strength (pushes lower).

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 5 +/-3 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Committee members agreed that substantial additional purchases of longer-term assets eligible for open market operations would be appropriate."
- Agreed to purchase up to $750B agency MBS, $100B agency debt, $300B Treasuries -- a massive QE expansion.
- "Such purchases would provide further monetary stimulus to help address the very weak economic outlook and reduce the risk that inflation could persist for a time below rates."

**Center of gravity**: This is a "do whatever it takes" moment -- launching QE1 at massive scale with rates already at zero. One of the most dovish meetings in FOMC history.
**Minority views**: Differences only about tactics (composition of purchases, size), not direction. Narrow band.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 35 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "One member preferred to focus additional purchases on longer-term Treasury securities, whereas another member preferred to focus on agency MBS."
- "Members expressed a range of views as to the preferred size of the increase in purchases."
- "Some participants expressed concern about the risks that might arise from the possible extension of the TALF to include older and lower-quality assets."

**Center of gravity**: Broad agreement on direction with moderate tactical differences on composition and scale. No formal dissents. Differences are within the framework of consensus.
**Minority views**: The range of views on size and concerns about TALF extension show some meaningful disagreement on implementation details.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 78 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Many viewed the strengthening of the banking system as essential for a sustained and robust recovery."
- Extensive discussion of TALF, asset purchases, credit conditions, financial market strains, swap lines.
- "Participants shared comments received from financial industry contacts on their experiences with and concerns about recent government programs to stabilize the financial system."

**Center of gravity**: Financial stability is a dominant topic, discussed at length by most participants and central to the policy response. It rivals the economic assessment in emphasis.
**Minority views**: Minimal -- nearly all participants engaged with this topic extensively.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 6 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Launching $1.15 trillion in asset purchases over 6-9 months -- a massive forward easing commitment.
- "The Committee believed that purchases of these amounts would help to promote a return to economic growth and price stability."
- Purchases designed to "reduce the risk that inflation could persist for a time below rates that best foster longer-term economic growth and price stability."

**Center of gravity**: The forward signal is maximum easing accommodation. The extended purchase horizons (6-9 months) provide an explicit forward commitment.
**Minority views**: Some wanted even larger purchases; none wanted less. Direction is uniformly dovish.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 78 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants expressed a variety of views about the strength and timing of the recovery."
- "Some believed that the natural resilience of market forces would become evident later this year. Others, who saw recovery as delayed and potentially weak."
- "Working across a range of assets and instruments was appropriate when the effects of any one tactic were uncertain."
- "Most participants viewed downside risks as predominating."

**Center of gravity**: Very high uncertainty, particularly about the timing and strength of recovery and the effectiveness of policy tools. Participants share a common assessment of current severe conditions but diverge substantially on the forward path.
**Minority views**: Range from "natural resilience evident later this year" to "delayed and potentially weak recovery" -- a wide spectrum.

**BANKING_STRESS: 82 +/-5 [High confidence]**
