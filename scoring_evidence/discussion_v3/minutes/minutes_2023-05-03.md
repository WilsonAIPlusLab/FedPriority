# FOMC Participant Discussion Scoring — 2023-05-03

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Post-2009 segmented format with clear "Participants' Views" section.

**Era context**: May 2023 — the Fed is deep into its tightening cycle, having just raised the target range to 5.00-5.25%. Inflation remains well above 2% but is showing some deceleration. Banking stress from the SVB/Signature Bank failures in March 2023 is a prominent backdrop. The labor market remains historically tight.

**Quantifier distribution**: Heavy use of "participants" (broad consensus language), "many," "several," "some," "a few," "almost all," "all." Notable: "all participants agreed" on the 25bp hike; "almost all" on downside growth risks and upside inflation risks; "many" focused on optionality; "some" wanted more firming; "several" said no more firming needed.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants agreed inflation was "unacceptably high" and "well above the Committee's longer-run 2 percent objective." Core inflation showed "only some signs of moderation." Core nonhousing services inflation "had shown few signs of slowing." Core goods inflation "had decelerated less rapidly than expected." This is clearly elevated — "unacceptably high" is strong language. However, there are signs of moderation starting (housing services expected to decline, goods moderating since mid-2022). The language "well above" and "unacceptably" points to 70-80 range. Compared to 2022 peak anchors (82-88), inflation is still high but showing initial moderation. Score: ~73.
Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. "Unacceptably high" = high pressure. Score above 50. Correct.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic activity had expanded at a modest pace in the first quarter." Participants "generally expected real GDP to grow at a pace below its longer-run trend rate in 2023." They noted effects of "restrictive financial conditions." Growth in business fixed investment was "subdued." Consumer spending showed some strength in January but fell in February-March. The language is about modest/below-trend growth, not contraction. "Below its longer-run trend rate" suggests operating slightly below potential or near it with deceleration. However, there's no "slack" language — rather the economy is still running but decelerating. The need for "below-trend growth" to reduce inflationary pressures implies the economy is still at or slightly above potential. Score: ~48-50. The economy is near potential but the trajectory is toward below-trend. I'll score 49.
Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Modest growth, below-trend expected, but no slack language. Near potential trending down. 49 fits.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "The labor market remained very tight, with robust payroll gains in March and an unemployment rate near historically low levels." However, "some signs that the imbalance of supply and demand in the labor market was easing" — prime-age participation returning to pre-pandemic level, reductions in job openings and quits. Wage growth "well above what would be consistent over the longer run with the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective." District contacts reported "less difficulty in hiring, lower turnover rates, and some layoffs." The center of gravity is clearly tight but with early signs of easing. "Very tight" with nascent softening suggests 63-68. The anchor for 2022-23 is 65-75. "Very tight" with beginning easing signs: ~65.
Polarity check: Higher = tighter. "Very tight" = high score. Correct.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "showed strength in the first quarter" but was "driven mainly by very strong spending growth in January, while real spending fell modestly over February and March." Anticipated to "grow at a subdued rate over the remainder of 2023." Business fixed investment was "subdued" with "relatively high borrowing costs" and "weak growth." Tighter bank lending expected to weigh further. The net picture: recent strength fading, forward-looking subdued. Score near or slightly below trend — around 42-45. The January strength pulls it up, but the forward guidance is clearly below trend.
Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending. Mixed-to-weakening = below midpoint. 43 fits.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Participants assessed that "cumulative tightening of monetary policy over the past year had contributed significantly to more restrictive financial conditions." Banking-sector stress contributed to "some tightening of lending standards beyond that which had occurred during previous quarters." Participants judged banking stress would "likely induce banks to tighten lending standards by more than they would have in response to higher interest rates alone." Higher interest rates "continue to restrain interest-sensitive expenditures." This is clearly tight — "restrictive financial conditions" combined with additional bank credit tightening. The anchor for 2022 rate hikes is 60-70. The addition of banking stress pushes slightly higher. Score: ~67.
Polarity check: Higher = tighter. "Restrictive" + additional banking tightening. Correct.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All agreed on 25bp hike. Forward outlook: "the extent to which additional increases... may be appropriate after this meeting had become less certain." "Many participants focused on the need to retain optionality." "Some" commented additional firming "would likely be warranted." "Several" noted further firming "may not be necessary." This is a meeting where they hiked but signaled potential pause. The center of gravity is hawkish (just hiked) but with significant uncertainty about continuing. The split between "some" wanting more and "several" saying enough creates a moderately hawkish but cautious lean. Score: ~62. They hiked and remain attentive to inflation, but the forward lean is decidedly less certain.
Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Hiked 25bp but signaling possible pause. 62 fits — moderate hawkish, leaning toward data dependence.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All participants agreed" on the 25bp hike. However, notable divergence on the forward path: "some" want more firming, "several" say no more needed, "many" want optionality. No formal dissents. The policy action was unanimous but forward views are notably split. Score: ~35. Broad agreement on current action with meaningful differences on path forward.
Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Unanimous vote but split forward views. 35 fits.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Extensive discussion. An entire paragraph devoted to financial stability with "various participants" commenting on banking sector. Discussion of specific vulnerabilities (unrealized losses, uninsured deposits, CRE exposure), nonbank financial institutions (money market funds, hedge funds, mortgage servicers, digital assets), debt ceiling, Treasury market functioning. "A number of participants emphasized" maintaining readiness of liquidity and regulatory tools. This is a prominent topic — well above typical. Score: ~65.
Polarity check: Higher = more attention. Extensive discussion. Correct.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is notably uncertain/mixed. "The extent to which additional increases... had become less certain." "Many participants focused on the need to retain optionality." "Some" want more tightening, "several" think it may not be necessary. The adjusted language was described as helpful for data-dependent communication. "Some participants stressed that it was crucial to communicate that the language... should not be interpreted as signaling either that decreases in the target range are likely" (text cuts off but clearly saying not signaling cuts). The forward tone is mildly hawkish but shifting toward neutral/data-dependent. Score: ~60. Still leaning tightening but much less committed than prior meetings.
Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Uncertain but still tilted toward possible further tightening. 60 fits.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants noted that risks associated with the recent banking stress had led them to raise their already high assessment of uncertainty around their economic outlooks." "The extent of these effects remained uncertain" and "highly uncertain" appear repeatedly. "Too early to assess with confidence the magnitude and persistence." Debt ceiling concerns add further uncertainty. This is elevated uncertainty — participants explicitly state they have raised their already-high uncertainty. Score: ~62.
Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. Explicitly elevated and raised further. Correct.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (49) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (65): These diverge, which is explained by the lagged nature — labor market still very tight from prior conditions while output growth is already decelerating. This is characteristic of late-cycle dynamics.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (62) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (60): These are aligned — moderate hawkish current action with slightly less hawkish forward signal as participants debate whether more hikes are needed. Consistent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 73 +/-6 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Participants agreed that inflation was unacceptably high."
- "Core nonhousing services inflation had shown few signs of slowing in the past few months."
- "Core goods inflation had... decelerated less rapidly than expected in recent months."
- Housing services inflation expected to decline as new-tenant rents feed through.

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants view inflation as clearly and persistently above target, with slower-than-expected progress on core measures, though some moderation channels (housing, goods) are beginning.

**Minority views**: Several noted tighter credit conditions "may not put much downward pressure on inflation" because supply could also be restrained. This reinforces the elevated assessment. No significant minority arguing inflation is under control.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 49 +/-5 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity had expanded at a modest pace in the first quarter."
- "Participants generally expected real GDP to grow at a pace below its longer-run trend rate in 2023, reflecting the effects of restrictive financial conditions."
- "A period of below-trend growth in real GDP and some softening in labor market conditions would be needed."

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is near potential but decelerating toward below-trend growth. No recession language, no slack language, but not running hot either. Modest expansion with downward trajectory.

**Minority views**: "A few assessed that there were upside risks to economic growth," but "almost all participants commented that downside risks to growth... had increased."

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 65 +/-5 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "The labor market remained very tight, with robust payroll gains in March and an unemployment rate near historically low levels."
- "Some signs that the imbalance of supply and demand in the labor market was easing, with prime-age labor force participation returning to its pre-pandemic level."
- "Nominal wage growth... was still running at a pace... well above what would be consistent... with the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective."

**Center-of-gravity**: Very tight labor market with early but incomplete signs of normalization. Still historically tight levels but directional improvement evident.

**Minority views**: "Some participants noted that their District contacts reported less difficulty in hiring, lower turnover rates, and some layoffs" — these suggest faster easing at the margins.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 43 +/-6 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending showed strength in the first quarter" but "quarterly strength was driven mainly by very strong spending growth in January, while real spending fell modestly over February and March."
- "Consumer spending would likely grow at a subdued rate over the remainder of 2023."
- "Growth in business fixed investment was subdued in the first quarter, reflecting relatively high borrowing costs."

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending front-loaded in January then weakened; business investment subdued. Forward outlook is below-trend, weighted down by tighter financial and credit conditions.

**Minority views**: None notable — broad agreement on the subdued forward path.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 67 +/-6 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Cumulative tightening of monetary policy over the past year had contributed significantly to more restrictive financial conditions."
- "Many participants commented that the recent developments in the banking sector had contributed to some tightening of lending standards beyond that which had occurred during previous quarters."
- "Banking-sector stress would, in coming quarters, likely induce banks to tighten lending standards by more than they would have in response to higher interest rates alone."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly restrictive from rate hikes, with an additional layer of credit tightening from banking stress. Conditions are tight but not crisis-level — "some participants mentioned that access to credit had not yet appeared to have declined significantly."

**Minority views**: "Some participants mentioned that access to credit had not yet appeared to have declined significantly" — suggesting the tightening impact may be less than feared.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 62 +/-8 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "All participants agreed that it was appropriate to raise the target range for the federal funds rate 25 basis points."
- "Some participants commented that... additional policy firming would likely be warranted at future meetings."
- "Several participants noted that if the economy evolved along the lines of their current outlooks, then further policy firming after this meeting may not be necessary."
- "Many participants focused on the need to retain optionality after this meeting."

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous 25bp hike but meaningful split on further action. The center of gravity is moderately hawkish — they hiked and remain "highly attentive to inflation risks," but the forward lean has softened considerably with banking stress creating additional uncertainty.

**Minority views**: "Several" see no need for more hikes (dovish wing); "some" see likely need for more (hawkish wing). The middle ground of "optionality" dominates.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 35 +/-7 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "All participants agreed" on the 25bp hike (unanimous).
- Forward path divergence: "some" want more firming vs. "several" say it may not be necessary.
- "Almost all participants" agreed on downside growth risks and upside inflation risks.

**Center-of-gravity**: No dissents on current action, but notable spread of views on the forward path. The "some" vs. "several" split on further hikes is a meaningful divergence in assessment, though not rising to formal disagreement.

**Minority views**: The divergence is primarily about the forward path rather than the current decision.

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability is a prominent topic at this meeting, driven by recent banking stress and debt ceiling concerns. Discussed at length by various and many participants across multiple dimensions.

**Minority views**: None — broad agreement on the importance of monitoring financial stability.

- "Many participants focused on the need to retain optionality after this meeting."

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is elevated and explicitly acknowledged as having increased due to banking stress layered on top of already-uncertain inflation dynamics. Multiple sources of uncertainty cited (banking, debt ceiling, policy lags).

**Minority views**: Broad agreement on elevated uncertainty; no participants expressed high confidence in the outlook.
