# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2014-04-30

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented — participant views are clearly separated from staff content.

**Era context**: April 2014 — midway through the post-GFC recovery. The Fed is in the midst of tapering asset purchases (QE3 taper began Dec 2013). The federal funds rate is at the effective lower bound. Inflation is running below 2%, unemployment is declining but still elevated. The economy had a weather-related Q1 slowdown.

**Quantifier distribution**: "generally" (several uses), "most" (2 uses), "many" (1), "a number of" (3-4 uses), "some" (4 uses), "a few" (3 uses), "a couple of" (3 uses). The language is broadly consensual with minority caveats.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW target. "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective." "Many participants" saw stabilization. "Most participants expected inflation to return to 2 percent within the next few years." "A few others expressed the concern that the return to 2 percent inflation could be even more gradual." Inflation was also "seen as posing possible risks to economic performance" (i.e., too-low inflation was the risk). Direction: below target, stabilizing but still low. Score should be in the 30-40 range — below target but showing signs of stabilizing. The anchor range for 2013-19 is 38-42. Given the "below objective" language but with stabilization signals, I'll place this at 36.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants describe the economy as on "a trajectory of moderate growth" after a weather-related Q1 slump. "Some participants remarked that it was too early to confirm that the bounceback...would put the economy on a path of sustained above-trend economic growth." The unemployment rate "remained elevated." "Fiscal policy was restraining economic growth." This is clearly below potential — moderate growth recovering but gap persists. Bright-line: unemployment elevated = below potential = must be < 50. Anchor for 2010-14 recovery is 35-42. Score: 40.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Conditions in the labor market continued to improve" but "a number of them argued that several indicators of labor underutilization — including the low labor force participation rate and the still-elevated rates of longer-duration unemployment and of workers employed part time for economic reasons — suggested that there is more slack in the labor market than is captured by the unemployment rate alone." "Low nominal wage inflation was also viewed as consistent with slack in labor markets." However, "some participants reported that labor markets were tight in their Districts." Center of gravity: still significant slack, with localized tightness. This is clearly below full employment. Anchor for 2011-14 is 35-40. Score: 37.

Coherence check: OUTPUT_GAP (40) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (37) both below 50, consistent.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Household spending appeared to be rising more rapidly." Business contacts were "generally optimistic" with "increased sales of automobiles, higher production in the aerospace industry." "Contacts in several Districts pointed to plans for increasing capital expenditures." However, "business fixed investment had edged down" and "the recovery in the housing sector remained slow." Net: mixed — consumer spending picking up, business investment soft, housing weak. Slightly below trend. Anchor for 2011-13 is 35-42, and for 2014-19 is 50-55. April 2014 is transitional, with weather rebound. Score: 43.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "A couple of participants noted that mortgage credit availability remained constrained and lending standards were tight compared with historical norms." However, the financial stability discussion notes "declining credit spreads, particularly on speculative-grade corporate bonds" and "low level of expected volatility" and "increase in investors' appetite for risk" — all signs of loose conditions. The broad thrust is that financial conditions are generally accommodative/loose, with some tightness remaining in mortgage credit. Score: 32 (somewhat loose to neutral, reflecting accommodative conditions with some mortgage credit tightness).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The Committee is in the middle of tapering QE but rates remain at zero. Members discussed communication about eventual rate increases but no one is pushing for near-term hikes. "Members noted that there had been little change" in the policy outlook. The action was to continue tapering at $10B/meeting pace. Members agreed to "monitor carefully" inflation. This is a continuation of accommodative policy with gradual removal of accommodation via taper. Anchor for 2014-15 pre-liftoff is 40-50. The forward guidance discussion about "the first increase in the federal funds rate moves closer" suggests a mild tightening lean developing. Score: 43.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Changes to the Committee's forward guidance at the March FOMC meeting had been well understood by investors." Discussion of communicating about eventual rate increases. "A few participants judged that additional clarity about the Committee's reaction function could be particularly important in the event that future economic conditions necessitate a more rapid rise in the target federal funds rate." The tone is pointing toward eventual normalization but emphasizing patience and data-dependence. The statement language about rates remaining "below levels the Committee views as normal in the longer run" for "some time" is dovish forward guidance. Anchor for 2014 taper is 8-15, but this is post-taper-initiation and moving toward rate liftoff discussion. Score: 30 — easing lean with data dependence, moving toward neutral.

Coherence check: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (43) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (30) — the current lean is slightly dovish-to-balanced (continuing taper, no rate hike imminent), while forward guidance remains more explicitly accommodative ("below normal for some time"). The divergence is reasonable: current action (tapering) is mildly hawkish relative to pure QE, but forward guidance still emphasizes accommodation.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: The document shows broad agreement. "Generally" and "most" language dominates. Differences exist on labor market slack assessment ("a number" vs "some") and inflation return pace ("most" vs "a few"), but these are emphasis differences, not fundamental disagreements. No mention of formal dissents. Score: 25 — broad agreement with minor emphasis differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: A dedicated paragraph on financial stability. "Participants generally did not see imbalances that posed significant near-term risks" but reviewed "potential future risks." Discussion of leveraged loans, credit spreads, low volatility, risk appetite. "Some stated that it would be helpful to continue to explore the appropriate regulatory, supervisory, and monetary policy responses." This is moderate — a meaningful but not dominant part of the discussion. Anchor for 2013-19 is 20-35. This meeting has a dedicated paragraph with "several participants" and "some" weighing in. Score: 35.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants continued to view the risks to the outlook for the economy and the labor market as nearly balanced." "Some participants remarked that it was too early to confirm that the bounceback...would put the economy on a path of sustained above-trend economic growth." "A number of participants pointed to possible sources of downside risk to growth, including a persistent slowdown in the housing sector or potential international developments." This is standard-level uncertainty — risks roughly balanced with some downside concerns noted. Not elevated. Anchor for 2013-14 is 25-35. Score: 30.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 36 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective over the intermeeting period."
- "Many participants saw the recent behavior of the prices of food, energy, shelter, and imports as consistent with a stabilization in inflation."
- "Most participants expected inflation to return to 2 percent within the next few years."
- "A few others expressed the concern that the return to 2 percent inflation could be even more gradual."

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation is running below target with signs of stabilization, but remains a concern on the downside. The majority expects a gradual return to 2%.

**Minority views**: A few worried about even slower return to target, pushing the uncertainty band slightly downward.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 40 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Recent indicators pointed to a rebound and suggested that the economy had returned to a trajectory of moderate growth."
- "Some participants remarked that it was too early to confirm that the bounceback in economic activity would put the economy on a path of sustained above-trend economic growth."
- "The unemployment rate, however, remained elevated."
- "Fiscal policy was restraining economic growth, but the extent of that restraint had diminished."

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is below potential, recovering at a moderate pace from a weather-related Q1 slump. Gap is narrowing but persists.

**Minority views**: Minor disagreement about the pace of recovery, not its direction.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 37 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "A number of them argued that several indicators of labor underutilization...suggested that there is more slack in the labor market than is captured by the unemployment rate alone."
- "Low nominal wage inflation was also viewed as consistent with slack in labor markets."
- "Some participants reported that labor markets were tight in their Districts or that contacts indicated some sectors or occupations were experiencing shortages of workers."

**Center-of-gravity**: Significant labor market slack remains, though conditions are gradually improving. The center of gravity sees more slack than the headline unemployment rate suggests.

**Minority views**: Some reported localized tightness in certain Districts/sectors, widening the uncertainty band.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 43 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Household spending appeared to be rising more rapidly."
- "Business contacts in many parts of the country were generally optimistic about economic prospects, with reports of increased sales of automobiles, higher production in the aerospace industry."
- "Business fixed investment had edged down and the recovery in the housing sector remained slow."

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending is strengthening and business optimism is present, but business fixed investment declined and housing remains weak. Net assessment is slightly below trend.

**Minority views**: Minimal divergence; the picture is consistently mixed.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 32 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad financial conditions are loose (compressed spreads, low volatility, risk appetite up), though mortgage credit remains tight. The net is somewhat loose.

**Minority views**: Mortgage credit tightness provides some offset to overall looseness.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 43 +/-5 [High confidence]

- "Economic conditions may, for some time, warrant keeping the target federal funds rate below levels the Committee views as normal in the longer run."
- "Members agreed that inflation developments should be monitored carefully."

**Center-of-gravity**: Lean dovish to balanced. Continuation of gradual taper with no urgency to raise rates. Below-target inflation reinforces patience.

**Minority views**: Some discussion of eventual rate normalization, but no push for near-term action.
