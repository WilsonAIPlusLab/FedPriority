# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2018-09-26

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized participant views). Segmented format — participant discussion section is clearly separated from staff review.

**Era context**: September 2018 is in the middle of the Fed's gradual hiking cycle. The economy is strong, unemployment is low, inflation is near the 2% target, and the Committee raised rates 25bp at this meeting (to 2.00-2.25%). Tax cuts enacted in late 2017 are providing fiscal stimulus. Trade policy uncertainty (tariffs) is a growing concern. This is broadly a "late-cycle expansion" environment.

**Quantifier distribution**: Key quantifiers observed:
- "all participants" — appropriate to raise rates 25bp
- "almost all" — little change in outlook; appropriate to remove "accommodative" language
- "participants generally agreed" — labor market strengthening; risks roughly balanced
- "a number of" — favorable economic factors; financial conditions accommodative; contacts citing trade concerns
- "several" — acceleration in labor costs; inflation may modestly exceed 2%
- "some" — trade uncertainty; leveraged loan concerns; dollar strength concern
- "a few" — stronger-than-expected activity; weak residential construction; policy to become modestly restrictive; yield curve discussion
- "a couple" — not favor restrictive stance without overheating signs; systematic review of framework

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as AT target. "Both overall inflation and inflation for items other than food and energy remained near 2 percent." "Several participants commented that inflation may modestly exceed 2 percent for a period of time." Some contacts report firming input prices due to tariffs. A couple note inflation expectations still below 2%. The center of gravity is right at target with slight upside risk. Direction: at the midpoint. Score should be near 50, maybe slightly above (51-53) given the firming pressures and tariff pass-through commentary.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as ABOVE potential. "Economic activity rose at a strong rate." "Above-trend GDP growth" is explicitly mentioned. Fiscal stimulus, strong confidence, accommodative financial conditions all cited. The economy is running above potential. Per the bright-line rule, "above-trend" language requires > 50. The language "strong rate" and "above-trend" suggests modestly to clearly above potential. Score: 57-60.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see labor markets as TIGHT. "Conditions continued to strengthen." "Tight labor markets, with difficulty finding qualified workers" in many Districts. Firms increasing salaries, benefits, amenities. However, "wage growth remained moderate by historical standards" due to tepid productivity. This is solidly tight. Score: 60-65.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Participants characterize consumption growth as "strong" and business fixed investment as having "grown strongly." Robust disposable income, high confidence, solid balance sheets. However, residential investment is weak. Some contacts foregoing investment due to labor shortages and trade uncertainty. Net position: clearly above trend. Score: 60-64.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "A number of participants noted that financial conditions remained accommodative." The rise in rates and dollar appreciation was offset by equity price increases. Broader measures point to accommodative conditions. Some concern about leveraged loans (loosening standards). This is clearly on the loose/accommodative side. Score: 30-35 (lower = looser).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: ALL participants supported the 25bp hike. "Almost all" favored removing "accommodative" language from the statement. Forward: "further gradual increases" anticipated. "A few" expected policy to become modestly restrictive. "A number" judged it necessary to raise above longer-run neutral temporarily. Only "a couple" did not favor restrictive stance. This is solidly hawkish — consensus for continued hiking with forward lean toward more. The 2017-18 hiking anchor is 70-75. Score: 70-72.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: All participants agreed on the 25bp hike. Almost all agreed on removing "accommodative" language. There is some range of views on the forward path (a few want restrictive, a couple don't), but the current action had near-consensus. This is broad agreement with minor differences about future path. Score: 18-22.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: "Some participants commented about the continued growth in leveraged loans, the loosening of terms and standards on these loans" as reasons to "remain mindful of vulnerabilities and possible risks to financial stability." "A few participants" discussed yield curve inversion signals. Financial conditions discussed as accommodative. Some mention of financial imbalances risk. This is limited but present discussion. Score: 28-33.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Further gradual increases" is the consensus forward signal. Removing the word "accommodative" was seen as not changing the expected path. "A few" expect modestly restrictive policy. "A number" see need to temporarily exceed longer-run neutral. This is a clear tightening bias going forward. Score: 70-73.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants generally agreed that risks to the outlook appeared roughly balanced." Trade policy is "a source of uncertainty." EME financial stress cited as risk. A few discussed yield curve inversion signals. But overall, risks are balanced and uncertainty is at normal levels. Score: 28-33.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (58) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (62): Both above 50, consistent with above-potential economy and tight labor market. Labor slightly higher reflects the direct "tight" language vs. more moderate "above trend" for output. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (71) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (72): Both strongly hawkish, consistent with consensus for continued gradual hiking. Coherent.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says "near 2 percent" with slight firming. Score ~52. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says "accommodative." Score ~32. Matches (low score = loose).
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Unanimous hike + further gradual increases. Score ~71. Matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 52 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "On a 12-month basis, both overall inflation and inflation for items other than food and energy remained near 2 percent."
- "Several participants commented that inflation may modestly exceed 2 percent for a period of time."
- "Some contacts indicated that input prices had been bolstered by strong demand or import tariffs."
- "A couple of participants emphasized that... some measures of trend inflation or longer-term inflation expectations were below levels consistent with the 2 percent objective."

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation is perceived as essentially at the 2% target, with modest upside pressures from tariffs and strong demand partially offset by below-target expectations measures. The net assessment is very slightly above neutral.

**Minority views**: A couple see below-target expectations as a concern (pulling score down); several see potential for modest overshoot (pulling up). The band is narrow because the core assessment is convergent.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 58 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity rose at a strong rate."
- "Participants noted a number of favorable economic factors that were supporting above-trend GDP growth."
- "A few of them judged that recent data pointed to a pace of economic activity that was stronger than they had expected earlier this year."

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is clearly running above potential with "above-trend GDP growth" supported by fiscal stimulus, strong labor markets, and accommodative financial conditions. The "strong rate" characterization and explicit "above-trend" language place this solidly above 50.

**Minority views**: "A couple of participants commented that recent strong growth in GDP may also be due in part to increases in the growth rate of the economy's productive capacity" — suggesting the gap may be smaller than it appears. This modestly widens the band.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Contacts in many Districts reported tight labor markets, with difficulty finding qualified workers."
- "Firms were coping with labor shortages by increasing salaries, benefits, or workplace amenities in order to attract and retain workers."
- "Wage growth remained moderate by historical standards, which was due in part to tepid productivity growth."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor markets are clearly tight with worker shortages reported across many Districts, but the tightness has not yet produced strong wage acceleration. The level is tight but not at the extremes seen in historical episodes.

**Minority views**: Minimal dissent on the labor characterization; views are convergent.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants generally characterized consumption growth as strong, and they judged that robust increases in disposable income, high levels of consumer confidence, and solid household balance sheets had contributed to the strength in spending."
- "Business fixed investment had grown strongly so far this year."
- "A few participants noted weak residential construction activity at the national or District level."

**Center-of-gravity**: Both consumption and business investment are characterized as "strong," clearly above trend. The weakness in residential investment is a partial offset but receives less emphasis. Trade policy uncertainty is causing some contacts to forgo investment opportunities, but the overall tone is robustly positive.

**Minority views**: A couple note the higher saving rate may not be a precursor to higher future consumption. Residential weakness is a modest counterpoint.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "A number of participants noted that financial conditions remained accommodative."
- "The rise in interest rates and appreciation of the dollar over the intermeeting period had been offset by increases in equity prices, and broader measures continued to point to accommodative financial conditions."
- "Some participants commented about the continued growth in leveraged loans, the loosening of terms and standards on these loans."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly accommodative despite the ongoing rate hiking cycle. The loosening in leveraged loan markets reinforces the picture of easy conditions. Score well below 50 (accommodative = not tight).

**Minority views**: None significant — the characterization of conditions as accommodative is uncontested.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 71 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All participants expressed the view that it would be appropriate for the Committee to continue its gradual approach to policy firming by raising the target range for the federal funds rate 25 basis points at this meeting."
- "Almost all considered that it was also appropriate to... remove the language stating that 'the stance of monetary policy remains accommodative.'"
- "A few participants expected that policy would need to become modestly restrictive for a time and a number judged that it would be necessary to temporarily raise the federal funds rate above their assessments of its longer-run level."

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous support for the 25bp hike with consensus expectation of further gradual increases. A meaningful faction expects policy to move into restrictive territory. Only a couple resist the idea of restrictive policy. This is solidly hawkish, consistent with mid-hiking-cycle anchors.

**Minority views**: "A couple of participants indicated that they would not favor adopting a restrictive policy stance in the absence of clear signs of an overheating economy and rising inflation."

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 20 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All participants expressed the view that it would be appropriate... to [raise] the target range... 25 basis points at this meeting."
- "Almost all considered that it was also appropriate to revise the Committee's postmeeting statement in order to remove the language stating that 'the stance of monetary policy remains accommodative.'"
- Minor differences on forward path: "a few" want restrictive, "a couple" oppose restrictive stance.

**Center-of-gravity**: Near-consensus on the current action. Minor differences about the future path (how far to go) represent typical tactical variation, not fundamental disagreement. This is broad agreement with minor emphasis differences.

**Minority views**: The spread on future policy path creates slight upward pressure on the score, but the unanimity on the current action keeps it low.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 30 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Some participants commented about the continued growth in leveraged loans, the loosening of terms and standards on these loans, or the growth of this activity in the nonbank sector as reasons to remain mindful of vulnerabilities and possible risks to financial stability."
- "A few participants offered perspectives on the term structure of interest rates and what a potential inversion of the yield curve might signal about economic prospects."
- Risk of "significant financial imbalances" mentioned as one reason to continue hiking.

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability receives limited but meaningful attention. The leveraged loan discussion and yield curve inversion commentary constitute notable but not dominant discussion. It is a secondary topic, not a primary focus.

**Minority views**: Most participants did not address financial stability at length.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 72 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants generally anticipated that further gradual increases in the target range for the federal funds rate would most likely be consistent with a sustained economic expansion."
- "A few participants expected that policy would need to become modestly restrictive for a time."
- "A number judged that it would be necessary to temporarily raise the federal funds rate above their assessments of its longer-run level."
- Removing "accommodative" language was explicitly stated as not signaling a change in expected path.

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is clearly hawkish — continued gradual tightening with a meaningful contingent expecting to go above neutral. The removal of "accommodative" from the statement, while described as not changing the path, is itself a signal of progressing normalization.

**Minority views**: "A couple of participants indicated that they would not favor adopting a restrictive policy stance" — a mild dovish minority on the forward path.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants generally agreed that risks to the outlook appeared roughly balanced."
- "Some participants commented that trade policy developments remained a source of uncertainty for the outlook."
- "Some participants noted that financial stresses in a few EMEs could pose additional risks."
- "Considerable uncertainty surrounding all estimates of the neutral federal funds rate."

**Center-of-gravity**: Risks are described as roughly balanced with standard-level caveats. Trade policy and EME stress are noted uncertainties but do not elevate the overall uncertainty assessment beyond normal levels. The neutral rate uncertainty is acknowledged but treated as a practical matter rather than a source of alarm.

**Minority views**: None — the balanced risk assessment is broadly shared.
