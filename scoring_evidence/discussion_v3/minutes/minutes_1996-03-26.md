# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1996-03-26

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format -- staff and participant content mixed. I will score only portions with participant language cues ("members commented," "members observed," etc.).

**Era context**: March 1996 -- mid-1990s expansion. The economy had experienced a period of subpar growth in late 1995 (inventory adjustment, government shutdowns, severe weather, strikes) and is now rebounding. Unemployment is at 5.5%, which was near NAIRU estimates for the era. The Fed had been easing through 1995 and is now on hold. Inflation is moderate.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all the members" (2x -- both for hold decision), "many" (1x -- risks more balanced), "a number of members" (2x -- uncertainty, inflation vigilance), "several members" (1x -- broad money growth), "some members" (4x -- various observations), "a few" (2x -- housing slowing, inflation could decline). The distribution suggests broad consensus with nuanced disagreement on inflation risks.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional analysis:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Members' expectations "tended to center on expectations of little change in average consumer price inflation." Range: "essentially unchanged to slightly higher inflation." Some cite risks of wage pressures given low unemployment and labor shortages; others see "only a limited risk of higher inflation" with "sufficient capacity." The center of gravity is near-stable inflation with a mild upside tilt. Inflation is not seen as problematic now but there are watchful concerns. This is near the 41-49 range -- slightly below or at the implicit target, with some upside worry. Score ~47.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Members view "trend growth at a pace near the economy's potential as the most probable outcome." The economy is "operating at or close to its estimated capacity." Others note "sufficient capacity in the economy to allow room for moderate growth." This is clearly "near potential" language -- score 48-52 per bright-line rule. Score ~50.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Relatively low unemployment nationwide and widespread anecdotal reports of labor shortages." Unemployment at 5.5%. "Increases in labor costs had been unusually subdued in light of the relatively low unemployment." This describes a tight labor market (shortages, low unemployment) but with subdued wage pressures. Score in the 53-60 range -- tight but moderate. Score ~56.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Appreciable strengthening in retail sales," "sustained growth in consumer spending," consumer sentiment more favorable, "sharply increased household net worth." Business investment "remained a supportive element" but "growth in such spending was expected to slow considerably." Housing "well maintained" with "considerable underlying strength." Overall a modestly above-trend picture. Score ~55.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Some members commented that financial conditions had tightened somewhat as a consequence of the recent rise in intermediate- and long-term interest rates." But also: "relatively low long-term interest rates and the related substantial appreciation in the value of stock and bond market holdings had been important factors helping to sustain spending." "Ready availability and fairly low cost of business finance in equity and debt markets." Mixed -- conditions were loose but have tightened somewhat recently. Net: slightly below neutral toward the loose side. Score ~40.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All the members endorsed a proposal to maintain an unchanged degree of pressure." "The economy seemed to have adequate forward momentum and did not appear to require any further stimulus." "Information on the economy and prices did not seem to indicate developing inflation pressures that needed to be contained by tightening policy at this juncture." They favor an "unbiased instruction" -- no presumption either direction. This is a balanced hold. But note: "a number of members noted that inflation was not expected to moderate further...and that it could move higher and the Committee would need to be particularly vigilant." This adds a slight hawkish lean to a balanced posture. Score ~52.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Unanimous vote (0 dissents). "Members did not differ greatly in their assessments of the most probable course of inflation." But "members expressed somewhat differing views about possible deviations of inflation from their expectations" -- two camps on inflation risk direction. This is broad agreement with minor emphasis differences. Score ~20.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Brief mentions: stock prices "risen to comparatively high levels in relation to earnings," "might be vulnerable." Consumer indebtedness mentioned. These are passing references, not sustained discussion. Score ~15.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Unbiased instruction...did not prejudice possible intermeeting adjustments to policy in either direction." "Slightly greater or slightly lesser reserve restraint would be acceptable." Pure neutral forward signal. But vigilance on inflation noted. Score ~52.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Substantial uncertainties attended the economic outlook." "A number of members observed that an economic performance that differed considerably in either direction from their current forecasts might well materialize." "Considerable volatility could be expected in the short-run performance." This is moderately elevated uncertainty language. Score ~40.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (50) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (56): Slightly divergent but explainable -- labor market is somewhat tighter than the overall output gap because unemployment is low while capacity utilization has declined. The text explicitly notes this.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (52) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (52): Aligned -- both balanced with slight hawkish lean.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 47: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says near-stable with mild upside risk. 47 is slightly below midpoint -- matches "not quite at the concern threshold but close." Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS 40: Higher = tighter. Conditions are somewhat loose but tightening. 40 = somewhat loose to neutral. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 47 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Members' assessments tended to center on expectations of little change in average consumer price inflation over the projection horizon."
- "Their expectations ranged from essentially unchanged to slightly higher inflation in comparison with 1995."
- "Those who emphasized the risks of higher-than-projected inflation tended to cite the potential for increasing wage and price pressures in an economy that already was operating at or close to its estimated capacity."
- "Other members saw only a limited risk of higher inflation, and a few indicated that they did not rule out some reduction in consumer price inflation."

**Center-of-gravity**: Members see inflation as essentially stable and contained, but with an asymmetric upside risk that keeps the score near the midpoint rather than well below it.

**Minority views**: Those citing wage/labor shortage risks (upside) vs. those seeing "sufficient capacity" and limited risk (downside). The uncertainty band reflects this divergence.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 50 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Members continued to view trend growth at a pace near the economy's potential as the most probable outcome."
- "An economy that already was operating at or close to its estimated capacity."
- "Sufficient capacity in the economy to allow room for moderate growth."

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is viewed as operating right at potential, with the recovery from late-1995 weakness bringing it back to trend. Classic "near potential" language.

**Minority views**: Some lean toward above-capacity (labor shortages) while others see remaining capacity. Narrow band reflects the consensus on "near potential."

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 56 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Relatively low unemployment nationwide and widespread anecdotal reports of labor shortages."
- "Increases in labor costs had been unusually subdued in light of the relatively low unemployment."
- "Only scattered evidence of accelerating increases in worker compensation associated with labor shortages."

**Center-of-gravity**: The labor market is moderately tight -- unemployment is low and shortages are reported, but wage pressures have not materialized. This is above the midpoint but not deeply into tight territory.

**Minority views**: Some members are more concerned about latent wage pressures; others note compensation has been subdued. The debate is about future trajectory, not current level.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 55 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Appreciable strengthening in retail sales that tended to support forecasts of sustained growth in consumer spending."
- "The prospects for business capital spending remained a supportive element...but growth in such spending was expected to slow considerably from its rapid pace over the past few years."
- "Recent data were indicative of considerable underlying strength in housing markets."

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending strengthening, investment moderating from high levels, housing strong. Overall modestly above trend.

**Minority views**: Crosscurrents from rising consumer debt and interest rates noted as dampening factors.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 40 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Financial conditions had tightened somewhat as a consequence of the recent rise in intermediate- and long-term interest rates."
- "Relatively low long-term interest rates and the related substantial appreciation in the value of stock and bond market holdings had been important factors helping to sustain spending."
- "The ready availability and fairly low cost of business finance in equity and debt markets."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are somewhat accommodative overall -- credit is available, equity wealth has supported spending -- but conditions have tightened at the margin due to the recent long-rate backup. Net position is somewhat loose to neutral.

**Minority views**: The tension between the generally supportive financial environment and the recent rate increase creates the uncertainty band.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 52 +/-4 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All the members endorsed a proposal to maintain an unchanged degree of pressure in reserve markets."
- "The economy seemed to have adequate forward momentum and did not appear to require any further stimulus."
- "Information on the economy and prices did not seem to indicate developing inflation pressures that needed to be contained by tightening policy at this juncture."
- "A number of members noted that inflation was not expected to moderate further...and that it could move higher and the Committee would need to be particularly vigilant."

**Center-of-gravity**: Firmly on hold with a symmetric directive. The slight hawkish lean comes from vigilance language about inflation and the explicit statement that no further stimulus is needed. But no tightening is indicated either.

**Minority views**: Essentially none -- unanimous hold with balanced directive. Narrow band.
