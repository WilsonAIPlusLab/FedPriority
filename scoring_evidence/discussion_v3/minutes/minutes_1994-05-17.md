# FOMC Discussion Scoring: 1994-05-17 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format, but participant language cues are clear throughout ("members commented," "members favored," "all the members agreed"). The directive and vote sections at the end are formal language, not participant discussion per se.

**Era context**: May 1994 -- mid-cycle tightening. The Fed had begun raising rates from 3% in February 1994. This meeting produced a 50bp hike (reflected in the discount rate increase). The economy was expanding robustly after the early-1990s recession, with unemployment around 6.4%.

**Quantifier distribution**: "members" (general/all) appears frequently; "many members" used for expansion strength and consumer durables views; "some members" for caution on sales softening; "a number of members" for uncertainty about whether policy is sufficient; "one member" for asymmetric directive and export optimism. "All the members" for tightening action agreement.

**Dimension-by-dimension reasoning**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as currently subdued but with significant upward risk. "Broad measures of inflation remained subdued and increases in labor costs had been moderate." However, "inflation could begin to rise if growth in excess of potential were to persist." Prices of raw materials and intermediate goods had risen substantially. Some industries at capacity, some worker shortages. Price discounting lessening. The center of gravity is: inflation currently moderate but rising risks are prominent. This is slightly above neutral -- not elevated yet, but members are clearly worried about emerging pressures. Direction: slightly above midpoint, around 51-55. The emphasis on "subdued" current readings but "concerned" about future puts this at about 53.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: "Considerable momentum in the economic expansion." Growth "slightly above the economy's potential" is the most likely prospect. "Slack in the economy already had been reduced to a fairly low level." Capacity limits reached in a number of industries. This clearly signals above potential. Bright-line rule: "above potential" language --> must be >50. "Slightly above" and "fairly low" slack suggests modestly above, around 55-58.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 6.4% (mentioned in directive). "Indications of shortages of qualified workers in some labor markets or industries" but "only limited signs of upward wage pressures." This is approaching tight but not clearly tight yet. Shortages emerging in pockets. Near NAIRU or slightly above -- around 52-55.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "well maintained" especially durables, though some moderation expected. Business fixed investment expanding "at a pace substantially above that of the economy." Housing "robust." Order books grown considerably. Inventory building possible. This is clearly above trend -- strong investment, solid consumption, robust housing. Around 62-66.

5. **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Large additional increases" in market interest rates. "Rise in interest rates over the course of recent months was cited by business contacts as a potential source of restraint." However, "more aggressive lending practices at banks" offsetting. "Financial health of the banking system was greatly improved." Speculative sentiment reduced. Bond and stock price declines mentioned. Conditions have tightened from very accommodative but are being partially offset by bank lending. Somewhat tight, around 53-57.

6. **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All the members agreed that a further tightening action was needed." They favored removing "much of the remaining accommodation." The 50bp hike was unanimous. "Members were concerned that continued policy accommodation could be expected to lead before long to... a fresh outbreak of inflation." Several cautioned further tightening might still be needed. This is clearly hawkish -- unanimous support for a larger-than-previous hike. Around 70-73.

7. **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Unanimous vote (0 dissents). All agreed on the action. Only disagreement: one member wanted asymmetric (tightening bias) directive vs. all others wanting symmetric. Very low dissent. Around 12-18.

8. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Some discussion of financial market volatility, stock/bond price declines, speculative sentiment reduction. "Speculative sentiment and holdings seemed to have been reduced to a marked extent." Market volatility mentioned as reason earlier actions were small. This is more than passing but not a central focus. Around 25-30.

9. **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Symmetric directive adopted -- "no presumption about the likely direction." But "the Committee would have to remain alert to economic and financial developments that might signal the need for further tightening." One member wanted asymmetric toward tightening. The forward signal is mildly hawkish -- they just did 50bp, adopted symmetric, but explicitly flagged possible further tightening. Around 62-66.

10. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The extent of such a slowing was an important source of uncertainty in the outlook." "A number of members cautioned that the Committee could not be sure that today's policy actions... had produced a policy stance that would foster economic growth at a sustainable, non-inflationary pace." Moderate uncertainty about whether they've done enough and how much the economy will slow. Around 40-45.

**Cross-dimension coherence check**:
- OUTPUT_GAP (57) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (53): Both above 50, consistent with economy running slightly above potential with emerging tightness. Labor slightly lower because wage pressures are still limited despite some shortages -- reasonable.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (72) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (64): Hawkish current action (unanimous 50bp) with somewhat less hawkish forward signal (symmetric directive but alert to further tightening). This divergence is explained in the text -- they want to see if the accumulated moves are sufficient.

**Polarity verification**:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says "subdued" currently but "concerned" about emerging pressures. Score 53 = slightly above neutral. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says "slightly above potential," "fairly low slack." Score 57 = modestly above. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter conditions. Rates up sharply, but bank lending offsetting. Score 55 = somewhat tight. Matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 53 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The near-term prospects for inflation were favorable. Wage and price trends generally remained moderate"
- "inflation could begin to rise unless monetary policy were adjusted further from its accommodative stance"
- "prices of some raw materials and intermediate goods had risen substantially over the past year"
- "an increasing number of contacts were reporting that business firms were taking advantage of opportunities to adjust prices upward"

**Center-of-gravity**: Members see current inflation as subdued but perceive clearly emerging upward pressures from capacity constraints, raw material costs, and diminishing discounting. The score sits just above neutral reflecting the tension between benign current readings and growing forward concern.

**Minority views**: None significantly diverge; the concern about emerging pressures is broadly shared.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 57 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "members commented on widespread indications... of considerable momentum in the economic expansion"
- "The members continued to see moderate growth at a rate in line with or slightly above the economy's potential as the most likely prospect"
- "slack in the economy already had been reduced to a fairly low level and could decline further"
- "reports of industries that were operating at or near capacity limits were becoming more numerous"

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is running modestly above potential with shrinking slack. Multiple industries at capacity. The "slightly above potential" language maps to 55-60 range.

**Minority views**: Some regional variation noted (Southern California, Hawaii depressed), but the national center of gravity is clearly above potential.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 53 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "indications of shortages of qualified workers in some labor markets or industries"
- "to date there were only limited signs of upward wage pressures and these did not seem to signal a near-term emergence of general upward cost pressures"
- Unemployment at 6.4% (directive section)
- "Price and wage pressures were most clearly evident and widespread in the construction industry, where resource constraints appeared to be most pronounced"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is tightening with pockets of shortage, but broad wage pressures have not materialized. Slightly above the balanced point, reflecting early-stage tightening.

**Minority views**: None notably diverge.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 64 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "consumer expenditures were viewed by many members as likely to be well maintained, particularly for motor vehicles and other consumer durables"
- "Members expected business fixed investment to continue to expand at a pace substantially above that of the economy as a whole"
- "order books at producers of capital equipment had grown considerably"
- "Residential construction was indicated to be robust across much of the country"

**Center-of-gravity**: Spending is clearly above trend across all major categories. Business investment "substantially above" overall growth; consumer durables strong; housing robust. Some moderation expected but current momentum is strong.

**Minority views**: "Some members noted... sales had been less ebullient since late winter" -- modestly tempers the score.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 55 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Market interest rates have posted large additional increases"
- "rise in interest rates over the course of recent months was cited by business contacts as a potential source of restraint on interest-sensitive expenditures, but thus far relatively few contacts had reported actual examples of adverse interest-rate effects on spending"
- "their effects probably would be offset in part by more aggressive lending practices at banks and other institutions"
- "speculative sentiment and holdings seemed to have been reduced to a marked extent"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions have tightened from accommodative levels via sharp rate increases and stock/bond declines, but bank lending aggressiveness is offsetting. Conditions are somewhat tight of neutral.

**Minority views**: Wider band reflects tension between rate tightening and bank lending offset.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 72 +/-4 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the members favored prompt further action to remove much of the remaining accommodation in the stance of monetary policy"
- "all the members agreed that a further tightening action was needed at this point; and... the adjustment should fully reflect the 1/2 percentage point increase in the discount rate"
- "members were concerned that continued policy accommodation could be expected to lead before long to growing pressures on production resources and to a fresh outbreak of inflation"

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous support for a substantial 50bp tightening move, explicitly motivated by inflation prevention. This is clearly in the "strong hawkish" range -- consensus for continued tightening with a larger step size than previous moves.

**Minority views**: No dovish dissent. One member wanted even more hawkish signal (asymmetric directive).

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 15 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Votes for this action: Messrs. Greenspan, McDonough, Broaddus, Forrestal, Jordan, Kelley, LaWare, Lindsey, Parry, and Ms. Phillips. Votes against this action: None."
- Only disagreement: "One member expressed a preference for an asymmetric directive" vs. "all but one of the members indicated a preference for retaining a symmetric directive"

**Center-of-gravity**: Near-unanimous on all fronts. Zero dissents. The only disagreement is one member wanting an asymmetric directive -- a minor tactical difference, not a substantive policy disagreement.

**Minority views**: The single asymmetric-directive preference is very mild dissent.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 28 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "concerns that more aggressive steps might generate substantial uncertainty and undue disruptions in financial markets"
- "financial markets remained volatile, speculative sentiment and holdings seemed to have been reduced to a marked extent"
- "declines in household wealth owing to the drop in stock and bond prices"
- "the recently increased cost of external capital associated with higher interest rates and lower equity prices"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are discussed as context for policy calibration (how fast to tighten without destabilizing markets) rather than as a primary concern. Limited but present discussion -- a few references scattered through the text.

**Minority views**: None significantly.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 64 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the Committee would have to remain alert to economic and financial developments that might signal the need for further tightening"
- "all but one of the members indicated a preference for retaining a symmetric directive" -- symmetric = no strong forward lean
- "additional tightening would not be triggered unless inflation pressures greater than those currently anticipated were to emerge"
- One member: "a symmetric directive might be mistakenly interpreted... as a signal that the Committee believed that policy neutrality definitely had been achieved"

**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal is mildly hawkish. They adopted symmetric (no explicit bias), but the text clearly flags alertness to further tightening. The forward lean is toward tightening but data-dependent.

**Minority views**: One member wanted an explicitly asymmetric (tightening bias) forward signal.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 42 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the extent of such a slowing was an important source of uncertainty in the outlook"
- "A number of members cautioned that the Committee could not be sure that today's policy actions, along with those implemented earlier this year, had produced a policy stance that would foster economic growth at a sustainable, non-inflationary pace"
- "an appreciable risk of faster growth for a period"

**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate uncertainty, centered on whether the economy will slow enough and whether policy has been tightened sufficiently. Not extreme uncertainty, but clearly "somewhat elevated" beyond normal caveats.

**Minority views**: "some cautioned" about fragility elements -- minority concern.
