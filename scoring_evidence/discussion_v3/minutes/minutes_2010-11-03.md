# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2010-11-03 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Segmented — the participant discussion section is clearly separated from staff content. The document begins mid-sentence from a staff section, then transitions to "Participants' Views on Current Conditions and the Economic Outlook" and "Committee Policy Action."

**Era context**: November 2010. The economy is in a slow recovery from the GFC. Unemployment remains very high. This is the meeting where QE2 was launched ($600 billion in additional Treasury purchases). The fed funds rate is at the zero lower bound.

**Quantifier distribution**: "generally agreed" (frequent), "most" (frequent — e.g., "most saw benefits as exceeding costs"), "many" (used for downside risk views, structural unemployment), "several" (risks from balance sheet expansion, protection against deflation), "some" (limited effect of QE, dollar concerns, downside inflation risk), "a few" (further disinflation, upside inflation), "one member" (dissent). The dominant tone is broad agreement on slow growth with some disagreement on QE efficacy and inflation risks.

**Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:**

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target. "Most participants indicated that underlying inflation was somewhat low relative to levels that they judged to be consistent with the Committee's statutory mandate." Inflation had "generally trended lower since the start of the recession." "Subdued" is used. "A few participants expected that continuing resource slack would lead to some further disinflation." Only "a few others" saw upside. Small odds of deflation noted. Direction: clearly below target but not deflationary panic. Score should be in 20-28 range — "subdued," "somewhat low," with deflation risk acknowledged but seen as low probability.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: "Progress toward the Committee's dual objectives...was described as disappointingly slow." "Continued slow growth and high levels of resource slack." "Many participants pointed to substantial slack in resource utilization." "Several noted that the recent rate of output growth, if continued, would more likely be associated with an increase than a decrease in the unemployment rate." Clear below-potential language. Score in 31-38 range — recovering but large slack persists. The anchor suggests 35-42 for 2010-14 recovery; this is early in recovery with "substantial slack."

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Progress in reducing unemployment was disappointing." Unemployment rate well above structural levels per "many participants." Long-term unemployment concerns about skills erosion. Firms refraining from adding workers. Very slack labor market. Score in 22-28 range — high unemployment, significant slack, well below full employment.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Indicators of spending by households and businesses remained mixed." "Consumer spending was expanding gradually." Firms "investing to reduce costs but were refraining from adding workers or expanding capacity." Balance sheet repair restraining consumer spending. Business contacts reporting cost-cutting not top-line growth. Housing "remained depressed." Score in 32-38 — below trend, sluggish, mixed.

5. **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Participants saw financial conditions as having become more supportive of growth." Credit spreads tightened, equity prices rose, longer-term rates declined. "Credit remained readily available...for larger corporations" with "some signs that credit conditions had begun to improve for smaller firms." However, CRE lending limited, mortgage put-back risk noted. On balance, conditions are loosening but not fully loose. Score around 38-42 — somewhat loose to neutral, improving but residual tightness for some sectors.

6. **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: This is a QE2 launch meeting. "Most members judged it appropriate to take action to promote a stronger pace of economic recovery." $600 billion in additional purchases decided. "Nearly all Committee members agreed." One dissent (hawkish). The rubric says QE discussion places this in the 15-25 range. The committee is actively expanding accommodation via large-scale asset purchases. Score around 18-22.

7. **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal dissent ("One member dissented"). But there is meaningful disagreement in the discussion: "some participants...anticipated that additional purchases would have only a limited effect"; "some noted concerns that additional expansion...could put unwanted downward pressure on the dollar"; "several saw a risk...could cause an undesirably large increase in inflation." Multiple participants expressed reservations even if they voted for it. Score around 45-52 — notable disagreement with camps forming, one dissent plus multiple reservations.

8. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial conditions discussed meaningfully — credit spreads, equity prices, bank lending, CRE losses, mortgage put-back risks. Several paragraphs address financial conditions. But it is not a dominant topic — it is contextual to the QE discussion. Score around 40-48.

9. **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The committee launched QE2 and maintained the "extended period" language for low rates. "The Committee will regularly review the pace of its securities purchases and the overall size of the asset-purchase program." The forward signal is dovish — continued accommodation with possibility of adjusting QE. Score around 10-15 — strong easing bias, signaling further/extended accommodation.

10. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants held a range of views about the risks to that outlook." "Most saw the risks to growth as broadly balanced, but many saw the risks as tilted to the downside." Continuing uncertainty about tax/regulatory environment noted. Disagreement about structural vs. cyclical unemployment. Disagreement about QE efficacy. Score around 45-52 — elevated uncertainty with multiple sources of disagreement.

- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says "substantial slack," below potential. Low score correct.

- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Extended period + QE = dovish. Low score correct.

**Cross-dimension coherence**:

- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (20) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (12): Both dovish, forward guidance slightly more dovish as QE commitment extends accommodation into the future. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 24 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Most participants indicated that underlying inflation was somewhat low relative to levels that they judged to be consistent with the Committee's statutory mandate."
- "Many participants pointed to substantial slack in resource utilization, along with well-anchored inflation expectations, as likely to contribute to subdued inflation for some time."
- "A few participants expected that continuing resource slack would lead to some further disinflation in coming years."

**Center-of-gravity**: The broad majority views inflation as subdued and below mandate-consistent levels, with slack expected to keep it low. Small probability of deflation acknowledged. This places inflation pressure clearly in the "below target; slack keeping prices in check" range.

**Minority views**: "A few others thought that the exceptionally accommodative stance of monetary policy...made it more likely that inflation would increase" — this widens the band slightly upward.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 34 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants generally agreed that the incoming data indicated that output and employment were continuing to increase, but only slowly."
- "Many participants pointed to substantial slack in resource utilization."
- "Continued slow growth and high levels of resource slack could leave the economic expansion vulnerable to negative shocks."

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is growing but far below potential with substantial slack. Recovery is underway but disappointingly slow. This places it squarely in the "below potential; recovering but gap persists" range.

**Minority views**: Relatively narrow range — agreement that slack is substantial. Some disagreement about how much is structural vs. cyclical, but all agree output is below potential.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 25 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants agreed that progress in reducing unemployment was disappointing."
- "Many participants saw evidence that the current unemployment rate was well above levels that could be explained by structural factors alone."
- "A number of participants noted that continued high unemployment, particularly with large numbers of workers suffering very long spells of unemployment, would lead to an erosion of workers' skills."

**Center-of-gravity**: Severe labor market slack. Unemployment far above full employment. Firms actively refraining from hiring. Even structural factors cannot explain the slack fully. Firmly in the "high unemployment; significant slack; far from full employment" range.

**Minority views**: Some participants attributed more to structural factors, but even they acknowledged high unemployment.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Indicators of spending by households and businesses remained mixed."
- "Consumer spending was expanding gradually" but "households were continuing their efforts to repair their balance sheets, a process that was restraining growth in consumer spending."
- "Contacts generally reported that they were investing to reduce costs but were refraining from adding workers or expanding capacity."
- "Housing sector, including residential construction and home sales, remained depressed."

**Center-of-gravity**: Spending is below trend — gradual but constrained consumer spending, cautious business investment focused on cost-cutting not expansion, depressed housing. "Mixed" and "gradually" indicate sluggish conditions.

**Minority views**: Foreign demand was strong ("firms continued to report strong foreign demand...particularly from Asia"), but this was a partial offset to broadly weak domestic demand.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 40 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants saw financial conditions as having become more supportive of growth over the course of the intermeeting period."
- "Credit remained readily available...for larger corporations, and there were some signs that credit conditions had begun to improve for smaller firms."
- "Banks continued to report elevated losses on commercial real estate loans."
- "The availability of credit for commercial real estate transactions remained limited."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are loosening — credit spreads narrowing, equity prices up, rates down. But significant residual tightness remains in CRE and for some smaller firms. Net assessment: somewhat loose to neutral, improving but not fully accommodative across all sectors.

**Minority views**: The improvement was largely attributed to QE anticipation rather than organic improvement, suggesting fragility.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 20 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Most members judged it appropriate to take action to promote a stronger pace of economic recovery" — launching $600 billion QE2.
- "Nearly all Committee members agreed to keep the federal funds rate at its effective lower bound."
- "Several participants argued that the stimulus provided by additional securities purchases would help protect against further disinflation and the small probability that the U.S. economy could fall into persistent deflation."
- "One member dissented from this action, judging that the risks of additional securities purchases outweighed the benefits."

**Center-of-gravity**: The committee is launching a major new easing program (QE2) on top of zero rates. This is a strong dovish action. The rubric anchors QE-era meetings at 15-25.

**Minority views**: One dissent and "some participants" who thought QE would have limited effect push the band slightly upward.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 48 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "One member dissented from this action."
- "Some participants...anticipated that additional purchases of longer-term securities would have only a limited effect."
- "Some participants noted concerns that additional expansion of the Federal Reserve's balance sheet could put unwanted downward pressure on the dollar."
- "Several participants saw a risk that a further increase in the size of the Federal Reserve's asset portfolio...could cause an undesirably large increase in inflation."
- "Participants...differed in their assessments of the likely benefits and costs associated with a program of purchasing additional longer-term securities."

**Center-of-gravity**: One formal dissent plus multiple distinct camps expressing reservations about QE — concerns about efficacy, dollar effects, and future inflation risks. Disagreement extends beyond tactics to fundamental assessment of the policy tool's merit. This constitutes notable disagreement.

**Minority views**: The diversity of concerns (limited effect, dollar, inflation risk) suggests multiple axes of disagreement.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 12 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- QE2 launched at $75 billion/month through Q2 2011 — a forward commitment to purchase.
- Maintained zero-rate policy with "extended period" language (implied by maintaining 0-0.25% range).
- "The Committee will regularly review the pace of its securities purchases and the overall size of the asset-purchase program" — data-dependent but within a firmly easing framework.

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is strongly dovish — committing to months of additional QE purchases while maintaining zero rates for an extended period. The review clause adds mild conditionality but the direction is unambiguously further easing.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 50 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants held a range of views about the risks to that outlook."
- "Most saw the risks to growth as broadly balanced, but many saw the risks as tilted to the downside."
- "A majority saw the risks to inflation as balanced; some, however, saw downside risks predominating while a couple saw inflation risks as tilted to the upside."
- "Continuing uncertainty about the future tax and regulatory environment."
- Debate about structural vs. cyclical unemployment adds to uncertainty.

**Center-of-gravity**: Elevated uncertainty — participants disagree on risk direction for both growth and inflation, debate the nature of unemployment, and face uncertainty about QE efficacy. Multiple sources of genuine uncertainty, not just rhetorical hedging.
