# FOMC Discussion Scoring — 2024-06-12 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented — the participant discussion section begins clearly at "Participants' Views on Current Conditions and the Economic Outlook" and is separate from the staff review.

**Era context**: June 2024. The Fed is holding the fed funds rate at 5.25-5.50%, the highest level in over two decades. Inflation has come down substantially from 2022 peaks but remains above the 2% target. The labor market is gradually cooling but still strong. No rate cuts have occurred yet; participants are debating when conditions will warrant a first cut.

**Quantifier distribution**: The text uses "participants" (collective, implying all or nearly all) frequently; "many" appears several times; "several" appears multiple times; "some" appears multiple times; "a few" appears several times; "a couple" appears twice. This is a well-populated minutes document with broad discussion.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants note inflation "remained elevated" but there was "modest further progress toward the 2 percent goal." They see diminishing price pressures — firms' pricing power has declined, wage growth is slowing, retailers cutting prices. But shelter inflation is "slow to come down" and core import prices showed "strong increases." The center of gravity: elevated but moderating. Direction: above the midpoint. "Somewhat elevated" with progress = 58-62 range. Score: 60.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic activity had continued to expand at a solid pace." GDP growth expected "below the strong pace recorded in 2023" but still positive. "Restrictive monetary policy stance" is "having a restraining effect on growth." Growth is "gradually cooling." The economy is expanding at a solid pace — not overheating, not contracting. "Solid pace" with expected slowing from strong 2023 suggests modestly above potential. Direction: above 50 but not far. Score: 53.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Key tension in the text. Many indicators point to "reduced degree of tightness" — declining job openings, lower quits rate, uptick in unemployment. But the labor market "remained strong." Vacancies-to-unemployment ratio "returned to pre-pandemic levels." "Continued labor market strength could be consistent with the Committee achieving both its employment and inflation goals." Participants generally see easing but still-tight conditions. The center of gravity is: tight but normalizing. Direction: above 50 (still tight, just less so). Score: 55.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Economic activity had continued to expand at a solid pace." Restrictive policy is "moderating spending and production." Lower/moderate-income households encountering "increasing strains" — rising credit card utilization and delinquency rates. But higher-income households bolstered by "increasing asset prices." Net: solid but moderating, with stress at the lower end. "Solid pace" = roughly at or slightly above trend. Score: 52.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Most participants remarked that they viewed the current policy stance as restrictive." "Higher rates for home mortgage loans and other longer-term borrowing" are "moderating spending and production." Some noted financial conditions "might be or could become insufficiently restrictive." Some noted uncertainty about "the degree of restrictiveness of current policy" — conditions "may be less restrictive than they might appear." The center of gravity: conditions are tight/restrictive but with some uncertainty about how tight. Direction: above 50. Score: 62.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All participants judged it appropriate to maintain the rate at 5.25-5.50%. "They did not expect that it would be appropriate to lower the target range" until additional confidence on inflation. "Some participants emphasized the need for patience" in maintaining restrictive stance. "Several participants observed that, were inflation to persist at an elevated level or to increase further, the target range... might need to be raised." But "a number of participants remarked that monetary policy should stand ready to respond to unexpected economic weakness." This is a hold-with-hawkish-bias meeting — holding rates high, needing more evidence before cutting, some willing to raise further. Direction: above 50 (hawkish lean). Score: 60.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Unanimous decision to hold rates. Views are broadly aligned — all agree to hold, all want more confidence on inflation. Some disagreement at the edges: "several" say rates might need to be raised if inflation persists vs. "a number" say policy should respond to weakness. "Some" uncertainty about degree of restrictiveness. But these are nuanced differences, not sharp camps. Direction: below midpoint, modest disagreement. Score: 28.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Limited but present. Mentions include: "fragility of some parts of the CRE sector," "vulnerable balance sheet positions of some banks," rising credit card and auto loan delinquencies, "financial conditions that might be or could become insufficiently restrictive." These are scattered mentions by "a few" participants, not a dominant topic. Direction: moderate-low. Score: 30.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "They did not expect that it would be appropriate to lower the target range... until additional information had emerged to give them greater confidence." "Several participants observed that... the target range... might need to be raised." "Some participants emphasized the need for patience." The forward signal is: hold for longer, data-dependent, with some upside risk to rates. This is a mild hawkish forward lean — not signaling cuts are imminent, but not signaling further hikes as the base case either. Score: 62.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants noted the uncertainty associated with the economic outlook and with how long it would be appropriate to maintain a restrictive policy stance." "Some participants noted that there was uncertainty about the degree of restrictiveness of current policy." Members "viewed the economic outlook as uncertain." But this is not extreme — it's standard elevated uncertainty language. Direction: somewhat elevated. Score: 45.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:

- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (60) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (62): Both lean hawkish. Consistent — holding rates high and signaling patience before any cuts. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 60 +/-7 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Participants judged that although inflation remained elevated, there had been modest further progress toward the 2 percent goal in recent months."
- "Participants suggested that a number of developments in the product and labor markets supported their judgment that price pressures were diminishing."
- "Some participants commented that sustained achievement of the 2 percent inflation objective would be aided by lower overall services price inflation, and some noted that shelter price inflation had so far been slow to come down."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants broadly see inflation as still elevated (above 2% target) but with recent modest progress and declining price pressures from reduced firm pricing power, slowing wages, and improving supply. The score of 60 reflects "modestly above target" with expected continued disinflation.

**Minority views**: "A few participants also highlighted the strong increases recorded this year in core import prices." Some flagged risks inflation could remain above 2% longer than expected. These widen the band modestly upward.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 53 +/-5 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Participants noted that recent indicators suggested that economic activity had continued to expand at a solid pace."
- "Participants expected that real GDP growth this year would be below the strong pace recorded in 2023, and they remarked that recent data on economic activity were largely consistent with the anticipated slowing."
- "The vast majority of participants assessed that growth in economic activity appeared to be gradually cooling."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is expanding at a solid pace but decelerating from 2023's strong growth. Restrictive policy is restraining demand. The "solid pace" language with "gradually cooling" places this modestly above potential — not overheating but not at or below potential either.

**Minority views**: Narrow range of views here; virtually all participants agreed on the basic picture.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 55 +/-6 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Participants observed that many labor market indicators pointed to a reduced degree of tightness in labor market conditions."
- "The ratio of job vacancies to unemployed workers... had returned to pre-pandemic levels."
- "Participants generally observed that continued labor market strength could be consistent with the Committee achieving both its employment and inflation goals, though they noted that some further gradual cooling in the labor market may be required."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The labor market is still tight but has meaningfully eased. Vacancies-to-unemployment has normalized to pre-pandemic levels. Wage growth is slowing. The score of 55 reflects "still tight but normalizing" — moderately above balanced conditions.

**Minority views**: "Contacts in several Districts continued to report tight labor market conditions in certain sectors, such as health care, construction, or specialty manufacturing." Some risk that "further cooling... could be associated with an increased pace of layoffs." These create a modest spread.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 52 +/-6 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity had continued to expand at a solid pace."
- "Participants generally viewed the Committee's restrictive monetary policy stance as having a restraining effect on growth in consumption and investment spending."
- "Lower- and moderate-income households were encountering increasing strains as they attempted to meet higher living costs... evident in rising credit card utilization and delinquency rates as well as motor vehicle loan delinquencies."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Spending continues at a solid pace overall, but policy tightening is moderating it, and lower-income households face increasing financial strain. Higher-income households are bolstered by asset prices. Net assessment: roughly at trend, with divergence across income groups.

**Minority views**: "A few participants remarked that spending by some higher-income households was likely being bolstered by increasing asset prices," while many noted lower-income strain. This income divergence widens uncertainty.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 62 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Most participants remarked that they viewed the current policy stance as restrictive."
- "Higher rates for home mortgage loans and other longer-term borrowing... were moderating spending and production."
- "Some remarked that the continued strength of the economy, as well as other factors, could mean that the longer-run equilibrium interest rate was higher than previously assessed, in which case both the stance of monetary policy and overall financial conditions may be less restrictive than they might appear."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions are seen as restrictive, with higher borrowing costs weighing on spending. However, some participants question how restrictive conditions truly are given the economy's continued strength. The score of 62 reflects "somewhat tight" with a range of views on the true degree.

**Minority views**: Some participants flagged that conditions might be "insufficiently restrictive" — suggesting they see conditions as less tight than others believe. This widens the uncertainty band.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 60 +/-7 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "All participants judged that... it was appropriate to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 5-1/4 to 5-1/2 percent."
- "They emphasized that they did not expect that it would be appropriate to lower the target range... until additional information had emerged to give them greater confidence that inflation was moving sustainably toward... 2 percent."
- "Several participants observed that, were inflation to persist at an elevated level or to increase further, the target range... might need to be raised."
- "A number of participants remarked that monetary policy should stand ready to respond to unexpected economic weakness."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Unanimous hold at restrictive levels, with emphasis on needing more evidence before cutting. Several participants open to raising rates further if needed. This is a lean-hawkish posture — the bar for cuts is high, the door to hikes is ajar.

**Minority views**: "A number" want readiness to respond to weakness (dovish contingency), while "several" want readiness to raise (hawkish contingency). The center holds steady with a hawkish tilt.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 28 +/-5 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "All participants judged that... it was appropriate to maintain the target range."
- Differences are about the outlook and contingencies rather than the current decision — "several" see possible need to raise rates; "a number" want readiness to respond to weakness.
- "Some participants noted that there was uncertainty about the degree of restrictiveness of current policy."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Unanimous agreement on the current decision with mild disagreement about the future direction and risks. This is broad agreement with minor emphasis differences, consistent with the 25-30 range.

**Minority views**: The dispersion is primarily about future scenarios (raise vs. cut) rather than the current action, keeping dissent low.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 30 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "A few participants pointed to downside risks to economic activity associated with the fragility of some parts of the CRE sector or the vulnerable balance sheet positions of some banks."
- "Rising credit card utilization and delinquency rates as well as motor vehicle loan delinquencies, were a significant concern."
- "Financial conditions that might be or could become insufficiently restrictive" listed as an upside inflation risk.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial stability receives limited but notable mentions — CRE fragility, bank balance sheets, household credit stress. These are raised by "a few" or "some" participants as risk factors, not as dominant topics. Score of 30 reflects limited discussion by a minority.

**Minority views**: N/A — this is more about breadth of discussion than direction.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 62 +/-7 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "They did not expect that it would be appropriate to lower the target range for the federal funds rate until additional information had emerged to give them greater confidence that inflation was moving sustainably toward the Committee's 2 percent objective."
- "Some participants emphasized the need for patience in allowing the Committee's restrictive policy stance to restrain aggregate demand."
- "Several participants observed that, were inflation to persist at an elevated level or to increase further, the target range for the federal funds rate might need to be raised."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal is "hold for longer" with a high bar for cuts and an open door to further hikes if needed. This is a mild hawkish forward lean — not signaling imminent tightening but clearly not signaling imminent easing either.

**Minority views**: "A number of participants remarked that monetary policy should stand ready to respond to unexpected economic weakness" provides a dovish contingency that slightly moderates the forward tone.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 45 +/-6 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Members viewed the economic outlook as uncertain and agreed that they remained highly attentive to inflation risks."
- "Participants noted the uncertainty associated with the economic outlook and with how long it would be appropriate to maintain a restrictive policy stance."
- "Some participants noted that there was uncertainty about the degree of restrictiveness of current policy."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Uncertainty is somewhat elevated — participants flag uncertainty about the economic outlook, the degree of policy restrictiveness, and the appropriate duration of the current stance. But this is not "uncharted territory" language; it is standard policy-juncture uncertainty. Score of 45 reflects "somewhat elevated" uncertainty.

**Minority views**: N/A — the range of uncertainty expressions is fairly narrow.
