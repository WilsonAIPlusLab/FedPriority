# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2000-06-28

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Unsegmented (pre-2009), so I must focus on participant-attributed language ("members cited," "members observed," "members agreed," "several members," etc.) and ignore staff-reported content.

**Era context**: Late 1990s/2000 boom period. The Fed had been raising rates aggressively (funds rate at 6.5%). Economy was running hot but showing initial signs of slowing. This is the classic late-cycle period where the question is whether the economy is cooling enough.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all the members" (unanimous hold, unanimous balance-of-risks toward inflation), "members generally," "many Committee members," "a number of members," "several," "some." The document shows broad agreement on hold with risks tilted to inflation, with nuance on degree of inflation concern.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as above the midpoint. "Steep increases in energy prices had boosted overall rates of inflation somewhat"; "core measures of consumer prices had been rising more rapidly this year"; "a number of members...were concerned that these prices might well continue to accelerate gradually." However, there is a split: some see core inflation as containable with indirect energy effects explaining the rise. Unit labor costs "quite subdued." The center of gravity is modestly above target with concern about upside risk. "Somewhat" and "modestly" language suggests 55-63 range. Given the unanimous balance-of-risks toward inflation and broad concern, I score ~60. Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence supports above-midpoint. Matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants describe the economy as operating above potential but slowing. "Producer resources...were already operating at very high levels"; growth had been "overly rapid" in H2 1999/early 2000; members project deceleration to near-potential pace. The economy is still above potential but moving toward it. "Above potential" language mandates > 50. The slowing suggests not as far above as before. Score ~58. Anchors: late 90s boom 57-63. This fits.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Unsustainably tight labor markets"; "labor markets remained unusually tight"; unemployment forecasts centered on ~4%. This is clearly tight. Anchor for 1999-2000 is 65-75. Score ~70. Higher = tighter. Evidence strongly supports above midpoint.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending "decelerated noticeably" from "unusually robust pace." Housing shows "clear evidence of weakening demand." But business fixed investment "remained quite robust," especially high-tech. Exports strengthening. Net: moderating from very strong levels but still solid. "Slower but still solid expansion" is the center of gravity. Score ~53. This reflects above-trend investment offset by moderating consumption and weakening housing.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Cumulative effects of the firming in financial conditions this year"; "less favorable financial conditions in debt and equity markets"; "rise in interest rates and leveling out in equity prices"; "higher mortgage interest rates." Conditions have clearly tightened. "Appreciable tightening over the past year." Score ~60. This reflects somewhat tight conditions resulting from rate hikes, but not crisis-level. Anchors: 2022 rate hikes 60-70; this is the earlier end of that range.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All members support holding steady. But "all the members agreed...risks remained weighted toward rising inflation." "Many Committee members noted that...additional firming of policy could well be needed at some point." But also "less tightening probably would be required than they had thought at the time of the May meeting." This is a hold with hawkish bias but less hawkish than before. Score ~60. They've paused but still lean toward further tightening. Anchor: lean hawkish 51-60 fits.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All the members supported" the hold. "All the members agreed" on balance of risks. There are nuanced differences on inflation outlook (some more concerned, others more optimistic), but no formal dissent and broad agreement on action. Score ~18. Near-consensus with minor emphasis differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Limited discussion of financial stability per se. Equity prices mentioned as context for wealth effects, mortgage rates for housing, but no extended discussion of financial stability risks, systemic concerns, or market dysfunction. Score ~20. Brief contextual mentions, not a focus.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Many Committee members noted that...additional firming of policy could well be needed at some point in the future." "Several emphasized that the press release should not convey the impression that the Committee now viewed further policy tightening moves as an unlikely prospect." But "less tightening probably would be required." This is a mild tightening bias. Score ~63. The forward signal points toward possible further tightening but with less urgency than before.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Eventual extent and duration of the slowing in overall economic growth were subject to substantial uncertainty." "The uncertainties surrounding the outlook for the economy, notably the extent and duration of the recent moderation in spending." "Indications of slowing economic expansion were still tentative." Multiple explicit uncertainty references. "Substantial uncertainty" language. Score ~48. Elevated but not extreme.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (58) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70): Both above 50, consistent with late-boom economy. Labor tightness is higher because markets remain very tight even as output growth is moderating.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (60) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (63): Both lean hawkish, consistent. Forward guidance slightly more hawkish as the signal is explicitly about possible further tightening.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 60 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Steep increases in energy prices had boosted overall rates of inflation somewhat, and in addition the higher energy prices likely had contributed indirectly to the rise in core measures of inflation."
- "A number of members...were concerned that these prices might well continue to accelerate gradually, even assuming that economic expansion would be sustained at a pace close to the economy's potential."
- "Other members were more optimistic that core inflation might be contained near current levels."

**Center of gravity**: Members broadly see inflation as modestly above comfort levels with meaningful upside risk, though some see current core increases as largely energy-related. Unanimously agree inflation is the principal risk.

**Minority views**: The more optimistic members who see core inflation as containable pull the range down somewhat.

---

### OUTPUT_GAP: 58 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Producer resources...were already operating at very high levels."
- "Growth in aggregate demand was moderating to a pace closer to that of potential supply were still partial and tentative."
- Real GDP forecasts centered on 4-4.5% for 2000, decelerating to 3.25-3.75% for 2001.

**Center of gravity**: Economy clearly above potential but decelerating toward it. The gap is narrowing but still positive.

**Minority views**: Some see deceleration as more tentative, implying the gap could persist or widen.

---

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 70 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Unsustainably tight labor markets."
- "Labor markets remained unusually tight."
- Unemployment forecasts centered on ~4% for Q4 2000 and 4-4.25% for Q4 2001.

**Center of gravity**: Broad agreement that labor markets are very tight, with unemployment well below historical NAIRU estimates. "Unsustainably tight" is strong language.

**Minority views**: Narrow band; no one argues labor markets are slack. Some uncertainty about what constitutes sustainable utilization given productivity growth.

---

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 53 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Growth in consumer spending had slowed appreciably in recent months from the unusually robust pace."
- "Growth in business fixed investment had moderated in recent months but was expected to remain quite robust."
- "The housing market also provided clear evidence of weakening demand."

**Center of gravity**: Net spending is moderating from very strong levels but remains above trend. Business investment is still robust; consumer spending is slowing but "solid"; housing is weakening.

**Minority views**: Some sectors (business investment) remain very strong while others (housing) are clearly weakening, creating a wide range.

---

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 60 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Cumulative effects of the firming in financial conditions this year had been accompanied by an increasing number of anecdotal reports of more cautious business sentiment."
- "Less favorable financial conditions in debt and equity markets."
- "Real interest rates were already relatively elevated."

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions have tightened materially due to rate hikes and equity market flattening. Somewhat tight but not in crisis territory.

**Minority views**: Limited disagreement on this dimension.

---

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 60 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All the members supported a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance."
- "Many Committee members noted that...additional firming of policy could well be needed at some point in the future."
- "A number also expressed the opinion that less tightening probably would be required than they had thought at the time of the May meeting."

**Center of gravity**: Unanimous hold with tightening bias. Less hawkish than at the May meeting but still leaning toward further tightening. Balance-of-risks unanimously toward inflation.

**Minority views**: Some members see less need for further tightening than others, but all agree on the directional lean.

---

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 18 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All the members supported a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance."
- "All the members agreed that the [balance of risks] should continue to express...risks remained weighted toward rising inflation."
- Nuanced differences on inflation outlook (some more concerned vs. others more optimistic about containment).

**Center of gravity**: Near-unanimous consensus on action and assessment. Minor differences in emphasis on inflation outlook.

**Minority views**: Differences in inflation views are within-consensus nuances, not fundamental disagreements.

---

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 20 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Equity prices mentioned as wealth effect driver: "leveling out in equity prices."
- "Less favorable financial conditions in debt and equity markets" mentioned as factor for business caution.
- No extended discussion of systemic risk, financial imbalances, or stability per se.

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions discussed instrumentally (as transmission mechanism for policy), not as a stability concern in its own right.

---

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 63 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Many Committee members noted that...additional firming of policy could well be needed at some point in the future."
- "Several emphasized that the press release should not convey the impression that the Committee now viewed further policy tightening moves as an unlikely prospect."
- "Less tightening probably would be required than they had thought at the time of the May meeting."

**Center of gravity**: Mild tightening bias in forward signal. Not signaling imminent action, but keeping the door open for further hikes. Less aggressive than prior meetings.

**Minority views**: Some see less need for tightening; several want to keep signal clearly hawkish-leaning.

---

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 48 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The eventual extent and duration of the slowing in overall economic growth were subject to substantial uncertainty."
- "Indications of slowing economic expansion were still tentative."
- "The uncertainties surrounding the outlook for the economy, notably the extent and duration of the recent moderation in spending."

**Center of gravity**: Elevated uncertainty, mainly around whether the slowdown is real and durable or temporary. "Substantial uncertainty" language places this above normal levels.

**Minority views**: Some members seem more confident the slowdown will persist; others note the economy "could rebound to a clearly unsustainable pace."
