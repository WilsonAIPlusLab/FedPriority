# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2002-03-19

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Unsegmented pre-2009 format — must score only participant-attributed content. The text uses member/participant language cues throughout ("members commented," "members viewed," "members expected," etc.), so nearly all content is participant-attributable.

**Era context**: March 2002 — early recovery from the 2001 recession. The fed funds rate was at 1.75% after aggressive easing in 2001. The economy was beginning to turn, with inventory restocking driving near-term growth, but the durability of the recovery was uncertain. This is a post-recession, early-recovery environment.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all the members" (supporting hold, supporting balanced risks statement), "most" (anticipated subdued expansion), "nearly all" (balanced risks statement), "several" (fiscal stimulus), "some" (various specific points). The dominant tone is consensus with mild uncertainty about recovery strength.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional assessment:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW the midpoint. "Outlook for core price inflation as still quite benign," "ample availability of labor and other producer resources," "quiescent inflation pressures," "price pressures to remain relatively contained." Some mention potential negatives (wage pressures, steel/energy/insurance costs) but these are future risks, not current pressure. Direction: clearly below midpoint. Magnitude: "quite benign" and "quiescent" = substantially below. Score ~25-30.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as BELOW potential. "Only gradually erode current margins of underutilized productive resources," "relatively high unemployment rate, low capacity utilization rates in numerous industries," "large margins of excess capacity in many industries." The economy is recovering but slack is substantial. Direction: clearly below 50. The recovery is underway but with "substantial" underutilization. Score ~32-36.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Below full employment. "Relatively high unemployment rate," "ample availability of labor," "excess capacity in labor...markets," employers "remained cautious in their hiring policies." Some stabilization in manufacturing payrolls anticipated. Direction: clearly below midpoint. Score ~30-34.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending showed "surprising strength," "consumer confidence had improved considerably," housing "renewed vigor." But business investment in steep decline with only "scattered indications of an upturn" and "no evidence at this point of any broad-based improvement," "large margins of excess capacity." The near-term is boosted by inventory swing but final demand is uncertain. Net: somewhat below trend given investment weakness offsetting consumer strength. Score ~38-42.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Limited direct discussion of financial conditions. Low mortgage rates noted as supportive. "Accommodative stance of monetary policy." No discussion of credit stress, spreads, or tight conditions. The overall tone suggests accommodative/loose conditions. Score ~30-35.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All the members supported...maintain an unchanged policy stance" at 1.75%. "Currently accommodative stance...appropriate for now." Forward discussion notes need to tighten "at some point" but explicitly cautions against premature action — "raising rates prematurely or too precipitately could weaken or abort the recovery." Shift to balanced risks statement from weakness bias. This is a hold with a very mild lean toward eventual tightening but firmly patient. Score ~43-47 (lean dovish to balanced).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All the members" supported the proposal. "Nearly all" agreed on balanced risks. Very broad consensus. Score ~12-16.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Minimal discussion. Brief mention of "negative stock market wealth effect" and mortgage rates. No substantive discussion of financial stability risks, systemic concerns, or market stress. Score ~10-15.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is mildly hawkish — they explicitly discuss needing to tighten "at some point" and shifting the balance of risks to neutral. The press announcement would "alert the public to the need to firm policy at some point in the future." But this is very tentative, data-dependent, with explicit caution about acting too soon. Score ~50-55 (neutral to very mild tightening bias).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Substantial uncertainty" about recovery strength. "Considerable uncertainty surrounded the outlook for final demand." "Wide range of possible outcomes." "Significant structural changes...adding to the usual difficulty of projecting." This is elevated uncertainty. Score ~50-58.

### Cross-dimension coherence check:
- OUTPUT_GAP (~34) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (~32): Both below 50, consistent — economy in early recovery with significant slack. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (~45) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (~52): Slight divergence is explained — current stance is accommodative (dovish lean) but forward signal is shifting toward neutral/mild tightening bias. This is the classic "on hold now, will tighten later" posture. Makes sense.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says "benign/quiescent." Low score = less pressure. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says "below potential/excess capacity." Low score. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says accommodative/low rates. Low score = looser. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 27 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The members viewed the outlook for core price inflation as still quite benign, largely reflecting the ample availability of labor and other producer resources"
- "members expected price pressures to remain relatively contained over the next several quarters"
- "quiescent inflation pressures" cited as supporting the hold decision
- Minor upside risks: "rising wage pressures as labor markets became more fully employed and upward price pressures stemming from increasing steel, energy, and insurance costs"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants unanimously view inflation as benign and well contained due to ample slack, with only potential future risks mentioned as caveats.
**Minority views**: Some members mention potential future wage and commodity-price pressures, widening the band slightly.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 34 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "most continued to anticipate a relatively subdued rate of expansion that would only gradually erode current margins of underutilized productive resources"
- "relatively high unemployment rate, low capacity utilization rates in numerous industries"
- "large margins of excess capacity in many industries"
- Economy recovering: "an economic recovery was now under way" but from deep slack

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy clearly below potential with substantial excess capacity, but recovery is underway and closing the gap gradually.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 31 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "ample availability of labor" and "excess capacity in labor...markets"
- "relatively high unemployment rate" and "somewhat elevated rate of unemployment"
- "employers in manufacturing and other sectors...generally remained cautious in their hiring policies"
- Some positive: "improving employment picture," some firms "anticipating increases in their payrolls"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market has significant slack with high unemployment, though early signs of stabilization in some sectors.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 40 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Consumer spending: "consumer expenditures had displayed surprising strength," "consumer confidence had improved considerably"
- Business investment: "no evidence at this point of any broad-based improvement," "business confidence remained at a low level"
- Housing: "renewed vigor in recent months" — a bright spot
- Inventory: "swing toward smaller drawdowns was giving a boost to industrial production" — temporary

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending and housing are positive but business investment remains deeply weak. Net position is below trend, with the near-term boost from inventories recognized as temporary.
**Minority views**: The range of outcomes for capital spending is wide, creating uncertainty.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 32 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "currently accommodative stance of monetary policy"
- "relatively low mortgage interest rates were expected to sustain a high level of housing expenditures"
- No discussion of tight credit conditions, widening spreads, or financial stress
- "negative stock market wealth effect stemming from large earlier declines" — equity losses mentioned but as wealth effect, not financial stress

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are accommodative/supportive, with low interest rates and no evidence of credit tightness. Limited direct discussion keeps confidence moderate.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 45 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all the members supported a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance" at 1.75%
- "currently accommodative stance of monetary policy to be appropriate for now"
- "raising rates prematurely or too precipitately could weaken or abort the recovery"
- "all the members indicated that they could accept a proposal to move the balance of risks statement from potential weakness to a neutral position"

**Center-of-gravity**: Firmly on hold with accommodative stance deemed appropriate. The shift to balanced risks from downside bias represents a small step toward neutral but the overall lean remains mildly dovish — patient hold, not yet considering action.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 12 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all the members supported a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance"
- "nearly all the members agreed that a balanced risks statement now best represented their consensus"
- No expressed reservations or minority positions on the rate decision

**Center-of-gravity**: Near-unanimous consensus on both the rate decision and the risk assessment shift. The "nearly all" rather than "all" on the risk statement introduces minimal divergence.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 12 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Brief mention of "negative stock market wealth effect" as a consumer spending risk
- Low mortgage rates noted
- No discussion of financial stability concerns, systemic risk, asset bubbles, or market functioning

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability is essentially absent from the discussion — mentioned only tangentially through wealth effects on spending.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 52 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "stance of policy would need to be adjusted at some point to provide less stimulus"
- "a reference to the Committee's currently accommodative policy stance...would alert the public to the need to firm policy at some point in the future"
- BUT: "waiting too long could risk a pickup in inflationary pressures" balanced against "raising rates prematurely or too precipitately could weaken or abort the recovery"
- "Committee would be in a better position to assess the appropriate timing of a policy change at the May meeting"

**Center-of-gravity**: Neutral to very mildly hawkish forward signal. The Committee acknowledges tightening will be needed eventually but is firmly data-dependent and in no rush. The shift from downside risk bias to neutral is itself a mild hawkish forward signal.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "prospective strength remained subject to substantial uncertainty"
- "considerable uncertainty surrounded the outlook for final demand over the quarters ahead"
- "economy was undergoing significant structural changes and those changes were adding to the usual difficulty of projecting the trajectory"
- "a wide range of possible outcomes could not be ruled out for this key sector"

**Center-of-gravity**: Elevated uncertainty — multiple explicit references to "substantial" and "considerable" uncertainty, structural unknowns, and wide outcome ranges. This goes beyond standard hedging.
