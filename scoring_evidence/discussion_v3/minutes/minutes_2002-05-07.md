# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2002-05-07

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format with mixed staff/participant content. I will focus on participant-attributed language ("members commented," "members noted," "members generally viewed," "all the members").

**Era context**: May 2002 -- early recovery from the 2001 recession. The fed funds rate is at 1.75%. The economy is recovering but growth has slowed from earlier in the year when inventory restocking provided a temporary boost. Business investment remains weak. Inflation is low and decelerating.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all the members" (2x -- on maintaining rates and neutral balance of risks), "members generally viewed" (2x), "nearly all measures" (on price deceleration), "many business contacts," "some" (on upside/downside risks), "a few" (not used). The language is dominated by consensus-level qualifiers, suggesting broad agreement.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional assessment:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW the midpoint. "Nearly all measures of total and core prices had decelerated over the past year." "Continued sizable gap between actual and potential output" limits inflation risk. "Inflation might edge a bit lower." Some upward cost pressures noted (energy, insurance, medical) but "widespread absence of pricing power." This is clearly low inflation -- subdued and decelerating. Score in the 26-35 range.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as BELOW potential. "Continued sizable gap between actual and potential output." "Margins of underutilized resources." "Large margins of excess capacity in numerous industries." "Excess capacity" is mentioned multiple times. Clear below-potential language. Must be < 50 per bright-line rule. The recovery is early and tentative. Score in the 31-38 range.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see labor markets as SLACK. "Excess capacity in many labor and product markets." "Employment growth had been very sluggish." "Employers remaining quite cautious in their hiring practices." "Continued softness in labor markets." This is clearly below full employment. Score in the 28-35 range.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending "well maintained" and "elevated" but growth potential limited. Business fixed investment "contracted further in the first quarter" though decline smallest in a year. Outlook is for "sluggish and delayed upturn." Housing elevated but growth expected to be "subdued." Inventory contribution fading. Net picture is below trend -- consumer holding up but investment dragging. Score in the 35-42 range.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Limited direct discussion of financial conditions. Mortgage rates described as "low" (supporting housing). "Monetary and fiscal policy ease" noted. The accommodative stance suggests loose conditions. But there is also mention of "gloomy business sentiment" and "disappointing sales and profits" affecting investment -- more economic than financial conditions. Dollar "edged lower." Score somewhat below neutral -- conditions are accommodative. Score around 30-35.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All the members agreed on the desirability of maintaining an unchanged policy stance." They explicitly say "a firming of policy at this time would be premature." They acknowledge the current stance "probably was inconsistent with the Committee's inflation objective over time" but inflation pressures are "subdued." All favor neutral balance of risks. This is hold-steady with mild acknowledgment of future tightening need but no urgency. Score 43-48 range (lean dovish to balanced, hold with no easing bias but no tightening bias either).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All the members agreed" on maintaining rates. "All the members favored the retention of a neutral balance of risks statement." Near-unanimous on both action and assessment. Very low disagreement. Score 8-15.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Very little attention to financial stability per se. No discussion of systemic risk, credit market stress, or financial imbalances. Brief mentions of equity prices are absent; the only financial condition mentions are low mortgage rates and dollar movements. Score 10-15.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Neutral balance of risks statement adopted. "Would not preclude a preemptive tightening adjustment... should new evidence warrant." The forward signal is essentially neutral -- data dependent, with an acknowledgment that current policy stance will eventually need to tighten but not now. Score around 48-52 (neutral).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The economy's future course was subject to a marked degree of uncertainty." "How much final demand would strengthen going forward was still uncertain." Outlook for consumer spending "subject to uncertainties in both directions." Multiple explicit references to uncertainty. "Marked degree" is fairly strong language. Score 50-58 range.

### Polarity verification:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says low/subdued. Low score. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says well below potential. Low score. Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says slack. Low score. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says hold steady, premature to tighten. Slightly below 50. Correct.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (~35) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (~32): Both below 50, consistent with early-recovery slack. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (~45) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (~50): Both near neutral. The lean is slightly dovish (hold because premature to tighten) while forward signal is neutral (balanced risks, could tighten later). Slight divergence is explained by the temporal distinction -- dovish now, neutral forward. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Nearly all measures of total and core prices had decelerated over the past year"
- "A continued sizable gap between actual and potential output, the risk that inflationary pressures would intensify significantly over coming quarters appeared to be quite limited; indeed, inflation might edge a bit lower"
- "The widespread absence of pricing power in highly competitive markets"
- Some upward pressures noted: "significant increases in energy costs," "sharp increases in many insurance costs," "continuing upward pressures on medical costs"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants see inflation as clearly subdued and decelerating, with structural factors (productivity, slack, no pricing power) keeping it contained, though they note scattered cost pressures.

**Minority views**: No dissenting view on inflation direction; the cost pressures are noted as context rather than a contrarian camp.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 33 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "A continued sizable gap between actual and potential output"
- "Large margins of excess capacity in numerous industries"
- "Margins of underutilized resources"
- Recovery "continuing" but "rate of advance had moderated considerably"

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is clearly operating well below potential with substantial slack, though the recovery is underway and the gap is no longer widening.

**Minority views**: None identified; broad agreement on substantial slack.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Excess capacity in many labor and product markets"
- "Employment growth had been very sluggish to date, with employers remaining quite cautious in their hiring practices"
- "Continued softness in labor markets could damp consumer confidence"
- "Deceleration in labor costs over the past several quarters"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor markets have significant slack with sluggish hiring and decelerating wage costs, consistent with early recovery well below full employment.

**Minority views**: None; consensus on labor slack.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 38 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Consumer spending: "household spending had continued to be well maintained" but "more limited than usual scope for further growth"
- Business investment: "contracted further in the first quarter" with "sluggish and delayed upturn" expected; "persistently gloomy business sentiment and large margins of excess capacity"
- Housing: "at an elevated level" but growth "subdued"
- Inventory boost fading: "much less impetus to overall economic activity from inventories over coming quarters"

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending holds up at decent levels while business investment is still contracting, with the inventory boost fading. Net spending/investment is below trend.

**Minority views**: Some members see upside potential from fiscal/monetary stimulus and productivity gains; others worry about sluggish employment dampening consumer confidence. Reflected in the +/-7 band.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 32 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The currently stimulative stance of both fiscal and monetary policy would tend to undergird final demand"
- "Low mortgage rates" supporting housing
- "The current stance of policy probably was inconsistent with the Committee's inflation objective over time" (implying accommodative conditions)
- No discussion of credit stress, spreads, or tight lending conditions

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are broadly accommodative, supported by very low rates and easy monetary policy. No stress signals discussed.

**Minority views**: None on financial conditions specifically. Wider band reflects limited direct discussion.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 45 +/-4 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All the members agreed on the desirability of maintaining an unchanged policy stance"
- "A firming of policy at this time would be premature and would incur an undue risk to a healthy expansion"
- "The current stance of policy probably was inconsistent with the Committee's inflation objective over time" -- acknowledging future tightening need
- "All the members favored the retention of a neutral balance of risks statement"

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous hold with recognition that eventual tightening will be needed, but premature now. Lean dovish to balanced -- hold steady with no active easing bias but clearly not ready to tighten.

**Minority views**: Virtually none; all members agree on hold and neutral risks.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 8 +/-4 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All the members agreed on the desirability of maintaining an unchanged policy stance"
- "All the members favored the retention of a neutral balance of risks statement"
- No dissents, no reservations, no "reluctant" language

**Center-of-gravity**: Near-complete consensus on both the policy action and the risk assessment.

**Minority views**: None evident.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 12 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Brief mention of low mortgage rates supporting housing
- Dollar "edged lower" noted in passing
- No discussion of systemic risk, credit market conditions, equity valuations, or banking stress
- "Some expressed concern about the longer-term implications of what they saw as a decline in fiscal discipline" -- fiscal, not financial stability per se

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability is barely on the radar. Discussion is overwhelmingly focused on real economic conditions and the pace of recovery.

**Minority views**: None.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 50 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All the members favored the retention of a neutral balance of risks statement"
- "A neutral statement regarding the risks to the economy in the foreseeable future would not preclude a preemptive tightening adjustment"
- "Providing adequate opportunity to evaluate ongoing developments and tighten policy as needed later"

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is explicitly neutral -- balanced risks statement, data-dependent, with acknowledgment that tightening may come but with no commitment to either direction.

**Minority views**: None; unanimous on neutral forward signal.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 53 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The economy's future course was subject to a marked degree of uncertainty"
- "How much final demand would strengthen going forward was still uncertain"
- Outlook for consumer spending "subject to uncertainties in both directions"
- Business investment timing uncertain: "sluggish and delayed upturn"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants express somewhat elevated uncertainty about the recovery's trajectory. The phrase "marked degree of uncertainty" is notable. Uncertainty is genuine -- they are unsure how strongly final demand will rebound.

**Minority views**: Not about different levels of uncertainty but about different risk directions (upside from policy ease vs. downside from weak hiring).
