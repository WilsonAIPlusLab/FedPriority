# FOMC Scoring: 1998-08-18 Minutes

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented (pre-2009), so I must focus only on participant-attributable language ("members," "the Committee's discussion," quantifier cues) and ignore staff-attributed content.

**Era context**: August 1998. The late-1990s expansion with the Asian financial crisis deepening. Strong domestic economy, tight labor markets, but external drag growing. The Russian crisis and LTCM collapse are weeks away. Fed funds rate at 5.5%.

**Quantifier distribution**: Key quantifiers found:
- "members" (general/most): focused on disparate forces, generally anticipated more moderate growth, remained concerned about inflation potential, generally agreed rising inflation remained a threat
- "all but one": maintaining steady policy
- "a majority": risks now more balanced, shift to symmetry
- "many": continued to emphasize Committee could not ignore inflation risks
- "several": saw little evidence Asia stabilizing; emerging signs of cautious business attitudes
- "a few": concern about rapid monetary aggregate growth
- "one": called for prompt firming (Jordan dissent)
- "other members": risks still tilted toward inflation but accepted symmetry
- "nearly all": cautious wait-and-see appropriate

**Dimension-by-dimension reasoning**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Members see inflation as currently subdued but with forward risks. "Consumer prices and inflation more generally had remained remarkably subdued in the context of very tight labor markets." Special factors (dollar appreciation, commodity declines, productivity gains, diminished expectations) holding inflation down. But "rising price inflation remained an important threat" looking ahead. The center of gravity is: inflation currently low, but upside risks from labor market tightness. This is a mixed picture -- currently below concern but with acknowledged future risk. Score should be slightly below neutral: inflation is "subdued" now (pulling toward 35-40) but the forward risk concern pulls it back up somewhat. Net: around 42. Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says inflation currently subdued but risks acknowledged. ~42 fits.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: "The economy could well be producing beyond its sustainable potential." "High level of output and strong domestic demand." Domestic final demand expanding at "robust pace." But moderation anticipated due to Asian drag. The bright-line rule: "producing beyond its sustainable potential" -> must be >50. The language suggests modestly above potential with some anticipated slowing. Score ~55. Consistent with late-90s anchor (57-63).

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Widespread tightness in labor markets," "very tight labor markets" used multiple times. Building projects delayed due to "shortages of labor." "At current levels these markets were tight." Clearly very tight. Score ~68. Consistent with 1999-2000 anchor (65-75).

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Considerable strength in private domestic spending." Consumer spending buttressed by "unusually favorable underlying factors." Business fixed investment on "solid upward trajectory." Housing "remained at a high level" with "few indications of any moderation." High-tech spending "surged to an undoubtedly unsustainable pace." Some anticipated moderation ahead but current strength is clear. Score ~63. Above trend, robust.

5. **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Domestic financial conditions remained generally accommodative." But "equity prices and risk spreads in U.S. financial markets were beginning to be adversely affected." "Recent developments in foreign exchange and domestic financial markets had tended on balance to decrease some of the stimulative effects." "Ample availability of financing on favorable terms." Net: conditions still accommodative but tightening at the margin. Score ~38 (somewhat loose but moving toward neutral).

6. **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All but one" favored maintaining steady policy. A majority favored shifting from asymmetric (tightening bias) to symmetric directive. "Other members" still saw risks tilted toward inflation but could accept symmetry. One dissent (Jordan) for tightening. The center of gravity is: hold steady, with the bias shifting from tightening-lean to neutral. This is around 50-52 -- balanced with a very slight residual hawkish lean from those who still worried about inflation. Score ~51.

7. **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal dissent (Jordan). A majority favored symmetry but "other members" preferred asymmetry but "could accept symmetry." This is a modest split -- one dissent with some minority preference differences but ultimate accommodation. Score ~30. Broad agreement with one dissent and some differing risk views.

8. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Significant discussion of Asian financial turmoil and its spillover to U.S. markets. Equity market declines, risk spreads widening, foreign financial fragility. Jordan's dissent mentions "speculative lending." Multiple paragraphs devoted to financial conditions/stability. Score ~45. Moderate discussion -- several participants address it meaningfully.

9. **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Shift from asymmetric (tightening bias) to symmetric directive. "Next policy move could be in either direction." "Cautious wait-and-see approach." "Greater difficulties abroad...had substantially diminished the chances of...a near-term tightening." The forward signal is moving from mildly hawkish to neutral. Score ~50.

10. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "External sector as a major source of uncertainty." "Could not be anticipated with any degree of confidence." "Uncertainties bearing on the economic outlook remained substantial." "Risks on the downside seemed to have increased appreciably further." Multiple members emphasize uncertainty. Score ~55. Elevated uncertainty.

**Cross-dimension coherence**:
- OUTPUT_GAP (55) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (68): Both above 50, consistent. Labor tightness is notably higher because the text emphasizes it more strongly with "very tight" and "widespread tightness" while output gap discussions acknowledge Asian drag moderating the overheating signal.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (51) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (50): Aligned -- both near neutral reflecting the shift to symmetric directive.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 42 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Consumer prices and inflation more generally had remained remarkably subdued in the context of very tight labor markets and upward pressure on labor compensation."
- "Rising price inflation remained an important threat. Significant additional tightening in labor markets would, of course, exacerbate that risk."
- "Members remained persuaded that a significant rise in price inflation was not likely to occur in the nearer term."

**Center of gravity**: Inflation currently subdued thanks to special factors (dollar, commodities, productivity), but members widely acknowledge forward risks from tight labor markets. Net perception is slightly below the midpoint -- low now with upside risk.
**Minority views**: "Many" continued to emphasize inflation risks could not be ignored; one member (Jordan) saw inflation acceleration risk from monetary growth.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 55 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The economy could well be producing beyond its sustainable potential."
- "Considerable strength in private domestic spending" offset by "damping influences stemming from foreign economic developments."
- "Members generally anticipated somewhat more moderate growth...at a pace near or somewhat below the growth of the economy's potential."

**Center of gravity**: Economy modestly above potential but expected to slow toward potential due to Asian drag. The bright-line rule applies: "producing beyond" = above 50.
**Minority views**: Some anticipate growth slowing to or below potential, but current position remains above.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 68 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Widespread tightness in labor markets and an upward tilt in the rise of labor compensation."
- "At current levels these markets were tight and at some point labor costs could increase more rapidly, pressing on prices."
- "Building projects were being delayed because of shortages of labor."

**Center of gravity**: Very tight labor markets with shortages reported and compensation pressures building. Broad agreement among members.
**Minority views**: None noted -- consensus on tightness.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 63 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Persistence of considerable strength in private domestic spending."
- "Business spending for various types of high-tech equipment had surged to an undoubtedly unsustainable pace in the first half of the year."
- "Housing...remained at a high level...few indications of any moderation in this sector."

**Center of gravity**: Strong domestic demand across consumer, investment, and housing sectors. Some anticipated moderation ahead but current levels clearly above trend.
**Minority views**: "Several members referred to emerging signs of slightly more cautious attitudes among their business contacts."

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 38 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Domestic financial conditions remained generally accommodative."
- "Ample availability of financing on favorable terms would continue to support this sector."
- "Equity prices and risk spreads in U.S. financial markets were beginning to be adversely affected."
- "Recent developments...had tended on balance to decrease some of the stimulative effects of financial conditions."

**Center of gravity**: Conditions still accommodative overall but tightening at the margin due to equity declines and widening risk spreads. Moving from loose toward neutral.
**Minority views**: Jordan cited "ample credit provision encourages speculative lending" (implying too loose).

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 51 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All but one of the members agreed on the desirability of maintaining a steady policy stance."
- "A majority of the members agreed that the risks to the economic outlook were now more balanced and called for a shift from asymmetry to symmetry."
- "Other members continued to believe that the risks were still tilted to some degree toward rising inflation...still preferred an asymmetrical directive but could accept symmetry."
- One dissent (Jordan) for tightening.

**Center of gravity**: Hold steady with a shift from tightening bias to neutral. The majority has moved to balanced risk assessment; a minority retains mild hawkish lean. Net is essentially balanced with a tiny hawkish residue.
**Minority views**: Jordan dissented for tightening; "other members" preferred asymmetric (hawkish) directive.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- One formal dissent (Jordan) for tightening.
- "A majority" favored symmetry; "other members" preferred asymmetry but "could accept symmetry."
- Final vote: 10-1.

**Center of gravity**: One dissent with some internal disagreement about risk balance, but the policy decision itself was broadly agreed upon. Typical one-dissent meeting with additional preference differences.
**Minority views**: Jordan's dissent was clearly articulated and substantive.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 45 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Financial turmoil in some foreign economies had deepened and spread and currently showed few signs of stabilizing."
- "Equity prices and risk spreads in U.S. financial markets were beginning to be adversely affected."
- "Extent and ultimate effects of the apparently spreading fragility in foreign financial markets and economies on U.S. financial and economic conditions were unknown."
- Jordan: "ample credit provision encourages speculative lending."

**Center of gravity**: Financial stability/conditions discussed by several participants as a meaningful factor shaping the outlook and policy. Not the dominant topic but a significant element.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 50 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Committee's next policy move could be in either direction depending on developments."
- Shift to symmetric directive: "slightly higher federal funds rate or a slightly lower federal funds rate would be acceptable."
- "Cautious wait-and-see approach to policy seemed appropriate."

**Center of gravity**: Neutral forward signal. Shift from tightening bias to explicitly balanced/symmetric directive. Data-dependent posture.
**Minority views**: Some members retained a mild hawkish forward lean; Jordan wanted immediate tightening.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "External sector as a major source of uncertainty."
- "Duration and extent of disruptions in Asian and other economies could not be anticipated with any degree of confidence."
- "Uncertainties bearing on the economic outlook remained substantial, and indeed the risks on the downside seemed to have increased appreciably further."

**Center of gravity**: Elevated uncertainty, primarily driven by the evolving Asian crisis and its unknown domestic spillovers. Multiple members emphasize this.
**Minority views**: None -- broad agreement on elevated uncertainty.
