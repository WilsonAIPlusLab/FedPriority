European sovereign debt crisis ongoing. Inflation below target. This is a period of continued accommodation with slow recovery.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW target. Evidence: "almost all participants expected inflation to remain subdued in coming quarters—that is, to run at or below the 2 percent level"; "inflation had been subdued in recent months, there was little evidence of wage or cost pressures"; "longer-term inflation expectations had remained stable"; "some remained concerned that...inflation might continue to drift down and run below mandate-consistent levels." A couple were concerned inflation could rise, but this is a clear minority. Direction: below midpoint. Language "subdued" and "at or below 2 percent" = moderately below target. Score range: 28-35. Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says subdued/below target. Score below 50 matches.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see economy as BELOW potential. Evidence: "current wide margins of slack in labor and product markets"; "the economy had been expanding moderately"; "expected that the unemployment rate would decline only gradually"; headwinds listed extensively. Bright-line rule: "slack" language = must be < 50. "Wide margins of slack" is strong below-potential language. Score range: 32-38. The word "moderately" for expansion and "gradually gain strength" suggests some recovery underway but still clearly below potential.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see labor market as LOOSE/slack. Evidence: "unemployment—including longer-term unemployment—remained elevated"; "discouraged workers and people working part time...were also still quite high"; "underutilization of labor was still substantial"; "some further gradual improvement." There is debate about structural vs. cyclical unemployment, but the center of gravity is clearly that the labor market has significant slack. Score range: 28-33. Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says elevated unemployment and substantial slack. Score below 50 matches.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Participants see spending as BELOW trend but not collapsing. Evidence: "consumer spending had grown moderately"; "growth in business fixed investment had slowed"; "businesses generally reported that they remained cautious regarding capital spending and hiring"; some positive signals (manufacturing, energy advancing; contacts "somewhat more optimistic"). Net position: moderate consumer spending, slowing business investment, depressed housing. Score range: 36-42. "Moderate" consumer spending with slowing CapEx and cautious businesses = below trend.

**5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see conditions as having EASED somewhat but still with risks. Evidence: "financial conditions improved and financial market stresses eased somewhat during the intermeeting period"; "Equity prices rose, volatility declined, and bank lending conditions appeared to improve"; "further improvement in the availability of loans to businesses"; but "a few other participants commented that small businesses...continued to face difficulty in obtaining bank loans"; "strains in global financial markets continued to pose significant downside risks." Net: conditions are somewhat loose to neutral given ZLB policy and easing, but European risks remain. Score range: 38-44. Not extremely loose (European risks linger) but improved.

**6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Participants are clearly DOVISH. Evidence: "members agreed that it would be appropriate to maintain the existing highly accommodative stance of monetary policy"; rate at 0-0.25%; Operation Twist continuing; discussion of forward guidance improvements (many exploring better communication of accommodation); "a couple of participants were concerned that inflation could rise" (hawkish minority). The discussion is about how to maintain and communicate accommodation, not about removing it. Active QE-era discussion. Score range: 18-24. Anchor: 15-25 for QE era 2010-12.

**7. DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Moderate differences exist. Evidence: "Participants expressed a range of views on the current extent of slack in the labor market" (structural vs. cyclical debate); "a couple of participants were concerned that inflation could rise" vs. "some remained concerned...inflation might continue to drift down"; forward guidance debate with different views. But on policy action, "members agreed" to maintain stance. Score range: 30-38. No formal dissent mentioned, but notable range of views on economic assessment and forward guidance approaches.

**8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Moderate discussion. Evidence: European sovereign debt/banking stresses discussed at length; "strains in global financial markets continued to pose significant downside risks"; ECB operations noted; U.S. bank lending conditions discussed; housing sector distress discussed. Financial stability is a meaningful but not dominant topic. Score range: 40-50.

**9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Very dovish forward signal. Evidence: extensive discussion of improving forward guidance communication, including "providing numerical thresholds for the unemployment and inflation rates"; extending date-based guidance; "members agreed to continue exploring approaches for providing the public with greater clarity." This is the meeting that introduced SEP policy rate projections. The entire forward guidance discussion is about how to communicate continued accommodation more clearly. Score range: 5-12. Anchor: 3-8 for 2012-13 Evans Rule/threshold era.

**10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Elevated uncertainty. Evidence: "high levels of uncertainty among households and businesses"; "strains in global financial markets continued to pose significant downside risks to the economic outlook"; European fiscal/banking issues; "the possibility of increased volatility in financial markets"; "some business contacts cited uncertainty about the economic outlook and about fiscal and regulatory policy." Multiple sources of uncertainty cited by participants. Score range: 48-58.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (35) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (30): Both well below 50, consistent — economy below potential with loose labor market. Labor slightly lower reflecting emphasis on "still quite high" unemployment and "substantial" underutilization.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (20) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (8): Both dovish. Forward guidance is even more dovish because the discussion is explicitly about strengthening accommodation communication (threshold-based guidance). ## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 30 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "almost all participants expected inflation to remain subdued in coming quarters—that is, to run at or below the 2 percent level"; "inflation had been subdued in recent months, there was little evidence of wage or cost pressures"; "some remained concerned that, with the persistence of considerable resource slack, inflation might continue to drift down and run below mandate-consistent levels for some time"
- Center-of-gravity: Near-consensus that inflation is subdued and at or below 2%, with some worried about further downward drift. Clearly below target but not in deflationary territory.
- Minority views: "a couple of participants were concerned that inflation could rise as the recovery continued" — this widens the band modestly.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "the modest expansion in economic activity that they were projecting would be consistent with a gradual reduction in the current wide margins of slack in labor and product markets"; "the economy had been expanding moderately"; participants listed multiple headwinds restraining pace of expansion.
- Center-of-gravity: Clear consensus that significant slack persists in both product and labor markets. Economy expanding but far too slowly to close the gap. "Wide margins of slack" is strong language.
- Minority views: None significantly challenge the below-potential assessment.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 30 +/-8 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "unemployment—including longer-term unemployment—remained elevated, and the numbers of discouraged workers and people working part time because they could not find full-time work were also still quite high"; "underutilization of labor was still substantial"; "almost all members viewed the unemployment rate as still elevated"
- Center-of-gravity: Labor market has significant slack with elevated unemployment and underemployment despite some gradual improvement.
- Minority views: Debate over structural vs. cyclical component — "Very high long-duration unemployment might indicate a mismatch" — this creates uncertainty about the degree of slack but not the direction. Wider band reflects this.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 38 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "consumer spending had grown moderately in recent months"; "growth in business fixed investment had slowed"; "businesses generally reported that they remained cautious regarding capital spending and hiring"; housing sector "still depressed"
- Center-of-gravity: Consumer spending moderate, business investment slowing, housing depressed. Below trend overall but not contracting. Some positive signals from energy/manufacturing sectors and contacts becoming "somewhat more optimistic."
- Minority views: "a few other participants pointed to increasing job gains...supporting continued moderate growth in consumer spending" vs. concerns spending might slow as it outpaced income growth.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 40 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "financial conditions improved and financial market stresses eased somewhat during the intermeeting period: Equity prices rose, volatility declined, and bank lending conditions appeared to improve"; but "strains in global financial markets continued to pose significant downside risks"; "a few other participants commented that small businesses...continued to face difficulty in obtaining bank loans"
- Center-of-gravity: Conditions improved and are somewhat loose to neutral — ZLB policy and QE are providing accommodation, bank lending improving — but lingering European stresses and small business credit difficulties keep conditions from being fully loose.
- Minority views: Mixed signals between improving credit availability and ongoing European financial strains widen the band.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 20 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "members agreed that it would be appropriate to maintain the existing highly accommodative stance of monetary policy"; keeping FFR at 0-0.25% and continuing Operation Twist; extensive discussion about improving forward guidance to better communicate accommodation.
- Center-of-gravity: Strong dovish consensus — maintaining maximum accommodation with active discussion about how to strengthen forward guidance communication. QE-era dovishness.
- Minority views: "a couple of participants were concerned that inflation could rise...and argued that providing additional monetary accommodation, or even maintaining the current highly accommodative stance...would erode the stability of inflation expectations and risk higher inflation." A small hawkish minority.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 35 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Debate over structural vs. cyclical unemployment; "some remained concerned" inflation could drift lower vs. "a couple" worried inflation could rise; substantial disagreement on forward guidance approaches ("several participants proposed dropping...forward guidance" vs. "several other participants emphasized" it was needed); but on policy action "members agreed."
- Center-of-gravity: Notable differences in economic assessment and forward guidance preferences, but broad agreement on maintaining current policy stance. No formal dissents mentioned in the truncated text.
- Minority views: Forward guidance debate shows genuine disagreement about communication strategy.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 45 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "participants agreed that strains in global financial markets continued to pose significant downside risks to the economic outlook"; discussion of ECB operations, European sovereign debt, U.S. bank lending conditions, equity prices, volatility; housing sector distress and foreclosure overhang discussed.
- Center-of-gravity: Financial stability/conditions get meaningful attention — European risks are a significant ongoing concern and banking/credit conditions are discussed at moderate length. Not the dominant topic but more than passing mentions.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 8 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Many participants thought it important to explore means for better communicating policymakers' thinking about future monetary policy"; "Several participants thought it would be helpful to provide...numerical thresholds for the unemployment and inflation rates"; this is the meeting introducing SEP policy rate projections; extended accommodation is the entire tenor of the forward-looking discussion.
- Center-of-gravity: Very dovish forward signal — the discussion is entirely about how to better communicate and strengthen the commitment to extended accommodation. Threshold-based guidance being actively explored. Squarely in the Evans Rule era.
- Minority views: "Several participants proposed either dropping or greatly simplifying the forward guidance" — but this was about communication method, not about hawkish policy direction.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 52 +/-8 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "high levels of uncertainty among households and businesses"; "strains in global financial markets continued to pose significant downside risks"; European fiscal/banking situation uncertain; multiple headwinds creating uncertainty about pace of recovery; structural vs. cyclical unemployment debate reflects genuine analytical uncertainty.
- Center-of-gravity: Elevated uncertainty. Multiple sources of downside risk and genuine analytical disagreement about the nature of labor market slack. Not "uncharted territory" but clearly above normal uncertainty levels.
