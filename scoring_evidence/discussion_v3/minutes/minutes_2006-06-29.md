# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2006-06-29

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Pre-2009 unsegmented format, but participant language is clearly marked with cues such as "participants observed," "all members agreed," "many noted," "several participants," "some participants," "a few participants." Staff content is minimal; I will score only participant-attributed language.

**Era context**: Mid-2006, near the end of the Greenspan/early-Bernanke tightening cycle. The fed funds rate was raised to 5.25% at this meeting (the 17th consecutive 25bp hike). Housing is cooling, core inflation has picked up, and the question is whether further tightening is needed.

**Quantifier distribution**: "All" (2 uses for inflation concern and rate hike agreement), "most" (inflation likely to edge down, difficulty characterizing policy stance), "many" (temporary factors boosting inflation, significant uncertainty about forward policy), "several" (housing decline in line with expectations, wage sensitivity to data, credit supporting business), "some" (difficulty hiring skilled workers, greater ability to pass costs, rents may decelerate), "a few" (tax receipts as sign of vigorous income).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: "All participants expressed concern about recent elevated readings on core inflation." Core inflation described as "elevated," "broadly based," with pass-through from energy, rising rents. Forecasts for core PCE: 2.25-2.5% central tendency for 2006 (above the implicit 2% target). "Most" see it likely to "edge down" but "several factors were cited as potentially sustaining upward pressure." Direction: clearly above target, concerning to all. The language is "elevated" and "of concern" -- this maps to 61-75 range ("clearly above target; broadening pressures; persistent, concerning"). The expectation of moderation tempers the score somewhat. Score: 68.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic growth as having moderated in the second quarter from its robust pace in the first quarter." "Most participants expected output to advance at a pace close to that which the economy can sustain over time." "Resource utilization was currently tight." "Relatively taut resource markets." The economy is described as at or slightly above potential -- growth is moderating toward sustainable rates but resource utilization is still "tight/taut." This suggests modestly above potential. Score: 54.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Resource utilization was currently tight." "Some participants noted that businesses in their Districts were experiencing difficulty hiring certain types of skilled workers, suggesting that increased wage pressures might emerge." Unemployment forecast central tendency is 4.75-5%, which is near estimates of NAIRU for 2006. The labor market is described as tight but not severely so -- difficulty hiring is limited to "certain types of skilled workers" by "some." Score: 57.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "dropped off significantly in the second quarter from a robust pace." Expected to "advance modestly in coming quarters." Business investment "would remain a source of strength." Capital expenditures shifting from efficiency to capacity expansion. Nonresidential structures "advancing robustly." This is a mixed picture: consumer spending weakening, business investment strong. Net position is roughly at trend, perhaps slightly below given the consumer slowdown dominates discussion. Score: 48.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Several participants observed that the continued ready availability of credit would support business expenditures." "Others, however, noted that the pullback from risk-taking that had been observed in some financial markets over the preceding few months could intensify." The description of "ready availability of credit" suggests relatively loose conditions, while there's some mention of pullback from risk-taking. Global central banks tightening. The "reduction in the attractiveness of home equity borrowing" and higher interest rates affecting consumers. On balance, conditions are transitioning from loose toward neutral but still on the looser side. Score: 38.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All Committee members agreed" on the 25bp hike. The statement acknowledges "some inflation risks remain." But "members recognized the value of accumulating more information for determining what, if any, additional policy action would be needed." The forward language is conditional -- "extent and timing of any additional firming... will depend on the evolution of the outlook." One member found the rate hike "a close call." This is hawkish (they hiked) but with increasing conditionality about the path forward. The center of gravity is moderate hawkish with data-dependent lean. Score: 65.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All Committee members agreed" on the rate hike. However, "members expressed a wide range of views" on statement wording. "Some members favored a shorter statement... others were inclined to provide more information." "Many members noted that significant uncertainty accompanied the appropriate setting of policy going forward." One member found the decision "a close call." No formal dissents, but notable disagreement about communication and forward path. Score: 35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Housing market discussed as cooling (orderly slowdown). "Pullback from risk-taking" in financial markets mentioned briefly. "Greater uncertainty about inflation pressures... had perhaps contributed to a reassessment of risks by investors globally." Credit availability discussed. This is moderate -- financial conditions discussed as context for the economic outlook, not as a primary stability concern. Score: 40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "The extent and timing of any additional firming that may be needed to address these risks will depend on the evolution of the outlook." This is conditional tightening language -- "additional firming... may be needed" is a mild hawkish lean but heavily data-dependent. They're signaling possible further tightening but not committing. Score: 63.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Many members noted that significant uncertainty accompanied the appropriate setting of policy going forward." "Characterizing the resulting stance of policy was quite difficult in the view of most members." Multiple sources of uncertainty about inflation trajectory, housing, consumer spending. Score: 52.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (54) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (57): Both modestly above 50, consistent with "tight" resource utilization. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (65) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (63): Both moderately hawkish. The forward guidance is slightly less hawkish due to conditionality. Coherent.

### Polarity checks:

- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says credit "readily available" with some tightening underway. 38 is below midpoint (loose side). Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 68 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All participants found the elevated readings on core inflation of recent months to be of concern and, if sustained, inconsistent with the maintenance of price stability."
- "The pickup in price increases had been broadly based rather than being limited to a few specific sectors."
- "Most" see inflation as "likely to edge down" but "several factors were cited as potentially sustaining upward pressure on inflation."

**Center-of-gravity**: All participants see inflation as elevated and concerning, with broadly based increases. Expectations of moderation temper the score, but the current readings and breadth of pressures place this clearly above target.

**Minority views**: None dissent from the "elevated" characterization; disagreement is about persistence, reflected in the uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 54 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Most participants expected output to advance over the next year and a half at a pace close to that which the economy can sustain over time."
- "Resource utilization was currently tight" and "relatively taut resource markets."
- "Economic growth as having moderated in the second quarter from its robust pace in the first quarter."

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is modestly above potential -- resource utilization is "tight/taut" but growth is moderating toward sustainable rates.

**Minority views**: Limited dispersion; participants broadly agree on the moderation narrative.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 57 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Resource utilization was currently tight."
- "Some participants noted that businesses in their Districts were experiencing difficulty hiring certain types of skilled workers, suggesting that increased wage pressures might emerge."
- Unemployment forecast central tendency: 4.75-5%.

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is tight but not severely so; skill-specific shortages rather than broad-based tightness.

**Minority views**: Some see emerging wage pressures; "modest advances in compensation" suggests others see limited pressure.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 48 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The growth of consumer spending had dropped off significantly in the second quarter from a robust pace earlier in the year."
- "Business investment would remain a source of strength going forward."
- "Businesses were now directing their capital expenditures toward expanding capacity rather than increasing efficiency."

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer weakness is offset by strong business investment. Net position is roughly at trend, with consumer softening pulling slightly below.

**Minority views**: "A few participants noted that the surge in federal tax receipts... could be a sign of vigorous gains in income" suggesting stronger spending ahead.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 38 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Several participants observed that the continued ready availability of credit would support business expenditures."
- "Others, however, noted that the pullback from risk-taking that had been observed in some financial markets over the preceding few months could intensify."
- Higher interest rates affecting housing and home equity borrowing.

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions remain on the loose side despite tightening monetary policy; credit is readily available though some risk repricing is occurring.

**Minority views**: Some see risk of greater financial tightening ahead; the uncertainty band captures this.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 65 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All Committee members agreed that raising the target for the federal funds rate 25 basis points... was appropriate given the recent readings on inflation."
- "Members recognized the value of accumulating more information for determining what, if any, additional policy action would be needed."
- "One indicated that the decision to raise the target federal funds rate at this meeting was a close call."

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous support for the hike driven by inflation concerns, but significant conditionality about the path forward. Moderate hawkish with increasing data dependence.

**Minority views**: One member found the hike a "close call"; "wide range of views" on statement wording.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 35 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All Committee members agreed" on the rate action (no formal dissent).
- "Members expressed a wide range of views" on statement wording.
- "One indicated that the decision... was a close call."
- "Characterizing the resulting stance of policy was quite difficult in the view of most members."

**Center-of-gravity**: No formal dissent, but moderate differences in views about the characterization of policy stance and communication approach.

**Minority views**: The "wide range of views" on communication and one "close call" member push this above the lowest disagreement ranges.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 40 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Housing slowdown discussed as "orderly" with awareness of "possibility of a sharper downturn."
- "Pullback from risk-taking that had been observed in some financial markets."
- "Greater uncertainty about inflation pressures... had perhaps contributed to a reassessment of risks by investors globally."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are discussed as context for the outlook, with moderate attention to housing risks and global risk repricing. Not a dominant topic but more than passing mention.

**Minority views**: Limited spread.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 63 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The extent and timing of any additional firming that may be needed to address these risks will depend on the evolution of the outlook for both inflation and economic growth."
- "Some inflation risks remain."
- "Policy action at future meetings was not foreordained."

**Center-of-gravity**: Mild tightening bias in forward guidance -- "additional firming... may be needed" -- but heavily conditional on data. This is a step down from committed tightening toward data dependence.

**Minority views**: Range from those wanting shorter statement focused on inflation to those wanting more forward information.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Many members noted that significant uncertainty accompanied the appropriate setting of policy going forward."
- "Characterizing the resulting stance of policy was quite difficult in the view of most members."
- Inflation path uncertain: "rents, while difficult to forecast"; "the extent to which this spring's increase in inflation reflected transitory or persistent influences."

**Center-of-gravity**: Elevated uncertainty, with "many" and "most" using uncertainty language. The key question -- whether inflation is transitory or persistent -- is genuinely unresolved.

**Minority views**: Limited; broadly shared sense of uncertainty.
