## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Over the medium term, most participants anticipated that inflation would run at or below the Committee's 2 percent objective." "Underlying inflation trends remained subdued, and indicators of longer-term inflation expectations were generally viewed as stable." "Inflation recently picked up somewhat, reflecting higher energy prices" (temporary).
- Center of gravity: Participants see inflation below target on an underlying basis, with temporary energy-driven uptick that is expected to subside. Consistent with "below target; slack keeping prices in check."
- Minority: "A couple of participants saw this increase [in inflation compensation] as a sign that the open-ended asset purchases posed a risk to the stability of longer-term inflation expectations." This is a small minority; others dismissed it.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 37 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Economic activity continued to expand at a moderate pace." "Without sufficient policy accommodation, economic growth might not be strong enough to generate sustained improvement in the labor market." "Fiscal policy assumed to be tighter next year" (staff, but participants echo fiscal drag concerns).
- Center of gravity: Economy expanding moderately but clearly below potential; the need for massive accommodation (QE3 + near-zero rates) itself signals substantial slack.
- Minority: None significant -- broad agreement on below-potential status.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 34 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Employment was still rising slowly, and the unemployment rate remained elevated." "Some participants expressed concern that the pace of job creation would generate only a slow decline in joblessness." "A steep drop in the index of hiring plans by small businesses."
- Center of gravity: Labor market gradually improving but far from full employment; unemployment elevated with slow improvement expected.
- Minority: No significant minority -- broad agreement on labor slack.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 40 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Household spending advanced more quickly in recent months than during the spring, and housing activity showed further signs of improvement. However, business fixed investment slowed noticeably." "Contacts generally reported slower activity in the business sector." "The economy was, on balance, expanding moderately, with a pickup in household spending... offset to some extent by a slowdown in the business sector."
- Center of gravity: Mixed -- consumer spending improving and housing recovering, but business investment clearly weakening. Net is below trend but not deeply so.
- Minority: Views fairly uniform on the mixed nature of spending.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 30 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "The initial effects were generally viewed as consistent with a marked easing in financial conditions." "Yields on MBS dropped noticeably, leading to a decline in mortgage interest rates, and corporate bond yields generally moved lower." "Low interest rates had increased demand for riskier financial products."
- Center of gravity: Financial conditions are clearly accommodative/loose following QE3 and prior actions. Credit conditions supportive for autos and housing, though mortgage underwriting still tight for lower-quality borrowers.
- Minority: "Several participants" noted underwriting standards "remained quite tight, particularly for borrowers with lower credit quality," which is why the score is not lower (looser).

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 22 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "All but one member judged that maintaining the current, highly accommodative stance of monetary policy was warranted." "A number of participants indicated that additional asset purchases would likely be appropriate next year." One member dissented opposing further purchases.
- Center of gravity: Firmly dovish -- maintaining QE3 with discussion of extending purchases. The committee is in the midst of open-ended asset purchases at the zero lower bound.
- Minority: "Several participants questioned the effectiveness of the current purchases." "Several expressed concerns that sizable asset purchases might eventually have adverse consequences." One formal dissent. This cluster pulls the uncertainty band wider.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 35 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "One member opposed further asset purchases." "Several participants questioned the effectiveness of the current purchases or whether a continuation of them would be warranted." "Several expressed concerns that sizable asset purchases might eventually have adverse consequences." "A couple of participants noted that an extended period of policy accommodation posed an upside risk to inflation."
- Center of gravity: One formal dissent with a notable cluster of participants expressing reservations about QE efficacy and risks. More than a simple one-dissent meeting but not a deep split.
- Minority: The questioning voices are a meaningful minority but the majority firmly supports current policy.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 45 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "A few participants observed that low interest rates had increased demand for riskier financial products, and a couple of participants saw a risk that holding interest rates low for a prolonged period could lead to financial imbalances and imprudent risk-taking." Extensive discussion of financial market effects of QE3 (MBS yields, mortgage rates, corporate bonds, equity prices, risk premiums). Euro area strains noted.
- Center of gravity: Moderate-to-substantial discussion of financial conditions and some stability concerns. Financial conditions are a meaningful part of the meeting discussion, particularly in assessing QE effects.
- Minority: N/A -- this is about attention level, not direction.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 8 +/-4 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Many members saw the adjustments in the Committee's forward guidance at the September meeting as having been effective in communicating its intention to maintain a highly accommodative stance of monetary policy for a considerable time after the economic recovery strengthens." "A number of participants indicated that additional asset purchases would likely be appropriate next year." "A couple of participants noted the likely usefulness of clarifying the range of indicators that would be evaluated" (moving toward threshold-based guidance).
- Center of gravity: Forward signal is deeply dovish -- extended accommodation promised, additional asset purchases anticipated, threshold-based guidance being discussed. This is the QE3 era with explicit commitments to prolonged accommodation.
- Minority: Some questioning QE continuation, but the forward signal from the committee is unambiguously dovish.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 52 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Many participants saw the uncertainty attending the unresolved U.S. fiscal situation and the ongoing fiscal and financial strains in the euro area as factors likely to restrain the pace of economic growth." "Many participants cited significant downside risks to the outlook." "Contacts stated that they were delaying or cutting back on hiring and capital spending because of the uncertain outlook for government spending, taxes, or regulatory policies."
- Center of gravity: Elevated uncertainty driven primarily by the fiscal cliff and euro area concerns. Multiple participants emphasize downside risks. Business contacts explicitly citing uncertainty as a drag. Above typical levels but not extreme/unprecedented.
- Minority: No one argues uncertainty is low; the question is degree.
