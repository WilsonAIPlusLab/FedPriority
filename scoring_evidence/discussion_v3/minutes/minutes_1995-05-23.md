# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1995-05-23

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented (pre-2009), so I must focus on participant language cues ("members reported," "members commented," "members cited," etc.) and ignore staff-reported content.

**Era context**: Mid-1995. The Fed had completed its 1994 tightening cycle (300bp of hikes from Feb 1994 to Feb 1995). The economy is now slowing, with an inventory correction underway. This is the "soft landing" period. Inflation had picked up modestly but remained moderate by historical standards.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all the members" (endorse hold, favor symmetric directive), "a number" (concern about inflation tilted upward; financial climate favorable), "several" (inflation reflecting cyclical pressures; Mexico trade adjustment; Japan trade concerns), "some" (risk of greater inventory softening; consumer credit unsustainable; fiscal contraction larger than expected), "one member" (costs higher on inflation side), "another member" (longer-term risks tilted downside).

### Dimension-by-dimension directional reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Members report "a worsening in key measures of inflation" including CPI and PPI. "A number of members expressed concern that the economy already producing at or even slightly above its sustainable potential, inflation pressures were likely to intensify." But others saw moderate growth averting further inflation. The center of gravity: inflation has risen, is concerning to several, but is not dramatically elevated. This is above the midpoint -- modestly elevated inflation pressure. Direction: above 50 but not dramatically so. Score range ~55-62.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Members report "further slowing in the expansion" and "some related easing of pressures on labor and other producer resources." However, critically, "a number of members expressed concern that, with the economy already producing at or even slightly above its sustainable potential." The bright-line rule: "at or even slightly above" potential = score just above 50. The economy is slowing toward potential but is described as at/slightly above. Score ~51-53.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "some related easing of pressures on labor and other producer resources." "Restraint in compensation increases" and "subdued increases in labor costs." The labor market had been tight but is loosening. Unemployment rose to 5.8%. Labor costs subdued. This suggests near NAIRU or slightly loose side. Score ~47-50.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending slowed "somewhat more pronounced than anticipated" but business fixed investment "remained on a strong uptrend." Housing expected to strengthen. Net exports expected to contribute. The inventory correction is the near-term drag. Overall: slightly below trend given the slowdown, but underlying demand "sufficiently robust." "Sluggish economic performance over coming months" but with underlying strength. Score ~42-48.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Very clear evidence of loosening. "Decline in interest rates had favorable implications for demand." "Rise in equity prices also was contributing to reductions in the cost of capital." "Banks continued to ease terms and standards on loans." "Rise in stock and bond prices had increased the net worth." "Generally accommodative financial and banking markets." This is clearly loose conditions. Score ~20-28.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All the members endorsed a proposal to maintain the current stance of policy." Symmetric directive with no bias. "No compelling case could be made at this point for potential adjustments to policy in either direction." One member leaned hawkish on risks, another leaned dovish. The center is firmly balanced/hold. Score ~50.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Unanimous vote (11-0). All favored hold with symmetric directive. Minor differences in risk assessment (one hawkish-leaning, one dovish-leaning), but these are shades within broad agreement. Score ~12-18.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: "A number of members referred to the general financial climate as an important element in the outlook." Discussion of equity prices, bond prices, bank lending terms, interest rate declines. "Some concern was expressed about the sustainability of the stock market's strong performance." Moderate attention to financial conditions as context for outlook. For 1993-96 era, this is actually somewhat elevated. Score ~20-28.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Symmetric directive -- no bias either way. "Wait for additional information before deciding on the next policy move." "No compelling case could be made at this point for potential adjustments to policy in either direction." This is squarely neutral/data-dependent. Score ~50.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The longer-run outlook remained subject to considerable uncertainty, especially given the undecided course of fiscal policy and the ongoing inventory correction." "The dimensions of the near-term deceleration and the potential strength of underlying demand remained uncertain." "Considerable uncertainty" is the key phrase. Multiple sources of uncertainty cited (fiscal policy, inventory correction, inflation trajectory). Score ~55-65.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (~52) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (~48): Slightly divergent but defensible. Economy at/slightly above potential but labor pressures easing -- the inventory correction is cooling the labor side while capacity is still near limits. Reasonable.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (~50) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (~50): Perfectly aligned -- balanced hold with symmetric directive.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 57 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "A worsening in key measures of inflation, including the consumer price index and the producer price index for finished goods, was a disappointing--if not unexpected--development."
- "A number of members expressed concern that, with the economy already producing at or even slightly above its sustainable potential, inflation pressures were likely to intensify if the current pause in the expansion were to be followed by a period of above-average growth."
- "Others were more inclined to the view that inflation was not likely to rise much further in a climate of moderate growth in demand, intense competitive pressures in many markets, and relatively subdued increases in labor costs."

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation has worsened and is modestly above comfort levels, with some members concerned about further intensification, but offset by others citing competitive restraints and subdued wage growth.

**Minority views**: Those seeing inflation as contained by competitive pressures and moderate growth push the lower band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 52 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "A number of members expressed concern that, with the economy already producing at or even slightly above its sustainable potential..."
- "Members reported on statistical and anecdotal indications of further slowing in the expansion of economic activity and some related easing of pressures on labor and other producer resources."
- "Underlying demand was likely to remain sufficiently robust... to return economic growth to a pace broadly in line with potential."

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy at or just barely above potential, with near-term slowing bringing it back toward potential. The bright-line rule: "at or even slightly above" = just above 50.

**Minority views**: The slowing/inventory correction could push assessment slightly below potential temporarily, but the consensus language is "at or slightly above."

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 48 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Some related easing of pressures on labor and other producer resources."
- "Relatively subdued increases in labor costs" and "restraint in compensation increases."
- Unemployment at 5.8% (from directive language, reflecting data context).

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market near balance but with easing pressures. Compensation growth subdued. Previously tight conditions moderating.

**Minority views**: Limited divergence on labor specifically.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 45 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The slowdown in the growth of consumer spending was somewhat more pronounced than anticipated earlier."
- "Business investment remained on a strong uptrend as numerous firms continued to respond to the need to relieve pressures on existing capacity."
- "Underlying demand was likely to remain sufficiently robust... to avert a cumulative decline in business activity."
- "They anticipated a relatively sluggish economic performance over coming months as production was cut back to bring inventories into better balance."

**Center-of-gravity**: Below trend overall due to consumer slowdown and inventory correction, but business investment strength and expected housing/trade improvement provide support. Net: slightly below trend.

**Minority views**: Some saw risk of cumulative weakness from inventory correction; others saw robust underlying demand.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 24 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The decline in interest rates had favorable implications for demand in interest-sensitive sectors of the economy."
- "The rise in equity prices also was contributing to reductions in the cost of capital to businesses, and banks continued to ease terms and standards on loans."
- "Generally accommodative financial and banking markets."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions clearly loose -- rates down, equities up, bank lending easing, capital costs declining. Multiple members highlighted these accommodative conditions.

**Minority views**: Minimal disagreement on the characterization of loose conditions.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 50 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All the members endorsed a proposal to maintain the current stance of policy."
- "No compelling case could be made at this point for potential adjustments to policy in either direction."

**Center-of-gravity**: Perfectly balanced. Unanimous hold with symmetric directive. No lean in either direction.

**Minority views**: One member saw costs higher on inflation side (mild hawkish lean); another saw longer-term risks tilted downside (mild dovish lean). These cancel out.
