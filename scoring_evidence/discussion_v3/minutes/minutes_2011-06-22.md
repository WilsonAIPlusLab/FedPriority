# FOMC Meeting Scoring — 2011-06-22

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented — "Participants' Views on Current Conditions and the Economic Outlook" section is clearly delineated from staff material.

**Era context**: June 2011, post-QE2 (the $600 billion asset purchase program completing at end of month). The economy is in a sluggish recovery from the GFC. Growth has slowed unexpectedly, inflation has risen due to commodity prices and supply disruptions (Japan earthquake), and the labor market remains weak. This is the "soft patch" period of mid-2011.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all" (agreed recovery continuing), "most" (pace slower than projected; inflation transitory), "many" (outlook unusually uncertain), "several" (structural unemployment concerns; delaying hiring), "a number of" (faster price increases widespread), "some" (additional accommodation if needed; less slack than thought), "a few" (upside inflation risk; additional stimulus; removing accommodation sooner; inflation expectations risk; explicit inflation objective).

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as above target NOW but expected to be transitory. "Higher prices for energy and other commodities had boosted inflation this year." "A number of participants pointed out that the recent faster pace of price increases was widespread across many categories." "Most participants expected that much of the rise in headline inflation this year would prove transitory." "A number of participants judged the risks to the outlook for inflation as tilted to the upside." Current inflation is elevated, but center of gravity expects it to subside. Per the rubric gotcha, score the CURRENT perception: elevated but expected transitory. This places it in the 55-62 range. The widespread nature and the "number" who see upside risks push it up a bit. Score: 58.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic recovery was continuing at a moderate pace, though somewhat more slowly than they had anticipated." "Persistent weakness in the housing market." "Recovery remained subject to some downside risks." "Unemployment rate would decline gradually... but at a more gradual pace." The economy is clearly below potential — "persistent slack in labor and product markets" mentioned explicitly. Several participants noted structural changes reducing potential output, but the overall tone is of substantial remaining slack. Anchor: 35-42 for 2010-14 recovery. The language of "moderate pace" recovering but "slack remains" points to 35-38. Score: 36.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Most recent data on employment had been disappointing." "New claims for unemployment insurance remained elevated." "Recent deterioration in labor market conditions was a particular concern." "Wage gains were generally reported to be subdued." Business contacts delaying hiring. Unemployment rate declining only gradually. This is clearly a loose labor market with elevated unemployment. Anchor: 35-40 for 2011-14. The "disappointing" and "deterioration" language pushes toward the lower end. Score: 33.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Business investment in equipment/software "generally remained robust" but "growth in new orders... appeared to have slowed." "Indicators of current and planned business investment... had weakened somewhat." Housing "remained depressed." Consumer spending affected by commodity prices and "sluggish growth of income and consumption." Overall below trend — robust capex offset by weak housing and sluggish consumption. Score: 37.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Investors had become more concerned about risk. Equity markets had seen a broad selloff, and risk spreads for many corporate borrowers had widened noticeably." Large businesses still have "ready access to credit... on relatively attractive terms" but "credit conditions remained tight for smaller, bank-dependent firms." European contagion risks noted. Debt ceiling concerns. This is somewhat tight — mixed conditions leaning toward tighter. Score: 55.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Committee agreed to complete QE2 and hold rates. "Appropriate to maintain the Committee's current policy stance and accumulate further information." "A few members noted... the Committee might have to consider providing additional monetary policy stimulus." "A few members viewed the increase in inflation risks as suggesting... removing policy accommodation sooner." The center is firmly at hold-and-wait. "Some participants noted that if economic growth remained too slow... it would be appropriate to provide additional monetary policy accommodation." The lean is mildly dovish — they just finished QE2, growth is disappointing, and the conditional language about more easing is present. But there are also voices for earlier withdrawal. Center of gravity: hold with very slight dovish tilt. Score: 42.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: There is a clear two-sided debate. "Some" want more accommodation if growth stays weak. "Others" see less slack and possible need for earlier withdrawal. "A few" on each side regarding policy direction. "Many members saw the outlook for both employment and inflation as unusually uncertain." The spread of views is notable — not just about timing but about the fundamental assessment (how much slack exists). No formal dissents mentioned. Score: 45.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Meaningful discussion of European spillovers, money market fund exposures, debt ceiling risks, aggressive leverage in agriculture sector, equity selloff, credit spreads widening. "A few participants expressed concern that credit conditions in some sectors... might have eased too much." Participants "also concerned about the possible effect on financial markets of a failure to raise the statutory federal debt ceiling." This is moderately prominent — several participants address it meaningfully across multiple angles. Score: 48.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is "accumulate further information regarding the outlook for growth and inflation before deciding on the next policy step." Conditional signals go both ways: "a few" mention possible additional stimulus, "a few" mention possible earlier accommodation removal. The extended period language is maintained. This is a neutral/slightly dovish-leaning forward signal — they just finished QE2 and are in wait-and-see mode with no tightening signal. Score: 30.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Many members saw the outlook for both employment and inflation as unusually uncertain." "Prospects for job growth were seen as an important source of uncertainty." "Business contacts in some regions... reducing capital budgets in response to the less certain economic outlook." "A few participants expressed uncertainty about the efficacy of monetary policy." Multiple downside risks enumerated (housing, fiscal, Europe). The word "unusually uncertain" from "many members" is strong. Score: 58.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

- OUTPUT_GAP (36) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (33): Both below 50, consistent. Labor slightly lower reflecting the "disappointing" recent employment data.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (42) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (30): Both dovish-leaning. Forward guidance is more dovish because the "extended period" language and QE completion signal patient accommodation, while the current lean is more balanced due to hawkish minority voices.

### Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 58: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says elevated but expected transitory. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP 36: Higher = more above potential. Economy is below potential. Matches.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS 33: Higher = tighter. Labor market is loose. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS 55: Higher = tighter conditions. Spreads widened, risk aversion up, but large firms still have access. Somewhat tight. Matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 58 ±10 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Higher prices for energy and other commodities had boosted inflation this year."
- "A number of participants pointed out that the recent faster pace of price increases was widespread across many categories of spending and was evident in inflation measures such as trimmed means or medians."
- "Most participants expected that much of the rise in headline inflation this year would prove transitory."
- "A number of participants judged the risks to the outlook for inflation as tilted to the upside."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Inflation is currently elevated above target due to commodity prices and supply disruptions, with widespread price increases, but most participants expect it to subside; the upside risk camp widens uncertainty.

**Minority views**: "A few participants saw a continuation of the current stance of monetary policy as posing some upside risk to inflation expectations." Others pointed to stable inflation expectations and slack limiting pressures.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 36 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The economic recovery was continuing at a moderate pace, though somewhat more slowly than they had anticipated."
- "Persistent slack in labor and product markets would likely limit upward pressures on prices."
- "Several participants observed that... loss of skills caused by high levels of long-term unemployment... may have temporarily reduced the economy's level of potential output."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is operating well below potential with persistent slack, though recovering at a moderate pace; some participants question whether potential output itself has fallen, which would narrow the gap.

**Minority views**: "Others... saw the recent configuration of slower growth and higher inflation as suggesting that there might be less slack in labor and product markets than had been thought."

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 33 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The most recent data on employment had been disappointing, and new claims for unemployment insurance remained elevated."
- "The recent deterioration in labor market conditions was a particular concern."
- "Wage gains were generally reported to be subdued."
- "Several participants reported feedback from business contacts who were delaying hiring."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The labor market is clearly loose with elevated unemployment, disappointing job growth, subdued wages, and businesses reluctant to hire, placing conditions well below full employment.

**Minority views**: "Wages for a few skilled job categories in which workers were in short supply were said to be increasing relatively more rapidly" — but this is a narrow exception.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 37 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Business investment in equipment and software generally remained robust, but growth in new orders... appeared to have slowed."
- "The housing sector, including residential construction and home sales, remained depressed."
- "Sluggish growth of income and consumption."
- "Business contacts in some regions reported that they were reducing capital budgets."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Spending and investment are below trend — capex had been robust but is softening, housing remains depressed, and consumer spending is sluggish due to weak income growth and commodity price headwinds.

**Minority views**: "Business contacts in some sectors—most notably energy and high tech—reported that activity and business sentiment had strengthened further."

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 55 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Equity markets had seen a broad selloff, and risk spreads for many corporate borrowers had widened noticeably."
- "Credit conditions remained tight for smaller, bank-dependent firms."
- "Large businesses that have access to capital markets continued to enjoy ready access to credit... on relatively attractive terms."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions are somewhat tight, with widening spreads, equity selloff, and tight credit for small firms, though large firms retain easy access; a mixed but moderately restrictive picture.

**Minority views**: "A few participants expressed concern that credit conditions in some sectors... might have eased too much" (agriculture).

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 42 ±10 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Members agreed that it was appropriate to maintain the Committee's current policy stance and accumulate further information."
- "A few members noted that... the Committee might have to consider providing additional monetary policy stimulus."
- "A few members viewed the increase in inflation risks as suggesting... removing policy accommodation sooner than currently anticipated."
- "Some participants noted that if economic growth remained too slow... it would be appropriate to provide additional monetary policy accommodation."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The committee is in a hold posture with a slight dovish lean, having just completed QE2; the majority favors patience, with conditional voices on both sides but more weight given to the possibility of additional easing than to early tightening.

**Minority views**: Hawks see less slack and want earlier removal of accommodation; doves contemplate additional QE if growth stays weak.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 45 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Some participants noted... it would be appropriate to provide additional monetary policy accommodation. Others, however, saw the recent configuration of slower growth and higher inflation as suggesting that there might be less slack."
- "A few participants expressed uncertainty about the efficacy of monetary policy in current circumstances but disagreed on the implications."
- "A few members" on each side regarding policy direction (more easing vs. earlier tightening).

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Notable disagreement with distinct camps — not just about the pace of policy but about the fundamental assessment of slack and the appropriate direction; no formal dissents mentioned but clear philosophical divisions.

**Minority views**: The split is unusual in that it reflects disagreement about the economic assessment itself (how much slack exists), not just policy calibration.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 48 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "An escalation of the fiscal difficulties in Greece and spreading concerns about other peripheral European countries could cause significant financial strains in the United States."
- "Some U.S. money market mutual funds have significant exposures to financial institutions from core European countries."
- "Participants were also concerned about the possible effect on financial markets of a failure to raise the statutory federal debt ceiling."
- "A few participants expressed concern that credit conditions in some sectors... might have eased too much."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial stability receives moderate attention, with several participants addressing European contagion risks, debt ceiling concerns, and pockets of excessive risk-taking — a meaningful but not dominant topic.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 30 ±10 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Appropriate to maintain the Committee's current policy stance and accumulate further information regarding the outlook for growth and inflation before deciding on the next policy step."
- "Some participants noted that if economic growth remained too slow... it would be appropriate to provide additional monetary policy accommodation."
- QE2 just completed; "extended period" language maintained.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal is mildly dovish — the committee is maintaining maximum accommodation with the extended period language, has just completed QE2, and the conditional next-step discussion leans toward possible further easing rather than tightening.

**Minority views**: "A few members" see conditions potentially warranting earlier removal of accommodation — but "several" and "some" lean toward more accommodation if needed.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 58 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Many members saw the outlook for both employment and inflation as unusually uncertain."
- "The prospects for job growth were seen as an important source of uncertainty in the economic outlook."
- "A few participants expressed uncertainty about the efficacy of monetary policy in current circumstances."
- Multiple downside risks enumerated: housing, fiscal tightening, European spillovers, debt ceiling.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Uncertainty is elevated, with "many members" explicitly describing the outlook as "unusually uncertain" and multiple sources of downside risk identified across housing, fiscal, European, and debt ceiling channels.
