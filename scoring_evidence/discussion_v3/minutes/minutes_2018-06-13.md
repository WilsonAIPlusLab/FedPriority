# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2018-06-13

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented — participant discussion section is clearly separated from staff review.

**Era context**: June 2018 — mid-hiking cycle. The Fed had been gradually raising rates since December 2015. Tax cuts enacted in late 2017. Economy growing above trend, unemployment at 3.8%, inflation just reaching the 2% target after years of undershooting. Trade policy uncertainty emerging (tariffs on steel/aluminum). This is a 25bp rate hike meeting.

**Quantifier distribution**: "participants agreed" / "participants generally" (broad consensus language used frequently), "almost all" (on the rate hike), "many" (trade concerns, rising input costs), "several" (labor market slack caveats, yield curve concerns), "a number of" (premature to claim 2% achieved, yield curve monitoring), "some" (trade policy dampening yield curve signal, concern about prolonged above-potential operation), "a few" (fiscal upside risk, unsustainable fiscal path, inflation expectations not anchored, temporary above-2% helpful).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as AT target. "Both overall and core PCE prices had recently moved close to 2 percent." Energy prices pushing overall above 2% temporarily. "Many participants anticipated that high levels of resource utilization and stable inflation expectations would keep overall inflation near 2 percent." However, "a number of participants noted that it was premature to conclude that the Committee had achieved that objective" and core remained "a little below 2 percent." Some firming from business contacts (rising input costs, pass-through). Direction: essentially at the 2% target, maybe just barely reaching it. This matches the anchor of 50 for "early 2018 at target." Score around 50-52.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Inflation just reaching 2%, with some firming signals but also caveats about not yet durably there. Score ~51. Matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy operating ABOVE potential. "above-trend GDP growth" explicitly stated. "The economy already very strong." "A prolonged period in which the economy operated beyond potential" mentioned by some. Unemployment at 3.8%, "below the estimate of each participant who submitted a longer-run projection." Economic activity "rising at a solid rate." This is clearly above potential. Bright-line rule: must be >50. Given "above trend" and "very strong" language, this is modestly above potential — around 55-58.

Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says above potential. Score ~57. Matches.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see the labor market as TIGHT. Unemployment at 3.8%, below every participant's longer-run estimate. "Very high rate of job openings," "elevated quits rate," "difficulties finding qualified workers" across several Districts. However, "several participants suggested that there may be less tightness in the labor market than implied by the unemployment rate alone" due to scope for drawing people in. Wage increases "remained moderate" despite tightness. Center of gravity: tight but not extremely so, with some caveats. Score ~60-63.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says tight. Score ~62. Matches.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Growth of household spending had picked up." "Business fixed investment had continued to grow strongly." "Substantial investment growth in the energy sector." Manufacturing and service sectors "reportedly strong." However, trade uncertainty causing some to scale back or postpone capital spending. Net position: above trend, solidly. Score ~58-62.

Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Evidence says strong. Score ~60. Matches.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Participants describe "accommodative financial conditions" as one of the factors supporting growth. "Even after such an increase in the target range, the stance of monetary policy would remain accommodative." Financial conditions are described as supportive, not tight. Score in the loose range, ~30-35.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says conditions are accommodative/loose. Score ~32. Matches.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Almost all participants" supported a 25bp hike. Forward-looking: "it would likely be appropriate to continue gradually raising the target range." "Many noted that... the federal funds rate could be at or above their estimates of its neutral level sometime next year." Discussion of removing "accommodative" language from the statement soon. Only one participant advocated postponing the hike. This is clearly hawkish — gradual tightening with broad consensus, ongoing hikes anticipated. Anchors: 70-75 for 2017-18 hiking. Score ~70-72.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence: broad support for hike and continued hikes. Score ~71. Matches.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Almost all" supported the hike — only one participant argued for postponement. Views on the economy were broadly shared. Trade policy was a concern "most" shared. Some differences on yield curve interpretation, and "several" vs "some" on various sub-issues, but no deep divisions. One implicit dissenter at most. Score ~22-28.

Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Broad agreement with minor differences. Score ~25. Matches.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Yield curve discussion was notable — a substantial portion of the text is devoted to what a flattening yield curve signals. "Some participants raised the concern that a prolonged period in which the economy operated beyond potential could give rise to... financial imbalances." Discussion of term premiums, yield curve slope, recession indicator. This is more than passing reference but not a dominant topic. Score ~35-40.

Polarity check: Higher = more attention. Moderate attention given to yield curve and financial conditions. Score ~38. Matches.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Continue gradually raising the target range" to "at or somewhat above... neutral level by 2019 or 2020." Discussion of dropping "accommodative" language. "Further gradual increases" expected. This is a clear tightening signal for the path ahead. Score ~70-73.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence: continued gradual rate increases toward/above neutral. Score ~72. Matches.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants generally viewed the risks to the economic outlook as roughly balanced." However, "most participants noted that uncertainty and risks associated with trade policy had intensified." "Many participants saw potential downside risks... associated with political and economic developments in Europe and some EMEs." Trade policy uncertainty causing firms to scale back investment plans. This is somewhat elevated — trade policy is a new uncertainty factor but overall outlook risks are "roughly balanced." Score ~35-40.

Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. Evidence: trade policy uncertainty elevated but overall risks balanced. Score ~37. Matches.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (57) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (62): Both above 50, labor tighter than output gap. Reasonable since unemployment is very low while above-trend growth is moderate.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (71) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (72): Well-aligned — both indicate continued gradual tightening.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 51 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the 12-month changes in both overall and core PCE prices had recently moved close to 2 percent"
- "many participants anticipated that high levels of resource utilization and stable inflation expectations would keep overall inflation near 2 percent over the medium term"
- "a number of participants noted that it was premature to conclude that the Committee had achieved that objective"

**Center of gravity**: Inflation is seen as essentially at the 2% target — just arriving there after years of undershooting — with firming signals from input costs but not yet clearly above target.

**Minority views**: A few noted market-based inflation expectations remained somewhat below 2%, keeping uncertainty narrow but present.

---

### OUTPUT_GAP: 57 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "a number of favorable economic factors that were supporting above-trend GDP growth"
- "the economy already very strong"
- "a prolonged period in which the economy operated beyond potential" (raised by some participants)

**Center of gravity**: The economy is clearly operating above potential with above-trend growth, strong labor market, and fiscal stimulus, though the degree is moderate rather than extreme.

**Minority views**: None notable; broad agreement on above-trend performance.

---

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 62 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The unemployment rate fell to 3.8 percent in May, below the estimate of each participant who submitted a longer-run projection"
- "Contacts in several Districts reported difficulties finding qualified workers"
- "several participants, however, suggested that there may be less tightness in the labor market than implied by the unemployment rate alone"

**Center of gravity**: Labor market is clearly tight with historically low unemployment and widespread hiring difficulties, though wage growth remains moderate and some see scope for additional labor supply.

**Minority views**: Several participants argued for less tightness than headline unemployment implies, reflected in the wider uncertainty band.

---

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 60 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "growth of consumer spending picked up after slowing earlier in the year"
- "business fixed investment had continued to expand at a strong pace in recent months"
- "many District contacts expressed concern about the possible adverse effects of tariffs... on future investment activity; contacts in some Districts indicated that plans for capital spending had been scaled back"

**Center of gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment are above trend and strong, though trade policy uncertainty is tempering some investment plans.

**Minority views**: Trade concerns creating some drag on investment outlook, but not dominant.

---

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 32 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "accommodative financial conditions" listed as a factor supporting above-trend growth
- "even after such an increase in the target range, the stance of monetary policy would remain accommodative"

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are described as accommodative/loose and supportive of growth, placing them clearly in the loose range.

**Minority views**: None significant; consensus that conditions are accommodative.

---

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 71 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "almost all participants expressed the view that it would be appropriate... to continue its gradual approach to policy firming by raising the target range... 25 basis points at this meeting"
- "it would likely be appropriate to continue gradually raising the target range... to a setting that was at or somewhat above their estimates of its longer-run level by 2019 or 2020"
- One participant argued for postponing the hike (market-based inflation compensation still low)

**Center of gravity**: Strong consensus for the 25bp hike with clear intent to continue gradual tightening toward and possibly above neutral. Solidly in the hiking-cycle hawkish range.

**Minority views**: One participant preferred to hold, keeping the uncertainty band modest.

---

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 25 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "almost all participants expressed the view" supporting the hike (near-unanimous)
- Differences on yield curve interpretation ("some... noted" vs "several others expressed doubt") but not on policy prescription
- Only one participant advocated postponing

**Center of gravity**: Broad agreement on both the current action and the outlook, with minor differences in emphasis on risks and interpretation of signals.

**Minority views**: One participant's dissent and some debate on yield curve interpretation, but no deep divisions.

---

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 38 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Extended yield curve discussion: "what a flattening of the yield curve might signal about economic activity going forward"
- "Some participants raised the concern that a prolonged period in which the economy operated beyond potential could give rise to... financial imbalances"
- "a number of participants thought it would be important to continue to monitor the slope of the yield curve"

**Center of gravity**: Meaningful discussion of yield curve flattening and its implications, plus some concern about financial imbalances from running above potential. More than passing reference but not a dominant theme.

**Minority views**: None; yield curve discussion was broadly shared.

---

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 72 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "it would likely be appropriate to continue gradually raising the target range... to a setting that was at or somewhat above their estimates of its longer-run level by 2019 or 2020"
- "many noted that... the federal funds rate could be at or above their estimates of its neutral level sometime next year"
- "a number of them noted that it might soon be appropriate to modify the language... indicating that 'the stance of monetary policy remains accommodative'"

**Center of gravity**: Clear forward signal of continued gradual tightening, with rates expected to reach or exceed neutral by 2019-2020. Discussion of removing "accommodative" language reinforces the hawkish forward lean.

**Minority views**: A few participants emphasized inflation expectations still not firmly anchored, suggesting slightly less aggressive forward path.

---

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 37 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants generally viewed the risks to the economic outlook as roughly balanced"
- "Most participants noted that uncertainty and risks associated with trade policy had intensified"
- "Many participants saw potential downside risks to economic growth and inflation associated with political and economic developments in Europe and some EMEs"

**Center of gravity**: Overall outlook risks are seen as balanced, but trade policy is a notably intensifying source of uncertainty. Somewhat above normal uncertainty levels due to trade policy, but not dramatically elevated.

**Minority views**: None; the characterization of balanced risks with trade concerns was broadly shared.
