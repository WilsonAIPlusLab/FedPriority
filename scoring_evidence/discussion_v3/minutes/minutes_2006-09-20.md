# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2006-09-20

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format — I will score only participant-attributed language ("participants noted," "members agreed," "many," "several," etc.) and ignore staff-attributable content.

**Era context**: September 2006. The Fed had been on a tightening cycle since June 2004, raising the funds rate from 1% to 5.25%. Housing was cooling noticeably. Core inflation was running above comfort level. The pause in rate hikes began in August 2006. This meeting is the second consecutive hold at 5.25%.

**Quantifier distribution**: "nearly all" (hold rates), "most" (inflation to decline gradually), "many" (concerned about inflation; growth near potential; labor shortages), "several" (worried expectations could rise), "some" (upside risks from compensation; potential output growth solid), "a few" — not heavily used. The document uses "considerable uncertainty" and "substantial uncertainty" repeatedly.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as ABOVE target. "Many meeting participants emphasized that they continued to be quite concerned about the outlook for inflation." "Recent rates of core inflation, if they persisted, were seen as higher than consistent with price stability." "Most participants thought... the uncertainties around that forecast were skewed toward higher rather than lower inflation rates." Very recent data suggested "some improvement" and energy prices had declined, providing partial offset. But the center of gravity is clearly elevated concern. Direction: above midpoint. Magnitude: "quite concerned," "undesirably high" — this is clearly above target, maybe 15-20 points above midpoint. Score ~65. Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says elevated, concerned, above price stability. Matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as moderating but still near potential. "Economic expansion would probably track close to the rate of growth of the economy's potential next year and in 2008." "Elevated rates of resource utilization." Currently growth is moderating — "pace below the rate of growth of potential output in the near term before strengthening." The housing correction is pulling growth down, but resource utilization is described as "elevated." The bright-line rule: "elevated rates of resource utilization" implies above potential, score must be >50. But "pace below the rate of growth of potential output in the near term" suggests the gap is closing/narrowing. Net: slightly above potential but moderating. Score ~52. Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. "Elevated resource utilization" supports slightly above 50. Matches.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Many business contacts continued to experience shortages of labor and accelerating wages, particularly for certain types of professionals and skilled workers." "Steady gains in employment... roughly in line with expansion of the labor force." "Elevated rates of resource utilization." Labor shortages noted but employment growth roughly matching labor force growth. Tight but not extremely so. Score ~58. Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Shortages and accelerating wages = tight. Matches.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer expenditures "expanding moderately." Business investment "expanding at a reasonably good pace." Nonresidential construction strengthening. Housing contracting considerably but the rubric says NOT to score housing construction here. Net of consumer spending (moderate) and business investment (reasonably good) = roughly at/slightly above trend. Score ~52. Polarity check: Higher = stronger. "Moderately" expanding + "reasonably good pace" = near trend, slightly positive. Matches.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Still-supportive financial conditions." "The ready availability of funding from financial markets and institutions." This is clearly on the loose side. Score ~30. Polarity check: Higher = tighter. "Supportive" and "ready availability" = loose = low score. Matches. Anchors suggest 10-20 for 2005-06 pre-crisis ease; this might be slightly tighter than the loosest because conditions are described as "supportive" not "excessively easy." Score ~25.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Nearly all members favored keeping the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent." But "the Committee agreed that the statement should again cite such risks to inflation and explicitly reference the possibility of additional policy firming." They're holding but with a clear tightening bias. The August decision was a "particularly close call" — suggesting some wanted to hike then. Score ~60. They're pausing but leaning hawkish with explicit reference to possible further firming. Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Holding with tightening bias = moderate hawkish lean. Matches.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Nearly all members favored keeping the target." This implies perhaps 1 member did not favor holding. No formal dissent mentioned explicitly but the "nearly all" language suggests close to consensus but not unanimous. "Considerable uncertainty" and some differences in inflation outlook, but general agreement on the action. Score ~25. Broad agreement with minor differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Housing discussed extensively for its economic impact, not as a financial stability concern per se. Financial conditions mentioned as "supportive" and "ready availability of funding" — brief, not alarming. No discussion of systemic risk, spreads, or financial stress. Score ~30. Limited discussion, mentioned as context. Anchors: 40-55 for 2006-07 pre-crisis, but this meeting has relatively limited financial stability focus. Score ~30.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "The Committee agreed that the statement should again cite such risks to inflation and explicitly reference the possibility of additional policy firming." This is a clear hawkish forward signal — not committing to hike but signaling it's possible. Score ~65. Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. "Possibility of additional policy firming" = hawkish forward lean. Matches.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Substantial uncertainty attended this outlook." "Considerable uncertainty was expressed regarding the ultimate extent of the downturn in the housing sector." "The uncertainties were substantial." "Significantly more sluggish performance than anticipated could not be entirely ruled out." "The uncertainties around that forecast were skewed toward higher rather than lower inflation rates." Multiple uses of "considerable" and "substantial" uncertainty. Score ~55. This is elevated uncertainty. Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. Multiple "substantial" and "considerable" = elevated. Matches.

**Cross-dimension coherence**:
- OUTPUT_GAP (52) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (58): Both slightly above neutral, labor a bit tighter — consistent with economy near potential with some labor shortages. Reasonable.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (60) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (65): Both lean hawkish, forward guidance slightly more so given explicit mention of possible firming. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 65 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Many meeting participants emphasized that they continued to be quite concerned about the outlook for inflation."
- "Recent rates of core inflation, if they persisted, were seen as higher than consistent with price stability."
- "Most participants thought that the most likely outcome was a reduction in inflation pressures, but the anticipated decline was only gradual and the uncertainties around that forecast were skewed toward higher rather than lower inflation rates."

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants see inflation as clearly above target and are quite concerned; some very recent improvement noted but the outlook remains tilted toward upside inflation risks.

**Minority views**: Very recent data showed "some improvement" and energy price declines could help, but this is the expected path, not the current assessment. Uncertainty band reflects the acknowledged skew toward higher inflation.

---

### OUTPUT_GAP: 52 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Economic expansion would probably track close to the rate of growth of the economy's potential next year and in 2008."
- "Elevated rates of resource utilization."
- "Members generally expected economic activity to expand at a pace below the rate of growth of potential output in the near term before strengthening some over time."

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is near potential with elevated resource utilization, but growth is moderating and expected to run slightly below potential growth temporarily. Net position is just slightly above potential.

**Minority views**: Some participants noted "significantly more sluggish performance than anticipated could not be entirely ruled out," widening the uncertainty band downward.

---

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 58 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Many business contacts continued to experience shortages of labor and accelerating wages, particularly for certain types of professionals and skilled workers and, in some areas, unskilled workers."
- "Participants reported steady gains in employment in various regions, roughly in line with expansion of the labor force."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is tight with shortages and wage acceleration in specific areas, though overall employment growth is tracking labor force growth. Meaningfully above neutral.

**Minority views**: One participant highlighted slowing labor force growth ahead from baby-boom retirements; some viewed potential output growth as remaining solid.

---

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 52 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Consumer expenditures appeared to have been expanding moderately over the previous few months."
- "Business investment spending generally was seen as expanding at a reasonably good pace."
- "Nonresidential construction activity continued to strengthen."

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending moderate, business investment reasonably good, net position slightly above trend.

**Minority views**: Housing downturn spillover risk could weigh on consumption going forward; "considerable uncertainty" about that transmission.

---

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 25 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Still-supportive financial conditions."
- "The ready availability of funding from financial markets and institutions."
- "Investment spending would likely continue to be supported by... strong balance sheets and profits."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly loose/accommodative — funding readily available, conditions supportive, strong corporate balance sheets. Consistent with the pre-crisis easy financial conditions of 2006.

**Minority views**: None noted; there is no discussion of tightening financial conditions.

---

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 60 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Nearly all members favored keeping the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent at this meeting."
- "The Committee agreed that the statement should again cite such risks to inflation and explicitly reference the possibility of additional policy firming."
- "The policy decision today [was] somewhat less difficult than it was in August, when it was seen as a particularly close call."

**Center-of-gravity**: Holding rates but with an explicit hawkish tilt — retaining language about possible further firming. The August decision to pause was a "close call," suggesting some wanted to hike. Lean hawkish but not actively tightening.

**Minority views**: The "somewhat less difficult" language suggests the hawkish pressure eased slightly from August due to better data, but the tightening bias remains.

---

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 25 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Nearly all members favored keeping the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent." (implies 1 or possibly 0 formal objections, but not unanimous)
- "In August... it was seen as a particularly close call." (suggests underlying tension even if not expressed as dissent here)

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on holding, with minor differences in emphasis on inflation risks and growth outlook. "Nearly all" suggests near-consensus.

**Minority views**: Some participants emphasized upside inflation risks more strongly; the range of views on housing spillover and inflation outlook creates moderate underlying tension.

---

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 30 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Financial conditions mentioned as "still-supportive" and funding "readily available" — brief contextual references.
- Housing discussed extensively for its macroeconomic impact but not framed as a financial stability concern.
- No discussion of systemic risk, credit spreads, or financial stress.

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability is a peripheral topic — mentioned only in passing as part of the economic outlook. Housing is an economic concern, not yet a financial stability concern at this meeting.

---

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 65 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The Committee agreed that the statement should again cite such risks to inflation and explicitly reference the possibility of additional policy firming."
- "Members continued to see a substantial risk that inflation would not decline as anticipated."

**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal is clearly hawkish — explicitly referencing possible further tightening. Not committing to hike, but maintaining a tightening bias in communications.

**Minority views**: Recent better data made the decision "somewhat less difficult," suggesting a slight softening of the hawkish lean relative to August, but the forward signal remains pointed toward possible firming.

---

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Substantial uncertainty attended this outlook."
- "Considerable uncertainty was expressed regarding the ultimate extent of the downturn in the housing sector and the degree to which the slowing in housing activity... would affect consumption."
- "The uncertainties around that forecast were skewed toward higher rather than lower inflation rates."

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is elevated, with "substantial" and "considerable" used repeatedly. Two major sources: housing downturn magnitude/spillover and inflation path. Risks described as skewed.
