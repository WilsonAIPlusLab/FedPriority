# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2015-12-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized participant views). This is a segmented document with a clear "Participants' Views" section separated from staff content.

**Era context**: December 2015 -- the historic "liftoff" meeting where the FOMC raised the federal funds rate for the first time since 2006. The economy had been recovering from the GFC for several years, with unemployment declining toward its longer-run level but inflation persistently below the 2% target. The strong dollar and falling energy/commodity prices were headwinds to inflation. Global risks (China slowdown, EM stress) had been a concern but were viewed as diminishing.

**Quantifier distribution**: "almost all" (3x), "nearly all" (2x), "most" (3x), "many" (6x), "a number of" (4x), "several" (5x), "some" (6x), "a few" (3x), "a couple" (2x). The center of gravity is well-established across most dimensions with meaningful minority views on inflation risks.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target. Core PCE was 1.3%, "continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective." Energy prices falling further. "Nearly all continued to anticipate that inflation would rise to or very close to 2 percent over the medium term" but that is a forecast, not current perception. Many participants lowered near-term forecasts. "Many viewed the persistent weakness in those prices as adding uncertainty or posing important downside risks to the inflation outlook." A majority saw risks as balanced, but many remained concerned about downside risks. Several noted alternative indicators closer to 2%. Direction: clearly below target but not alarming -- "subdued" territory with confidence in medium-term return. Score should be in the 30-38 range. Polarity check: higher = more pressure. Inflation is below target with downside risks. Score ~34.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic activity was expanding moderately." "Underutilization of labor resources had diminished appreciably since early in the year." Unemployment at 5.0%, "close to most participants' estimates of its longer-run normal level." But "some others indicated that further progress in reducing labor market slack would be required." "Many members judged that additional progress would be required to reach the Committee's maximum-employment objective." This is a mixed picture: close to potential but some remaining slack. The unemployment rate is near normal but broader measures still elevated. Direction: slightly below potential, approaching full capacity. Bright-line rule: some slack language remains but near potential. Score ~47.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 5.0%, near longer-run level. "Improvement in labor market conditions had been substantial." But "some others indicated that further progress in reducing labor market slack would be required." Broader underemployment measures fell but still elevated. "Labor compensation was still increasing at a subdued rate." Wage growth just beginning to pick up. "Most participants expected that the unemployment rate would edge below their estimates of its longer-run level in the coming year." Direction: near full employment but not yet tight. Score ~48.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumer spending continued to rise at a solid rate." "Motor vehicle sales remained strong." Housing "recovering gradually." Services sector solid. But manufacturing restrained by international exposures, energy sector under significant pressure. Net exports soft. Federal spending modest boost. Overall mixed but consumer side solid. Direction: at or slightly above trend. Score ~52.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Financial conditions tightened modestly over the intermeeting period." High-yield bond market concerns, junk bond fund closure, widening credit spreads. But markets well-prepared for the rate hike. Direction: modestly tighter than neutral. Score ~55.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: This is the liftoff meeting. "Almost all participants agreed that the improvements... now satisfied the Committee's criteria for beginning the policy normalization process." They raised rates 25bp. But "participants saw several reasons why a gradual removal of policy accommodation would likely be appropriate." "Some members said that their decision to raise the target range was a close call." Emphasis on gradual, accommodative stance remaining. Direction: moderate hawkish -- they hiked but with strong gradualism language. The anchor says Dec 2015 liftoff ~60-65. Score ~62.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Almost all participants agreed" on liftoff. "Some members said that their decision... was a close call." "Many members judged that additional progress would be required." There are shades of emphasis differences but broad agreement on the action. No formal dissents mentioned. The anchor for Dec 2015 liftoff is 25-35. Score ~28.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Several participants commented on leveraged finance correction, high-yield bond market concerns, junk bond fund suspension. "A few participants also indicated that significant risks to financial stability, should they emerge, could alter their view of the appropriate policy path." Moderate but not dominant discussion. Score ~35.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Strong emphasis on "gradual" normalization. "The stance of policy would remain accommodative." Neutral rate near zero. Asymmetric risk at lower bound argued for caution. "Avoid appearing to commit to any specific pace of adjustments." But the direction is clearly toward further tightening. Path could be shallower or steeper depending on data. Direction: mild tightening bias with heavy data-dependence and gradualism. Score ~63.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The outlook for inflation was subject to considerable uncertainty." Many viewed persistent weakness as "adding uncertainty or posing important downside risks." Inflation expectations potentially moving lower. But most saw balanced risks for economic activity. Some members called the rate hike "a close call, particularly given the uncertainty about inflation dynamics." Direction: somewhat elevated uncertainty, concentrated on inflation. Score ~42.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (47) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (48): Both near 50 but slightly below -- consistent. Economy near potential with residual slack.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (62) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (63): Both lean moderately hawkish -- consistent. Liftoff with gradual forward path.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 34 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective, partly reflecting declines in energy prices and prices of non-energy imports."
- "The 12-month change in the core PCE price index was 1.3 percent in October."
- "A majority of participants saw the risks to the outlook for inflation as balanced" but "many participants remained concerned about downside risks attending the outlook for inflation."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Inflation was clearly running below the 2% target at 1.3% core PCE, with further downward pressure from energy and dollar appreciation, though nearly all participants expected a return to 2% over the medium term.

**Minority views**: Several noted alternative inflation indicators near or above 2%, pulling somewhat higher. Some expressed concern inflation expectations may have moved lower, pulling the uncertainty band downward.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 47 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity was expanding moderately and confirming that underutilization of labor resources had diminished appreciably since early in the year."
- "The unemployment rate, at 5.0 percent in November, was... close to most participants' estimates of its longer-run normal level."
- "Many members judged that additional progress would be required to reach the Committee's maximum-employment objective."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy was approaching potential, with unemployment near its longer-run level, but with residual slack in broader labor measures keeping it slightly below full capacity.

**Minority views**: Some participants stressed remaining underutilization across broader dimensions; others were more sanguine that the gap had essentially closed.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 48 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The unemployment rate, at 5.0 percent in November... close to most participants' estimates of its longer-run normal level."
- "Some others indicated that further progress in reducing labor market slack would be required before conditions would be consistent with the Committee's objective of maximum employment."
- "Labor compensation was still increasing at a subdued rate."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The labor market was near but not quite at full employment, with the headline unemployment rate at its estimated longer-run level but broader measures still showing some slack and wage growth still subdued.

**Minority views**: Some emphasized remaining broader slack (part-time for economic reasons, labor force dropouts). Most expected unemployment to edge below the longer-run level soon.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 52 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending continued to rise at a solid rate in recent months; retail sales picked up... and motor vehicle sales remained strong."
- "Business activity was solid outside of sectors adversely affected by low energy prices and weak exports."
- "Manufacturing activity overall continued to be restrained by weakness in industries with significant international exposures."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumer spending was solid and the services sector was strong, but manufacturing weakness from the strong dollar and energy price declines, along with soft net exports, kept the overall assessment at roughly trend pace.

**Minority views**: Energy sector and export-oriented manufacturing were notably weak, dragging the overall assessment down from an otherwise firm consumer picture.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 55 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Financial conditions tightened modestly over the intermeeting period."
- "Concerns among investors about the high-yield bond market increased notably... after an open-ended mutual fund specializing in junk bonds suspended redemptions and closed."
- "Several participants commented that markets for leveraged finance had been correcting since midyear."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions tightened modestly, with notable stress emerging in the high-yield and leveraged finance segments, though broader conditions remained relatively orderly with the rate hike well-anticipated.

**Minority views**: The high-yield stress was concentrated in energy-related credits; broader financial conditions were not severely strained.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Almost all participants agreed that the improvements that had occurred in the labor market and their confidence in a return of inflation to 2 percent over the medium term now satisfied the Committee's criteria for beginning the policy normalization process."
- "Participants saw several reasons why a gradual removal of policy accommodation would likely be appropriate."
- "Some members said that their decision to raise the target range was a close call, particularly given the uncertainty about inflation dynamics."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Near-consensus to raise rates 25bp for liftoff, but with strong emphasis on gradualism, accommodative remaining stance, and close-call language from some members, placing this in the moderate hawkish range.

**Minority views**: Some members viewed it as a close call; a few emphasized financial stability risks could alter the path.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 28 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Almost all participants agreed that the improvements... now satisfied the Committee's criteria for beginning the policy normalization process."
- "Some members said that their decision to raise the target range was a close call."
- Various emphasis differences on inflation risks and remaining slack, but no formal dissents mentioned.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Broad agreement on the liftoff decision with no apparent formal dissents, though some members expressed reservations about the close-call nature of the decision and inflation uncertainty.

**Minority views**: "Close call" language and differing views on remaining labor slack create modest but not significant disagreement.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 35 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Several participants commented that markets for leveraged finance had been correcting since midyear."
- "Concerns among investors about the high-yield bond market increased notably."
- "A few participants also indicated that significant risks to financial stability, should they emerge, could alter their view of the appropriate policy path."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial stability received limited but meaningful discussion, focused on the high-yield bond market correction and leveraged finance, with a few flagging it as a potential policy consideration.

**Minority views**: Most participants did not dwell on financial stability as a primary concern.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 63 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants saw several reasons why a gradual removal of policy accommodation would likely be appropriate."
- "The neutral short-term real interest rate was currently close to zero and was expected to rise only slowly."
- "They stressed the importance of communicating clearly that the future policy path could become shallower if the economic expansion weakened."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal pointed to further gradual tightening, but with heavy emphasis on data dependence, a low neutral rate, and asymmetric risks favoring caution -- a mild tightening bias rather than a commitment to aggressive normalization.

**Minority views**: A few emphasized that significant financial stability risks could alter the path; the overall signal was clearly data-dependent.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 42 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The outlook for inflation was subject to considerable uncertainty."
- "Many viewed the persistent weakness in those prices as adding uncertainty or posing important downside risks to the inflation outlook."
- "Some members said that their decision to raise the target range was a close call, particularly given the uncertainty about inflation dynamics."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Uncertainty was somewhat elevated, concentrated particularly around the inflation outlook and inflation expectations, though most saw balanced risks for economic activity and the labor market.

**Minority views**: Some expressed concerns about inflation expectations potentially moving lower, adding to uncertainty; most were more confident about the growth outlook.
