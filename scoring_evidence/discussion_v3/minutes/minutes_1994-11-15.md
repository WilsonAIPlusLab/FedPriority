# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1994-11-15 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format -- staff and participant content mixed. I will score only portions with participant language cues ("members commented," "members agreed," "members noted," etc.).

**Era context**: November 1994 -- mid-cycle tightening campaign. The Fed had been raising rates since February 1994. At this meeting, the Committee delivered an unusually large 75bp rate hike. The economy was expanding strongly, with capacity utilization at high levels and inflation pressures building in the pipeline (materials, labor shortages) even though finished goods inflation had not yet accelerated markedly.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all the members agreed" (on unacceptable inflation risk), "a majority" (preferred 75bp), "other members" (preferred 50bp but acquiesced), "some members," "a few," "one member." The vote was unanimous (12-0) on the policy directive.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional assessment:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as above the midpoint. Current finished goods/services inflation is "not trending higher," BUT there is "considerable potential for higher inflation," "rising prices of many materials," "increasing reports of labor shortages," "inflationary expectations might be in the process of worsening." Pipeline pressures are clearly building. This is not yet realized high inflation but strong forward-looking concern. Direction: above 50, moderately so. "Considerable potential" and "rising risks" suggest 58-65 range.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as clearly above potential. "Growth in aggregate demand at a pace appreciably above that of the economy's long-run potential," "numerous industries now operating at or beyond historic, long-run capacity levels," "utilization of labor and other producer resources was already at, if not above, sustainable full employment levels." Bright-line rule: above potential language means > 50. The language is strong -- "appreciably above," "at or beyond historic capacity." Direction: 62-68.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Growing difficulties in hiring and retaining some types of labor," "increasing number of reports of labor shortages," "utilization of labor... at, if not above, sustainable full employment levels." This is tight -- beyond full employment. Direction: above 50, in the 58-65 range.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending is "strong," business contacts "expressing considerable optimism," business fixed investment has "strong growth," "favorable profit margins," inventory investment "robust." Nearly every component is described as strong. Direction: clearly above trend, 62-68.

5. **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Despite higher interest rates, "financial conditions generally remained conducive to further business expansion," "lending constraints... had given way to increasingly accommodative loan policies," "ready access to market sources of financing," "easing of non-price credit terms by depository institutions," "ample availability of funds in debt and equity markets." Conditions are loose/accommodative despite rate hikes. Direction: below 50, in the 25-32 range.

6. **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All the members agreed that the current stance of monetary policy presented unacceptable risks of embedding higher inflation." A majority endorsed 75bp. Even dissenters (who preferred 50bp) "indicated that they could accept the degree of restraint preferred by the majority." Unanimous vote for a 75bp hike is strongly hawkish. Direction: 75-82.

7. **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Unanimous vote (12-0). However, "other members" preferred 50bp but acquiesced. One member wanted asymmetric directive toward restraint. The differences are about degree, not direction. All agreed tightening was needed. This is "broad agreement with minor differences about pace." Direction: 25-32.

8. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Brief mention of "unsettling effect on financial markets" and that a big move "might contribute to market stability." Also mentions of FX intervention. Financial conditions discussed in context of being accommodative. This is contextual, not a primary focus. Direction: 15-22 (consistent with 1993-96 era anchor).

9. **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "A majority of the members favored associating the more substantial policy adjustment with a symmetric intermeeting instruction." They signal a pause: "a further policy action was not likely to be called for over the near term." One member said "especially important to follow a steady policy course for some period of time." But the big hike itself signals hawkishness. The forward signal is data-dependent/neutral after a big hawkish action. Direction: 58-65 (mildly hawkish -- they just did a big hike, signaling they may pause but are not done if needed).

10. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The reasons for that outcome remained unclear," "the members acknowledged the difficulty of judging the precise degree of monetary restraint," "how much, if any, additional monetary restraint might be needed... would depend on further developments," inflationary expectations were "very difficult to gauge." Moderate-to-somewhat-elevated uncertainty. Direction: 38-45.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (~65) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (~62): Both above 50, consistent -- economy running hot.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (~78) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (~62): HAWKISH is higher because the current action is very hawkish (75bp), while forward guidance signals a pause/symmetric. This divergence is explained and appropriate.

### Polarity checks:
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (28): Higher = tighter. Text says conditions are loose/accommodative. Low score is correct.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (62): Higher = more pressure. Pipeline pressures building, concern about worsening. Score above 50 is correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 62 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "members saw considerable potential for higher inflation if growth in demand and output continued at an unsustainable pace"
- "They noted indications of greater inflation pressures, especially in the rising prices of many materials used in the production process and the increasing number of reports of labor shortages"
- "at least some modest worsening of inflation seemed quite likely over the quarters immediately ahead"
- "inflationary expectations might be in the process of worsening"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants see current finished goods inflation as not yet elevated, but strong pipeline pressures (materials, labor shortages) and forward-looking concern about worsening inflation place this clearly above the midpoint. Not yet at "substantially above target" because realized inflation in final goods has not accelerated.

**Minority views**: One member suggested a cyclical rise in inflation "would not necessarily augur a permanent uptick" -- slightly less alarmed, but still acknowledged the rise.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 65 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "considerably greater strength in the business expansion than they had anticipated... numerous industries now operating at or beyond historic, long-run capacity levels"
- "persisting growth in aggregate demand at a pace appreciably above that of the economy's long-run potential"
- "utilization of labor and other producer resources was already at, if not above, sustainable full employment levels"

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is clearly running above potential with broad-based strength. "Appreciably above" and "at or beyond historic capacity" are strong language pushing this into the 60s.

**Minority views**: None noted -- this was a consensus assessment.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "growing difficulties in hiring and retaining some types of labor"
- "increasing number of reports of labor shortages"
- "utilization of labor and other producer resources was already at, if not above, sustainable full employment levels"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is tight, at or beyond full employment, with reports of shortages in some categories. Not yet at "severe shortages" or "unsustainable wage growth" -- overall wage inflation "did not appear to be trending higher" -- so tightness is real but not extreme.

**Minority views**: None -- broadly shared assessment.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 65 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "current strength of consumer spending... business contacts were expressing considerable optimism about the prospects for retail sales"
- "Expanding sales and favorable profit margins were fostering strong growth in business fixed investment"
- "inventory investment remained substantial... continuing at a robust pace"
- "few signs that growth in aggregate demand might be moderating toward a more sustainable pace"

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending strong, business investment strong, inventories robust, housing surprisingly resilient. Virtually every spending component is described as above-trend. "Strong" and "robust" language across the board places this well above the midpoint.

**Minority views**: Members expected some future moderation but saw no evidence of it yet.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 28 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "despite higher interest rates financial conditions generally remained conducive to further business expansion"
- "lending constraints that had tended to retard the expansion earlier seemed to have given way to increasingly accommodative loan policies by depository institutions and ready access to market sources of financing"
- "easing of non-price credit terms by depository institutions, the ample availability of funds in debt and equity markets"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly loose/accommodative despite the rate hiking cycle. Members explicitly noted this as a puzzle and a reason for further tightening. This is in the "loose" to "somewhat loose" range.

**Minority views**: None -- this was a consensus observation.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 78 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "all the members agreed that the current stance of monetary policy presented unacceptable risks of embedding higher inflation in the economy"
- "A majority of the members believed that an unusually sizable firming of monetary policy was desirable at this time"
- "they endorsed a proposal to tighten reserve conditions in line with a 3/4 percentage point increase in the discount rate"
- Unanimous 12-0 vote

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous agreement that more restriction was needed, with a majority favoring an aggressive 75bp move. Even those preferring 50bp acquiesced. This is strong hawkish consensus for continued tightening with an unusually large action.

**Minority views**: "Other members" preferred 50bp, citing risk of "overly restrictive course" -- but they accepted the majority view.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 28 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Vote: 12-0, unanimous
- "Views differed to some extent, however, regarding the degree of additional restraint"
- "Other members indicated that they preferred a less forceful policy move... Despite their reservations, these members indicated that they could accept the degree of restraint preferred by the majority"
- One member wanted asymmetric directive toward restraint

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous vote but with acknowledged differences about magnitude (75bp vs 50bp) and directive (symmetric vs asymmetric). All agreed on direction. This is "broad agreement with minor emphasis differences."

**Minority views**: The 50bp camp and the asymmetric-directive advocate add some range.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 18 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "recent developments were having an unsettling effect on financial markets, and a tightening move of this magnitude might contribute to market stability"
- "the action would tend to reinforce the recent intervention in the foreign exchange markets"
- Discussion of FX swap lines (procedural)

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability receives passing mentions -- market dislocation as a reason for a decisive action, FX intervention discussed procedurally. Not a primary focus of the economic or policy discussion. Consistent with mid-1990s low emphasis.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 62 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "a majority of the members favored associating the more substantial policy adjustment with a symmetric intermeeting instruction"
- "a further policy action was not likely to be called for over the near term"
- "one member expressed the view that the unusually large move made it especially important to follow a steady policy course for some period of time"
- "another member indicated a preference for an asymmetric directive toward restraint because... the likely need... for further monetary restraint"

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is mildly hawkish -- they just delivered a large hike and the base case is a pause, but the door is open to more tightening. The symmetric directive signals data dependence rather than a strong commitment to further tightening. One member explicitly wanted a tightening bias.

**Minority views**: One member wanted asymmetric toward restraint (more hawkish forward signal).

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 42 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The reasons for that outcome remained unclear" (referring to why the economy was stronger than expected)
- "the members acknowledged the difficulty of judging the precise degree of monetary restraint that would be needed"
- "To what extent such expectations would become more pervasive... was very difficult to gauge at this point"
- "The Committee could not prejudge how much, if any, additional monetary restraint might be needed in the future"

**Center-of-gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty, primarily about the appropriate degree of restraint and about inflation dynamics. Members are surprised by the economy's resilience and uncertain about the lag effects of prior tightening. Not extreme uncertainty, but more than routine caveats.
