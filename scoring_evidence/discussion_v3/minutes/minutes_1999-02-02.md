# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1999-02-02 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is **minutes** (synthesized summary of participant views). It is an unsegmented pre-2009 minutes document, so I must score only portions with participant language cues ("members noted," "members referred," "many agreed," etc.) and ignore staff-attributable content.

### Era Context
February 1999: Late-1990s expansion/boom. The U.S. economy was growing strongly with very tight labor markets and remarkably low inflation (the "New Economy" period). The Fed had cut rates three times in fall 1998 in response to LTCM/global financial turmoil, which had since calmed. Brazil was experiencing financial crisis. Productivity growth was surging.

### Quantifier Distribution
- "members" (general/all): referred to continuing exceptional performance; saw few signs of moderation; generally anticipated some pickup in inflation; generally agreed further tightening of labor would push up costs
- "most": continued to anticipate substantial slowing
- "many": agreed upside risk greater than anticipated; referred to Brazil problems
- "a number": referred to wide uncertainty in inflation forecasts
- "some": expressed concern about rapid money growth; moderation in housing starts likely
- "all": endorsed monetary aggregate ranges

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants perceive inflation as BELOW midpoint. Key: "quite low inflation despite very high...output and employment," "no evidence of accelerating price inflation despite...very tight labor markets," "persistence of very low inflation." However, members "generally anticipated some pickup in inflation, though to a still relatively low rate." Some concern about further tightening of labor markets creating pressure, and "somewhat less favorable inflation performance" expected. Current inflation is low but there is mild forward concern. This is in the 35-42 range -- below target, slack keeping prices in check via structural factors (productivity, global competition), though there is awareness that this is a puzzle. Score: ~38.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants clearly see the economy as ABOVE potential. "Very high and rapidly rising levels of overall output and employment," "exceptional economic performance," "few signs that the economic expansion had moderated to a more sustainable rate." They project slowing "to a pace close to or somewhat above that of the economy's long-run potential." The economy is currently running above potential. Per the bright-line rule, must be >50. The language is quite strong -- "very high" levels, "exceptional." Score: ~60.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Very tight. "Very tight labor markets across most of the nation," "very low rates of unemployment," "current degree of tautness in labor markets," unemployment rate expected to stay around 4.25-4.5%. This is clearly well above NAIRU. Anchors suggest 65-75 for 1999-2000. Score: ~68.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Above trend but expected to moderate. "Robust domestic demand clearly had offset weakness in net exports by a large margin," "consumer spending had exhibited considerable vigor," "business capital spending also was expected to moderate...but growth...would continue to exceed that of overall spending," housing "impressive strength." Despite expected moderation, current levels are strong. Score: ~62.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Conditions are described as somewhat loose/supportive. "Supportive credit conditions," "low mortgage interest rates," the earlier financial turmoil had calmed ("abatement of market turmoil"). The earlier LTCM crisis had tightened conditions, but by Feb 1999, markets had calmed. Members reference "calming of financial markets." Score: ~32 (somewhat loose).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The committee held rates steady. The text focuses on monitoring and the outlook for slowing. There is no discussion of imminent rate changes. Members note upside risks but also downside (foreign weakness, Brazil). "Some members also expressed concern that rapid money growth...would suggest that monetary policy was too accommodative." The lean is mildly hawkish -- they are watchful of inflation risks and some worry policy is too easy, but the center of gravity is hold-and-watch. Score: ~53.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All the members endorsed" the monetary aggregate ranges. The text shows broad agreement on the outlook with minor differences -- most anticipated slowing, many agreed upside risk was greater, some expressed concern about money growth. No formal dissents mentioned, no sharp disagreements. Score: ~20.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Brazil crisis is discussed by "many members" as a risk. Earlier market turmoil referenced as having calmed. Stock market wealth effects discussed. But financial stability is not a dominant theme -- it's mentioned as context/risk factor, not a central concern. Score: ~30.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: No explicit forward guidance. The tone is watchful/data-dependent. Members anticipate slowing but note upside risks. Some worry policy may be too accommodative. The forward lean is mildly hawkish -- if the economy doesn't slow as expected, tightening may be needed. Score: ~55.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "A number referred...to the experience of recent years, which suggested that the inflation process was not well understood and that inflation forecasts were subject to a wide range of uncertainty." "Substantial uncertainty still surrounded any projection of monetary expansion." "The course of underlying inflation pressures was more difficult to gauge." "Uncertainty about evolving relationships among economic activity, productivity growth, and wages." This is somewhat elevated uncertainty. Score: ~45.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (60) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (68): Both above 50, consistent with late-90s boom. Labor is tighter than output gap because the text emphasizes labor tightness more strongly ("very tight" vs. "above potential").
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (53) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (55): Both mildly hawkish, consistent. The committee is on hold but watching for signs that tightening may be needed.

### Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (38): Higher = more pressure. Evidence says low inflation. 38 < 50. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP (60): Higher = more above potential. Evidence says above potential. 60 > 50. Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS (68): Higher = tighter. Evidence says very tight. 68 > 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (32): Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence says supportive/loose. 32 < 50. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 38 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "continuing indications of an exceptional economic performance that was characterized by the persistence of quite low inflation despite very high and rapidly rising levels of overall output and employment"
- "members saw no evidence of accelerating price inflation despite high levels of business activity and very tight labor markets across most of the nation"
- "members generally anticipated some pickup in inflation, though to a still relatively low rate"
- "a somewhat less favorable inflation performance was viewed as likely over the year ahead"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Members see current inflation as remarkably low given the strength of the economy, attributing it to productivity gains and competitive pressures, but expect a modest pickup ahead as temporary favorable factors wane.

**Minority views:** "Some members also expressed concern that rapid money growth...would suggest that monetary policy was too accommodative to contain inflation pressures" -- this pushes the upper uncertainty band slightly higher.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 60 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "very high and rapidly rising levels of overall output and employment"
- "few signs that the economic expansion had moderated to a more sustainable rate"
- "most continued to anticipate substantial slowing over the year ahead to a pace close to or somewhat above that of the economy's long-run potential"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The economy is clearly operating above potential with very strong output, though members expect growth to slow toward potential over the year. The current position is firmly above trend.

**Minority views:** The text notes "greater upside risk" than anticipated, with some members seeing even stronger above-potential performance. Downside risk from foreign economies provides the lower bound.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 68 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "very tight labor markets across most of the nation"
- "very low rates of unemployment" with projections of 4.25-4.5% unemployment
- "the current degree of tautness in labor markets"
- "productivity gains had essentially matched increases in labor costs for nonfinancial corporations over the past year"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Labor markets are very tight nationally, with unemployment well below historical norms, though remarkably, wage pressures have been largely offset by productivity gains.

**Minority views:** None notable -- broad agreement on the tightness assessment.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 62 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Robust domestic demand clearly had offset weakness in net exports by a large margin"
- "Consumer spending had exhibited considerable vigor during the recent holiday season"
- "business plans continued to call for substantial outlays for business equipment"
- "Housing activity had continued to display impressive strength"
- But: "Growth in business capital spending also was expected to moderate"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Consumer spending, business investment, and housing are all running above trend, though moderation is expected across all categories. Current strength is well above normal.

**Minority views:** The expected moderation creates some range, but all members agree current levels are robust.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 32 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "supportive credit conditions"
- "low mortgage interest rates"
- "calming of financial markets" after the 1998 turmoil
- "abatement of market turmoil and positive business and consumer sentiment"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions are described as supportive and accommodative, with the earlier LTCM-related tightening having receded. Credit is freely available and mortgage rates are low.

**Minority views:** Some concern about financial instability in Brazil and potential contagion provides upside uncertainty.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 53 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "members generally agreed that if labor markets continued to tighten, cost and price pressures would begin to pick up"
- "Some members also expressed concern that rapid money growth, should it persist, would suggest that monetary policy was too accommodative"
- Members also note downside risks: "potentially greater weakness in foreign economies"
- No discussion of imminent rate action in either direction

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The committee is on hold with a very mild hawkish lean. Most members see the need to monitor the economy's failure to slow, with some explicitly worried about accommodative policy, but foreign risks and low inflation keep the center near neutral.

**Minority views:** "Some" see policy as potentially too accommodative (more hawkish); foreign risks provide a dovish counterweight.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 20 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "All the members endorsed a proposal to adopt the growth ranges"
- Broad agreement on the economic outlook with minor emphasis differences
- No formal dissents mentioned; no sharp disagreements

**Center-of-gravity summary:** There is broad agreement on the outlook and policy stance, with only minor differences in emphasis regarding upside risk and money growth concerns.

**Minority views:** None rising to the level of formal disagreement.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 30 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "many members referred in particular to the problems facing Brazil and the risk that further financial and economic instability in that nation would spread to other Latin American countries"
- References to earlier "turbulent behavior of financial markets" and its calming
- Stock market wealth effects discussed as a driver of consumption

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial stability receives limited but meaningful discussion, primarily regarding Brazil contagion risk and the aftermath of 1998 market turmoil. It is context rather than a central focus.

**Minority views:** None notable.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 55 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "members generally agreed that if labor markets continued to tighten, cost and price pressures would begin to pick up"
- "Some members also expressed concern that rapid money growth...would suggest that monetary policy was too accommodative"
- "most continued to anticipate substantial slowing over the year ahead"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The forward signal is mildly hawkish -- members are on alert that continued economic strength could require a policy response, but this is conditional on the economy not slowing as expected. No firm directional commitment.

**Minority views:** The conditional nature of the hawkish lean and the foreign downside risks keep the forward tone near neutral.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 45 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "inflation forecasts were subject to a wide range of uncertainty"
- "the inflation process was not well understood"
- "The course of underlying inflation pressures was more difficult to gauge"
- "substantial uncertainty still surrounded any projection of monetary expansion"
- "uncertainty about evolving relationships among economic activity, productivity growth, and wages"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Uncertainty is somewhat elevated, particularly around the inflation-output relationship and the sustainability of productivity-driven non-inflationary growth. Multiple members explicitly reference uncertainty about the inflation process.

**Minority views:** None -- the uncertainty is widely shared.
