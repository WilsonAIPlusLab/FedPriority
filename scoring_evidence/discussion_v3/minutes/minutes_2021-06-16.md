# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2021-06-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is **minutes** (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented format with clear participant discussion section beginning at "Participants' Views on Current Economic Conditions and the Economic Outlook." Quantifier language is used throughout (e.g., "most," "many," "several," "some," "a few," "various").

### Era Context
June 2021: The economy is in a rapid recovery from COVID-19. Vaccinations are progressing, the economy is reopening, and fiscal stimulus is substantial. Inflation has surprised to the upside (PCE at 3.6% in April), but the dominant narrative is still "transitory." The fed funds rate is at 0-0.25%, and the Fed is continuing $120B/month in asset purchases. The labor market is improving rapidly but unemployment is still elevated at 5.8%. Supply disruptions and labor shortages are prominent themes.

### Quantifier Distribution
- "All/unanimous": members agreed on rate, members reaffirmed framework
- "Most": saw largest inflation contributors from supply bottlenecks; noted service sector rebounding; noted benefits of bank counterparties for SRF
- "Many": commented on accommodative financial conditions supporting spending; pointed to job openings; judged labor shortages putting upward pressure on wages; remarked economy still far from maximum employment
- "Several": expected supply chain limitations to pressure prices into next year; highlighted housing valuation pressures; emphasized patience on assessment; saw benefits of reducing MBS purchases faster
- "Some": noted disruptions complicated assessment; saw less clear signal from data; commented on trimmed mean stability
- "A few": mentioned conditions for forward guidance met earlier than projected
- "Various": expected conditions for taper to be met earlier than anticipated

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as clearly above target. The actual rise was "larger than anticipated" with PCE at 3.6%. "Most participants observed that the largest contributors to the rise in measured inflation were sectors affected by supply bottlenecks." However, participants "generally expected inflation to ease as the effect of these transitory factors dissipated." A "substantial majority" judged inflation risks tilted to the upside. The transitory framing does NOT reduce the score per the rubric -- I score the perceived level NOW. Direction: above midpoint. The language is "elevated" and "larger than anticipated" but the dominant narrative is still transitory with bottleneck-driven. This places it in the 60-68 range. The anchor for 2021 Q3-Q4 is 65-72. This is June 2021, slightly earlier, so 63.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says inflation elevated and above expectations. Score above 50. Matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic activity was expanding at a historically rapid pace." A "vast majority" revised up GDP growth projections. But "supply disruptions and labor shortages" were "constraining the expansion." The economy is clearly recovering strongly but participants also note it is "still far from achieving the Committee's broad-based and inclusive maximum-employment goal." The economy is growing fast but hasn't yet closed the gap fully -- unemployment at 5.8% is still elevated. The language "historically rapid pace" of expansion suggests above-trend growth, but the level may not yet be above potential. The recovery is rapid, demand is outpacing supply in many areas, but slack remains in labor. I'd place this at 50-54 -- growth is at or slightly above potential pace, with demand hitting supply constraints, but the output gap from COVID hasn't fully closed. Score: 52.
- Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Mixed signals -- rapid growth but labor slack remains. Score near 50 is appropriate.
- Bright-line check: There is language about supply constraints binding and demand outpacing supply in some sectors, but also "far from maximum employment." Score near 50 with slight above-potential lean is consistent.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Complex picture. "Many participants remarked that the economy was still far from achieving the Committee's broad-based and inclusive maximum-employment goal." Unemployment at 5.8%. But simultaneously, "contacts had reported having trouble hiring workers to meet demand" and "many participants judged that labor shortages were putting upward pressure on wages." This is a dual signal: the level is still below full employment (suggesting sub-50), but there are acute shortages creating tightness in specific areas. The center of gravity tilts toward recognizing significant labor supply constraints creating tight conditions in many sectors, even as aggregate unemployment remains elevated. Score: 48 -- near NAIRU level reflecting the tension between elevated unemployment and acute shortages.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence: "far from maximum employment" (loose) but "trouble hiring" and "labor shortages putting upward pressure on wages" (tight). The simultaneous slack and tightness lands near the middle. 48 fits.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Indicators of consumer spending had continued to surge." "Robust gains in consumer spending." Spending is "bolstered by... elevated level of accumulated savings and generally healthy balance sheets." Equipment investment "rising at a strong pace" though manufacturing constrained by bottlenecks. Housing "remained strong." This is clearly above trend -- "surge" language. Score: 65.
- Polarity check: Higher = stronger. "Surge" in consumer spending, "strong pace" in equipment investment. 65 fits the above-trend, robust category.
- Anchor check: 62-68 for 2021 Q3-2022 Q1 surge. 65 fits.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Overall financial conditions remained highly accommodative." "Accommodative financial conditions" are noted as supporting spending. Credit spreads at "historically tight levels." Treasury yields declined. VIX at lowest since Feb 2020. Corporate bond yields at historically low levels. This is clearly very loose. Score: 18.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says conditions are "highly accommodative" -- very loose. Low score matches.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The Committee maintained the 0-0.25% rate and $120B/month purchases. "Participants generally agreed that the economic recovery was incomplete." They expected to maintain accommodation "until those outcomes were achieved." However, "various participants mentioned that they expected the conditions for beginning to reduce the pace of asset purchases to be met somewhat earlier than they had anticipated." "A few participants mentioned that they expected the economic conditions set out in the Committee's forward guidance for the federal funds rate to be met somewhat earlier." There is a nascent taper discussion but patience is still the dominant theme. Several emphasized the Committee "should be patient." This is in the lean-dovish-to-balanced range with a modest emerging hawkish lean from some participants. The current action is fully accommodative (hold + QE), but forward discussion is tilting toward taper. Score: 42 -- still accommodative but with emerging discussion about approaching taper conditions.
- Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Maintaining max accommodation but beginning taper talk. 42 fits lean-dovish-to-balanced.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissents -- unanimous vote. But there are meaningful differences on: (1) timing of taper -- "various participants" expected conditions met earlier vs. "several" emphasized patience; (2) MBS vs. Treasury purchase pace -- "several" favored faster MBS taper vs. "several other" preferred commensurate reduction; (3) inflation outlook -- "substantial majority" saw upside inflation risks but "several other participants" saw downside risks persisting. These are notable but manageable differences in pace and emphasis, not fundamental disagreements. Score: 30.
- Anchor check: typical one-dissent meeting is 25-35. This has zero dissents but notable differences on taper timing and MBS composition. 30 fits.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: "Several participants highlighted that low interest rates were contributing to elevated house prices and that valuation pressures in housing markets might pose financial stability risks." This is the main financial stability mention in the participant discussion. The SRF discussion also relates to market functioning. There's also some discussion of financial conditions more broadly. But financial stability per se gets limited but pointed attention from "several" participants. Score: 30.
- Anchor check: 20-35 for 2013-19 typical. 30 fits for a meeting with a few pointed mentions.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is mixed. The Committee maintains outcome-based guidance (rates until max employment + inflation goals met; purchases until "substantial further progress"). "A few participants mentioned that they expected the economic conditions set out in the Committee's forward guidance for the federal funds rate to be met somewhat earlier than they had projected in March." "Various participants mentioned that they expected the conditions for beginning to reduce the pace of asset purchases to be met somewhat earlier." But "several of these participants emphasized that the Committee should be patient." The forward tone is easing-to-neutral -- still firmly accommodative guidance but with emerging signals that taper discussion is approaching. Score: 30 -- mild easing bias with data dependence, edging toward neutral.
- Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Guidance still firmly accommodative but taper nearing. 30 fits the easing lean with data dependence.
- Coherence with HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (42): The forward guidance tone (30) is slightly more dovish than the current lean (42), which makes sense -- the current lean incorporates the active taper discussion while the formal forward guidance language remains accommodative.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants judged that uncertainty around their economic projections was elevated." "The process of reopening the economy was unprecedented and likely to be uneven across sectors." "Some participants judged that supply chain disruptions and labor shortages complicated the task of assessing progress." "Several participants noted that, during the early months of the reopening, uncertainty remained too high to accurately assess how long inflation pressures will be sustained." "Several participants emphasized... that uncertainty around the economic outlook was elevated and that it was too early to draw firm conclusions." Score: 58 -- elevated uncertainty explicitly stated by participants, with specific sources identified (supply chains, labor, reopening).
- Anchor check: 55-65 for China slowdown; this is "elevated" language fitting the 46-60 range. 58 fits.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (52) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (48): These are close together, which is appropriate. The slight divergence (output slightly above, labor slightly below) reflects the unique 2021 reopening dynamics where demand was running hot but labor supply hadn't fully returned.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (42) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (30): Forward guidance is more dovish than current lean, consistent with the fact that formal guidance language hadn't changed yet while the committee was beginning to lean toward taper in discussion.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 63 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "The actual rise in inflation was larger than anticipated, with the 12-month change in the PCE price index reaching 3.6 percent in April."
- "Most participants observed that the largest contributors to the rise in measured inflation were sectors affected by supply bottlenecks or sectors where price levels were rebounding from levels depressed by the pandemic."
- "A substantial majority of participants judged that the risks to their inflation projections were tilted to the upside."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants see inflation as clearly elevated above target, driven by supply bottlenecks and surging demand, and with upside risks. The "transitory" framing moderates alarm but does not negate the acknowledged elevation.

**Minority views:** "Several other participants cautioned that downside risks to inflation remained because temporary price pressures might unwind faster than currently anticipated." Some noted trimmed mean measures "had been relatively stable at or just below 2 percent."

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 52 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Economic activity was expanding at a historically rapid pace, led by robust gains in consumer spending."
- "Participants generally saw supply disruptions and labor shortages as constraining the expansion of economic activity this year."
- "Many participants remarked... that the economy was still far from achieving the Committee's broad-based and inclusive maximum-employment goal."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The economy is growing at a historically rapid pace with demand outstripping supply in many sectors, but the overall gap from COVID has not yet fully closed. The net position is near potential with demand pressures starting to slightly exceed capacity in specific areas.

**Minority views:** The uncertainty band captures the tension between those emphasizing demand-supply mismatches (higher) and those emphasizing remaining labor slack (lower).

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 48 +/-10 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Many participants remarked... that the economy was still far from achieving the Committee's broad-based and inclusive maximum-employment goal."
- "Contacts had reported having trouble hiring workers to meet demand" and "many participants judged that labor shortages were putting upward pressure on wages, particularly in lower-wage occupations."
- "The unemployment rate edged down, on net, to 5.8 percent."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** A dual labor market -- aggregate unemployment still elevated at 5.8% with incomplete recovery, but acute shortages and hiring difficulties creating tightness in many sectors. The center of gravity sits near NAIRU, reflecting these offsetting forces.

**Minority views:** Wide band (+-10) reflects the genuine split between those emphasizing aggregate slack ("far from maximum employment") and those emphasizing acute shortages driving wage pressures.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 65 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Indicators of consumer spending had continued to surge and expected that further gains in spending would contribute significantly to the economic recovery."
- "Business equipment investment was rising at a strong pace, but growth in manufacturing activity was being restrained by production bottlenecks."
- "A majority of participants observed that housing market activity remained strong."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Consumer spending is surging with strong support from fiscal stimulus, pent-up demand, and savings. Business investment is robust in equipment though constrained in some areas. Well above trend overall.

**Minority views:** Some supply-side constraints are limiting realized investment and spending, which modestly tempers the strength.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 18 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Overall financial conditions remained highly accommodative, in part reflecting policy measures to support the economy and the flow of credit to U.S. households and businesses."
- "Several participants highlighted... that low interest rates were contributing to elevated house prices and that valuation pressures in housing markets might pose financial stability risks."
- Staff review (context): "credit spreads were little changed at historically tight levels," VIX at lowest since Feb 2020, corporate bond yields at historical lows.

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions are very loose by any measure -- highly accommodative policy, tight credit spreads, low volatility, historically low yields. The participant discussion explicitly characterizes conditions as "highly accommodative."

**Minority views:** Narrow band -- no participants characterize conditions as anything other than accommodative.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 42 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Participants generally agreed that the economic recovery was incomplete and that risks to the economic outlook remained."
- "Various participants mentioned that they expected the conditions for beginning to reduce the pace of asset purchases to be met somewhat earlier than they had anticipated at previous meetings."
- "Several of these participants emphasized that the Committee should be patient in assessing progress toward its goals and in announcing changes to its plans for asset purchases."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The committee maintains maximum accommodation (0% rates + full QE) but is beginning to actively discuss conditions for taper. The center of gravity is lean-dovish -- still accommodative but the direction of the next move is clearly toward less accommodation.

**Minority views:** "A few" already see rate-hike conditions met earlier; "several" emphasize patience. This creates a meaningful spread captured in the +-8 band.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Unanimous vote on all policy actions.
- Differences on taper timing: "various participants" expected earlier vs. "several" urged patience.
- Differences on MBS purchases: "several" favored reducing MBS more quickly vs. "several other" preferred commensurate reduction.

**Center-of-gravity summary:** No formal dissents and broad agreement on maintaining current stance, but notable differences on the pace and composition of eventual taper and on inflation risk assessment.

**Minority views:** The disagreements are about timing and emphasis rather than fundamental direction.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 30 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Several participants highlighted... that low interest rates were contributing to elevated house prices and that valuation pressures in housing markets might pose financial stability risks."
- "Several participants saw benefits to reducing the pace of these [MBS] purchases more quickly or earlier than Treasury purchases in light of valuation pressures in housing markets."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial stability receives limited but pointed attention, primarily through the lens of housing market valuations. It is not a dominant theme but is raised by "several" participants and influences the MBS composition debate.

**Minority views:** Most participants do not raise financial stability concerns directly.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 30 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Participants also reiterated that the existing outcome-based guidance implied that the paths of the federal funds rate and the balance sheet would depend on actual progress toward reaching the Committee's maximum-employment and inflation goals."
- "A few participants mentioned that they expected the economic conditions set out in the Committee's forward guidance for the federal funds rate to be met somewhat earlier than they had projected in March."
- "The Committee's standard of 'substantial further progress' was generally seen as not having yet been met, though participants expected progress to continue."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The formal forward guidance remains accommodative and outcome-based, with no imminent signal of tightening. The tone is easing-bias with data dependence, though a few participants hint that conditions could be met sooner.

**Minority views:** "A few" see conditions met earlier (more hawkish forward lean), while "several" emphasize patience and heightened uncertainty about the path.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 58 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Participants judged that uncertainty around their economic projections was elevated."
- "The process of reopening the economy was unprecedented and likely to be uneven across sectors."
- "Several participants noted that, during the early months of the reopening, uncertainty remained too high to accurately assess how long inflation pressures will be sustained."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Uncertainty is explicitly elevated. Multiple sources are cited: reopening dynamics, supply chain duration, labor supply behavior, and inflation persistence. Not extreme but clearly above normal.

**Minority views:** While all acknowledge uncertainty, the degree varies -- some see data providing clearer signals while others emphasize the unprecedented nature of the reopening.
