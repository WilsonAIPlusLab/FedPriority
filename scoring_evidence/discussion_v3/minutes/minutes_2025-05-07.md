# FOMC Meeting Scoring — May 6–7, 2025

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). The document has some OCR/column alignment issues but is largely readable. This is a post-2009 segmented document with clear participant vs. staff sections.

**Era context**: May 2025. The macroeconomic backdrop is dominated by the tariff shock from trade policy changes announced in early April 2025. Inflation has come down significantly from its 2022 peak but remains "somewhat elevated" above the 2% target. The labor market is broadly in balance. GDP declined slightly in Q1 but PDFP (a better underlying indicator) grew solidly. Massive uncertainty surrounds the effects of tariffs on both inflation and growth.

**Quantifier distribution**: The minutes use "all participants" (maintain target range), "almost all" (risk inflation could be more persistent), "most" (inflation expectations well anchored), "many" (firms planning to pass on tariff costs), "several" (various specific observations), "some" (various concerns), "a few" (persistent effects, supply chain disruptions), "a couple of" (minor observations).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as above target. "Inflation remained somewhat elevated" relative to the 2% goal. PCE was 2.3%, core PCE was 2.6%. Disinflation progress has been "uneven recently." Tariffs are expected to boost inflation further. Many participants noted firms planning to pass on tariff costs. "Almost all participants commented on the risk that inflation could prove to be more persistent than expected." This is clearly above 50 but not at the alarming levels of 2022. The language "somewhat elevated" with significant upside risks from tariffs suggests ~60-65. Direction: above midpoint. Higher = more pressure. Evidence says elevated and rising risks. Score matches.

No language says "above potential" or "below potential" currently — it's "solid pace" which suggests near potential. Score ~48-50. Direction: near potential. Higher = more above potential. Evidence says near balance currently but risks tilted down. Score matches.

Higher = tighter. Evidence says conditions tightened. Score matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 62 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Inflation remained somewhat elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent longer-run goal."
- "Many participants remarked that reports from their business contacts or surveys indicated that firms generally were planning to either partially or fully pass on tariff-related cost increases to consumers."
- "Almost all participants commented on the risk that inflation could prove to be more persistent than expected."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Inflation is perceived as modestly above target with significant upside risks from tariffs, though longer-term expectations remain mostly anchored.

**Minority views**: "Several participants highlighted factors that might help mitigate the magnitude and persistence of potential increases in inflation" — this widens the uncertainty band modestly.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 49 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Recent indicators suggested that economic activity had continued to expand at a solid pace."
- "PDFP—which comprises PCE and private fixed investment and which often provides a better signal than GDP of underlying economic momentum—posted a solid gain in the first quarter."
- Staff projected "the output gap was projected to widen significantly over the forecast period" (downside risk, but this is staff view, not participant).

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants view the economy as currently operating near potential with solid growth, though risks are tilted to the downside from tariff effects.

**Minority views**: Several participants noted forward-looking indicators of weakening (business sentiment, hiring pauses), which contributes to the uncertainty band.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 51 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants judged that labor market conditions remained broadly in balance."
- "The unemployment rate remained low and had stayed in a narrow range over the past year."
- "Several participants commented that labor market conditions were unlikely to be a source of inflationary pressure."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The labor market is assessed as near full employment/NAIRU — balanced, neither tight nor slack — with moderate downside risks from uncertainty-related hiring pauses.

**Minority views**: "Some participants noted, however, that their contacts and business survey respondents reported limiting or pausing hiring because of elevated uncertainty."

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 46 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending grew solidly in March" and "PDFP rose at a solid pace in the first quarter."
- "Participants also noted that their contacts or surveys reported sharp declines in business sentiment, and many participants remarked that those reports also revealed that many firms were pausing or delaying their capital expenditure plans."
- "Various surveys indicated a sharp deterioration in consumer sentiment."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Current hard data on spending and investment remain solid, but soft data (sentiment, surveys, business contacts) show significant deterioration, placing the net assessment slightly below trend.

**Minority views**: "A few participants observed that the strength in the balance sheets of many households could help them absorb a tariff-induced reduction in their purchasing power."

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 61 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Most borrowing costs for businesses, households, and municipalities increased notably in response to the tariff news."
- "Credit spreads widened, on net, especially for speculative-grade bonds."
- "Money market functioning had remained orderly" and markets "continued to function and were able to accommodate a surge in trading volumes despite lower measures of liquidity."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions have tightened somewhat from the tariff shock — higher borrowing costs, wider spreads, volatility — but markets remain functional and orderly, placing this at moderately tight rather than crisis-level.

**Minority views**: Some noted that conditions improved after the initial tariff shock; equity prices retraced.

- "Participants emphasized the importance of ensuring that longer-term inflation expectations remained well anchored."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Unanimous hold with a data-dependent posture. The slight hawkish tilt comes from the emphasis on inflation risks and the importance of anchored expectations, but the dual mandate concern (both inflation and employment risks rising) keeps this very close to balanced.

**Minority views**: Tension between those emphasizing inflation persistence risks and those noting downside risks to growth/employment, but no formal dissent or advocacy for action in either direction.
