# FOMC Discussion Scoring — 2025-03-19 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Segmented — participant views section is clearly delineated from staff content.

**Era context**: March 2025. The Fed is in a holding pattern after a hiking cycle that brought rates to 4.25-4.50%. Inflation has come down substantially from 2022 peaks but remains "somewhat elevated" above the 2% target. The economy continues to grow solidly. A major new factor is tariff policy uncertainty creating significant anxiety about both inflation and growth.

**Quantifier distribution**: Key quantifiers observed: "all participants" (hold rates, continue balance sheet reduction), "almost all" (risks to inflation tilted upside, supported slowing runoff), "a majority" (tariff inflationary effects potentially more persistent; federal job cuts affecting employment), "most" (business sentiment deteriorated; risk premiums low), "many" (survey deterioration; contacts pausing hiring; interest in balance sheet discussion), "several" (tariff increases broader than expected; layoff announcements; earnings forecasts downgrade; auto industry risks; contacts optimistic; no compelling case for slowing runoff), "some" (inflation data higher than expected; financial conditions tightened; tradeoffs if inflation persistent and growth weakens), "a few" (consumption supported by disposable income; repricing risk concern; agricultural sector strains).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants say inflation "eased significantly over the past two years but remained somewhat elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent longer-run goal." Some noted data "higher than they had expected." Housing services moderating but nonhousing services "remained high." Core goods inflation "had increased" partly reflecting tariff anticipation. Tariffs expected to boost inflation further. Direction: above target but not drastically so. "Somewhat elevated" = roughly 55-62 range. The tariff concerns push upward expectations but present-state language is "somewhat elevated." Score ~60.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economy that continued to grow at a solid pace." No language about "above potential" or "below potential" explicitly. "Solid pace" suggests at or modestly above trend. Consumer spending may be moderating. Business investment pausing. The economy is neither overheating nor contracting. Score ~51-53, reflecting solid growth but some emerging softness signals.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor market conditions remained broadly in balance." Unemployment "stabilized at a relatively low level." Wage growth "continued to moderate." "Broadly in balance" is textbook neutral/near-NAIRU language. Some concerns about federal layoffs and hiring pauses, but also concerns about immigration reducing labor supply. Score ~50, right at balanced.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed signals. Economy "continued to grow at a solid pace" but "some indications that consumer spending growth might be moderating from its rapid pace." Business sentiment deteriorated, firms "pausing capital spending plans." Auto industry faces tariff risks. But some contacts express optimism about regulatory changes and AI productivity. Net: slightly below recent trend due to uncertainty-driven pullbacks. Score ~45-48.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Some participants noted that financial conditions had tightened since the January meeting, reflecting lower equity prices and higher risk spreads on corporate bonds." But "most of them commented that risk premiums in bond and equity markets remained low by historical standards" and "businesses and consumers with higher credit scores continued to be able to obtain financing." Net: a modest tightening from loose levels, still on the accommodative-to-neutral side. Score ~48-52.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All participants viewed it as appropriate to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 4-1/4 to 4-1/2 percent." Policy described as "restrictive." Participants "well positioned to wait for more clarity." No one pushing for cuts or hikes right now. "Cautious approach." "Not on a preset course." Restrictive policy can be maintained if inflation elevated, eased if labor market deteriorates. This is a hold with a wait-and-see posture. Slightly hawkish lean because: (1) policy is restrictive and they're comfortable keeping it there, (2) inflation risks tilted to upside, (3) majority notes potential for persistent tariff inflation effects. Score ~55.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: All participants agreed on the hold decision. "Almost all" supported slowing runoff but "several participants did not see a compelling case for slowing the pace of runoff at this meeting" — this is a notable minority disagreement on balance sheet. "Some participants observed that the Committee may face difficult tradeoffs" — this is flagging future disagreement potential, not current. Overall broad agreement on rates with some disagreement on balance sheet pace. Score ~28.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: "Some participants noted that financial conditions had tightened." "A few participants cautioned that an abrupt repricing of risk in financial markets could exacerbate the effects of any negative shocks." Discussion of risk premiums, equity prices, credit availability. Moderate but not dominant discussion. Score ~30.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Well positioned to wait for more clarity." "Not on a preset course." "Restrictive policy could be maintained for longer if inflation were to remain elevated, and policy could be eased if labor market conditions were to deteriorate." This is data-dependent, slightly hawkish-leaning since they emphasize inflation persistence risks more than easing. Score ~55.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: This is extremely prominent. "High degree of uncertainty facing the economy." "Elevated uncertainty about government policies." "High uncertainty surrounded their economic outlooks." "Uncertainty about the net effect of an array of government policies on the economic outlook was high." "A majority of participants noted the potential for inflationary effects... to be more persistent than they projected." "Almost all participants viewing risks to inflation as tilted to the upside and risks to employment as tilted to the downside." This is clearly elevated uncertainty — pervasive, mentioned by nearly all participants, driven by tariff/trade policy. Score ~68.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (~52) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (~50): Both near neutral, consistent. Economy is "solid" but labor market is "balanced" — coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (~55) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (~55): Both lean mildly hawkish / hold-with-upside-inflation-bias. Coherent.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 60: Higher = more pressure. "Somewhat elevated" + tariff upside risks. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP 52: Higher = more above potential. "Solid pace" without explicit above/below potential language. Slightly above neutral. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS 50: Higher = tighter. Some tightening noted but still historically loose. Neutral is correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 60 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "inflation had eased significantly over the past two years but remained somewhat elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent longer-run goal"; "Some participants observed that inflation data over the first two months of this year were higher than they had expected"; "inflation in core nonhousing services remained high"; "core goods inflation had increased"; "inflation was likely to be boosted this year by the effects of higher tariffs"
- Center-of-gravity: Inflation moderating but still "somewhat elevated" with tariff-related upside risks making participants expect further near-term pressure.
- Minority views: "A couple of participants highlighted factors that might limit the inflationary effects of tariffs" (depleted excess savings, immigration reducing housing demand).

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 52 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "economy that continued to grow at a solid pace"; "consumer spending growth might be moderating from its rapid pace"; business contacts pausing capital spending; but no explicit "above/below potential" language.
- Center-of-gravity: Economy growing solidly, roughly at or just above potential, with emerging softness signals from sentiment deterioration.
- Minority views: Some see continued solid growth supported by disposable income; others see more meaningful moderation.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 50 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "labor market conditions remained broadly in balance"; "unemployment rate had stabilized at a relatively low level"; "nominal wage growth continued to moderate"; "a couple of participants noted that the continued balance in the labor market suggested that labor market conditions were unlikely to be a source of inflationary pressure."
- Center-of-gravity: Textbook balanced labor market — low unemployment but no overheating, moderating wage growth.
- Minority views: Several noted layoff announcements and federal job cut spillovers; several others worried immigration restrictions could tighten labor supply.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 46 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "economy had continued to grow at a solid pace but that there were some indications that consumer spending growth might be moderating"; "most participants commented that contacts or surveys reported increased uncertainty... and a deterioration in business sentiment, which had led many firms to pause their capital spending plans"; "recent downgrade in earnings forecasts from retailers and airline companies pointed to weaker consumer demand"
- Center-of-gravity: Spending still positive but moderating, with business investment being held back by uncertainty. Net assessment is near trend but tilting softer.
- Minority views: "A few participants noted that consumption spending could continue to be supported by solid growth in real personal disposable income."

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 50 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Some participants noted that financial conditions had tightened since the January meeting, reflecting lower equity prices and higher risk spreads on corporate bonds"; "most of them commented that risk premiums in bond and equity markets remained low by historical standards or that businesses and consumers with higher credit scores continued to be able to obtain financing."
- Center-of-gravity: Modest tightening from loose levels puts conditions roughly at neutral — some tightening but historically still not restrictive.
- Minority views: "A few participants cautioned that an abrupt repricing of risk in financial markets could exacerbate the effects of any negative shocks."

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 55 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "all participants viewed it as appropriate to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 4-1/4 to 4-1/2 percent"; "participants assessed that the Committee was well positioned to wait for more clarity on the outlook for inflation and economic activity"; "a restrictive policy could be maintained for longer if inflation were to remain elevated"; "a majority of participants noted the potential for inflationary effects arising from various factors to be more persistent than they projected."
- Center-of-gravity: Unanimous hold with a mild hawkish lean — comfortable with restrictive stance, leaning toward keeping it longer given inflation risks, but not pushing for hikes.
- Minority views: "policy could be eased if labor market conditions were to deteriorate or economic activity were to weaken" — easing option acknowledged.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 28 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: All participants agreed on rate hold. "Almost all participants supported a slowing of the pace of runoff at this meeting" but "Several participants did not see a compelling case for slowing the pace of runoff at this meeting" — a notable minority on the balance sheet question.
- Center-of-gravity: Broad agreement on rates; mild disagreement on balance sheet pace. No formal dissent indicated on rates.
- Minority views: The runoff pace disagreement and future tradeoff concerns add modest tension.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 30 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Some participants noted that financial conditions had tightened"; "risk premiums in bond and equity markets remained low by historical standards"; "a few participants cautioned that an abrupt repricing of risk in financial markets could exacerbate the effects of any negative shocks."
- Center-of-gravity: Limited but present discussion — a few participants raise financial stability concerns but it is not a focal topic.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 55 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "well positioned to wait for more clarity"; "policy decisions were not on a preset course"; "restrictive policy could be maintained for longer if inflation were to remain elevated, and policy could be eased if labor market conditions were to deteriorate"; "a majority of participants noted the potential for inflationary effects... to be more persistent than they projected."
- Center-of-gravity: Data-dependent with a mild hawkish tilt — the asymmetry in risk discussion (inflation upside vs. employment downside) leans toward maintaining restriction.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 68 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Participants generally noted the high degree of uncertainty facing the economy"; "high uncertainty surrounded their economic outlooks"; "uncertainty about the net effect of an array of government policies on the economic outlook was high, making it appropriate to take a cautious approach"; "almost all participants viewing risks to inflation as tilted to the upside and risks to employment as tilted to the downside."
- Center-of-gravity: Elevated and widespread uncertainty driven by tariff/trade policy, expressed by nearly all participants. Clearly above normal but not "uncharted territory" level.
