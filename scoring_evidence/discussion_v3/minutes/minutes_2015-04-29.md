# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2015-04-29 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented -- participant discussion section clearly separated from staff content.

**Era context**: April 2015, pre-liftoff period. The fed funds rate is at 0-1/4%. Economy in later stages of recovery from the GFC, with ongoing discussion about when to begin normalizing rates. Q1 2015 GDP came in weak (as it had in prior years). Inflation running below 2% target. This is consistent with the 2013-2019 anchor period for many dimensions.

**Quantifier distribution**: "most" (several uses: expectations of resumption, risks nearly balanced, inflation returning), "many" (labor market pace slowed, thought June unlikely for liftoff), "several" (dollar discussion), "some" (concern about spending momentum, financial stability), "a number of" (reasons for transitory weakness, dollar effects), "a few" (downside risks risen, June possible for liftoff), "one participant" (various individual views).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target. "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's longer-run objective." "Many participants noted that measures of inflation... continued to run below the Committee's longer-run objective." Partly due to energy prices and import prices. Some noted recent monthly readings "firmed a bit." Market-based inflation compensation "remained low." Most expected gradual rise toward 2% over medium term. Direction: below target but not alarmingly so. Energy prices no longer declining. This is "below target; slack keeping prices in check" territory. Score ~35. Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says below target = less pressure. 35 < 50. Matches.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**: "economic growth had slowed during the winter months, in part reflecting transitory factors." "Unexpectedly weak household expenditures and investment spending." But "most participants expected that... real economic activity would resume expansion at a moderate pace." "underutilization of labor resources was little changed." The text discusses "resource slack" and slow reduction thereof. The economy is below potential but recovering. "Most still viewed the risks to the outlook for economic growth and the labor market as nearly balanced." Score: ~40. Below potential, recovering but gap persists. Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence = below potential. 40 < 50. Matches.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "underutilization of labor resources was little changed." "Many participants judged that the pace of improvement in labor market conditions had slowed." Unemployment rate "remained steady." But there are some tightening signals: "shortages of skilled labor reported in some areas and sectors," "increase in transitions of workers to better-paying jobs," "larger wage gains... reported in some regions, although in other parts of the country wage pressures reportedly remained muted." Mixed picture but center of gravity is still below full employment with slack remaining. Score: ~40. Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says slack remains but improving. 40 < 50. Matches. Coherent with OUTPUT_GAP ~40.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "data on private spending for the first quarter had been disappointing, with unexpectedly weak household expenditures and investment spending." "Retail sales had continued to be tepid." "Business fixed investment softened." "recovery in the housing sector remained slow." "Exports contracted." But consumer fundamentals remain favorable: "low interest rates, high consumer confidence, and rising household real income." Most expected bounce-back. Current conditions are clearly below trend but expected to be temporary. Score: ~35. Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Evidence = weak spending. 35 < 50. Matches.

**5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Discussion focuses on low term premiums and possible bond market volatility risk. "policymakers highlighted possible risks related to the low level of term premiums." "Some participants noted the possibility that... term premiums could rise sharply." Low interest rates, accommodative stance still in place. Financial conditions described are loose/accommodative, not tight. Score: ~28. Somewhat loose. Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence = conditions are accommodative/loose. 28 < 50. Matches.

**6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Committee held rates at 0-1/4%. "Many participants, however, thought it unlikely that the data available in June would provide sufficient confirmation that the conditions for raising the target range... had been satisfied." "A few anticipated that the information... by the time of the June meeting would likely indicate sufficient improvement." They are discussing when to lift off, not whether to ease further. "Participants continued to judge that it would be appropriate to raise the target range... when they had seen further improvement." "A few... questioned whether the Committee was providing sufficient accommodation... and cautioned against initiating policy firming in the near future." Center of gravity: holding at zero, approaching liftoff but delayed by weak data. This is pre-liftoff, lean dovish to balanced. Anchor: 40-50 (2014-15 pre-liftoff). Score: ~45. Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence = holding, mostly not ready for June, data-dependent. Mildly dovish lean. 45 < 50. Matches.

**7. DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissents mentioned. The range of views is moderate: most agreed on holding, some differences on timing. "A few" thought June possible, "many" thought June unlikely. "Different views were expressed" on financial stability implications. One participant suggested raising inflation objective. The disagreement is about timing of liftoff and some analytical differences, not sharp divisions. Score: ~30. Broad agreement with minor differences. Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Evidence = broad agreement with timing differences. 30 is in the 16-30 range. Matches.

**8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: A dedicated paragraph discusses financial market developments and financial stability issues: term premiums, bond market volatility, high-frequency traders, broker-dealer inventories, regulatory changes. "Some" and "a couple" of participants engaged. This is a meaningful but not dominant discussion. Score: ~35. Limited to moderate discussion. Polarity check: Higher = more attention. Evidence = one substantial paragraph, several participants engaged. 35 is in 25-35 range. Matches.

**9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Forward signal is clearly about approaching normalization but not yet ready. "Most participants felt that the timing of the first increase... would appropriately be determined on a meeting-by-meeting basis." "Many participants... thought it unlikely that the data available in June would provide sufficient confirmation." "A few anticipated" June might be time. Committee retained language about raising rates "when it has seen further improvement in the labor market and is reasonably confident that inflation will move back to its 2 percent objective." This is a mild tightening lean forward: they are discussing when to hike, not whether. Anchor: 25-40 (2015 pre-liftoff). The Q1 data weakness pushed this toward the lower end. Score: ~38. Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence = approaching normalization but delayed. 38 < 50 but above 25. Matches. Note: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (45) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (38) are close; the slight divergence reflects that current stance is near-neutral hold but forward guidance was slightly more cautious due to weak Q1 data.

"Different views about the likely timing and pace of policy firming." Discussion of whether Q1 weakness is transitory or persistent -- genuine analytical uncertainty. "Estimates of such equilibrium real interest rates were highly uncertain." "A few indicated that, in their assessment, such risks had risen since the March meeting." But "most... continued to see the risks... as nearly balanced." Elevated uncertainty but not extreme. Score: ~45. Somewhat elevated. Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. Evidence = increased uncertainty, Q1 ambiguity, equilibrium rate uncertainty. 45 > 30. Matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 35 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Many participants noted that measures of inflation averaged over several months or more continued to run below the Committee's longer-run objective."
- "some participants pointed out that, by some measures, the most recent monthly inflation readings had firmed a bit"
- "Market-based measures of inflation compensation had risen slightly but remained low."

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation clearly running below the 2% target, partly due to transitory factors (energy, imports), but with recent firming. Expected to gradually return to target.

**Minority views**: One participant argued low market-based inflation compensation should concern policymakers; another suggested it had little predictive value. These differ on diagnostics but not on the basic inflation assessment.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 40 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "economic growth had slowed during the winter months, in part reflecting transitory factors"
- "underutilization of labor resources was little changed"
- "Most participants expected that, following the slowdown in the first quarter, real economic activity would resume expansion at a moderate pace"

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy below potential with slack remaining, but expected to resume moderate growth. Q1 weakness partly transitory.

**Minority views**: "A few indicated that... downside risks had risen since the March meeting." But most saw risks as nearly balanced.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 40 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "underutilization of labor resources was little changed"
- "Many participants judged that the pace of improvement in labor market conditions had slowed"
- "shortages of skilled labor reported in some areas and sectors... larger wage gains were also reported in some regions, although in other parts of the country wage pressures reportedly remained muted"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market below full employment, improvement pace slowing. Mixed signals with some sectoral tightness but overall slack remains.

**Minority views**: Sectoral tightness reports suggest some parts of the labor market approaching full employment, creating uncertainty.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 35 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "data on private spending for the first quarter had been disappointing, with unexpectedly weak household expenditures and investment spending"
- "Retail sales had continued to be tepid... Business fixed investment softened... recovery in the housing sector remained slow... Exports contracted"
- "A number of the fundamental factors that drive consumer spending remained favorable, among them low interest rates, high consumer confidence, and rising household real income"

**Center-of-gravity**: Current spending data clearly weak/below trend, though many participants attributed this partly to transitory factors and expected recovery.

**Minority views**: "Some participants expressed particular concern" that underlying consumer spending momentum may be weaker than thought.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 28 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "policymakers highlighted possible risks related to the low level of term premiums"
- "Some participants noted the possibility that... term premiums could rise sharply"
- Fed funds rate at 0-1/4%, accommodative stance maintained.

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions described as accommodative/loose, with low term premiums and supportive conditions. Concern is about potential future volatility, not current tightness.

**Minority views**: None expressing tight conditions.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 45 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Many participants, however, thought it unlikely that the data available in June would provide sufficient confirmation that the conditions for raising the target range for the federal funds rate had been satisfied"
- "A few anticipated that the information that would accrue by the time of the June meeting would likely indicate sufficient improvement"
- "a few of these participants questioned whether the Committee was providing sufficient accommodation at the present time and cautioned against initiating policy firming in the near future"

**Center-of-gravity**: Committee holding at zero with liftoff discussion active but pushed back by weak Q1 data. Lean slightly dovish: most not ready for June, a few cautioning against near-term firming.

**Minority views**: A few hawkish voices thought June could be appropriate; a few dovish voices questioned whether accommodation was sufficient.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "participants expressed a range of views about when economic conditions were likely to warrant an increase"
- "Although participants expressed different views about the likely timing and pace of policy firming, they agreed that the Committee's decision... would appropriately depend on the incoming data"
- No formal dissents mentioned; unanimous vote implied.

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on holding, with moderate disagreement about timing of liftoff. Agreement on data-dependent framework unified the committee.

**Minority views**: Differences are about pace/timing rather than fundamental policy direction.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 35 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "In their discussion of financial market developments and financial stability issues, policymakers highlighted possible risks related to the low level of term premiums"
- "A couple of participants underscored the need for a better understanding of the structure of the bond market in the current environment"
- "different views were expressed about the potential implications for financial stability of monetary policy tightening in current economic conditions"

**Center-of-gravity**: A dedicated paragraph addresses financial stability (term premiums, bond volatility, market structure), but it is a secondary topic, not dominant. "Some" and "a couple" engaged.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 38 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "most participants felt that the timing of the first increase in the target range for the federal funds rate would appropriately be determined on a meeting-by-meeting basis"
- "Many participants, however, thought it unlikely that the data available in June would provide sufficient confirmation"
- "Members agreed to retain the indication that the Committee anticipates that it will be appropriate to raise the target range... when it has seen further improvement in the labor market"

**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal is data-dependent with easing lean -- approaching normalization but Q1 weakness pushed back expectations. Not yet signaling imminent hike.

**Minority views**: "A few" thought June data might be sufficient, providing a mild hawkish pull.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 45 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants judged that recent domestic economic developments had increased uncertainty regarding the economic outlook"
- "Participants discussed whether the weakness of spending in the first quarter primarily reflected temporary factors or instead suggested a longer-lasting loss of momentum"
- "Estimates of such equilibrium real interest rates were highly uncertain"

**Center-of-gravity**: Elevated uncertainty driven by ambiguity about Q1 weakness (transitory vs. persistent), dollar effects, equilibrium rate estimates, and inflation outlook. Above normal but not extreme.

**Minority views**: Most saw risks as nearly balanced despite uncertainty, suggesting the uncertainty was acknowledged but not paralyzing.
