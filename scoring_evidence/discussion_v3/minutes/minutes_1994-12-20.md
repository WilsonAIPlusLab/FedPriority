# FOMC Discussion Scoring - 1994-12-20 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented (pre-2009), so I must carefully distinguish participant language from staff language. The text uses participant cues like "members referred to," "members noted," "several observed," "a number commented."

**Era context**: December 1994 -- the Fed had been in an aggressive tightening cycle throughout 1994, raising rates from 3% to 5.5%. The economy was growing robustly with high resource utilization, but inflation had not yet visibly accelerated. This is the meeting where the Committee paused after the 75bp hike in November.

**Quantifier distribution**: "members" (general consensus) used repeatedly; "several observed" (momentum carrying into early 1995); "a number commented" (slowing expected); "some" (financial market resilience, consumer debt concerns); "many members" (rising capacity pressures); "a majority" (unchanged policy); "a few" (preferred tightening now); "all but one" (supported the directive).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Members see the economy at or slightly above noninflationary potential, with "rising pressures on capacity." Key measures of inflation "did not display any evident uptrend at this juncture" -- so CURRENT inflation is contained. But members see the risk as clearly tilted upward: "a number of members commented that inflation probably would worsen somewhat over the near term," "numerous reports of business plans to raise prices early in the new year." The center of gravity is: inflation is currently contained but pressures are building and a modest worsening is expected. This is above midpoint but not dramatically so. Score ~55-58. Direction: somewhat above midpoint. Higher = more pressure. Evidence says building but not yet manifest. Score 56.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "robust expansion," "very high and rising levels of resource utilization," "the economy now operating at or even slightly above its noninflationary potential." This is clearly at or slightly above potential. Bright-line rule: "at or slightly above" -> must be >= 50. "Very high and rising levels of resource utilization" and "high levels of capacity utilization" push this above 50. Score ~56-60. The language is strong: "robust," "very high," "scant evidence of moderation." Score 58.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "labor markets were widely described as tight," "labor shortages appeared to have increased further recently," "pronounced shortages of skilled labor in many local areas," "numerous anecdotal reports of tightening labor markets." This is clearly tight. Workers have some leverage but firms are resisting wage increases. Unemployment at 5.6%. Score ~58-63. The language of "tight" and "shortages" is clear. Score 60.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "consumer spending had increased briskly," "Business fixed investment, which was contributing substantially to the current strength," "few signs of any slowing in the current data." "Robust expansion in employment, output, and spending." This is clearly above trend. Score ~62-66. The language is strong -- "briskly," "substantially," "robust." Score 63.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "despite the increases that had occurred in interest rates, financial conditions remained generally supportive of vigorous economic activity." "Banking institutions remained aggressive in their efforts to extend loans." "Financial markets were displaying a great deal of resilience." This clearly indicates loose-to-neutral conditions despite rate hikes. Score ~32-38. "Generally supportive" maps to the 31-45 range. Score 35.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: A majority favored holding steady but with asymmetric directive tilted toward restraint. "A few members expressed a preference for some additional tightening." "The need for further monetary restraint was highly likely." One dissent (LaWare) for immediate tightening. The center of gravity is: hold now but lean hawkish going forward. This maps to ~60-65. The pause is tactical, not dovish -- they just hiked 75bp in November and want to assess. Score 63.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal dissent (LaWare for tightening). Within the majority, there's a split between asymmetric and symmetric directive preferences, but "these members said that they would not dissent." The "a few" who wanted tightening "indicated they could accept an unchanged directive." Moderate differences with one dissent. Score ~32-38. Score 35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Brief mentions of financial market resilience, losses by local governments and private corporations, year-end market adjustments. "Financial markets were displaying a great deal of resilience" and concerns about "the sizable market losses incurred by some investors in 1994." Plus the Mexico swap arrangement at end. This is modest attention -- more than zero but not a focus. Score ~20-25. The Mexico issue was a separate procedural matter. Score 22.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Asymmetric directive tilted toward restraint. "The need for further monetary restraint was highly likely." "Should the need for more restraint become apparent, it would be desirable... for the appropriate policy move to be made promptly." This is a clear hawkish forward lean. Score ~68-72. Score 70.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "the timing and extent of the slowing in the expansion were subject to considerable uncertainty." "Inventory developments are always subject to a great deal of uncertainty." "The government sector constituted another source of considerable uncertainty." Multiple uses of "considerable uncertainty" and "great deal of uncertainty." But these are somewhat standard. Score ~40-48. The word "considerable" used twice elevates this. Score 45.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (58) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (60): Both above 50, consistent with an economy running above potential with tight labor markets. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (63) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (70): Forward guidance is a bit more hawkish than current action, which makes sense -- they paused but signaled likely further tightening. Coherent.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says building but currently contained. 56 is slightly above midpoint. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. "At or slightly above potential." 58 matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says conditions "generally supportive" (loose). 35 is below midpoint. Matches.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Asymmetric toward tightening. 70 matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 56 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Key measures of inflation including consumer prices, wages, and producer prices of finished goods did not display any evident uptrend at this juncture"
- "many members commented that rising pressures on capacity, should they persist or intensify, could be expected to foster greater inflation at some point"
- "a number of members commented that inflation probably would worsen somewhat over the near term"

**Center-of-gravity**: Current inflation is contained but members see building pressures from high resource utilization and anticipate modest worsening. The score sits modestly above 50 reflecting the forward-looking concern despite benign current readings.

**Minority views**: Some members emphasized that productivity/flexibility could explain the absence of inflation. "A few" wanted immediate tightening partly on inflation grounds (LaWare: "serious risk of rising inflation"). Band reflects range from optimists to hawks.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 58 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "robust expansion in employment, output, and spending and to very high and rising levels of resource utilization"
- "the economy now operating at or even slightly above its noninflationary potential"
- "scant evidence at this point of any moderation in the growth of overall economic activity"

**Center-of-gravity**: Members clearly view the economy as operating at or somewhat above potential with continuing momentum. The "slightly above" language places this modestly above the midpoint.

**Minority views**: "A number also commented that appreciable slowing during the year to a more sustainable and less inflationary pace remained a reasonable expectation." Some expected moderation, but current conditions are unambiguously above potential.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "labor markets were widely described as tight and labor shortages appeared to have increased further recently"
- "pronounced shortages of skilled labor in many local areas"
- "numerous anecdotal reports of tightening labor markets"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor markets are clearly tight and tightening further, though firms are successfully resisting broad wage increases through bonuses and worker insecurity. Above full employment but not at crisis-level tightness.

**Minority views**: Narrow range. All members seem to agree labor markets are tight; the question is whether wage pressures will follow. Band is narrow.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 63 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "consumer spending had increased briskly in recent months"
- "Business fixed investment, which was contributing substantially to the current strength of the expansion"
- "few signs of any slowing in the current data or anecdotal reports"

**Center-of-gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment are running well above trend with little sign of moderation. Housing is mixed but overall spending is robust.

**Minority views**: "Some members emphasized that sales volumes were being buttressed by unusual promotional efforts, including relatively large discounts." Some noted consumer debt growth as a future drag. Band reflects minor disagreements about sustainability.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 35 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "despite the increases that had occurred in interest rates, financial conditions remained generally supportive of vigorous economic activity"
- "Banking institutions remained aggressive in their efforts to extend loans to businesses and consumers"
- "financial markets were displaying a great deal of resilience"

**Center-of-gravity**: Despite a year of rate hikes, financial conditions have not tightened meaningfully. Banks are lending aggressively and markets are resilient. Conditions remain on the loose side of neutral.

**Minority views**: Some noted rising mortgage rates and wealth effects from bond/stock losses as emerging restraints, preventing the score from being even lower.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 63 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "a majority of the members agreed on the desirability of maintaining an unchanged policy posture at least through the beginning of 1995" (current hold)
- "A few members expressed a preference for some additional tightening of policy at this meeting"
- "a majority of the members expressed a preference for an asymmetric directive tilted toward restraint"

**Center-of-gravity**: The committee paused after aggressive tightening but the lean is decidedly hawkish. The majority sees further tightening as "highly likely" and adopted an asymmetric directive. One dissent for immediate tightening. Score reflects a hold with strong hawkish tilt.

**Minority views**: "A few" wanted immediate tightening (hawkish wing). Some preferred a symmetric directive (less hawkish). LaWare formally dissented for tightening.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 35 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all but one member indicated that they could support a directive" (LaWare dissented)
- "A few members expressed a preference for some additional tightening... All but one of these members indicated, however, that they could accept an unchanged directive"
- Split between asymmetric vs. symmetric directive preferences, but "these members said that they would not dissent"

**Center-of-gravity**: One formal dissent plus a meaningful split on directive language (asymmetric vs. symmetric) and a few who preferred tightening. Moderate level of disagreement, mostly about pace rather than direction.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 22 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "they had on balance weathered fairly readily the recent financial problems of a number of local governments and private corporations that had experienced large unanticipated losses"
- "a number of members viewed conditions in financial markets as arguing for a steady policy course"
- Mexico swap arrangement discussed separately

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability receives brief mentions -- market resilience, investment losses, year-end concerns -- but is not a prominent focus of the economic assessment. The Mexico swap was a procedural add-on, not central to policy discussion.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 70 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the need for further monetary restraint was highly likely"
- "Should the need for more restraint become apparent, it would be desirable... for the appropriate policy move to be made promptly"
- Asymmetric directive tilted toward restraint

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is clearly hawkish. The majority expects further tightening contingent on data, with the asymmetric directive formalizing this bias. The signal is "likely more tightening ahead, soon."

**Minority views**: Some preferred symmetric directive, suggesting less certainty about the forward path. "Current forecasts were subject to some risks in both directions."

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 45 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the timing and extent of the slowing in the expansion were subject to considerable uncertainty"
- "inventory developments are always subject to a great deal of uncertainty"
- "The government sector constituted another source of considerable uncertainty in the outlook for 1995"

**Center-of-gravity**: Multiple references to "considerable uncertainty" about whether/when the economy will slow, fiscal policy effects, and inventory dynamics. This is somewhat elevated above normal, though the overall policy direction seems clear to most members.

**Minority views**: The range of views on the economic outlook (from continued robust growth to appreciable slowing) itself reflects the uncertainty.
