# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2015-10-28

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented -- participant discussion is clearly separated from staff content.

**Era context**: October 2015 -- the FOMC is in the pre-liftoff period after years at the zero lower bound. The economy has been recovering gradually since the GFC. Inflation persistently below 2%. The committee is actively debating whether to raise rates for the first time since 2006. China/global volatility from summer 2015 had abated somewhat. This is one meeting before the December 2015 liftoff.

**Quantifier distribution**: "most" (participants anticipated conditions could be met by next meeting), "several" (downside risks, labor views), "some" (conditions already met; unlikely December), "a few" (downside risks from abroad; firming inflation signals), "a number of" (reasons to avoid delay; hiring challenges), "almost all" (agreed to wait, leave rates unchanged), one dissent.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below the 2% target. "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective." Core PCE "also remained low." Some trimmed mean measures "had recently moved up modestly" and "a few participants noted that the September CPI data appeared consistent with some firming in inflation." But "several participants cited downside risks to inflation." Center of gravity: below target, expected to rise gradually. This maps to the 30-38 range -- below target with slack keeping prices in check, but not deeply concerning. Direction: below midpoint. Score ~35.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic activity had been expanding moderately." "Private domestic final demand expanding at a solid pace." But there's still "underutilization of labor resources" that "had diminished since early this year." "A number of participants expressed the view that further progress would be necessary before labor market conditions were fully consistent with maximum employment." Some others judged "little or no remaining underutilization." The economy is approaching potential but hasn't fully closed the gap. Direction: slightly below potential. Score ~46. Bright-line rule: mixed language -- "underutilization diminished" but some say slack remains while others say it's gone. Center of gravity is approaching potential but slightly below.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Underutilization of labor resources had diminished since early this year." But "a number of participants expressed the view that further progress would be necessary." "Some others judged that there was little or no remaining underutilization." "Pace of wage gains remained subdued." Job gains slowed. Some contacts reporting "increasing challenges in hiring." Center of gravity: approaching full employment but not yet there -- some remaining slack. Score ~44.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Household spending and business fixed investment increased at solid rates." "Solid pace of consumption growth in the third quarter." "Business fixed investment appeared to be increasing at a solid rate despite the sharp contraction in energy-related investment." Housing "continuing to recover." Net exports "remained soft." Overall quite positive -- "solid" repeated many times. Score ~53. Above trend modestly given the repeated "solid" language.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Volatility in global financial markets had abated." Equity prices "largely retracing the declines experienced late in the summer." The U.S. financial system "weathered the turbulence without any sign of systemic stress." "Widening of credit spreads on corporate bonds" noted. Some concern about "unwarranted tightening of financial conditions" if normalization began. Overall conditions appear neutral to slightly loose -- markets had calmed. Score ~42. Somewhat loose/neutral given the calming of volatility but noted credit spread widening.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Some participants thought that the conditions for beginning the policy normalization process had already been met." "Most participants anticipated that... these conditions could well be met by the time of the next meeting." "Almost all members agreed it was appropriate to wait" at this meeting. One dissent for a 25bp hike. "Gradually remove policy accommodation" was the consensus path. The committee is clearly leaning toward liftoff soon but chose to wait this meeting. This is pre-liftoff with a strong signal toward December. Score ~55. Lean hawkish -- holding steady but strong tightening bias for next meeting.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal dissent (preferred 25bp hike). "Members expressed a range of views regarding the extent of further progress... they would need to see." Several wanted to wait longer, some thought conditions already met. Moderate disagreement about timing. Score ~35. One dissent with a range of views but broad agreement on waiting this meeting.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: A full paragraph discussing financial stability topics: "valuation risks in leveraged loan and real estate markets," "widening of credit spreads on corporate bonds," "potential risks to financial stability stemming from interest rates remaining low for a prolonged period," Puerto Rico debt. Also "increasing risk of a buildup of financial imbalances." Moderate discussion -- a meaningful section but not dominant. Score ~40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Very clear forward signal toward tightening. "Most participants anticipated that... these conditions could well be met by the time of the next meeting." The statement was changed to "indicate that... it would assess both realized and expected progress toward its objectives" at the next meeting -- explicitly pointing to December liftoff. "Members emphasized that this change was intended to convey the sense that... it may well become appropriate to initiate the normalization process at the next meeting." Strong signal toward tightening at next meeting. Score ~65.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Several participants observed that the recent employment reports had increased the uncertainty about the outlook for the labor market." "Some participants were concerned that the recent slowdown in employment growth might prove more than temporary." "Appreciable downside risks to the outlook remained." Uncertainty about whether economic growth "robust enough to withstand potential adverse shocks." But "most participants saw the downside risks... as having diminished." Somewhat elevated uncertainty. Score ~40.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (46) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (44): Both slightly below 50, consistent -- economy approaching potential but not quite there.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (55) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (65): Forward guidance is more hawkish than current lean. This makes sense -- they held steady at this meeting but strongly signaled December liftoff. The forward signal is more hawkish than the current action.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (35): Higher = more pressure. Evidence says below target. Score below 50. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP (46): Higher = more above potential. Evidence says approaching but slightly below potential. Score slightly below 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (42): Higher = tighter. Conditions calmed, somewhat loose to neutral. Score slightly below 50. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (55): Higher = more hawkish. Lean toward liftoff soon. Score slightly above 50. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective, partly reflecting declines in energy prices and prices of non-energy imports."
- "Core PCE inflation also remained low, but some other measures of inflation, such as the trimmed mean PCE and trimmed mean CPI measures, continued to run at higher levels than core PCE inflation and had recently moved up modestly."
- "Several participants, however, cited downside risks to inflation, pointing, for example, to declines in market-based measures of inflation compensation."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants broadly agreed inflation was running below the 2% objective with transitory factors depressing it, and expected a gradual return to target. The score reflects below-target inflation with limited near-term concern but no deflation alarm.

**Minority views**: "A few participants noted that the September CPI data appeared consistent with some firming in inflation." Several cited downside risks. Narrow band reflects broad agreement on the below-target assessment.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 46 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity had been expanding moderately." "Private domestic final demand expanding at a solid pace."
- "A number of participants expressed the view that further progress would be necessary before labor market conditions were fully consistent with maximum employment, while some others judged that there was little or no remaining underutilization of labor resources."
- "Most participants saw the downside risks arising from economic and financial developments abroad as having diminished and judged the risks to the outlook for domestic economic activity and the labor market to be nearly balanced."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy was expanding moderately and approaching potential, but the center of gravity still saw some remaining slack, placing the output gap slightly below potential.

**Minority views**: "Some others judged that there was little or no remaining underutilization" -- these participants would score the gap at or above 50.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 44 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Underutilization of labor resources had diminished since early this year" but "a number of participants expressed the view that further progress would be necessary before labor market conditions were fully consistent with maximum employment."
- "The pace of wage gains remained subdued."
- "Some others judged that there was little or no remaining underutilization of labor resources." "A number of participants reported that some of their business contacts were experiencing increasing challenges in hiring."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The labor market had improved significantly but the center of gravity saw remaining slack, with subdued wage growth reinforcing the view that full employment had not yet been reached.

**Minority views**: Some saw no remaining slack and contacts reported hiring difficulties, while others worried the slowdown in job gains might persist. Wider band reflects this dispersion.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 53 +/-4 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Household spending and business fixed investment increased at solid rates in recent months, and the housing sector improved further."
- "Participants were encouraged by the solid pace of consumption growth in the third quarter and generally expected consumer spending to rise moderately going forward."
- "Business fixed investment appeared to be increasing at a solid rate despite the sharp contraction in energy-related investment."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumer spending and business investment were both characterized as "solid," with housing recovering. Net exports were a drag but domestic demand was the clear driver. This is modestly above trend.

**Minority views**: Energy-sector weakness and soft net exports partially offset the positive picture; "disappointing retail sales data in September" noted but outweighed by broader solid readings.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 42 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Volatility in global financial markets had abated since the previous FOMC meeting, with equity prices in the United States largely retracing the declines experienced late in the summer."
- "The U.S. financial system appeared to have weathered the turbulence in global financial markets without any sign of systemic stress."
- "Widening of credit spreads on corporate bonds" was noted.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions had eased from the summer turbulence, returning to broadly neutral to somewhat loose conditions. Credit spread widening was noted but did not dominate the assessment.

**Minority views**: Several participants worried that beginning normalization "might be associated with an unwarranted tightening of financial conditions," suggesting some concern about potential tightening ahead.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 55 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Some participants thought that the conditions for beginning the policy normalization process had already been met. Most participants anticipated that... these conditions could well be met by the time of the next meeting."
- "Almost all members agreed it was appropriate to wait for additional information" at this meeting.
- "One member, however, preferred to raise the target range for the federal funds rate by 25 basis points at this meeting."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The committee held steady but with a clear tightening bias -- most anticipated conditions for liftoff could be met by December. This is a lean-hawkish hold with strong forward tightening intent.

**Minority views**: "Several participants" cited appreciable downside risks and argued normalization "may not yet be warranted." One member dissented in favor of immediate hike.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All but one member agreed to leave the target range for the federal funds rate unchanged at this meeting. One member, however, preferred to raise the target range... by 25 basis points."
- "Members expressed a range of views regarding the extent of further progress in labor market indicators they would need to see."
- "Some others, however, judged it unlikely that the information available by the December meeting would warrant raising the target range."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: One formal dissent with a meaningful range of views on timing -- some wanted to go now, most wanted to wait, and some were skeptical about December. Moderate but not sharp disagreement.

**Minority views**: The range from "already met" to "unlikely by December" shows notable breadth, but the broad agreement on waiting this time keeps dissent moderate.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 40 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants also discussed a range of topics related to financial market developments and financial stability."
- "Participants commented on issues related to financial stability monitoring and the use of macroprudential tools, the assessment of valuation risks in leveraged loan and real estate markets, the widening of credit spreads on corporate bonds, and potential risks to financial stability stemming from interest rates remaining low for a prolonged period."
- "Increasing risk of a buildup of financial imbalances after a prolonged period of very low interest rates" cited as a reason to avoid delay.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial stability received a dedicated discussion section covering multiple topics (leveraged loans, real estate valuations, credit spreads, low-rate risks, Puerto Rico). This represents moderate attention -- several participants addressed it meaningfully.

**Minority views**: None clearly articulated as minority; this was a broad-based discussion topic.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 65 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Most participants anticipated that... these conditions could well be met by the time of the next meeting."
- The statement changed to indicate it "would assess both realized and expected progress toward its objectives" at the next meeting -- "this change was intended to convey the sense that... it may well become appropriate to initiate the normalization process at the next meeting."
- "Beginning the normalization process relatively soon would make it more likely that the policy trajectory after liftoff could be shallow."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal was clearly tilted toward tightening, with an explicit signal pointing to December liftoff. The statement language was specifically revised to telegraph this. This is a mild-to-moderate tightening bias in forward guidance.

**Minority views**: "Several participants indicated that... appreciable downside risks to the outlook remained" and argued normalization "may not yet be warranted."

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 40 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Several participants observed that the recent employment reports had increased the uncertainty about the outlook for the labor market."
- "Some participants were concerned that the recent slowdown in employment growth might prove more than temporary."
- "Most participants saw the downside risks arising from economic and financial developments abroad as having diminished."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Uncertainty was somewhat elevated due to the labor market slowdown and lingering global risks, but overall had diminished from the previous meeting. The center of gravity saw risks as "nearly balanced" with some residual uncertainty about the labor outlook.

**Minority views**: "A few participants noted that downside risks from abroad were still significant."
