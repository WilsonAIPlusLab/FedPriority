# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2005-09-20

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Unsegmented pre-2009 format — staff and participant content are mixed. I will score only participant-attributed language ("participants agreed," "members favored," "several voiced," etc.) and ignore staff-reported content.

**Era context**: September 2005. The FOMC is in the midst of its "measured pace" tightening cycle that began June 2004. The federal funds rate target was raised to 3.75% at this meeting. Hurricane Katrina had just devastated the Gulf Coast (August 29, 2005). Energy prices were surging. The economy had been growing at a good pace before the storm.

**Quantifier distribution**: "participants agreed" (multiple times), "nearly all members," "most anecdotal information," "several participants voiced concern," "some evidence," "some sentiment was expressed." The language leans toward broad agreement with some pockets of concern.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as above the midpoint. Key evidence: "participants' concerns about inflation prospects generally had increased over the intermeeting period," "the surge in energy prices was boosting overall inflation," "some of that increase would probably pass through for a time into core prices," "several participants voiced concern that the effects of the hurricane were likely to add to already considerable pressures on prices." However, there are tempering remarks: "core inflation in recent months had been quite damped," "market-based measures of longer-term inflation expectations had risen only modestly." The center of gravity is elevated but moderated by the core-damped observation. Direction: above midpoint, "somewhat elevated" to "concerning." Score range: 60-65.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy operating near or modestly above potential. Key: "output and employment appeared to have been growing at a good pace," "the margin of unutilized resources in the overall economy was probably thin," "the fundamental factors influencing the longer-term path of the economy probably had not been affected by the hurricane." The "thin margin of unutilized resources" directly implies slightly above potential. Hurricane effects are seen as temporary. Score: 52-55.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "anecdotal information continued to point to shortages of certain types of labor," "some business contacts reported difficulties in hiring more generally, a development that had prompted some firms to boost wages." Employment had been growing at a "good pace." This suggests moderately tight conditions. Score: 55-58.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Household spending had been "fairly robust" and "especially strong" before the hurricane. Housing markets "generally remained brisk." But business fixed investment had been "somewhat weaker than expected" and "a little softer than expected." Energy prices were expected to restrain consumer spending near-term. Overall, consumption was expected to "expand at a moderate pace." Net position: at or slightly above trend for consumption, but offset by soft investment. Score: 52-55.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "financing was readily available and relatively inexpensive for most firms," "strong advances in income and continuing gains in wealth that reflected in part further large increases in home prices." These indicate loose financial conditions. Score: 18-22 (loose).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "nearly all members favored raising the target federal funds rate 25 basis points." "Even after today's action, the federal funds rate would likely be below the level that would be necessary to contain inflationary pressures, and further rate increases probably would be required." "A pause in policy tightening at this meeting had the potential to mislead the public." This is clearly hawkish — consensus for hiking plus expectation of more hikes. Score: 70-73.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Nearly all members favored" the 25bp hike — this implies very broad agreement with perhaps one who was uncertain. "Some sentiment was expressed to consider changes to forward-looking aspects of the statement at upcoming meetings" — a minor divergence on communication, not on policy direction. Low dissent. Score: 18-22.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Limited discussion. Home prices are mentioned as supporting wealth, and "further large increases in home prices" are noted, but there is no explicit financial stability concern language from participants. Financial conditions are mentioned in passing (financing readily available). Not a prominent topic. Score: 20-25.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "further rate increases probably would be required," the Committee reiterated "policy accommodation can be removed at a pace that is likely to be measured," "some sentiment was expressed to consider changes to forward-looking aspects of the statement." The forward signal is clearly hawkish — more tightening ahead. Score: 72-75.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "uncertainty had increased," "the size and timing of those effects were uncertain," "underlying economic trends would be particularly difficult to assess over the next several months," "uncertainties about the outlook for spending had increased." Multiple references to increased uncertainty, primarily hurricane-related. Score: 50-55.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (53) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (57): Both modestly above 50, consistent — economy near/slightly above potential with somewhat tight labor.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (72) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (73): Aligned — both moderately hawkish, reflecting ongoing tightening cycle with more expected.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says elevated and increasing concerns. Score above 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says loose/readily available. Score well below 50. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: **62** ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants' concerns about inflation prospects generally had increased over the intermeeting period."
- "Several participants voiced concern that the effects of the hurricane were likely to add to already considerable pressures on prices."
- "Core inflation in recent months had been quite damped, and market-based measures of longer-term inflation expectations had risen only modestly of late."

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants see inflation pressure as clearly elevated and rising, driven by energy prices and potential pass-through, though core remains damped — placing this in the "clearly above target" range with some moderation.

**Minority views**: The observation that core inflation was "quite damped" and pass-through historically limited provides a less alarmed counterweight, widening the band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: **53** ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Output and employment appeared to have been growing at a good pace before Hurricane Katrina's landfall."
- "The margin of unutilized resources in the overall economy was probably thin."
- Hurricane effects seen as "essentially temporary" with "probably little effect on the economy's intermediate-term growth prospects."

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is modestly above potential with thin slack, though hurricane disruption introduces near-term volatility.

**Minority views**: None significant — broad agreement on this assessment.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: **57** ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Anecdotal information continued to point to shortages of certain types of labor, such as truck drivers."
- "Some business contacts reported difficulties in hiring more generally, a development that had prompted some firms to boost wages."
- "Measures of labor costs were giving conflicting signals."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is moderately tight with scattered shortages and some wage pressures, though signals are mixed.

**Minority views**: Conflicting labor cost measures suggest some participants saw less tightness than others.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: **53** ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Household spending had been especially strong" and "fairly robust before the hurricane."
- "Business fixed investment had been somewhat weaker than expected."
- "Consumption would probably expand at a moderate pace--perhaps a little below the pace of income growth."

**Center-of-gravity**: Net spending is slightly above trend — strong consumption offset partly by softer-than-expected investment, with hurricane expected to cause temporary dislocation.

**Minority views**: The investment softness was "somewhat puzzling" and may indicate broader drag.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: **20** ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Financing was readily available and relatively inexpensive for most firms."
- "Business balance sheets appeared quite strong in the aggregate, profitability was high."
- "Strong advances in income and continuing gains in wealth that reflected in part further large increases in home prices."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly loose — easy credit, rising asset values, strong balance sheets. Consistent with 2005 pre-crisis era anchor (10-20).

**Minority views**: None noted; broad agreement on easy conditions.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: **72** ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Nearly all members favored raising the target federal funds rate 25 basis points to 3-3/4 percent."
- "Even after today's action, the federal funds rate would likely be below the level that would be necessary to contain inflationary pressures, and further rate increases probably would be required."
- "A pause in policy tightening at this meeting had the potential to mislead the public."

**Center-of-gravity**: Strong hawkish consensus — hiking now and expecting more, even in the face of hurricane uncertainty.

**Minority views**: "Some sentiment was expressed to consider changes to forward-looking aspects of the statement at upcoming meetings," suggesting a few may be thinking about slowing the pace.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: **20** ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Nearly all members favored raising the target federal funds rate 25 basis points" — implies near-unanimity.
- "Members agreed" appears multiple times regarding the statement language.
- "Some sentiment was expressed to consider changes to forward-looking aspects" — minor tactical divergence, not fundamental disagreement.

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on both the action and the assessment, with only minor differences about future communication.

**Minority views**: The "nearly all" qualifier suggests at most one member was not fully on board.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: **22** ±7 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Further large increases in home prices" mentioned as supporting wealth, not as a stability concern.
- "Financing was readily available and relatively inexpensive" — noted factually.
- No explicit participant discussion of financial stability risks, asset bubbles, or systemic concerns.

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are mentioned in passing as context for the economic outlook, but financial stability per se is not a focus of participant discussion.

**Minority views**: None.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: **73** ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Further rate increases probably would be required."
- Committee reiterated "policy accommodation can be removed at a pace that is likely to be measured."
- "Some sentiment was expressed to consider changes to forward-looking aspects of the statement at upcoming meetings, in part because of the considerable reduction in monetary policy accommodation that had already been accomplished."

**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal is clearly hawkish — more tightening ahead at a measured pace. The mention of possible statement changes introduces slight uncertainty about the pace but not the direction.

**Minority views**: The discussion of potentially changing the "measured pace" language suggests some may be reconsidering the trajectory.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: **52** ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Although uncertainty had increased, in the Committee's judgment the fundamental factors influencing the longer-term path of the economy probably had not been affected by the hurricane."
- "Underlying economic trends would be particularly difficult to assess over the next several months."
- "The pace at which reconstruction activity would take place, however, was uncertain."

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is elevated above normal due to hurricane effects, but participants express confidence in the fundamental trajectory. This is genuine but bounded uncertainty — not deep confusion about the outlook.

**Minority views**: None — fairly uniform acknowledgment of increased uncertainty.
