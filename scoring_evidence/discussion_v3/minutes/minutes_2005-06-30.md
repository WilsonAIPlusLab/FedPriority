# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2005-06-30

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Unsegmented pre-2009 format -- staff and participant content are mixed. I will score ONLY portions with participant language cues ("meeting participants noted," "members agreed," "several expressed," etc.) and ignore staff-attributable content.

**Era context**: Mid-2005, the Fed is in a "measured pace" tightening cycle that began in June 2004. The federal funds rate was raised from 3.00% to 3.25% at this meeting. Economy expanding moderately, housing market booming, energy prices rising, core inflation slightly above comfort but contained. This meeting featured a special discussion on housing valuations.

**Quantifier distribution**: "many meeting participants" (reassuring expansion), "most" (inflation modestly higher), "some participants" (concerned about underlying inflation stepping up), "several" (domestic/global imbalances), "all members" (25bp hike), "views differed" (amount of further tightening needed).

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly above comfort but not alarming. "Core consumer price inflation had been higher than most participants had expected at the start of the year." Views were "mixed" -- some worried about "underlying pace of inflation might be in the process of stepping up," others emphasized "recent core inflation data had been relatively restrained" and "pricing power at many firms remained quite limited." Long-term expectations "well-anchored." The center of gravity is modestly elevated -- somewhat above target but contained. Direction: above midpoint. Score range: 55-62.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "The economy was expanding at a moderate pace." "Incoming data had been reassuring about the strength of the expansion." The economy "remained firm, led by residential and business investment." Members noted "the federal funds rate remained below the level members anticipated would prove necessary... to keep output near potential." Language of "margins of slack resources narrowing" implies still slightly below but closing. The staff forecast was "just above potential." Direction: near potential, perhaps very slightly above. Score: 50-54.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor market conditions continued to strengthen gradually." Unemployment at 5.1%. "Some limited remaining slack in labor markets." "Anecdotal reports of labor market conditions continued to point to shortages of labor only for certain, mostly skilled, occupations." Members note "margins of slack resources narrowing." This suggests approaching full employment with modest remaining slack. Direction: near NAIRU with slight remaining slack. Score: 48-52.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "moderate gain," housing "remained robust," business investment "continued to rise" with "solid" outlook, "strong business balance sheets." "Fundamentals underlying household spending remained firm." Direction: above trend. Score: 55-60.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Financial conditions remained accommodative, supporting growth going forward." "Policy still seen as accommodative." Long-term rates moved "considerably lower" despite tightening. "Spreads on speculative-grade bonds contracted notably." "Low mortgage rates." This is clearly loose financial conditions. The anchor for 2005-06 pre-crisis ease is 10-20. Direction: well below midpoint (loose). Score: 15-22.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All members agreed on 25bp hike. "Additional tightening would probably be necessary." "The federal funds rate remained below the level members anticipated would prove necessary." Retained "measured pace" language. "The Committee needed to be particularly alert to signs of a further increase in inflation." But "views differed on the amount of tightening that would likely be required." This is a steady tightening cycle with consensus on continued hikes. Direction: moderately hawkish. Score: 67-72.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All members agreed on a 25 basis point increase." No dissents. But "views differed on the amount of tightening that would likely be required." Inflation views were "mixed." There are two camps on inflation but consensus on the action. Direction: modest disagreement within consensus action. Score: 25-32.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: The meeting featured a special discussion on housing valuations and monetary policy on day one. Participants discussed "increased speculative activity in housing markets," "the continued run-up in home prices," the "low level of long-term interest rates," and "riskier types of mortgages." This was a prominent topic. Also discussed "domestic and global imbalances." Direction: well above typical. Score: 50-58.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Policy accommodation can be removed at a pace that is likely to be measured." "Additional tightening would probably be necessary." "The federal funds rate remained below the level members anticipated would prove necessary." Clear signal of continued tightening ahead. Direction: hawkish forward lean. Score: 68-74.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Considerable uncertainty attended the outlook for both long-term interest rates and home prices." "The degree of slack remaining in labor and resource markets was very uncertain." "Uncertainties regarding the nature and timing of the potential correction of these imbalances." Multiple sources of uncertainty flagged. But core economic outlook is relatively confident ("reassuring about the strength of the expansion"). Direction: somewhat elevated. Score: 40-48.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 58 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Core consumer price inflation had been higher than most participants had expected at the start of the year, reflecting, at least in part, the pass-through effects of higher energy, commodity, and import prices."
- "Some participants expressed concern that, with policy still accommodative, the underlying pace of inflation might be in the process of stepping up, perhaps to a level that was at the upper end of the range that they viewed as compatible with the Committee's price stability objective."
- "Other meeting participants emphasized that recent core inflation data had been relatively restrained, and anecdotal reports suggested that pricing power at many firms remained quite limited."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Core inflation was running modestly above comfort levels but the center of gravity saw it as contained, with pass-through effects from energy expected to fade and long-term expectations anchored. Views were mixed but the majority saw modestly elevated pressure, not an alarming acceleration.

**Minority views**: Some participants worried about inflation "stepping up" (hawkish camp pushing score higher); others saw recent data as "relatively restrained" (dovish camp pulling it lower). This widens the uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 52 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Many meeting participants noted that incoming data had been reassuring about the strength of the expansion."
- "Margins of slack resources narrowing and inflation somewhat higher."
- GDP forecasts in the 3 to 3-3/4 percent range, with members expecting output "near potential."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy was expanding at roughly its potential rate, with slack nearly exhausted. The expansion was "firm" and slightly above trend, but not overheating. Members discussed keeping "output near potential" suggesting they were essentially at it.

**Minority views**: Limited divergence; broad agreement on moderate expansion near potential.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 50 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Some limited remaining slack in labor markets likely damping growth in compensation."
- "Anecdotal reports of labor market conditions continued to point to shortages of labor only for certain, mostly skilled, occupations."
- Unemployment at 5.1%, forecasts of 5-5.25% for Q4 2005 (near estimated NAIRU).

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The labor market was essentially at full employment with perhaps slight residual slack. Skilled worker shortages coexisted with modest overall slack -- a balanced picture near NAIRU.

**Minority views**: Some saw more slack ("likely damping growth in compensation"), while others noted rising unit labor costs. Narrow band.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 57 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Fundamentals underlying household spending remained firm. With rising home and equity prices buoying household wealth, consumer expenditures continued to advance."
- "Business outlays for capital goods continued to rise, and the outlook for investment spending remained solid, supported by increased sales, low interest rates, robust profits, and strong business balance sheets."
- "Activity in the housing sector remained robust."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Both consumer spending and business investment were growing solidly, supported by favorable fundamentals. Housing was robust. The overall picture was above trend -- not booming, but clearly healthy with broad-based strength.

**Minority views**: "In some regions businesses apparently remained cautious about the outlook." Minor drag noted from energy prices on consumption. Modest offset.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 18 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Financial conditions remained accommodative, supporting growth going forward."
- "Spreads on speculative-grade bonds contracted notably." "Spreads on investment-grade corporate bonds were little changed."
- "Low mortgage rates." Long-term Treasury yields moved "considerably lower" despite policy tightening.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions were clearly loose/accommodative despite the ongoing tightening cycle. Long-term rates had fallen, credit spreads were narrow and tightening further, equity prices rose, and mortgage rates remained low. Classic pre-crisis easy conditions.

**Minority views**: None meaningfully. Participants acknowledged conditions remained accommodative even as they tightened policy.

**Polarity check**: Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence says loose/accommodative. Low score = correct.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 69 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All members agreed on a 25 basis point increase in the target federal funds rate to 3-1/4 percent."
- "Even with this action, the federal funds rate remained below the level members anticipated would prove necessary in the long run to contain inflation pressures."
- "Additional tightening would probably be necessary, but views differed on the amount of tightening that would likely be required."
- "The Committee needed to be particularly alert to signs of a further increase in inflation."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Unanimously hawkish in action (25bp hike) with clear expectation of further tightening needed. The rate was still seen as below equilibrium. The tone was steadily hawkish but not aggressive -- "measured pace" language retained, and the exact endpoint was uncertain.

**Minority views**: "Views differed on the amount of tightening that would likely be required" -- some wanted more, some less, but all agreed on continued tightening direction.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 28 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All members agreed on a 25 basis point increase." No dissents.
- "Participants' views on the inflation outlook were mixed" -- two clear camps on inflation assessment.
- "Views differed on the amount of tightening that would likely be required."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Unanimous on the immediate action, but meaningful disagreement about the inflation outlook and the total amount of tightening needed. This amounts to tactical differences within a broad strategic consensus.

**Minority views**: The inflation hawks vs. the "relatively restrained" camp represent a genuine split in assessment, but not in current policy action.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 55 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "At this meeting the Committee reviewed and discussed staff presentations on the topic of housing valuations and monetary policy."
- "Meeting participants noted that the rise in house prices had been accompanied by a modest shift toward potentially riskier types of mortgages, including adjustable-rate and interest-only loans."
- Extended discussion of "the low level of long-term interest rates and the continued run-up in home prices" and "increased speculative activity in housing markets."
- "Several meeting participants expressed some concern about domestic and global imbalances."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial stability was a prominent topic at this meeting due to the special housing discussion. Participants addressed housing valuations, speculative activity, risky mortgage products, the yield curve conundrum, and global imbalances at considerable length. However, the conclusion was relatively reassuring (no direct policy response to asset prices), which moderates the intensity somewhat.

**Minority views**: Broad participation in the discussion; no strong divergence on the financial stability topic itself.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 71 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Members also agreed that the statement language indicating that 'policy accommodation can be removed at a pace that is likely to be measured' correctly characterized the outlook for policy for now."
- "Additional tightening would probably be necessary."
- "The federal funds rate remained below the level members anticipated would prove necessary in the long run."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal was clearly hawkish -- further tightening was expected and the "measured pace" language signaled continued rate increases ahead. The rate was explicitly below its anticipated terminal level. This is a steady tightening signal, not an acceleration.

**Minority views**: "Views differed on the amount of tightening" -- some saw more needed, some less, but direction was unanimously tightening.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 44 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "It was agreed that considerable uncertainty attended the outlook for both long-term interest rates and home prices."
- "The degree of slack remaining in labor and resource markets was very uncertain."
- "Uncertainties regarding the nature and timing of the potential correction of these imbalances complicated the assessment of the intermediate-term prospects."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants flagged meaningful uncertainty on several fronts -- housing, long-term rates, labor slack, and global imbalances. However, the core economic outlook was described with reasonable confidence ("reassuring about the strength of the expansion"). Uncertainty was somewhat elevated but not dominant.

**Minority views**: Uncertainty was broadly shared rather than contested.
