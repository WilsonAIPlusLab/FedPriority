# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2020-09-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented — participant views section is clearly separated from staff review.

**Era context**: September 2020. Six months after the COVID-19 emergency. The economy is in early-stage recovery from the sharpest contraction since the Great Depression. The Fed had cut rates to 0-0.25% in March 2020, launched multiple emergency facilities, and was purchasing Treasuries and MBS. At this meeting, the Committee adopted new outcome-based forward guidance tied to employment and inflation criteria, reflecting the recently completed framework review (average inflation targeting). Two dissents occurred — one wanting weaker guidance (more flexibility) and one wanting stronger guidance.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all/unanimous" used for agreement on maintaining rate target, incorporating consensus statement elements; "almost all" for modifying forward guidance; "most" for supporting explicit outcome-based guidance; "many" for concern about fiscal expiration, service-sector weakness; "several" for retaining previous-style guidance, longer-run pandemic concerns; "a number of" for below-2% inflation projection, low rates supporting investment; "some" for supply chain disruptions, temporary layoff concerns; "a few" for household savings upside; "a couple" for stronger guidance preference, upside fiscal risk, financial imbalance concerns.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants note consumer prices increased "more quickly than expected" recently but inflation "remained subdued" and "the overall effect of the pandemic on prices was disinflationary." Inflation was running "persistently below" 2%. Market-based compensation increased but "remained low." A number projected inflation would "run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective for a significant period." Direction: clearly below target, disinflationary. Magnitude: "subdued" and "persistently below" = 15-25 points below midpoint. But there's a recent uptick noted, so not at the lowest range. Score ~25. Polarity check: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says low/subdued. 25 is correct (below midpoint). Anchor sanity: 2020 pandemic disinflation, anchors suggest 15-25 range for this period.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**: Economic activity "picked up in recent months but remained well below their levels at the beginning of the year." Recovery faster than expected but "well below pre-pandemic levels." Consumer spending recovered "about three-fourths of its earlier decline." The economy regained "roughly half of the 22 million jobs lost." Still "a long way from being fully recovered." Direction: clearly below potential. Magnitude: substantial slack remains despite recovery progress. The economy has recovered partially — not at the trough (Q2 2020) but still far below potential. Score ~28-32. Anchors: 15-22 for COVID Q2 2020 trough; this is several months into recovery, so higher than trough but still deep below potential. Score 30.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Roughly half of the 22 million jobs" regained. Labor market "a long way from being fully recovered." Some noted gains reflected temporary layoffs returning; future gains likely slower as "a greater share of remaining layoffs might become permanent." Disproportionate impact on lower-paid, minorities, women. Direction: very slack, far from full employment. Score ~25. Anchors: 15-22 for 2020 Q2; this is improved from trough but still very slack. Score 25.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "rebounding sharply" and recovered "about three-fourths of its earlier decline." Strong goods purchases (especially online), but services "slower to recover." Business investment "appeared to have begun to turn around" from its plummet. Motor vehicle and housing showed strength. But services-heavy sectors still struggling. Direction: recovering but still below trend. The rebound is strong from a very low base, but overall activity still well below pre-pandemic. Score ~38. Mix of sharp rebound in goods/durables but continued weakness in services and many industries. Anchors: 15-20 for Q2 2020 trough; this is a partial V-shaped recovery. Score 38.

**5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Financial conditions "generally accommodative." Fed actions supporting credit flow. But "important differences in credit quality and credit availability across borrowers." Bank lending was "tight" per surveys. Delinquency rates on business loans "risen noticeably." Small/medium banks facing stress from potential defaults. So conditions are mixed — loose for creditworthy borrowers due to Fed actions but tight for others. Direction: somewhat loose to neutral overall due to Fed support, but pockets of tightness. Score ~38. Anchors: accommodative conditions suggest below 50, but credit tightness for some borrowers pulls it up. Score 38.

**6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Committed to "full range of tools" to support economy. Maintaining 0-0.25% rate. New outcome-based forward guidance tying rates to employment and inflation outcomes. Asset purchases to continue "at least at the current pace." This is strongly accommodative. Most supported stronger forward guidance; several wanted to retain previous (less explicit) guidance; a couple wanted even stronger guidance. Two dissents — one for more flexibility (relatively less dovish) and one for stronger commitment (more dovish). The center of gravity is firmly dovish — maintaining maximum accommodation with new enhanced forward guidance. Score ~15. Anchors: 2-5 for March 2020 emergency; by September 2020 the acute emergency has passed but accommodation is being reinforced/extended. 15-25 range for QE era. Score 18.

**7. DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Two formal dissents — one in each direction (one wanting less commitment, one wanting more). "Several" preferred retaining prior guidance. "A couple" preferred stronger guidance. So there's a genuine three-way split on forward guidance formulation, though "most" supported the adopted approach. Direction: moderate disagreement. Two dissents is notable. Score ~45. Anchors: 25-35 for typical one-dissent; 40-50 for meetings with significant debate. Two dissents in opposite directions plus "several" preferring different approach = meaningful disagreement. Score 45.

**8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial conditions discussed in a dedicated paragraph. Participants note accommodative conditions, credit quality differences, bank lending tightness, delinquency rates, stress on small/medium banks, and "a couple" flagged excessive risk-taking and financial imbalances. Several paragraphs touch on financial conditions. Moderate discussion. Score ~40. Anchors: 20-35 for 2013-19 typical; 60-75 for 2020 recovery. This meeting has meaningful discussion but it's not dominant. Score 40.

**9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: This meeting is dominated by the adoption of new outcome-based forward guidance. The guidance ties rate increases to achieving maximum employment AND inflation at/above 2%. "Almost all" agreed on this formulation. Rates at ELB "until labor market conditions were judged to be consistent with... maximum employment and inflation had risen to 2 percent and was on track to moderately exceed 2 percent for some time." This is strongly dovish forward guidance — rates will remain at zero for an extended period until both employment and inflation criteria are met. Score ~8. Anchors: 2-10 for 2020 emergency guidance. The outcome-based guidance with dual criteria is very strong easing commitment. Score 8.

**10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Participants "continued to see the uncertainty surrounding the economic outlook as very elevated." Path "highly dependent on the course of the virus." Multiple downside risks cited — virus outbreaks, bankruptcies, insufficient fiscal support. "Many" assumed additional fiscal support; without it, recovery could be slower. Direction: very elevated uncertainty. Score ~72. Anchors: 70-80 for GFC unfolding; 85-95 for March 2020 emergency. By September 2020, uncertainty has moderated somewhat from peak but remains "very elevated." Score 72.

### Cross-dimension coherence check:
- OUTPUT_GAP (30) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (25): Both well below 50, consistent — deep slack in both output and labor markets. Labor slightly lower because "long way from fully recovered" and disproportionate impacts.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (18) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (8): Both dovish. Forward guidance is even more dovish than the current lean because the new outcome-based criteria signal rates at zero for a very long time. This divergence makes sense — the forward guidance is a particularly strong dovish commitment.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 25 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "inflation remained subdued, and participants still generally judged that the overall effect of the pandemic on prices was disinflationary"
- "a number of participants projected that inflation would run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective for a significant period"
- "consumer prices had increased more quickly than expected in recent months" (recent uptick, but from very low base)

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants see inflation as clearly below target with the pandemic exerting disinflationary pressure, though recent price increases are noted as a partial offset.

**Minority views**: The recent uptick in consumer prices and rising market-based inflation compensation provide a small upward pull, widening the band slightly.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 30 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity and employment had picked up in recent months but remained well below their levels at the beginning of the year"
- "economic activity was recovering faster than expected from its depressed second-quarter level"
- "overall business activity had been stronger than they expected, it remained well below pre-pandemic levels"

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is recovering from the Q2 2020 trough faster than expected but remains far below potential, with substantial slack persisting.

**Minority views**: "A few" cited potential upside from household savings unwinding, but "a couple" doubted this. Narrow disagreement on the pace, not the direction.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 25 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the economy through August had regained roughly half of the 22 million jobs that were lost in March and April"
- "the labor market was a long way from being fully recovered"
- "Some participants noted that the majority of gains in employment so far reflected workers on temporary layoffs returning to work... less likely for future job gains to continue at their recent pace"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market has substantial slack with only half the lost jobs regained and concerns that the pace of recovery will slow as easy gains from temporary layoffs diminish.

**Minority views**: Limited disagreement — all participants agree on significant slack. Some variation on pace of future improvement.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 38 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "consumer spending was rebounding sharply and appeared to have recovered about three-fourths of its earlier decline"
- "spending on these services to remain subdued for some time and thus to be a restraining factor on the pace of the recovery"
- "business investment, which had plummeted in the second quarter, appeared to have begun to turn around"

**Center-of-gravity**: Strong rebound in goods consumption and early recovery in business investment, but services spending remains depressed, leaving the overall level still below trend.

**Minority views**: "A few" saw household savings providing greater momentum; "a couple" doubted this. Several noted uneven recovery across industries.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 38 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "financial conditions were generally accommodative and that actions by the Federal Reserve... were supporting the flow of credit"
- "surveys of credit availability indicated that bank lending was tight"
- "several participants noted the stress that small- and medium-sized banks could face from defaults on loans to small businesses and CRE properties"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are broadly accommodative due to Fed actions, but with meaningful pockets of tightness in bank lending and credit availability, especially for smaller borrowers.

**Minority views**: "A couple" flagged that highly accommodative conditions could lead to excessive risk-taking, suggesting conditions are too loose in some segments.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 18 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "participants reaffirmed that they were committed to using the Federal Reserve's full range of tools in order to support the U.S. economy"
- "almost all members agreed on a specification for outcome-based forward guidance" tying rates to employment and inflation criteria
- "participants judged that it would be appropriate over coming months for the Federal Reserve to increase its holdings of Treasury securities and agency MBS at least at the current pace"

**Center-of-gravity**: Strongly dovish — maintaining zero rates, adopting new outcome-based forward guidance that commits to accommodation until employment and inflation goals are met, and continuing asset purchases.

**Minority views**: "Several" preferred retaining less explicit guidance (slightly less dovish commitment). "A couple" preferred even stronger guidance (more dovish). Two dissents in opposite directions.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 45 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Two members dissented from the policy decision" — one wanting more flexibility, one wanting stronger commitment
- "Several participants noted that... they preferred to retain forward guidance similar to that provided in recent FOMC statements" (distinct minority camp)
- "A couple of participants preferred even stronger, and less qualified, outcome-based forward guidance"

**Center-of-gravity**: Notable disagreement with three identifiable camps on forward guidance formulation and two formal dissents in opposite directions, though "most" supported the adopted approach.

**Minority views**: The dissents were on opposite sides of the majority, reflecting genuine disagreement about the degree of commitment in forward guidance.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 40 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Extended discussion of credit quality differences, bank lending tightness, delinquency rates, and small bank stress
- "a couple of participants indicated that highly accommodative financial market conditions could lead to excessive risk-taking and to a buildup of financial imbalances"
- "several participants noted the stress that small- and medium-sized banks could face from defaults"

**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate attention to financial stability — several participants addressed credit conditions, banking stress, and financial imbalance risks, but it was not a dominant topic relative to the recovery and forward guidance discussion.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 8 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "almost all members agreed on a specification for outcome-based forward guidance that indicated that the Committee expects that it will be appropriate to maintain the current setting of the target range... until labor market conditions had reached levels consistent with... maximum employment and inflation had risen to 2 percent and was on track to run moderately in excess of 2 percent for some time"
- "Members generally expected that it would be appropriate to maintain an accommodative stance of monetary policy until these outcomes were achieved"

**Center-of-gravity**: Very strong dovish forward signal — outcome-based criteria tying rate increases to achieving both full employment and above-2% inflation, which implies rates at zero for years.

**Minority views**: "Several" preferred less explicit guidance; one dissenter wanted more flexibility. "A couple" wanted even stronger formulation. But the adopted guidance is itself very dovish.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 72 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants continued to see the uncertainty surrounding the economic outlook as very elevated, with the path of the economy highly dependent on the course of the virus"
- "Participants cited several downside risks that could threaten the recovery... additional virus outbreaks... increases in bankruptcies and defaults"
- "many participants noted that their economic outlook assumed additional fiscal support and that if future fiscal support was significantly smaller or arrived significantly later than they expected, the pace of the recovery could be slower"

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is explicitly described as "very elevated" with the path highly dependent on the virus, fiscal support, and multiple downside risks, though somewhat moderated from the March 2020 peak.

**Minority views**: "A couple" saw upside fiscal risk, but the overwhelming weight is on downside risks.

- Expectations are below target but not at risk of becoming unanchored downward.
