# FOMC Meeting Scoring — 2014-06-18 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Segmented — participant discussion section is clearly separated from staff sections.

**Era context**: Mid-2014. The economy is in mid-recovery from the GFC. The Fed is tapering asset purchases (QE3 wind-down), with rate liftoff still distant. Unemployment is declining but still elevated. Inflation is running below 2% target. The Q1 2014 GDP was surprisingly weak (weather-related), but Q2 is rebounding.

**Quantifier distribution**: "Most" used for balanced risk views and taper-completion mechanics. "Many" for elevated slack and housing concerns. "Several" for labor improvement indicators, wage concerns, international risks. "Some" for consumer spending softness, inflation concerns (both directions), and forward guidance suggestions. "A couple" / "a few" for specific minority views.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target but recently moving up from low levels earlier in the year. "Inflation was still seen as running below the Committee's longer-run objective." "Some participants expressed concern about the persistence of below-trend inflation." However, some others "expected a faster pickup in inflation or saw upside risks." The center of gravity is clearly below target but with recent improvement. Direction: below midpoint. Score should be in the 36-42 range per anchors (2013-19 below target = 38-42).

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Economy is "rebounding" from Q1 decline, expanding at "moderate pace." "Many judged that slack remained elevated." Unemployment "though lower, remained elevated." Fiscal drag diminishing but still present. Housing recovery "remained slow." Direction: below potential. Per bright-line rule, must be < 50. Anchors for 2010-14 recovery = 35-42.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor market conditions generally continued to improve." However, "many judged that slack remained elevated" and "a number of them thought it was greater than measured by the official unemployment rate." Part-time for economic reasons and depressed LFPR cited. Wage growth "modest." Direction: below full employment. Anchors 2011-14 = 35-40.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Household spending "risen moderately." Motor vehicle sales "rising strongly." But "several participants read the recent soft information on retail sales... as raising some concern." Housing recovery "remained slow." Business contacts "generally optimistic" with plans to "increase capital expenditures." Net position: moderate, somewhat below trend given housing weakness and mixed consumer data. Anchors 2011-13 moderate = 35-42, 2014-19 solid = 50-55. This is transitional.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Favorable financial conditions appeared to be supporting economic activity." Increases in C&I lending, pickup in loan demand, better credit quality, small businesses reported improvements. However, mortgage lending "mixed" and housing credit "restrictive." Low implied volatility and "signs of increased risk-taking" suggest loose conditions. Direction: somewhat loose. Higher = tighter, so score below 50.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Continuing to taper ($10B reduction). Forward guidance maintained. "Participants generally supported the Committee's current guidance." Both dovish and hawkish concerns cited: "a number of participants noted their concern that a more gradual approach might be appropriate" vs "some other participants expressed concern that economic growth... might be faster than currently expected... calling for a more rapid move to begin raising the federal funds rate." Center of gravity: accommodative stance continuing, tapering on track, rate liftoff still distant. Anchors 2014-15 pre-liftoff = 40-50.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Most participants viewed the risks... as broadly balanced." The taper completion was viewed as a "technical issue with no substantive macroeconomic consequences." No dissents mentioned. Some differences on inflation outlook and pace of normalization, but generally broad agreement. Direction: moderate agreement with some differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Meaningful discussion of low implied volatility, risk-taking behavior, reach-for-yield concerns. "Participants also discussed whether some recent trends in financial markets might suggest that investors were not appropriately taking account of risks." They agreed to "carefully monitor financial conditions" and noted supervisory measures should address "excessive risk-taking." This is moderate — several participants address it meaningfully.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Taper continuing toward October completion. Rate liftoff still distant — "the eventual decision about the timing of the first increase in the federal funds rate." Data-dependent language. Some want stronger easing commitment ("strengthen its commitment to maintain sufficient policy accommodation"), others want faster normalization. Center: mild easing bias with data dependence, moving toward eventual normalization. Anchors 2014 taper = 8-15... but this is specifically about forward guidance tone. The taper is ending, and discussion is about when to raise rates. The forward signal is mildly toward eventual tightening but very patient.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Most participants viewed the risks to the outlook... as broadly balanced." Some uncertainty about housing, international (Iraq, Ukraine, Japan, China). Q1 GDP weakness created some uncertainty about underlying strength. But overall, "broadly balanced" risks suggest moderate, not elevated uncertainty.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (below 50) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (below 50): Both consistent — economy recovering but still below potential with labor slack. Good.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Both lean dovish/accommodative but moving toward normalization. Consistent.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Below target → score < 50. Check.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Slack remains → score < 50. Check.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Favorable/loose conditions → score < 50. Check.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Accommodative stance, tapering → below 50 but not deep dovish. Check.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 39 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Inflation was still seen as running below the Committee's longer-run objective, longer-run inflation expectations remained stable"
- "readings on a range of price measures... appeared to provide evidence that inflation had moved up recently from low levels earlier in the year"
- "Some participants expressed concern about the persistence of below-trend inflation"

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation below 2% target but showing recent improvement from earlier lows. The center of gravity acknowledges below-target inflation with gradual improvement expected.

**Minority views**: "Some others expected a faster pickup in inflation or saw upside risks to inflation and inflation expectations because they anticipated a more rapid decline in economic slack." This widens the band modestly.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 39 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "economic activity was rebounding in the second quarter following a surprisingly large decline in real GDP in the first quarter"
- "Many judged that slack remained elevated"
- "they continued to expect economic activity to expand at a rate sufficient to lead to a further decline in the unemployment rate to levels close to their current assessments of its longer-run normal value"

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy recovering but still operating below potential, with elevated slack. Q1 weakness was a setback but Q2 is rebounding. Growth expected to be moderate, gradually closing the gap.

**Minority views**: Some see potential output growth as lower than expected (implying gap is smaller), but center of gravity is clearly below potential.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 36 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Many judged that slack remained elevated, and a number of them thought it was greater than measured by the official unemployment rate, citing, in particular, the still-high level of workers employed part time for economic reasons or the depressed labor force participation rate"
- "Aggregate wage measures continued to rise at only a modest rate"
- "Labor market conditions generally continued to improve over the intermeeting period"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market improving but still well below full employment. Significant slack remains, especially when accounting for underemployment and LFPR. Wage growth confirms slack.

**Minority views**: "Several participants pointed out that both long- and short-term unemployment and measures that include marginally attached workers had declined." Some reports of "tight labor markets or shortages of skilled workers... in some areas or occupations."

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 45 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Household spending appeared to have risen moderately, on balance, in recent months, with sales of motor vehicles, in particular, rising strongly"
- "several participants read the recent soft information on retail sales and health-care spending as raising some concern about the underlying strength in consumer spending"
- "Information from participants' business contacts suggested capital spending was likely to increase going forward"
- "the recovery in the housing sector was reported to have remained slow"

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending moderate with some soft spots; business investment showing positive signs; housing still weak. Net position is slightly below trend.

**Minority views**: Motor vehicles strong; some districts report optimistic contacts. But housing weakness and soft retail data pull the overall assessment down.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 30 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Favorable financial conditions appeared to be supporting economic activity"
- "a number of participants reported increases in C&I lending by banks... a pickup in loan demand at banks, or better credit quality for borrowers"
- "low implied volatility in equity, currency, and fixed-income markets as well as signs of increased risk-taking were viewed by some participants as an indication that market participants were not factoring in sufficient uncertainty"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly loose/accommodative, supporting economic activity. Low volatility and risk-taking behavior noted. However, mortgage lending is mixed and housing credit remains restrictive for some borrowers. Overall: loose conditions.

**Minority views**: "restrictive credit conditions, particularly for households with low credit scores" in housing.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 44 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "participants generally supported the Committee's current guidance about the likely path of its asset purchases and about its approach to determining the timing of the first increase in the federal funds rate"
- "a number of participants noted their concern that a more gradual approach might be appropriate if forecasts of above-trend economic growth later this year were not realized"
- "some other participants expressed concern that economic growth over the medium run might be faster than currently expected... calling for a more rapid move to begin raising the federal funds rate"

**Center-of-gravity**: Maintaining accommodation via near-zero rates, continuing taper at measured pace. Forward guidance maintained. Center of gravity is lean-dovish to balanced — still in accommodative mode, but tapering signals gradual normalization ahead. Not deeply dovish (active QE expansion ended) but not yet hawkish.

**Minority views**: Dovish camp wants stronger accommodation commitment; hawkish camp wants faster normalization. These pull in opposite directions, widening the band.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 28 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Most participants viewed the risks to the outlook... as broadly balanced"
- "Most participants viewed this [taper mechanics] as a technical issue with no substantive macroeconomic consequences"
- Differences on inflation outlook and normalization pace noted but within a consensus framework

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on the current course of action (continue taper, maintain forward guidance). Some differences on inflation outlook and pace of future normalization, but no dissents mentioned and general agreement on the policy path.

**Minority views**: Differences exist on inflation risks (some worried about persistence of below-target inflation; others see upside risks) and on normalization pace (some want more gradual, some want faster), but these are within-consensus nuances.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 40 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "participants also discussed whether some recent trends in financial markets might suggest that investors were not appropriately taking account of risks in their investment decisions"
- "low implied volatility... as well as signs of increased risk-taking were viewed by some participants as an indication that market participants were not factoring in sufficient uncertainty"
- "they agreed that the Committee should continue to carefully monitor financial conditions" and "supervisory measures should be applied to address excessive risk-taking and associated financial imbalances"

**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate discussion of financial stability concerns. Several participants addressed reach-for-yield and low volatility meaningfully. The Committee agreed to monitor. This goes beyond passing reference but is not a dominant topic.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 35 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Taper continuing toward completion by October; "participants generally agreed that if incoming information continued to support its expectation... it would be appropriate to complete asset purchases"
- "Some participants suggested that the Committee's communications about its forward guidance should emphasize more strongly that its policy decisions would depend on its ongoing assessment"
- "a couple suggested that the Committee might need to strengthen its commitment to maintain sufficient policy accommodation"
- Rate liftoff framed as distant: "the eventual decision about the timing of the first increase"

**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal is easing lean with data dependence. Taper is winding down (end of QE), but rate liftoff is still framed as distant and data-dependent. The forward tone leans accommodative but is beginning to pivot toward eventual normalization. Anchors for 2014 taper = 8-15 seems too low since the taper itself is nearly complete; the discussion is really about pre-liftoff positioning, which anchors at 25-40.

**Minority views**: Some want stronger accommodation commitment; others want faster normalization.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 32 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Most participants viewed the risks to the outlook for the economy, the labor market, and inflation as broadly balanced"
- Q1 GDP surprise created some uncertainty: "a surprisingly large decline in real GDP in the first quarter"
- International risks mentioned: "developments in Iraq and Ukraine as posing possible downside risks to global economic activity"
- Housing outlook uncertain: range of factors discussed for "either a temporary delay in the housing recovery or a persistently lower level of homebuilding"

**Center-of-gravity**: Risks broadly balanced per most participants, but some specific uncertainties noted (Q1 GDP surprise, housing trajectory, geopolitical). This is modestly above baseline — somewhat elevated but not "particularly uncertain." Standard caveats plus a few specific concerns.
