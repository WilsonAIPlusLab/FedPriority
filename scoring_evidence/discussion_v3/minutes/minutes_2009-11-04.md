# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2009-11-04 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). This is a post-2009 segmented minutes document with clear participant-attributed language.

**Era context**: November 2009 -- roughly 5 months after the official end of the Great Recession (June 2009). The economy is in the very early stages of recovery from the deepest downturn since the Depression. GDP turned positive in Q3 2009, but unemployment is near its peak (~10%). The fed funds rate is at the zero lower bound, and the Fed is conducting large-scale asset purchases.

**Quantifier distribution**: "participants" (collective), "most" (several uses), "many" (a few uses), "some" (several uses), "a few" (not used prominently), "all" (one use -- on communicating exit strategy).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target. "Most participants anticipated that substantial slack in both labor and product markets would likely keep inflation subdued." "Considerable decelerations in wages and unit labor costs" cited as putting downward pressure. "Some" noted commodity/import price pressures could "boost inflation pressures." "Many participants viewed the risks to their inflation outlooks over the next few quarters as being roughly balanced." Some saw downside risk from slack; others saw upside risk from stimulus/expectations. Direction: below target, subdued, but not deflation alarm. Score should be in 20-30 range. Polarity check: higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says subdued/below target = low score. Matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants clearly see the economy as far below potential. "Economic activity was picking up as anticipated" but recovery "at a pace that would be rather slow." "Substantial slack in both labor and product markets." "Low industrial utilization rates." This is a deep negative output gap that is just beginning to close. Direction: well below potential. Score in 20-28 range. Bright-line rule: "substantial slack" language requires score < 50. Matches.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Weakness in labor market conditions remained an important concern." "Unemployment expected to remain elevated for some time." "Unusually large fraction working part time for economic reasons." "Unusually low level of the average workweek." "Business contacts reported cautious hiring." Direction: very loose labor market. Score in 16-22 range. Polarity: higher = tighter. Evidence = very loose. Low score. Matches.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed but weak overall. Consumer spending "picked up more than expected" but "partly reflecting special factors such as cash-for-clunkers." Uncertain job prospects, slow income growth, tight credit weighing on consumer spending. Capital spending: "Investment in E&S appeared to have stabilized" but "cautious business sentiment... likely to keep new capital spending subdued." Inventories improving (slower runoff). Housing: "home sales and construction increased from very low levels" but tentative. CRE: "continued to fall markedly." Direction: below trend but improving from trough. Score around 30-35. Polarity: higher = stronger. Evidence = below trend, tentative improvement. Matches.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Dual picture. Capital markets "substantially better than earlier in the year" -- equity prices up, credit spreads down. But "bank credit remained tight," "banks continued to tighten lending standards," "bank loans continued to contract sharply in all categories." Small firms face "substantial constraints in their access to credit." Large firms have "relatively little difficulty." This is a dichotomy: capital markets easing but banking sector still tight. Net assessment: still somewhat tight, improving. Score around 58-63. Polarity: higher = tighter. Evidence = mixed but still meaningfully tight for bank-dependent borrowers. Matches.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "No substantive changes to the Committee's federal funds target range or large-scale asset purchase programs were warranted." Maintaining 0-1/4% range. "Most members projected unemployment rate would remain quite elevated and inflation below rates consistent with objectives." "All participants agreed it was important for the Federal Reserve to clearly communicate its ability and intent to begin withdrawing monetary policy accommodation at the appropriate time." This is solidly dovish -- maintaining max accommodation, with discussion of exit strategy communication (but no action toward tightening). Score around 18-22. Polarity: higher = more hawkish. Maintaining zero rates + LSAPs = dovish. Matches.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Members agreed that no substantive changes were warranted." Growth risks "roughly balanced" per most. Inflation risks had some divergence: "some saw risks as tilted to the downside" while "others felt that risks were tilted to the upside over a longer horizon." Some concern about self-sustaining recovery vs. not. But no formal dissents mentioned and broad agreement on policy action. Score around 25-30. Moderate range of views but consensus on action.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Substantial discussion of financial conditions -- credit availability, bank lending, CRE stress, dichotomy between capital markets and banking sector, small vs. large firm access. Also dollar depreciation discussion. This is a meaningful but not dominant topic. Score around 50-55.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Continue to state their expectation that economic conditions were likely to warrant exceptionally low rates" for an extended period. Discussion of communicating "ability and intent to begin withdrawing monetary policy accommodation at the appropriate time and pace" -- but this is about communication, not action. The forward signal is firmly dovish: extended low rates. Score around 10-15. Polarity: higher = more hawkish forward signal. "Exceptionally low rates" for extended period = strongly dovish. Matches.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Uncertainty surrounding these forecasts was still viewed as quite elevated." Multiple sources of uncertainty discussed: sustainability of recovery without government support, saving rate behavior, labor market dynamics. "Most participants now viewed the risks to their growth forecasts as being roughly balanced" -- but uncertainty itself remains elevated. Score around 55-62. "Quite elevated" uncertainty language maps to the 55-65 range.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (~24) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (~19): Both well below 50, consistent -- deep slack in both.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (~20) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (~12): Both dovish, with forward guidance even more dovish (extended period language). Coherent -- current stance is max accommodation, forward signal promises continuation.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 23 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "most participants anticipated that substantial slack in both labor and product markets would likely keep inflation subdued"
- "the considerable decelerations in wages and unit labor costs this year were cited as factors putting downward pressure on inflation"
- "some participants noted that the recent rise in the prices of oil and other commodities, as well as increases in import prices stemming from the decline in the foreign exchange value of the dollar, could boost inflation pressures"

**Center of gravity**: Participants see inflation as meaningfully below target, held down by substantial economic slack and decelerating wages. The dominant view is subdued inflation, though a minority flags commodity/import price upside risks.

**Minority views**: Some participants see upside risks from commodities, dollar depreciation, and the possibility that inflation expectations could rise due to extraordinary stimulus. This widens the uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 22 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "substantial slack in both labor and product markets"
- "a modest pace of expansion would imply only slow improvement in the labor market next year, with unemployment remaining high"
- "low industrial utilization rates"

**Center of gravity**: The economy is far below potential. While recovery has begun (Q3 GDP positive), participants see substantial slack that will only close very gradually. Industrial utilization is low and businesses are cautious.

**Minority views**: None meaningfully diverge on the presence of a large output gap. Uncertainty is about the pace of closing, not its existence.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 18 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "weakness in labor market conditions remained an important concern to meeting participants, with unemployment expected to remain elevated for some time"
- "the unusually large fraction of those who were working part time for economic reasons and the unusually low level of the average workweek pointed to only a gradual decline in the unemployment rate"
- "business contacts reported that they would be cautious in their hiring and would continue to aggressively seek cost savings"

**Center of gravity**: The labor market is deeply slack. Unemployment is near 10%, involuntary part-time work is unusually elevated, and businesses plan to delay hiring by boosting hours and productivity first.

**Minority views**: None. Universal agreement on severe labor market weakness.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 32 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "consumer spending appeared to be rising even apart from the effects of fiscal incentives to purchase autos" but "participants saw that increase as partly reflecting special factors"
- "cautious business sentiment, together with low industrial utilization rates, was likely to keep new capital spending subdued"
- "CRE activity continued to fall markedly in most Districts"

**Center of gravity**: Spending and investment are below trend. Consumer spending is picking up but remains constrained by weak labor markets, tight credit, and reduced wealth. Business investment has stabilized but is subdued. CRE is a notable drag.

**Minority views**: Some note "consumer spending on items other than autos had been stronger than expected, which might be signaling more underlying momentum."

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 60 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "financial market conditions... were substantially better than earlier in the year" with "equity prices considerably higher, private credit spreads substantially lower"
- "bank credit remained tight... banks continued to tighten lending standards for C&I loans and consumer loans... bank loans continued to contract sharply in all categories"
- "smaller firms, which tend to be more dependent on commercial banks for financing, reportedly faced substantial constraints in their access to credit"

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions present a dichotomy. Capital markets have improved substantially, but bank lending remains tight and contracting. For the broad economy (especially small firms and consumers), conditions remain meaningfully tighter than normal.

**Minority views**: The split is not among participants but across market segments. All participants appear to recognize this dichotomy.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 20 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "members agreed that no substantive changes to the Committee's federal funds target range or large-scale asset purchase programs were warranted"
- "most members projected that over the next couple of years, the unemployment rate would remain quite elevated and the level of inflation would remain below rates consistent over the longer run with the Federal Reserve's objectives"
- "all participants agreed that it was important for... the Federal Reserve to continue to clearly communicate its ability and intent to begin withdrawing monetary policy accommodation at the appropriate time and pace"

**Center of gravity**: Strongly dovish. The Committee maintains maximum accommodation (zero rates + LSAPs) and sees no need for change given elevated unemployment and below-target inflation. The exit-strategy communication discussion is about credibility, not imminent tightening.

**Minority views**: Some participants express concern about longer-run inflation risks from stimulus, but none advocate for policy changes now.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 28 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "members agreed that no substantive changes... were warranted" (consensus on action)
- On inflation risks: "Some saw the risks as tilted to the downside in the near term... But others felt that risks were tilted to the upside over a longer horizon"
- "some participants expressed concerns about the ability of the economy to generate a self-sustaining recovery without government support"

**Center of gravity**: Broad agreement on policy action with no formal dissents. However, there are notable differences in inflation risk assessment (downside vs. upside) and in the degree of optimism about the recovery's sustainability. These are assessment differences, not policy differences.

**Minority views**: The inflation risk split and recovery sustainability concerns create moderate (but not unusual) intellectual disagreement.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 52 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Extended discussion of the "dichotomy between significant easing of conditions in capital markets and continuing tight conditions in the banking sector"
- "rising levels of nonperforming loans expected to continue to be a source of stress, and with many regional and small banks vulnerable to the deteriorating performance of CRE loans"
- "the recent fall in the foreign exchange value of the dollar had been orderly... but that any tendency for dollar depreciation to intensify... would bear close watching"

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions receive moderate-to-substantial attention. The bank stress/CRE nexus, the capital market vs. banking dichotomy, and dollar dynamics are all discussed meaningfully. Financial stability is not the dominant topic (that role belongs to the output/labor assessment), but it receives considerable attention.

**Minority views**: N/A -- broadly shared concern.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 12 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "continue to state their expectation that economic conditions were likely to warrant exceptionally low rates" for an extended period
- "the Federal Reserve to continue to clearly communicate its ability and intent to begin withdrawing monetary policy accommodation at the appropriate time and pace" (exit communication, not exit itself)

**Center of gravity**: The forward signal is strongly dovish -- extended period of exceptionally low rates. The exit-strategy discussion is about communication credibility, not about imminent tightening. The forward lean is firmly toward continued maximum accommodation.

**Minority views**: Some participants' concern about longer-run inflation risks suggests they may prefer earlier withdrawal, but this does not translate into forward guidance language changes.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 58 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "uncertainty surrounding these forecasts was still viewed as quite elevated"
- "it was not clear how much of the recent firming in final demand reflected the effects of temporary fiscal programs"
- "households' saving behavior was an important source of uncertainty in the outlook"

**Center of gravity**: Participants express elevated uncertainty about the recovery's sustainability and pace. Multiple sources of uncertainty are identified: fiscal program effects, household saving behavior, labor market dynamics, credit availability. The word "quite elevated" maps to meaningfully above normal.

**Minority views**: "Most participants now viewed the risks to their growth forecasts as being roughly balanced" -- the risk balance has improved even though uncertainty level remains high.

## Optional Dimensions

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS
**Score: 42 +/-10 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the possibility that inflation expectations could begin to decline in response to the low level of actual inflation" (some, downside)
- "the possibility that inflation expectations could rise as a result of the public's concerns about extraordinary monetary policy stimulus and large federal budget deficits" (others, upside)
- "To keep inflation expectations anchored, all participants agreed that it was important for policy to be responsive"

**Center of gravity**: Expectations are roughly anchored near or slightly below target, but participants see risks in both directions. The explicit concern about keeping them anchored suggests they are currently viewed as satisfactorily anchored but under some strain.

### CREDIT_STRESS
**Score: 65 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "bank credit remained tight... banks continued to tighten lending standards... bank loans continued to contract sharply in all categories"
- "smaller firms... reportedly faced substantial constraints in their access to credit"
- Capital market spreads "substantially lower" than earlier in the year

**Center of gravity**: Meaningful credit stress persists in banking sector, particularly for small firms and consumers. Capital markets have improved but bank lending remains severely constrained.

### BANKING_STRESS
**Score: 68 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "rising levels of nonperforming loans expected to continue to be a source of stress"
- "many regional and small banks vulnerable to the deteriorating performance of CRE loans"
- Banks "continued to tighten lending standards" and loans "continued to contract sharply"

**Center of gravity**: Banking sector under significant stress from nonperforming loans and CRE exposure. Regional and small banks particularly vulnerable.

### DOLLAR_CONCERN
**Score: 42 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the recent fall in the foreign exchange value of the dollar had been orderly and appeared to reflect an unwinding of safe-haven demand"
- "any tendency for dollar depreciation to intensify or to put significant upward pressure on inflation would bear close watching"

**Center of gravity**: Participants note dollar weakness but view it as orderly and fundamentally driven. The concern leans slightly toward weakness (depreciation) rather than strength. Score below 50 reflects concern about dollar weakness (low on the "strength concern" scale). Note: The rubric defines higher = more concern about dollar STRENGTH. The concern here is about dollar WEAKNESS, so score should be below 50.
