# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2022-03-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented format — participant views are clearly separated from staff review sections.

**Era context**: March 2022 — inflation running at multi-decade highs (PCE 6.1%, CPI 7.9%), very tight labor market (unemployment 3.8%), Russia-Ukraine war just started (Feb 24, 2022), first rate hike of the cycle (25bp). Supply chain disruptions persisting. This was the liftoff meeting.

**Quantifier distribution**: Heavy use of "all participants" and "participants agreed" (strong consensus language). "Many" used for those preferring 50bp and for those expecting future 50bp hikes. "Several" used for upside inflation risk from war. "A few" for signs of labor supply improvement and inflation expectations concerns. "Some" for broadening inflation into services and near-term inflation expectations sensitivity. "A couple" for various secondary points.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as far above target. "Inflation remained elevated" — "all participants concurred... inflation was high and well above the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective." "Recent inflation readings continued to significantly exceed the Committee's longer-run goal." Inflation broadening from goods into services. Russia-Ukraine adding upward pressure. Trimmed mean at highest since early 1980s. The March 2022 anchor range is 82-88 for peak 2022. This is early in 2022, not yet the peak quarter. PCE at 6.1% — very high but not yet the absolute peak. Inflation was clearly well above target, broadening, with upside risks. Direction: well above midpoint. Score should be in 76-85 range.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "All participants concurred that the U.S. economy was very strong" — "above-trend growth to continue, sustaining a strong labor market" — "the level of real GDP was expected to remain well above potential." GDP growth had slowed from Q4 2021 but fundamentals solid. The bright-line rule: "above potential" language means > 50. "Well above potential" and "very strong" push into 59-70 range. Anchor for 2022 is 57-63. Score ~60.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Extremely tight labor market" — "demand for labor continued to substantially exceed available supply" — "various indicators pointed to a very tight labor market" — "quits and job openings were at all-time highs" — unemployment at post-pandemic low of 3.8%. Wages rising at fastest pace in many years. "A few" noted labor supply improving. Anchor for 2022-23 is 65-75. "Extremely tight" and "substantially exceed" push toward upper end. Score ~73.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumption and business investment continued to rise solidly" — GDP growth slowed from Q4 but underlying demand solid. "Economic fundamentals remained solid." Business contacts not seeing demand pullback. Housing demand remained strong. Anchor for 2021 Q3-2022 Q1 is 62-68. "Solidly" is positive but not boom language. Growth was slowing from rapid pace. Score ~60.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Participants noted "tighter financial conditions" from both Committee communications and Ukraine invasion. "Financing conditions remained accommodative, although borrowing costs increased further" (staff section). Several participants noted Committee communications had "contributed to a tightening of financial conditions." Corporate spreads widened to median historical levels. Liquidity strained in some markets. Credit still broadly available. Conditions tightening but not yet restrictive. Anchor for 2022 rate hikes is 60-70. But this is very early — just liftoff. Conditions are tightening from accommodative, so mid-range. Score ~55.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All agreed to begin raising rates. "All participants indicated their strong commitment and determination to take the measures necessary to restore price stability." "Many" preferred 50bp at this meeting but settled for 25bp due to Ukraine uncertainty. "Many" noted 50bp increases could be appropriate at future meetings. "Move the stance of monetary policy toward a neutral posture expeditiously" — and "depending on developments, a move to a tighter policy stance could be warranted." One formal dissent (Bullard) wanting 50bp. This is a strong hawkish meeting — liftoff with signals of aggressive further tightening. Anchor: 70-75 for 2017-18 hiking, 80-85 for late 2022 75bp hikes. This is between those — the first hike with strong forward hawkish signal. Score ~76.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal dissent (50bp preferred vs 25bp delivered). "Many" preferred 50bp but acquiesced to 25bp due to Ukraine. The disagreement was about pace, not direction — all agreed on tightening. Broad agreement on direction with moderate tactical disagreement. Anchor: 25-35 for typical one-dissent meeting, but the "many preferred 50bp" pushes this higher — significant number wanted more. Score ~38.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Considerable discussion of financial conditions — strained liquidity in some markets, Ukraine-related volatility, corporate spread widening. Balance sheet reduction plans discussed at length. But financial stability was not the dominant concern — inflation was. Moderate discussion level. Score ~40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Very hawkish forward signal. "Ongoing increases in the target range would be appropriate." "Many" noted 50bp increases at future meetings. Move to neutral "expeditiously." Possibly tighter than neutral. Balance sheet runoff to begin as early as May. This is a strong forward tightening signal. Anchor for 2022 aggressive hiking is 80-95. This is early in the cycle — signaling further hikes but not yet at the 75bp-per-meeting phase. Score ~82.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The economic outlook was highly uncertain" — "implications of the war for the U.S. economy were highly uncertain." Multiple uncertainty sources cited: Russia-Ukraine, supply chains, inflation persistence, China lockdowns. "Participants agreed that uncertainty regarding the path of inflation was elevated." But participants still felt confident in their policy direction. "Highly uncertain" with multiple risk factors. Score ~65.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (60) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (73): Both above 50, labor tighter than output gap — consistent with supply-constrained economy where labor was the binding constraint.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (76) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (82): Forward guidance slightly more hawkish than current action (25bp hike but signaling 50bp ahead). Coherent — the current action was moderated by Ukraine uncertainty but forward tone was aggressive.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says very high inflation. Score 80 — matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says "well above potential." Score 60 — matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence says conditions tightening but still somewhat accommodative. Score 55 — matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: **80** ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All participants concurred that... inflation was high and well above the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective."
- "Recent inflation readings continued to significantly exceed the Committee's longer-run goal" and Russia's invasion "will add to near-term inflation pressures."
- "Some participants noted that elevated inflation had continued to broaden from goods into services, especially rents."

**Center of gravity**: Participants unanimously viewed inflation as very high and broadening, with upside risks from the Ukraine war. Not yet the peak 2022 readings, but clearly in the "substantially above target" range.

**Minority views**: "Several other participants noted that longer-term measures of inflation expectations... still appeared to remain well anchored," providing some balance on the outlook, though not disputing current elevated readings.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: **60** ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All participants concurred that the U.S. economy was very strong."
- "Participants... expected above-trend growth to continue, sustaining a strong labor market."
- Staff projected "the level of real GDP was expected to remain well above potential over the projection period."

**Center of gravity**: Economy clearly operating above potential with strong demand, though growth was slowing from its rapid 2021 pace. Ukraine adding downside risk to growth but fundamentals solid.

**Minority views**: "A couple of participants commented that the increased uncertainty might lead businesses and consumers to reduce spending," but contacts were not yet seeing such shifts.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: **73** ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All participants concurred that the U.S. economy was very strong, with an extremely tight labor market."
- "Demand for labor continued to substantially exceed available supply across many parts of the economy."
- "The unemployment rate had fallen to a post-pandemic low, and quits and job openings were at all-time highs."

**Center of gravity**: Near-universal agreement on extremely tight labor market conditions. Wages rising rapidly and pressures spreading across income/skill distributions.

**Minority views**: "A few participants noted that there were signs that the pandemic-related factors that had held back labor supply might be abating," pointing to slight easing ahead, but not disputing current extreme tightness.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: **60** ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Consumption and business investment continued to rise solidly."
- "Economic fundamentals remained solid and... participants expected above-trend growth to continue."
- GDP growth slowed from Q4 2021 "largely reflecting weaker inventory investment."

**Center of gravity**: Solid consumer spending and business investment growth, above trend but slowing from the rapid 2021 pace. Housing demand strong but supply-constrained. Ukraine war posing some downside risk.

**Minority views**: Some noted Ukraine could weigh on activity, but "business contacts currently were not seeing signs of such shifts or expecting a significant pullback in demand."

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: **55** ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "A number of participants noted that the Committee's previous communications had already contributed to a tightening of financial conditions, as evident in the notable increase in longer-term interest rates."
- "The invasion and related events... contributing to tighter financial conditions."
- Staff noted "financing conditions remained accommodative, although borrowing costs increased further" and "credit remained broadly available for most types of borrowers."

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions were tightening from an accommodative baseline due to rate expectations and geopolitical risk. Spreads widened, liquidity strained in some markets, but credit still broadly available. Tighter than neutral but not restrictive.

**Minority views**: Some concern about strained liquidity in Treasury and equity markets, but broad conditions still supportive.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: **76** ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All participants indicated their strong commitment and determination to take the measures necessary to restore price stability."
- "Many participants noted that... they would have preferred a 50 basis point increase in the target range for the federal funds rate at this meeting."
- "Participants judged that it would be appropriate to move the stance of monetary policy toward a neutral posture expeditiously... a move to a tighter policy stance could be warranted."

**Center of gravity**: Very strong hawkish consensus. All agreed on tightening; many wanted faster action. One dissent for 50bp. Forward signals aggressive. Only constraint on hawkishness was Ukraine uncertainty causing a tactical step down from 50bp to 25bp at this particular meeting.

**Minority views**: None on the dovish side. The tactical disagreement was between 25bp and 50bp at this meeting, with "many" favoring the latter.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: **38** ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "One member preferred to raise the target range for the federal funds rate by 0.5 percentage point."
- "Many participants noted that... they would have preferred a 50 basis point increase" but settled for 25bp due to Ukraine uncertainty.
- All agreed on direction; disagreement was on pace.

**Center of gravity**: One formal dissent plus a significant minority that preferred faster action. Disagreement was tactical (pace), not directional, which moderates the intensity. The "many preferred 50bp" language elevates this above a typical one-dissent meeting.

**Minority views**: The 50bp camp was large but ultimately acquiesced, limiting the formal dissent count.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: **40** ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Extensive discussion of balance sheet reduction plans and market functioning considerations.
- "Participants... noted that developments associated with Russia's invasion of Ukraine posed heightened risks for both the United States and the global economy."
- Discussion of strained liquidity in some markets and spread widening.

**Center of gravity**: Financial stability/conditions were discussed meaningfully but were secondary to inflation and labor market concerns. Balance sheet discussion was substantial but focused on operational details rather than stability fears.

**Minority views**: "A couple of participants commented that... the increased uncertainty might lead businesses and consumers to reduce spending" — some concern about financial spillovers from Ukraine.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: **82** ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Ongoing increases in the target range for the federal funds rate would be warranted."
- "Many participants noted that one or more 50 basis point increases... could be appropriate at future meetings, particularly if inflation pressures remained elevated or intensified."
- "Participants judged that it would be appropriate to move the stance of monetary policy toward a neutral posture expeditiously... a move to a tighter policy stance could be warranted."

**Center of gravity**: Very hawkish forward signal — not only ongoing rate increases anticipated, but 50bp increments explicitly on the table, plus balance sheet runoff starting potentially in May. Language of "expeditiously" and potential for above-neutral rates.

**Minority views**: Some participants emphasized data dependence and nimbleness, but no one signaled a pause or slower path.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: **65** ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants agreed that the economic outlook was highly uncertain."
- "The implications of the war for the U.S. economy were highly uncertain."
- "Participants agreed that uncertainty regarding the path of inflation was elevated and that risks to inflation were weighted to the upside."

**Center of gravity**: Elevated uncertainty driven by multiple factors — Ukraine war, supply chain disruptions, inflation persistence, China lockdowns. Multiple uses of "highly uncertain." However, participants remained confident in their policy direction, suggesting genuine uncertainty about outcomes but not paralysis.

**Minority views**: Wide agreement on elevated uncertainty; differences were more about which risks dominated (upside inflation vs. downside growth).
