# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2005-05-03

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Unsegmented pre-2009 format — staff and participant content potentially mixed, but this excerpt is clearly participant discussion, using quantifier language throughout ("all members," "most members," "some participants," "a few members," "many").

**Era context**: May 2005 — mid-tightening cycle. The Fed has been raising rates at a "measured pace" since June 2004. Funds rate going from 2.75% to 3.00% at this meeting. Economy was growing solidly but energy prices were a concern. Housing was hot. This is pre-crisis, with accommodative financial conditions still prevailing.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly above neutral, with upside risks. Evidence: "Core measures of price inflation had moved up over recent quarters and particularly so over the last few months." "Risks to that inflation outlook now might be skewed somewhat to the upside." But also: "measures of core inflation were thought likely to remain in check over the remainder of this year and next" and "underlying inflation appeared to remain contained." The center of gravity is that inflation has ticked up but is expected to stay contained — modestly above midpoint. Direction: somewhat above neutral. Score ~57. Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says uptick but contained. Matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see economy as slightly below potential with "remaining slack in resource utilization" to be "gradually eliminated." Growth has slowed recently but fundamentals expected to support continued expansion. The bright-line rule: "remaining slack" = below 50. But they also say "sufficient to gradually eliminate remaining slack," meaning close to potential. Score ~46. Small remaining slack.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Indicators of wages and benefits had registered only modest growth, suggesting to many that some slack in labor markets persisted." This is below full employment but not dramatically so. Score ~43.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Capital expenditures "advanced briskly" but below prior pace, "unexpected weakness in capital goods orders." Consumer spending "pace of consumption growth had fallen off appreciably." But fundamentals "strong income growth and low interest rates augured well" and capex "seen as likely to remain quite robust." Net: slightly below trend due to recent softening, but not deeply so. Score ~46.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Equity prices had fallen and risk spreads had widened" but "these financial developments did not appear to signal the onset of a sharp retrenchment in investors' willingness to bear risk." "All members regarded the stance of policy as accommodative." Low interest rates mentioned as supportive. Financial conditions still loose but marginally less so. Score ~25 (loose, consistent with 2005 anchor of 10-20, but spreads widening nudges it slightly higher).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All members favored raising the target federal funds rate 25 basis points." "All members regarded the stance of policy as accommodative" and "too low to be consistent with sustainable growth and stable prices in the long run." They agreed to continue "removing policy accommodation at a measured pace." Forward lean is clearly for continued tightening. But there's recognition of downside growth risks and "a few members" flagged larger-than-expected moderation possible. Score ~68 — moderate hawkish, consensus for continued tightening at measured pace.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All members favored raising the target federal funds rate 25 basis points" — unanimous. "All members agreed to retain the forward-looking language." But there were differences: "some members" wanted to eliminate forward-looking language, "a few members" worried about over-tightening. These are emphasis differences, not fundamental disagreement. Score ~22 — broad agreement with minor nuances.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Housing speculation mentioned ("signs of possible speculative excesses in some areas"), equity price declines and risk spread widening noted, but not dwelt upon extensively. A few passing references. Score ~25.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Policy accommodation could be removed at a pace that is likely to be measured" retained. This signals continued tightening. "Most members viewed the forward-looking language... as a reasonable characterization." Some wanted to pare it back for flexibility. The forward signal is for continued measured tightening — hawkish direction. Score ~68.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Broad recognition of greater uncertainty attending the outlook for both inflation and output growth." "For many, heightened economic uncertainty in the current environment implied greater uncertainty about the range of possible policy outcomes." "Considerable uncertainty surrounded the degree of pass-through from energy prices." This is genuinely elevated uncertainty. Score ~52.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (46) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (43): Both slightly below 50, consistent — remaining slack in both.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (68) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (68): Aligned — continued measured tightening.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (25) low despite tightening cycle — conditions still accommodative, consistent with text.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 57 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Core measures of price inflation had moved up over recent quarters and particularly so over the last few months."
- "Participants voiced concerns about recent price trends; they expected inflation to remain contained but also perceived that the risks to that inflation outlook now might be skewed somewhat to the upside."
- "Measures of core inflation were thought likely to remain in check over the remainder of this year and next."
**Center-of-gravity**: Participants see a recent uptick in core inflation with upside risks, but expect it to remain contained. Modestly above neutral.
**Minority views**: Some see more pass-through risk from energy; the upside risk language is broader than "a few."

### OUTPUT_GAP: 46 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Economic fundamentals... were expected to support economic growth at a pace sufficient to gradually eliminate remaining slack in resource utilization."
- "Most members regarded the recent slower growth of economic activity as likely to be transitory."
**Center-of-gravity**: Small remaining slack in the economy, with growth expected to close it gradually. Slightly below potential.
**Minority views**: "A few members" worried cumulative tightening could slow demand more than expected.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 43 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Available indicators of wages and benefits had registered only modest growth, suggesting to many that some slack in labor markets persisted."
- "The behavior of compensation suggesting a lack of pressure in labor markets."
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market has some remaining slack — below full employment but not dramatically so. Modest wage growth confirms this.
**Minority views**: None explicitly hawkish on labor.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 46 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Capital expenditures advanced briskly over the first quarter, but at a pace significantly below that registered over the latter half of last year."
- "The pace of consumption growth had fallen off appreciably toward the end of the first quarter."
- "Capital expenditures were seen as likely to remain quite robust, spurred by strong economic fundamentals."
**Center-of-gravity**: Recent softening in both consumption and investment, but fundamentals viewed as solid. Slightly below trend on net.
**Minority views**: "Some participants worried about the potential for continued sluggishness in consumer spending."

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 25 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All members regarded the stance of policy as accommodative."
- "Economic fundamentals including low interest rates" supporting growth.
- "Equity prices had fallen and risk spreads had widened" but "did not appear to signal the onset of a sharp retrenchment."
**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions remain accommodative/loose despite some recent tightening in spreads and equity prices.
**Minority views**: The spread widening and equity declines represent marginal tightening but are not viewed as problematic.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 68 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All members favored raising the target federal funds rate 25 basis points to 3 percent."
- "All members regarded the stance of policy as accommodative and judged that the current level of short-term rates remained too low."
- "Members agreed that they should continue along the course of removing policy accommodation at a measured pace."
**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous support for continued tightening; policy still seen as too accommodative. Moderate hawkish.
**Minority views**: "A few members" flagged risk of over-tightening; "some" wanted more flexibility in forward language.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 22 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All members favored raising the target" — unanimous on action.
- "All members agreed to retain the forward-looking language" — unanimous on communication.
- Minor differences: "some members commented that this greater uncertainty called for eliminating or paring back forward-looking language."
**Center-of-gravity**: Broad consensus on both action and communication, with only modest differences about emphasis and forward-looking language.
**Minority views**: Differences about forward-looking language timing, not substance.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 25 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "House price appreciation was expected to moderate... but a number of local real estate markets were still regarded as 'hot,' with signs of possible speculative excesses in some areas."
- "Equity prices had fallen and risk spreads had widened."
**Center-of-gravity**: Brief mentions of housing speculation and market conditions, but not a major focus of discussion.
**Minority views**: None prominent.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 68 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Most members viewed the forward-looking language... including the characterization of the stance of policy as accommodative as well as the judgment that policy accommodation could be removed at a pace that is 'likely to be measured' — as a reasonable characterization."
- "Some members commented that this greater uncertainty called for eliminating or paring back forward-looking language."
**Center-of-gravity**: Clear signal of continued measured tightening, though with growing debate about flexibility. Moderately hawkish forward signal.
**Minority views**: "Some" wanted to drop forward-looking language for more flexibility — this could imply either more or less hawkish action.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "There was also broad recognition of greater uncertainty attending the outlook for both inflation and output growth."
- "For many, heightened economic uncertainty in the current environment implied greater uncertainty about the range of possible policy outcomes and placed a premium on flexibility."
- "Considerable uncertainty surrounded the degree of pass-through from energy prices to core consumer prices."
**Center-of-gravity**: Genuinely elevated uncertainty acknowledged broadly — above normal but not extreme. Multiple sources of uncertainty identified.
**Minority views**: Uncertainty is broadly shared, not a minority view.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 55 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "A discernable upcreep was apparent in survey measures of short- and, to a limited extent, long-term inflation expectations over recent months."
- "Market measures of inflation compensation had ebbed in recent weeks, and survey measures of long-term inflation expectations... remained in the broad range of recent years."
**Center-of-gravity**: Slight uptick in expectations but still broadly anchored.

### INFLATION_HOUSING: 65 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "A number of local real estate markets were still regarded as 'hot,' with signs of possible speculative excesses in some areas."
- "Home sales and other indicators of activity in the residential real estate market remained at very high levels."
**Center-of-gravity**: Strong housing price pressures in many markets, with speculation concerns.

### CREDIT_STRESS: 35 ±8 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Risk spreads had widened" but overall conditions still accommodative.
- "Low cost of capital" cited as supporting investment.
**Center-of-gravity**: Below-normal stress (spreads still relatively tight in historical context) but some widening from compressed levels.
