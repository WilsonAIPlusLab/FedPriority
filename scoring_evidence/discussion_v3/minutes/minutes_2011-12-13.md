**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented format with clear "Participants' Views" and "Committee Policy Action" sections separated from staff content.

**Era context**: December 2011 -- roughly 2.5 years after the GFC trough. The economy is in a slow recovery with elevated unemployment, Operation Twist underway, fed funds at 0-0.25%, and the European sovereign debt crisis looming as a major downside risk.

**Quantifier distribution**: "agreed" (all), "most" (inflation moderation), "many" (Europe financial markets, commodity price trends, slack), "several" (cautious optimism, labor market slow, credit for contacts, community banks, forward guidance), "some" (consumer spending not sustained, households adjusting, contacts on pricing, inflation concerns both directions), "a few" (pent-up demand, accommodative eroding expectations), "one/another" (various individual views).

**Note**: The document is truncated mid-sentence in the policy action section. The forward guidance discussion is cut off. Scoring will proceed based on available content.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW target and moderating. "Inflation had moderated from the rates earlier in the year." "Most participants anticipated that inflation would continue to moderate." "Many participants judged that the moderate expansion... and the associated gradual reduction in the current wide margins of slack... would be consistent with subdued inflation going forward." "Some expressed the concern that... inflation might run below mandate-consistent levels for some time." However, minority views: "a couple of participants noted that the rate of inflation over the past year had not fallen as much as would be expected" and "some participants were concerned that inflation could rise as the recovery continued." Center of gravity is clearly toward subdued/below-target inflation. Score should be in the 30-38 range. Polarity check: higher = more pressure. Evidence says low pressure. Score below 50 -- matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as BELOW potential with significant slack. "Economic activity was expanding at a moderate rate" but "wide margins of slack in labor and product markets." "The unemployment rate would decline only gradually." Multiple headwinds cited: European crisis, fiscal tightening, weak housing, household deleveraging. Bright-line rule: "slack remains" language --> score MUST be < 50. The economy is growing moderately but from well below potential. Score ~34. Anchor: 35-42 for 2010-14 recovery fits.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see the labor market as LOOSE with some improvement. "Unemployment rate remained elevated despite a noticeable drop in November." "Part of the recent decline in the jobless rate was associated with a reduction in labor force participation, the drop in the unemployment rate likely overstated the overall improvement." "Unemployment, particularly longer-term unemployment, remained high." "Very modest increases in labor compensation of late suggested that underutilization of labor was still significant." Some improvement noted (5 months of payroll gains, claims drifting lower) but level remains very slack. Score ~32. Anchor: 35-40 for 2011-14 fits, but evidence of significant underutilization suggests low end of that range.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Participants see spending as MIXED -- consumer spending somewhat stronger than expected but investment decelerating and housing very weak. "Consumer spending in recent months had been stronger than expected" but "business fixed investment appeared to be decelerating" and "home sales and construction remained at very low levels." Several questioned sustainability of consumer spending. "Businesses generally reported that they remained cautious regarding capital spending and hiring." Net: modestly below trend. Score ~38. Anchor: 35-42 for 2011-13 "moderate" fits.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see financial conditions as SOMEWHAT TIGHT, driven primarily by European contagion risk. "Financial markets remained volatile... because of developments in Europe." "Dollar funding conditions facing European institutions... were still strained." However, domestically: "participants generally saw little evidence of significant new constraints on credit availability for domestic borrowers." "Several participants noted that their contacts had ready access to credit at attractive rates." But "some participants continued to view credit as tight, particularly in mortgage markets or among small businesses." Mixed picture with European stress but reasonably functional domestic credit. Score ~52 -- slightly above neutral, reflecting European-driven volatility and pockets of domestic tightness.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Participants lean DOVISH. "Almost all members agreed to maintain the existing stance of monetary policy." They are continuing Operation Twist, keeping rates at zero, reinvesting proceeds. The discussion is about downside risks to growth, significant slack, moderating inflation. No discussion of tightening. Forward guidance (truncated) was being discussed. This is firmly in accommodation-maintenance territory. Score ~22. Anchor: 15-25 for QE era 2010-12 fits.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Almost all members agreed" -- implying not fully unanimous but very close. There are diverse views on inflation outlook (some worried about below-mandate, others about above), labor market slack (cyclical vs. structural debate), and consumer spending sustainability. But these are differences of emphasis, not fundamental policy disagreement at this meeting. Score ~28. Anchor: 25-35 typical one-dissent meeting.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: European financial crisis is a PROMINENT theme. "Strains in global financial markets continued to pose significant downside risks." Extended discussion of European sovereign debt, banking issues, swap lines, European lenders pulling back. Also discussion of domestic banking conditions, community bank strains, credit availability. This is a meaningful component of the discussion. Score ~52. Anchor: 40-55 for pre-crisis-adjacent periods.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The document is truncated precisely where forward guidance is discussed. From what is available: rates at 0-0.25%, Operation Twist continuing, reinvestment ongoing. The overall tone is strongly accommodative with no suggestion of near-term tightening. "Several members noted that the forward guidance..." (truncated). Based on the known December 2011 statement language (which maintained "at least through mid-2013" guidance), and the dovish tone of the entire discussion, score ~10. This is strong easing guidance -- extended accommodation ahead. Confidence is reduced due to truncation.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Participants express ELEVATED uncertainty. "High level of uncertainty about the economic outlook and the political environment." "Strains in global financial markets continued to pose significant downside risks." "Uncertain about the economic outlook." "Uncertain fiscal outlook." Multiple headwinds cited with uncertain resolution. "Participants expressed a range of views on the current extent of slack in the labor market." Score ~55. Anchor: somewhat above the 40-50 range for trade tensions era -- the European crisis adds genuine uncertainty.

- OUTPUT_GAP (34) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (32): Both well below 50, consistent -- economy below potential with slack labor market. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (22) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (10): Both dovish, with forward guidance more dovish (threshold-based zero-rate commitment). ## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 33 +/-8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Inflation had moderated from the rates earlier in the year, and longer-term inflation expectations remained stable."
- "Most participants anticipated that inflation would continue to moderate."
- "Many participants judged that the moderate expansion in economic activity... and the associated gradual reduction in the current wide margins of slack in labor and product markets would be consistent with subdued inflation going forward."
- "Some expressed the concern that, with the persistence of considerable resource slack, inflation might run below mandate-consistent levels for some time."

Center of gravity: Participants see inflation as having moderated and trending further downward, with wide slack expected to keep it subdued. Most see inflation settling at or below mandate-consistent levels.

Minority views: "A couple of participants noted that the rate of inflation over the past year had not fallen as much as would be expected." "Some participants were concerned that inflation could rise as the recovery continued." "A few participants argued that maintaining a highly accommodative stance... would erode the stability of inflation expectations." These upside-risk views widen the uncertainty band.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 34 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Economic activity was expanding at a moderate rate."
- "Wide margins of slack in labor and product markets."
- "The unemployment rate would decline only gradually."
- Headwinds: "European fiscal/banking issues," "slowdown abroad," "fiscal tightening in the United States," "high levels of uncertainty," "weak housing market," "household deleveraging."

Center of gravity: Economy is well below potential with moderate growth insufficient to close the gap quickly. Multiple headwinds restrain recovery.

Minority views: Narrow spread -- participants broadly agree on the below-potential assessment.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 32 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "The unemployment rate remained elevated despite a noticeable drop in November."
- "Part of the recent decline in the jobless rate was associated with a reduction in labor force participation, the drop in the unemployment rate likely overstated the overall improvement."
- "Unemployment, particularly longer-term unemployment, remained high, and the number of involuntary part-time workers was still elevated."
- "The very modest increases in labor compensation of late suggested that underutilization of labor was still significant."

Center of gravity: Labor market has significant slack. Some improvement in payrolls and claims but the level remains far from full employment.

Minority views: Debate on cyclical vs. structural unemployment widens the band. "Some part of the increase in unemployment since the beginning of the recession had been structural rather than cyclical" -- if structural component is large, effective slack is smaller than headline suggests.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 38 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Consumer spending in recent months had been stronger than expected."
- "Business fixed investment appeared to be decelerating."
- "Home sales and construction remained at very low levels."
- "Businesses generally reported that they remained cautious regarding capital spending and hiring."
- "A number of those participants suggested that the recent stronger pace of consumer spending might not be sustained."

Center of gravity: Net spending picture is below trend -- consumer spending is a positive surprise but questioned as sustainable, while business investment is decelerating and housing remains depressed.

Minority views: "A few participants thought that the recent strength in motor vehicle sales and other consumer spending could reflect pent-up demand... and so thought that it might persist for a time."

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 52 +/-8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Financial markets remained volatile over the intermeeting period in large part because of developments in Europe."
- "Dollar funding conditions facing European institutions... were still strained."
- "Participants generally saw little evidence of significant new constraints on credit availability for domestic borrowers."
- "Several participants noted that their contacts had ready access to credit at attractive rates."
- "Some participants continued to view credit as tight, particularly in mortgage markets or among small businesses."

Center of gravity: Conditions are near neutral domestically but with pockets of tightness (mortgages, small business) and significant stress from European spillover volatility.

Minority views: Split between those seeing credit as freely available and those seeing continued tightness in specific sectors.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 22 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Almost all members agreed to maintain the existing stance of monetary policy at this meeting."
- Continuing Operation Twist, reinvestment, and zero rates.
- No discussion of reducing accommodation; discussion focused on downside risks and insufficient recovery.
- "Some expressed the concern that... inflation might run below mandate-consistent levels for some time."

Center of gravity: Solidly dovish -- maintaining maximum accommodation with focus on downside risks and persistent slack.

Minority views: "A few participants argued that maintaining a highly accommodative stance of monetary policy over the medium run would erode the stability of inflation expectations" -- a hawkish minority concern, but did not translate to policy dissent at this meeting.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY

- Range of views on inflation outlook (below mandate vs. potential rise).
- Debate on cyclical vs. structural unemployment.
- Consumer spending sustainability debated.

Center of gravity: Broad agreement on policy action with moderate differences in economic assessment. Near-consensus on maintaining stance.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

Evidence:

- Extended discussion of European sovereign debt crisis, swap lines, banking conditions.
- Discussion of domestic credit conditions, community bank strains, European lenders pulling back.

Center of gravity: Financial stability/conditions are a moderately prominent topic -- not dominant but meaningfully discussed as a key risk factor.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 10 +/-8 [Low confidence]**

Evidence:
- Federal funds rate at 0-0.25% with forward guidance language (truncated in document).
- Operation Twist and reinvestment policies continuing.
- Overall discussion tone suggests extended accommodation with no tightening on the horizon.
- "Several members noted that the forward guidance..." (document truncated).

Center of gravity: Strongly dovish forward guidance -- extended zero-rate commitment. Confidence is Low due to document truncation cutting off the forward guidance discussion.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 55 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "High level of uncertainty about the economic outlook and the political environment."
- "Strains in global financial markets continued to pose significant downside risks."
