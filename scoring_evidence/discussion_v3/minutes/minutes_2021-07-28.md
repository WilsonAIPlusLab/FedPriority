# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2021-07-28 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented format -- staff review sections are clearly labeled and separated from "Participants' Views on Current Economic Conditions and the Economic Outlook" and "Committee Policy Action" sections.

**Era context**: July 2021 -- the economy is recovering robustly from the COVID-19 pandemic. Inflation has surged well above 2% due to supply-demand imbalances, but the dominant narrative is still "transitory." The Delta variant is spreading. The Fed is still purchasing assets at full pace but actively discussing tapering. The federal funds rate is at 0-0.25%.

**Quantifier distribution in participant discussion**:
- "All" / "unanimous": economy made progress toward goals; keep target range at 0-0.25%
- "Most": substantial further progress on employment not yet met; price-stability standard achieved; appropriate to start reducing purchases this year; proportional tapering of Treasuries and MBS
- "Many": potential benefits in pace of tapering that ends before rate hike conditions met; contacts expressed uncertainty about supply constraints; risk-management considerations
- "Several": labor market recovery uneven; contacts expected hiring difficulties into fall; earlier start could be more gradual; employment well below pre-pandemic; upside inflation risks
- "Some": consumer spending bolstered by factors; upside inflation risks from supply/labor lingering; prudent to prepare for tapering relatively soon
- "A few": transitory nature of inflation cast doubt on price-stability progress; accommodative conditions could build financial stability risks; monetary policy limited ability to address supply shortages

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as clearly above target. "Inflation rate had increased notably and expected that it would likely remain elevated in coming months before moderating." "Inflation had increased generally more than expected this year." Many noted largest contributors were a "handful of sectors" affected by temporary bottlenecks. Per the rubric gotcha, "transitory" language does NOT reduce the score -- score the perceived current level. Several noted risks that high readings "could prove to be more persistent." The center of gravity is elevated but viewed as concentrated/transitory. Score in the 65-72 anchor range for 2021 Q3. Direction: above midpoint. Score ~67.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Economy expanding at a "rapid pace" but "capacity constraints were restraining the increase in output in some sectors." The economy has not fully recovered -- "sectors most adversely affected by the pandemic had shown improvement but had not fully recovered." Employment "remained well below its pre-pandemic level." Unemployment at 5.9%. Supply constraints are binding in some areas while others still have slack. This is a mixed picture: strong demand but supply-side constraints preventing full recovery, with the economy not yet at potential. The bright-line rule: participants note both "rapid pace" of growth but also sectors not fully recovered and employment well below pre-pandemic. The output gap is closing rapidly but not yet positive. Score slightly below 50 -- around 47-48.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: A complex picture. Unemployment at 5.9% is elevated. Labor force participation "little changed." "Economy had not yet achieved the Committee's broad-based and inclusive maximum-employment goal." BUT participants also note "strong demand for workers," contacts "having trouble hiring workers," "labor shortages" widespread, and "difficulties finding workers would likely extend into the fall." This is a dual labor market -- high unemployment coexisting with worker shortages. The center of gravity: the labor market is tightening but not yet at full employment. Score around 48-50, reflecting the tension between shortages and still-elevated unemployment.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumer spending had continued to increase at a very rapid pace." Housing demand "strong" but constrained by supply. Business activity rebounding but restrained by bottlenecks. Manufacturing "solid but restrained." Overall, strong demand is being held back somewhat by supply constraints. This is above trend. Score ~62, consistent with the 2021 Q3 anchor of 62-68.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Overall financial conditions remained accommodative, in part reflecting policy measures." Corporate bond spreads "near the low end of their historical ranges." Financing conditions "broadly accommodative." Municipal bond yields at "record lows." Consumer credit conditions "accommodative." This is clearly loose/accommodative. Score ~20-22.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Holding rates at 0-0.25% unanimously. Still purchasing assets at full pace. BUT "most participants" judged it "could be appropriate to start reducing the pace of asset purchases this year." "Some participants suggested that it would be prudent for the Committee to prepare for starting to reduce its pace of asset purchases relatively soon." However, "several others indicated that a reduction in the pace of asset purchases was more likely to become appropriate early next year." Several emphasized patience. The Committee is still in accommodation mode but clearly leaning toward tapering. This is a lean-dovish-to-balanced stance with emerging hawkish elements. Score ~43-45 -- still in accommodation but pivoting.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Notable range of views on tapering timing. "Most" vs "several others" on when to begin reducing purchases. Views range from "relatively soon" to "not for some time." On inflation: some see broad-based pressures, others emphasize concentrated/transitory. On labor market: some emphasize still-far-from-full-employment, others note shortages. However, all agreed on holding rates. No formal dissents on the main policy decision (only Bowman abstained on FIMA facility, which is procedural). Score ~38-40.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: A dedicated paragraph on financial stability. "Participants who commented on financial stability emphasized the risks associated with elevated valuations across many asset classes." "A few participants highlighted scenarios in which a prolonged period of low interest rates and broadly elevated asset valuations could generate imbalances." Discussion of housing valuations, cryptocurrencies, stablecoins. This is moderate discussion -- several participants address it meaningfully but it is not a dominant topic. Score ~40-45.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Forward signal is still dovish but pivoting. "Most participants" expect tapering could start this year. "Many participants saw potential benefits in a pace of tapering that would end net asset purchases before the conditions currently specified in the Committee's forward guidance on the federal funds rate were likely to be met." But participants "clearly reaffirm the absence of any mechanical link between the timing of tapering and that of an eventual increase in the target range." Rate hike conditions are a "higher standard." The forward signal points toward reducing accommodation but NOT toward rate hikes. Score ~35-38 -- easing lean with data dependence, moving toward neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Many participants remarked that uncertainty was quite high." Delta variant posing downside risks. "A number of participants judged that the effects of supply chain disruptions and labor shortages would likely complicate the task of interpreting the incoming data." "Considerable uncertainty about the likely resolution of the labor market shortages and supply bottlenecks." This is elevated uncertainty. Score ~58-62.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (47) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (49): Both slightly below 50, consistent -- economy recovering but not yet at potential/full employment, though worker shortages create tension. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (44) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (36): Forward guidance is more dovish than current lean because the rate-hike bar remains high even as tapering approaches. This divergence makes sense: current action is to begin discussing tapering (mild hawkish shift), but forward guidance still commits to low rates for an extended period.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says elevated, above target. Score 67 = above midpoint. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says not yet at potential but approaching. Score 47 = slightly below midpoint. Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says shortages but unemployment still elevated. Score 49 = near midpoint. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says accommodative/loose. Score 21 = well below midpoint. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says accommodative but beginning taper discussion. Score 44 = lean dovish. Correct.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence says still committing to low rates, tapering upcoming. Score 36 = easing lean with data dependence. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 67 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The inflation rate had increased notably and expected that it would likely remain elevated in coming months before moderating."
- "Inflation had increased generally more than expected this year and attributed this increase to supply constraints in product and labor markets and a surge in consumer demand."
- "Several participants remarked that larger-than-anticipated supply chain disruptions and increases in input costs could sustain upward pressure on prices into 2022."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants perceive inflation as clearly elevated above target, driven by supply-demand imbalances, with most expecting the pressures to be transitory but several flagging persistence risks.

**Minority views**: "A couple of participants noted that recent readings on forward inflation compensation could be read as suggesting investor concern that inflation over the longer term could run persistently below the Committee's 2 percent inflation goal." This dovish tail widens uncertainty somewhat.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 47 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity continued to expand at a rapid pace through the middle of the year even though capacity constraints were restraining the increase in output in some sectors."
- "Sectors most adversely affected by the pandemic had shown improvement but had not fully recovered."
- "Economy had not yet achieved the Committee's broad-based and inclusive maximum-employment goal."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is expanding rapidly and approaching potential but has not yet fully recovered from the pandemic, with supply constraints restraining output in key sectors.

**Minority views**: The range is relatively narrow -- all agree the economy is recovering but not yet at full capacity.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 49 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The unemployment rate remained elevated at 5.9 percent in June, and the labor force participation rate and employment-to-population ratio were little changed in recent months."
- "District contacts had continued to report having trouble hiring workers and had indicated that this difficulty was putting upward pressure on wages in some sectors."
- "Participants indicated that the economy had not yet achieved the Committee's broad-based and inclusive maximum-employment goal."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: A dual labor market where unemployment remains elevated but worker shortages are widespread, placing the net assessment near the full-employment threshold but not yet there.

**Minority views**: Some participants emphasize the still-large employment gap vs. pre-pandemic; others focus on acute hiring difficulties and supply-side constraints. This tension widens the band.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 63 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending had continued to increase at a very rapid pace, supported by the ongoing reopening of the economy along with the accommodation provided by fiscal policy and monetary policy."
- "Participants generally expected housing demand to remain strong but noted that construction had been restrained by shortages of materials."
- "Growth in manufacturing activity continued to be solid but was restrained by production bottlenecks and supply constraints."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumer spending is very strong and business activity is solid, though supply-side constraints are restraining the full expression of demand. Net assessment is well above trend.

**Minority views**: Limited disagreement on the direction -- the range reflects uncertainty about whether supply constraints will ease or persist.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 21 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Overall financial conditions remained accommodative, in part reflecting policy measures to support the economy and the flow of credit to U.S. households and businesses."
- Corporate bond spreads "near the low end of their historical ranges"; municipal bond yields at "record lows."
- "Market-based financing conditions were accommodative, and bank lending standards eased for most loan categories."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions are broadly and clearly accommodative across asset classes, credit markets, and lending standards.

**Minority views**: "A few participants expressed concerns that maintaining highly accommodative financial conditions might contribute to a further buildup in risk to the financial system." This view does not change the assessment of conditions but informs the stability discussion.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 44 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All members agreed to keep the target range for the federal funds rate at 0 to 1/4 percent."
- "Most participants noted that, provided that the economy were to evolve broadly as they anticipated, they judged that it could be appropriate to start reducing the pace of asset purchases this year."
- "A few other participants suggested that preparations for reducing the pace of asset purchases should encompass the possibility that the reductions might not occur for some time."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The Committee is still fully accommodative (rates at zero, full-pace purchases) but is clearly pivoting toward tapering, with most participants expecting asset purchase reductions to begin this year. The lean is mildly dovish but transitioning.

**Minority views**: Span from "relatively soon" tapering to "not for some time" -- this drives the wide uncertainty band.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 38 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Range of views on tapering: "most participants" vs "several others" who saw reduction "more likely to become appropriate early next year."
- On inflation: some see broad-based pressures vs others emphasizing "handful of categories."
- No formal dissents on the main policy decision; unanimous vote on rates.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Moderate differences on the timing and urgency of tapering, with notable disagreement on the inflation outlook, but broad agreement on holding rates and the general direction of policy.

**Minority views**: The taper-timing disagreement is the primary source of spread.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 42 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants who commented on financial stability emphasized the risks associated with elevated valuations across many asset classes."
- "A few participants highlighted scenarios in which a prolonged period of low interest rates and broadly elevated asset valuations could generate imbalances."
- Discussion of housing valuations, cryptocurrencies, and stablecoins.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial stability receives a dedicated discussion segment with multiple participants weighing in on asset valuations, housing, and crypto risks, but it is not a dominant theme relative to the tapering and inflation discussions.

**Minority views**: Some participants are more alarmed about financial stability risks than others.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 36 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Many participants noted that, when a reduction in the pace of asset purchases became appropriate, it would be important that the Committee clearly reaffirm the absence of any mechanical link between the timing of tapering and that of an eventual increase in the target range for the federal funds rate."
- "Several participants emphasized that an announcement of a reduction in the Committee's pace of asset purchases should not be interpreted as the beginning of a predetermined course for raising the federal funds rate."
- The rate hike bar remains a "higher standard" than the tapering bar.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal is still dovish -- rates will remain at zero even as tapering begins -- but the direction is moving toward less accommodation. The signal is one of gradual withdrawal of extraordinary support, not of tightening.

**Minority views**: A couple of participants cautioned that the public might interpret tapering as a rate-hike signal, suggesting potential confusion.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 60 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Many participants remarked that uncertainty was quite high, with slowing in progress on vaccinations and developments surrounding the Delta variant posing downside risks to the economic outlook."
- "A number of participants judged that the effects of supply chain disruptions and labor shortages would likely complicate the task of interpreting the incoming data."
- "Several participants emphasized that there was considerable uncertainty about the likely resolution of the labor market shortages and supply bottlenecks."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Uncertainty is elevated, driven by the Delta variant, supply chain unknowns, and the difficulty of interpreting incoming data during a period of unusual structural shifts.

**Minority views**: The range of uncertainty views is relatively consistent -- most agree uncertainty is high.
