# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2013-05-01

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Segmented -- participant views section is clearly separated from staff sections.

**Era context**: May 2013. The economy is in the post-GFC recovery. QE3 ($85B/month in MBS + Treasuries) is underway since September 2012. The "taper tantrum" is about to unfold (June 2013). Fiscal sequestration is a headwind. Unemployment is still elevated (~7.5%). Inflation is running below the 2% target. The key debate is about when/whether to begin tapering asset purchases.

**Quantifier distribution**: "generally" (multiple uses), "most" (used for purchase pace willingness), "a number of" (downside risks, concern about below-target inflation), "several" (caution on unemployment decline), "a few" (soft data warnings, sequestration effects, buoyant financial conditions), "one participant" (various outlier views), "a couple" (reduced downside risks, additional easing if inflation falls). The dominant quantifiers are "generally" and "most," indicating broad agreement on the core assessment.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW the midpoint. "Both headline and core PCE inflation in the first quarter came in below the Committee's longer-run goal of 2 percent." "A number of participants expressed concern that inflation was below the Committee's target." "Most of the recent reports from business contacts revealed little upward pressure on prices or wages." Some temporary factors noted, but center of gravity is clearly below target. One participant noted upside risks from accommodative policy (outlier). Score should be in the 30-38 range per anchor (2013 = 38-42). The text says below target with concern -- slightly below the typical anchor range. Direction: below 50. Score ~35.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as BELOW potential. "Economy was expanding at a moderate pace" but with "elevated" unemployment, "persisting slack in resource utilization," fiscal restraint. "A number of participants noted that the balance of risks to growth remained to the downside." The expansion only "somewhat exceed[s] the growth rate of potential output." This is classic 2010-14 recovery territory: growing but below potential. Bright-line: slack remains = must be < 50. Score ~38.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see the labor market as BELOW full employment. "Unemployment rate remained elevated." "Several participants cautioned that the drop in the unemployment rate... was also accompanied by another reduction in the labor force participation rate." Labor market "showed some improvement" but from a weak level. "Conditions in the labor market showed some continued improvement, although the unemployment rate remained elevated." This is a "below full employment; slack remains" situation. Score ~35.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. "Consumer spending was reported to be strong in a number of areas" -- supported by rising equity/house prices and easier credit. But "growth in business investment spending slowed somewhat." "Business contacts were reporting continued caution about expanding investment or payrolls." Housing was strengthening further. Net: consumer spending moderate-to-solid, business investment weak, housing improving. Overall slightly below trend but with some bright spots. Score ~40.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see financial conditions as LOOSE/accommodative. "Financial conditions appeared to have eased further over the intermeeting period." "Longer-term interest rates declined significantly, banks loosened their C&I lending terms." "Competition to make commercial and auto loans was strong." "A few participants expressed concern that conditions in certain U.S. financial markets were becoming too buoyant." This is clearly loose. Score ~22 (lower = looser).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The center of gravity is dovish but with growing taper discussion. "All but one member judged that a highly accommodative stance of monetary policy was warranted." The Committee continued $85B/month in purchases. "Most observed that the outlook for the labor market had shown progress... but many of these participants indicated that continued progress, more confidence in the outlook, or diminished downside risks would be required before slowing the pace of purchases." "A number of participants expressed willingness to adjust the flow of purchases downward as early as the June meeting." One dissent for less accommodation. The policy is highly accommodative (QE + near-zero rates + thresholds), but there is an emerging lean toward tapering. This is in the 25-35 range: maintaining QE but beginning to discuss tapering. Score ~30.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal dissent (concerned about risks from accommodation). Views differed on taper timing: "a number" willing to slow as early as June, "most" wanted more evidence, one wanted to decrease immediately, another wanted more accommodation. There's a range of views but the center holds well. Typical 1-dissent meeting with some tactical variation. Score ~33.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Moderate discussion. "A few participants expressed concern that conditions in certain U.S. financial markets were becoming too buoyant." Discussion of covenant-lite bonds. One participant cautioned about financial imbalances and suggested adjusting monetary policy. The one dissenter cited "risks of future economic and financial imbalances." Several participants pointed to positive effects of asset purchases on interest-sensitive sectors. This is more than passing mention but not a dominant topic. Score ~30.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The Committee "retained its forward guidance about the federal funds rate, including the thresholds on the unemployment and inflation rates" (the Evans Rule: 6.5% unemployment, 2.5% inflation thresholds). This is threshold-based easing guidance. However, the emerging taper discussion introduces a slightly less dovish forward element -- "a number" willing to taper as early as June. The thresholds anchor this at the dovish end, but the taper discussion nudges it up from the most dovish. Score ~12 (strong easing bias with threshold guidance, but taper discussion nudging slightly higher).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Various views on this prospect were offered." Economic data were "mixed, raising some concern." Concern about repeating the pattern of seasonal slowdowns. "A few participants warned that... economic data could remain soft for the next few months." Uncertainty about labor force participation trends ("admittedly subject to considerable uncertainty"). Uncertainty about fiscal drag magnitude. But no dramatic language about unprecedented uncertainty. This is somewhat elevated -- above normal caveats but not extreme. Score ~38.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (38) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (35): Both below 50, both indicating slack. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (30) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (12): Both dovish. The gap reflects that forward guidance (thresholds) is more dovish than the current policy lean (where taper talk is emerging). This divergence is logical and explainable: the formal guidance structure is extremely dovish, while the emerging discussion about tapering makes the current lean somewhat less so.

### Polarity verification:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (35): Higher = more pressure. Evidence says below target. 35 < 50. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP (38): Higher = more above potential. Evidence says below potential. 38 < 50. Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS (35): Higher = tighter. Evidence says slack remains. 35 < 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (22): Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence says conditions easing/loose. 22 < 50. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (30): Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says highly accommodative with emerging taper talk. 30 < 50. Correct.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (12): Higher = more hawkish signal. Evidence says threshold-based easing guidance. 12 is very dovish. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Both headline and core PCE inflation in the first quarter came in below the Committee's longer-run goal of 2 percent, but these recent lower readings appeared to be due, in part, to temporary factors."
- "A number of participants expressed concern that inflation was below the Committee's target and stressed that future price developments bore careful watching."
- "Most of the recent reports from business contacts revealed little upward pressure on prices or wages."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants broadly see inflation below the 2% target with little upward pressure, though some attribute recent softness partly to temporary factors.

**Minority views**: One participant focused on upside inflation risks from accommodative policy; a couple suggested additional easing if inflation fell further. These widen uncertainty slightly.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The economy was expanding at a moderate pace despite some softness in recent economic data."
- "Persisting slack in resource utilization."
- "A number of participants noted that the balance of risks to growth remained to the downside."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is recovering but remains below potential, with significant slack persisting and moderate growth only somewhat exceeding potential output growth.

**Minority views**: Some emphasized underlying momentum and strengthening private demand; others stressed growing fiscal restraint. The range is narrow within the "below potential" band.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Conditions in the labor market showed some continued improvement, although the unemployment rate remained elevated."
- "Several participants cautioned that the drop in the unemployment rate... was also accompanied by another reduction in the labor force participation rate; the decline in labor force participation... could indicate that the reduction in overall labor market slack had been substantially smaller than suggested by the change in the unemployment rate."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Labor market improving from weak levels but unemployment remains elevated and participation rate declines suggest even more hidden slack than headline unemployment indicates.

**Minority views**: A few noted businesses unable to find skilled workers, suggesting pockets of tightness, but this is clearly a minority observation.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 40 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending was reported to be strong in a number of areas of the country... supported by rising equity and house prices, improved household balance sheets, and easier credit conditions."
- "Growth in business investment spending slowed somewhat... business contacts were reporting continued caution about expanding investment or payrolls."
- "The housing sector improved further" with multiple positive indicators cited.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumer spending is moderately supportive and housing is strengthening, but business investment caution and fiscal drag keep overall spending/investment below trend.

**Minority views**: Concerns that consumer spending growth may not be sustainable without a pickup in business investment and hiring.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 22 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Financial conditions appeared to have eased further over the intermeeting period: Longer-term interest rates declined significantly, banks loosened their C&I lending terms and standards on balance, and competition to make commercial and auto loans was strong."
- "A few participants expressed concern that conditions in certain U.S. financial markets were becoming too buoyant, pointing to the elevated issuance of bonds by lower-credit-quality firms or of bonds with fewer restrictions on collateral and payment terms."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions are clearly loose and easing further, with QE and global central bank actions contributing. Some concern that conditions may be excessively easy in pockets.

**Minority views**: A few see conditions as potentially too buoyant, reinforcing the low (loose) score.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 30 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All but one member judged that a highly accommodative stance of monetary policy was warranted in order to foster a stronger economic recovery."
- "Most observed that the outlook for the labor market had shown progress since the program was started in September, but many of these participants indicated that continued progress, more confidence in the outlook, or diminished downside risks would be required before slowing the pace of purchases would become appropriate."
- "A number of participants expressed willingness to adjust the flow of purchases downward as early as the June meeting."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The dominant lean is dovish -- maintaining full QE pace -- but a meaningful minority is moving toward tapering, creating a somewhat wider band than a pure QE-era meeting.

**Minority views**: One member dissented hawkishly; a number willing to taper soon; one wanted more accommodation. The emerging taper discussion makes this less purely dovish than earlier QE meetings.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 33 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "One member dissented from the Committee's policy decision, expressing concern that the continued high level of monetary accommodation increased the risks of future economic and financial imbalances."
- "A number of participants expressed willingness to adjust the flow of purchases downward as early as the June meeting... however, views differed about what evidence would be necessary."
- "One participant preferred to begin decreasing the rate of purchases immediately, while another participant preferred to add more monetary accommodation at the current meeting."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: One formal dissent with a range of views on taper timing, but broad agreement on the current highly accommodative stance. Moderate tactical disagreement without fundamental division.

**Minority views**: The spread from "add more accommodation" to "decrease immediately" spans the full range but most cluster around maintaining the current pace.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 30 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "A few participants expressed concern that conditions in certain U.S. financial markets were becoming too buoyant, pointing to the elevated issuance of bonds by lower-credit-quality firms or of bonds with fewer restrictions on collateral and payment terms (so-called covenant-lite bonds)."
- "One participant cautioned that the emergence of financial imbalances could prove difficult for regulators to identify and address, and that it would be appropriate to adjust monetary policy to help guard against risks to financial stability."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial stability receives limited but meaningful discussion from a few participants, focused on reach-for-yield concerns. It is not a dominant topic but more than a passing reference.

**Minority views**: Most participants did not raise financial stability as a concern; it was driven by a few voices.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 12 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The Committee also retained its forward guidance about the federal funds rate, including the thresholds on the unemployment and inflation rates." (Evans Rule: 6.5% unemployment / 2.5% inflation thresholds)
- "Most participants emphasized that it was important for the Committee to be prepared to adjust the pace of its purchases up or down as needed."
- Emerging taper discussion ("a number" willing to slow as early as June) introduces some upward pull on the forward signal.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The formal forward guidance remains strongly dovish via threshold-based commitments, but the nascent taper discussion introduces a slightly less accommodative forward lean than pure threshold guidance alone would imply.

**Minority views**: The taper-willing participants push the forward signal slightly higher than the rock-bottom thresholds; one participant wanting more accommodation pulls it lower.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Economic data releases over the intermeeting period were mixed, raising some concern that the recovery might be slowing after a solid start earlier this year, thereby repeating the pattern observed in recent years."
- "A few participants warned that, in light of ongoing fiscal restraint and a weak global outlook, economic data could remain soft for the next few months."
- Labor force participation trend "admittedly subject to considerable uncertainty."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants express somewhat elevated uncertainty, driven by mixed data, concern about seasonal slowdown patterns, fiscal drag effects, and difficulty interpreting labor market signals. Not extreme but above routine hedging.

**Minority views**: Some put more emphasis on underlying momentum, suggesting less uncertainty; others stressed headwinds, implying more.
