# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2009-06-24

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). This is a post-2009 segmented document, so participant vs. staff content is relatively clear. The text uses standard quantifier language throughout.

**Era context**: June 2009 — near the official trough of the Great Recession (NBER dated the trough at June 2009). The economy was in deep contraction but showing early signs of stabilization. Fed funds rate at 0-0.25%, asset purchase programs underway (LSAP/TALF).

**Quantifier distribution**: "most" appears frequently (most saw economy as weak, most expected subdued inflation, most anticipated downbeat employment, most saw less downside risk). "Many" used for financial conditions, commercial real estate, productivity, global recession. "Several" used for labor compensation. "Some" used for various minority views. "A few" used for inflation expectations concern.

**Dimension-by-dimension reasoning**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target/subdued. "Most participants expected core inflation to remain subdued for some time." Substantial resource slack keeping prices low. Wages slowing. "Economy was almost certainly operating well below its potential and that significant price pressures were unlikely." Near-term commodity price increase noted but core expected subdued. A few worried about future inflation from expanded balance sheet. Deflation risk reduced ("reduced concerns about deflation") — expectations edging up from "very low readings." This is squarely in the 16-25 range: meaningful deflation risk diminishing but still well below target, subdued, muted. Score ~20.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economy was almost certainly operating well below its potential." "Substantial resource slack." Economic contraction slowing but still quite weak. Output would "begin to grow again in the second half" but "rate of increase was likely to be relatively slow." This is deep below potential — the bright-line rule mandates well below 50. The anchor is 15-22 for GFC trough. The contraction is slowing (not collapsing anymore), so the upper end of that range. Score ~20.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor market conditions were of particular concern." Job losses "remained substantial," unemployment rate "continued to rise rapidly." "Labor market conditions were even more difficult than indicated by the unemployment rate" due to part-time for economic reasons and permanent job reductions. "Employment situation was likely to be downbeat for some time." This is very loose labor market. Anchor 15-22 for 2009. Score ~17.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "appeared no longer to be declining but nonetheless remained weak." "Continued sluggishness" due to falling employment, lower wealth, tight credit. "Consumer spending would continue to be subdued for some time." Housing "may be leveling out" but "still vulnerable." Business sentiment improving but "elevated uncertainty was said to be inhibiting capital spending." No longer collapsing but still very weak. This fits 20-28 range: contracting/sharp pullback easing into below trend. Score ~25.

5. **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Mixed picture. "Financial market strains had eased further." SCAP results helped, corporate credit improved, equity prices up. BUT "underlying financial conditions remained fragile. Credit was tight, with some banks quite reluctant to lend." "Obtaining financing for commercial real estate projects remained extremely difficult." Credit "still quite tight in many sectors." Conditions improving from crisis extremes but still tight. This is around 65-70: tight, credit hard to obtain, but improving from crisis peak. Score ~67.

6. **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Committee members agreed that the stance of monetary policy should not be changed." Maintain 0-0.25% rate. "Federal funds rate would need to be maintained at an exceptionally low level for an extended period." No expansion of asset purchases warranted but no reduction either. This is maintaining maximum accommodation with extended period language. Anchor 15-25 for QE era. But they also decided NOT to expand purchases, suggesting slight less dovish than peak emergency. Score ~18.

7. **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Committee members agreed that the stance of monetary policy should not be changed" — broad agreement on hold. "A few participants were concerned that inflation expectations could continue to rise" — minor dissent on inflation outlook. But on policy, appears to be consensus. Score ~22: broad agreement with minor emphasis differences.

8. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Substantial discussion of financial conditions — entire paragraph on SCAP, bank lending, credit markets, commercial real estate, TALF. "Many participants noted" SCAP results. "Many participants noted" CRE financing difficulties. Government programs supporting conditions. Banking losses ahead. This is a prominent topic. Score ~60.

9. **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Federal funds rate would need to be maintained at an exceptionally low level for an extended period." No expansion of asset purchases but maintaining current programs. This is strong easing bias — extended period language is a commitment to low rates. Anchor 6-15 for strong easing bias. Score ~12.

10. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Given the significant uncertainties in the economic outlook." "Considerable uncertainty surrounding estimates of the output and unemployment gaps." Contacts "remained quite uncertain about the timing and extent of the recovery." "Elevated uncertainty was said to be inhibiting capital spending." "Most believed that downside risks...had diminished somewhat...but were still significant." Multiple references to uncertainty. Score ~62: high uncertainty, many emphasize difficulty.

**Cross-dimension coherence check**:
- OUTPUT_GAP (20) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (17): Both deep below 50, consistent for deep recession. Labor slightly lower because text emphasizes labor is even worse than headline suggests.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (18) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (12): Both strongly dovish, consistent. Forward guidance slightly more dovish because "extended period" language is an explicit commitment.

**Polarity verification**:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says subdued/low. Score 20 (low). Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says well below potential. Score 20 (low). Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says tight but improving. Score 67. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says strongly dovish. Score 18. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 20 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "most participants expected core inflation to remain subdued for some time"
- "the economy was almost certainly operating well below its potential and that significant price pressures were unlikely to materialize in the near and medium terms"
- "some measures of inflation expectations had edged up recently from very low readings, perhaps reflecting in part reduced concerns about deflation"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants see inflation as well below target with substantial slack keeping prices subdued, though active deflation fears have diminished somewhat from earlier in 2009.

**Minority views**: "A few participants were concerned that inflation expectations could continue to rise" given the expanded balance sheet — this informs the upward uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 18 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the economy was almost certainly operating well below its potential"
- "substantial resource slack"
- "the economic contraction was slowing and that the decline in activity could cease before long" but "the rate of increase was likely to be relatively slow"

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is deeply below potential with massive slack. The contraction is slowing toward stabilization but the gap remains enormous by any measure.

**Minority views**: None of note — broad agreement on the depth of the output gap.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 17 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Labor market conditions were of particular concern to meeting participants"
- "job losses remained substantial...and the unemployment rate continued to rise rapidly"
- "labor market conditions were even more difficult than indicated by the unemployment rate" (due to part-time for economic reasons, permanent layoffs)

**Center-of-gravity**: Extremely slack labor market with ongoing deterioration. Conditions described as worse than the headline unemployment rate suggests.

**Minority views**: "Some improvements were evident in new and continuing unemployment insurance claims" — very minor positive offset.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 25 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending appeared no longer to be declining but nonetheless remained weak"
- "consumer spending would continue to be subdued for some time"
- "elevated uncertainty was said to be inhibiting capital spending in many cases"

**Center-of-gravity**: Spending has stabilized from freefall but remains deeply below trend. Both consumer and business investment suppressed by wealth losses, tight credit, and uncertainty. Housing leveling off but vulnerable.

**Minority views**: Some noted inventory restocking could support production growth ahead, providing a mild upside possibility.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 67 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "underlying financial conditions remained fragile. Credit was tight, with some banks quite reluctant to lend"
- "obtaining financing for commercial real estate projects remained extremely difficult"
- "the improvement in market conditions was in part due to ongoing support from various government programs"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are tight but improving from crisis extremes. Credit remains restricted, especially for CRE. Improvements are partly government-dependent rather than organic.

**Minority views**: The positive direction is clear (SCAP results, corporate credit improvement, equity gains) but the level remains restrictive.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 18 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the Committee agreed that it should maintain its target range for the federal funds rate at 0 to ¼ percent"
- "the federal funds rate would need to be maintained at an exceptionally low level for an extended period"
- On asset purchases: "changes to its program of asset purchases were not warranted at this time" — neither expanding nor contracting

**Center-of-gravity**: Strongly dovish. Maximum accommodation maintained with extended period commitment. Not expanding purchases further, but clearly maintaining the full suite of emergency support.

**Minority views**: No apparent hawks at this meeting; the mild debate was whether to expand purchases further (even more dovish).

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 22 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Committee members agreed that the stance of monetary policy should not be changed" — consensus on action
- "A few participants were concerned that inflation expectations could continue to rise" — minor outlier concern
- No formal dissents mentioned in the text

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on maintaining the current stance. Some minor differences in the economic assessment (e.g., on inflation outlook) but not on the policy decision.

**Minority views**: The inflation hawks represent a small minority with different risk emphasis but not different policy prescriptions at this point.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 60 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Full paragraph devoted to financial market developments: SCAP, bank capital, corporate credit, ABS markets, TALF, equity prices
- "banks could face substantial losses in their loan portfolios in coming quarters"
- "obtaining financing for commercial real estate projects remained extremely difficult amid worsening fundamentals"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions and stability are a prominent topic, with many participants discussing credit markets, banking health, and CRE stress at length. Not the dominant topic (economic weakness and labor markets also feature heavily) but a major one.

**Minority views**: N/A — broad attention to this topic.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 12 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the federal funds rate would need to be maintained at an exceptionally low level for an extended period"
- "given their forecasts for only a gradual upturn in activity and the lack of inflation pressures"
- Asset purchase programs continuing at current levels

**Center-of-gravity**: Strong easing bias in forward signal. "Extended period" language is an explicit forward commitment to near-zero rates. No discussion of exit strategy or normalization timeline.

**Minority views**: None apparent — forward guidance direction is unanimously dovish.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 62 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Given the significant uncertainties in the economic outlook"
- "considerable uncertainty surrounding estimates of the output and unemployment gaps and the extent of their effects on prices"
- "contacts remained quite uncertain about the timing and extent of the recovery; elevated uncertainty was said to be inhibiting capital spending"

**Center-of-gravity**: High uncertainty expressed by multiple participants across economic outlook, output gap measurement, and recovery timing. Downside risks diminished but still significant.

**Minority views**: "Most believed that downside risks to economic growth had diminished somewhat" — uncertainty is declining from its peak but remains elevated.
