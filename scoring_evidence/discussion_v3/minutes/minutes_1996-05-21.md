# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1996-05-21 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format, but participant content is clearly marked with "members" language throughout. The directive/vote section at the end is formal language, not scored independently.

**Era context**: Mid-1996, mid-expansion. Economy growing above expectations. Unemployment around 5.4%. Fed funds rate steady (last move was a cut in Jan 1996). Bond yields rising. No formal inflation target yet, but Fed focused on price stability. This is a "near potential" economy with nascent inflation concerns.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all" (unanimous hold, symmetric directive), "members" (broadly shared views on growth moderation ahead), "a number of members" (no firm evidence of inflation pickup; appreciable risk of rising labor costs), "some" (balanced risks; stock market sustainability questions; appropriate to wait), "others" (concern about growth pace and resource pressures). Views are moderately dispersed on inflation risk but unified on the policy hold.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Members see the "balance of risks on inflation had shifted substantially since early in the year" toward higher inflation risk. But "no firm evidence had surfaced thus far to signal that labor compensation was increasing at a faster rate or that core inflation was worsening." Food and energy prices rising but core stable. This is a case of rising risk without realized inflation. Participants perceive inflation as modestly above neutral concern -- not yet elevated in actuals, but risk tilting up. Direction: slightly above midpoint. "Somewhat" elevated concern = ~55-58.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "economy had been stronger this year than they had anticipated and appeared to be growing at a quite robust pace." "Already operating in the neighborhood of its estimated long-term potential." Members expect slowing to keep economy "close to its potential." Bright-line: "in the neighborhood of its estimated long-term potential" with "robust" current growth = at or modestly above potential. Score should be 51-55.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "relatively full employment highlighted by anecdotal reports of scarcities of various types of labor in numerous parts of the country." Unemployment at 5.4%. Yet "increases in labor compensation had been comparatively subdued" -- tight but without wage pressure manifestation. This is a tight labor market. Direction: above midpoint. Score ~57-62.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "consumer spending had strengthened considerably this year." Business fixed investment "a source of considerable strength." Housing "displaying a good deal of strength." Expected to moderate but currently strong. Direction: clearly above trend. Score ~60-65.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "the rise in longer-term interest rates and the exchange rate meant that financial conditions were now exerting more restraint than earlier this year." But earlier: "favorable financial conditions" and "equity and other financing remained available on relatively attractive terms." Bond yields rising but credit still available. Conditions tightening from loose but now roughly neutral to slightly tight. Score ~48-52.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Unanimous hold with symmetric directive. But clear tightening bias in discussion: "policy might need to be firmed at some point to head off emerging inflation pressures." "Some members noted that the Committee would need to anticipate, and act to preclude, a rise in core inflation." "A firming in policy sooner rather than later was likely to end up promoting stability." This is a hold with a tightening bias but no presumption of action. Score ~55-58.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Unanimous vote (0 dissents). Views differ "to some extent" on risks. "Some saw those risks as fairly evenly balanced... Others expressed concern that economic growth might continue at a pace that could increase pressures." Moderate differences in risk assessment but unanimous on action. Score ~25-30.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Brief mention of stock market ("Some members also questioned the sustainability of the performance of the stock market; a correction in this market would help to restrain aggregate demand"). Wealth effects mentioned. But not a major focus or lengthy discussion. Score ~15-20.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Symmetric directive. "Policy might need to be firmed at some point." "Some members noted that the Committee would need to anticipate, and act to preclude, a rise in the core rate of inflation." Preference to come into July "without such a presumption." Mild tightening lean forward but deliberately non-committal. Score ~55-60.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The outlook for inflation was of key importance... but it was clouded by substantial uncertainty." "Considerable uncertainties surrounding the relationship of output to inflation." "Whether this favorable outcome would persist was an open question." Multiple sources of uncertainty explicitly discussed. Score ~50-58.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence = rising risk, shifted balance. Score above 50. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence = "neighborhood of potential" with robust growth. Score ~53. Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence = "relatively full employment," "scarcities." Score above 50. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence = hold with tightening bias. Score slightly above 50. Correct.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward. Evidence = mild tightening lean, symmetric directive. Score slightly above 50. Correct.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (53) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (59): Both above 50, labor somewhat tighter than output gap, consistent with "at potential" economy but tight labor market. Reasonable -- labor market is described as tighter than the output gap language suggests.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (56) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (57): Aligned -- mild tightening bias in both current lean and forward signal. Consistent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 56 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the balance of risks on inflation had shifted substantially since early in the year"
- "a number of members emphasized that no firm evidence had surfaced thus far to signal that labor compensation was increasing at a faster rate or that core inflation was worsening"
- "faster increases in energy and food prices could contribute to higher overall inflation, both directly and by boosting inflationary expectations"

**Center of gravity**: Members perceive inflation risk has increased notably, with food/energy prices rising, but core inflation remains contained. The balance of risks has shifted toward more inflation but no hard evidence yet. Modestly above neutral.

**Minority views**: Some see risks as evenly balanced; others see appreciable risk of rising inflation. Band reflects this spread.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 53 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the economy had been stronger this year than they had anticipated and appeared to be growing at a quite robust pace"
- "already operating in the neighborhood of its estimated long-term potential"
- "they generally expected the expansion to slow, keeping the economy close to its potential"

**Center of gravity**: Economy is at or slightly above potential with robust current growth, but expected to return to potential. The "neighborhood of potential" with above-trend growth warrants slightly above 50.

**Minority views**: Some worried growth might not slow enough, pushing further above potential.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 59 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "relatively full employment highlighted by anecdotal reports of scarcities of various types of labor in numerous parts of the country"
- "increases in labor compensation had been comparatively subdued over an extended period of what seemed to be relatively full employment"
- Unemployment rate at 5.4%

**Center of gravity**: Labor market is tight with widespread anecdotal reports of scarcities, but the puzzle is that wages have not responded. Tight on quantities (low unemployment, scarcities) but not yet on prices (wages subdued).

**Minority views**: Uncertainty about how long wage restraint would persist -- some see appreciable risk of rising labor costs.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "consumer spending had strengthened considerably this year after a period of sluggish growth in late 1995"
- "Business fixed investment was believed likely to remain a source of considerable strength in the expansion"
- "the housing sector was continuing to display a good deal of strength in many parts of the country"

**Center of gravity**: All three components -- consumption, business investment, and housing -- are described as strong or considerably strengthened. Expected to moderate but currently well above trend. Broad-based strength.

**Minority views**: Expected moderation ahead from higher rates, dollar appreciation. Some questioned stock market sustainability.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 48 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "financial conditions were not so obviously stimulative as to counsel a need for any immediate tightening of policy"
- "the rise in longer-term interest rates and the exchange rate meant that financial conditions were now exerting more restraint than earlier this year"
- "equity and other financing remained available on relatively attractive terms"

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions have tightened from earlier loose levels (via higher bond yields and dollar appreciation) but are not yet restrictive. Credit is still available. Near-neutral, perhaps very slightly on the loose side given equity financing availability.

**Minority views**: Limited disagreement on this dimension.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 56 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "all the members supported a proposal to maintain an unchanged degree of pressure in reserve markets" (hold)
- "policy might need to be firmed at some point to head off emerging inflation pressures"
- "a firming in policy sooner rather than later was likely to end up promoting stability in output and prices"
- "all the members indicated at least some preference for retaining a symmetric directive"

**Center of gravity**: Unanimous hold with symmetric directive, but clear tightening bias in the discussion. Members acknowledge inflation risks have shifted, see possible need to firm, but want more data. Lean hawkish but not yet ready to act.

**Minority views**: Some more urgently inclined toward tightening ("sooner rather than later"); others see risks as balanced.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 25 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Votes against this action: None."
- "Views differed to some extent with regard to the risks surrounding such an outlook"
- "Some saw those risks as fairly evenly balanced... Others expressed concern that economic growth might continue at a pace that could increase pressures"

**Center of gravity**: Unanimous vote with no dissents and a symmetric directive everyone supported. Some differences in risk assessment but not in policy prescription. Broad agreement with minor emphasis differences.

**Minority views**: The spread is on risk assessment, not on the policy action itself.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 15 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Some members also questioned the sustainability of the performance of the stock market; a correction in this market would help to restrain aggregate demand"
- Stock market wealth effects mentioned as supporting demand

**Center of gravity**: Brief, passing mentions of stock market valuations and wealth effects, but not a prominent topic of discussion. Consistent with 1993-96 era anchors (1-10 typical), though this meeting has slightly more stock market mention than minimal.

**Minority views**: None significant.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 57 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "ongoing developments would need to be reassessed at the upcoming meeting in early July"
- "Some members noted that the Committee would need to anticipate, and act to preclude, a rise in the core rate of inflation"
- "their preference was to come into the July meeting without such a presumption" (re: asymmetric toward tightening)

**Center of gravity**: Forward signal is mildly hawkish -- data-dependent but clearly leaning toward possible tightening if data warrant. Deliberately chose symmetric to avoid presumption, but the discussion itself telegraphs tightening bias. Mild tightening lean with data dependence.

**Minority views**: Some wanted to signal more readiness to tighten; others preferred no presumption.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 55 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The outlook for inflation was of key importance to the formulation of monetary policy at this time, but it was clouded by substantial uncertainty"
- "considerable uncertainties surrounding the relationship of output to inflation"
- "whether this favorable outcome would persist was an open question"

**Center of gravity**: Members explicitly flag "substantial uncertainty" and "considerable uncertainties" about the inflation-output relationship and the duration of favorable wage dynamics. Multiple sources of genuine uncertainty discussed. Elevated but not extreme.

**Minority views**: The uncertainty itself is shared broadly; not a source of disagreement.
