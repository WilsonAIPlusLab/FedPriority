# FOMC Participant Discussion Scoring — 2025-06-18

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented — participant discussion is clearly separated from staff content.

**Era context**: June 2025. Post-pandemic normalization era. Inflation has come down significantly from 2022 peak but remains somewhat elevated. Tariff policy under the current administration is a major source of uncertainty. The fed funds rate is at 4.25-4.50%, held steady at this meeting. The labor market is solid but showing early signs of cooling.

**Quantifier distribution**: "All" (hold rates), "most" (some rate reduction likely appropriate this year), "many" (various observations on tariffs, sentiment, growth moderation), "several" (labor market observations, business contacts, investment), "some" (various risk assessments, inflation sub-components), "a few" (softness signs, one-time tariff effects, expectations unanchoring risk), "a couple" (agriculture, AI investment, rate cut as soon as next meeting).

### Dimensional reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants observe inflation "had eased significantly since its peak in 2022 but remained somewhat elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent longer-run goal." Progress continued but was "uneven." Services inflation moved down recently while goods price inflation had risen. Tariffs are expected to put upward pressure on prices with "considerable uncertainty" about timing/size/duration. Some participants note risk of longer-term expectations becoming unanchored. Direction: above target but moderating. "Somewhat elevated" = 55-60 range. Tariff upside risks push this a bit higher. Score ~58.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic activity had continued to grow at a solid pace." Growth was solid. A majority expected pace to moderate going forward. No language about above/below potential explicitly, but "solid pace" with the economy continuing to expand suggests near or slightly above potential. The labor market is "at or near maximum employment." Score ~51-52, near potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Conditions remained solid and that the labor market was at, or near, estimates of maximum employment." Unemployment "remained at a low level." Several observed slowing in both hiring and layoffs. Most suggested higher tariffs/uncertainty would weigh on labor demand. Many expected gradual softening. A few noted indicators already showing softness. This is near full employment but with signs of cooling — score ~52.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending growth had been "solid" per some data, but other data suggested "softening." Lower/moderate income households switching to lower-cost items. Household sentiment remained low. Business activity "remained solid, although there have been signs of softening." Business sentiment indicators "remained low." Investment: firms proceeding with existing projects but cautious about new ones. Manufacturing showing softening. Net: solid but showing softening signs — slightly below trend. Score ~47.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Financing from both banks and financial markets was readily available for larger investment projects." Current monetary policy described as "moderately or modestly restrictive." Several participants noted the target range "may not be far above its neutral level." Financial conditions not described as tight in market terms — credit available. Score ~48 (neutral to slightly loose on the conditions side, despite restrictive policy rate).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All participants: hold rates at 4.25-4.50%. Forward: "most participants assessed that some reduction in the target range this year would likely be appropriate." A couple noted openness to a cut "as soon as at the next meeting." Some saw no reductions this year as most likely path. The center is hold-with-easing-bias. Score ~43 (lean dovish to balanced — hold steady with mild easing bias, most expect cuts this year but not urgently).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: All agreed to hold. Most see cuts ahead but some see no cuts this year. A couple want to cut soon, some want to hold all year. Notable spread in views on outlook and appropriate path, but no formal dissents. Score ~35 (broad agreement on hold, moderate differences on forward path).

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Very limited discussion of financial stability per se. Financing availability mentioned briefly. No extended discussion of market dysfunction, asset valuations, or systemic risk. Score ~15 (brief/passing references only).

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Most participants assessed that some reduction in the target range for the federal funds rate this year would likely be appropriate." But emphasis on "wait for more clarity," "careful approach," data dependence. Some see no cuts. A couple open to cutting at next meeting. The forward signal is mildly dovish but heavily conditioned on data. Score ~38 (easing lean with data dependence).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Uncertainty about the outlook was elevated amid evolving developments in trade policy, other government policies, and geopolitical risks." Uncertainty had "diminished" since previous meeting but "continued to be elevated." "Considerable uncertainty" about tariff effects. Participants emphasized taking a "careful approach." Score ~62 (elevated, multiple participants emphasize it, but somewhat diminished from prior meeting).

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (51) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (52): Both near 50, consistent — economy near potential, labor near full employment. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (43) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (38): Both lean dovish, with forward guidance slightly more dovish (most expect cuts). Makes sense — hold now but signal future easing. Coherent.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 58: Higher = more pressure. "Somewhat elevated" = above midpoint. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP 51: Higher = more above potential. "Solid pace" near potential. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS 48: Higher = tighter. Credit readily available, but policy moderately restrictive. Conditions are not tight. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 43: Higher = more hawkish. Lean dovish (most expect cuts). Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 58 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Inflation had eased significantly since its peak in 2022 but remained somewhat elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent longer-run goal."
- "Participants noted that increased tariffs were likely to put upward pressure on prices. There was considerable uncertainty, however, about the timing, size, and duration of these effects."
- "Some participants observed that because inflation has been elevated for some time, there was a heightened risk of longer-term inflation expectations becoming unanchored if there is a long-lasting rise in inflation."

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation is acknowledged as still somewhat elevated with tariff-related upside risks, but progress toward target has continued and is recognized. The balance is modestly above target with meaningful upside risk from tariffs.

**Minority views**: Some see more persistent inflation risks from tariffs and expectations unanchoring (pushes higher); a few note tariff effects may be one-time/temporary (pushes lower). This informs the ±7 band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 51 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity had continued to grow at a solid pace, although uncertainty remained elevated."
- "A majority of participants expected that the pace of growth was likely to moderate going forward."
- Labor market "at, or near, estimates of maximum employment."

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is growing solidly and near potential. No language suggesting above-trend or below-trend — just "solid" and "at or near" capacity. Slight positive lean given continued growth.

**Minority views**: Some see forward softening, but current assessment is near potential. Narrow band.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 52 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Conditions remained solid and that the labor market was at, or near, estimates of maximum employment."
- "Several participants observed that the recent stability of the labor market reflected a slowing in both hiring and layoffs."
- "Most participants suggested that higher tariffs or heightened policy uncertainty would weigh on labor demand, and many participants expected a gradual softening of conditions."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is near full employment but with cooling dynamics — hiring and layoffs both slowing. Not tight in the 2022 sense, but not slack either. Near NAIRU.

**Minority views**: A few see signs of softness already emerging; others see continued solidity. Narrow band.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 47 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Several participants observed that some recent data indicated continued solid consumer spending growth, whereas several other participants pointed to other data that suggested softening."
- "Lower- and moderate-income households were switching to lower-cost items and brands."
- Business investment: "firms were proceeding with existing investment projects but that heightened uncertainty was making them cautious about beginning new projects, especially larger ones."
- "Indicators of business sentiment remained low."

**Center-of-gravity**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending data split between solid and softening readings. Business investment cautious on new projects. Sentiment weak. Net assessment is near trend but tilting slightly softer. Below the "solid pace" label for the overall economy due to specific softening signals in components.

**Minority views**: Some see continued solidity, others see emerging weakness. The ±7 band reflects this split.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 48 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Financing from both banks and financial markets was readily available for larger investment projects."
- Current monetary policy described as "moderately or modestly restrictive."
- "Several participants commented that the current target range for the federal funds rate may not be far above its neutral level."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are roughly neutral. Credit is available. Policy is modestly restrictive but not far from neutral. No mention of spread widening, credit stress, or market dysfunction. Conditions are not tight despite the policy rate level.

**Minority views**: Limited — little disagreement on financial conditions assessment.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 43 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All participants viewed it as appropriate to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 4¼ to 4½ percent."
- "Most participants assessed that some reduction in the target range for the federal funds rate this year would likely be appropriate."
- "A couple of participants noted that, if the data evolve in line with their expectations, they would be open to considering a reduction in the target range for the policy rate as soon as at the next meeting."
- "Some participants saw the most likely appropriate path of monetary policy as involving no reductions in the target range for the federal funds rate this year."

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous hold, but most lean toward cuts later this year. The dominant stance is hold-with-easing-bias, firmly in the lean-dovish-to-balanced range. Not aggressively dovish — heavily conditioned on data.

**Minority views**: Some want no cuts at all this year (hawkish minority); a couple want to cut as soon as next meeting (dovish minority). This range informs the ±8 band.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 35 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- All agreed on the hold decision — no dissents.
- But meaningful spread on forward path: "most" see cuts, "some" see no cuts, "a couple" want to cut soon.
- "Some participants commented that they saw the risk of elevated inflation as remaining more prominent, or as having diminished by less, than risks to employment."
- "A few participants saw risks to the labor market as having become predominant."

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on current action (hold) but moderate differences on the forward outlook and risk assessment. No formal dissents. The spread in views on appropriate future path and risk balance creates some internal tension but within normal bounds.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 15 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Financing availability mentioned in passing: "financing from both banks and financial markets was readily available."
- No extended discussion of financial stability risks, asset valuations, systemic concerns, or market functioning.

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability is barely touched in this discussion. The meeting focuses overwhelmingly on inflation/tariffs, labor, and the policy path. Only passing reference to financial conditions.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 38 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Most participants assessed that some reduction in the target range for the federal funds rate this year would likely be appropriate."
- "The Committee was well positioned to wait for more clarity on the outlook for inflation and economic activity."
- "Participants emphasized the importance of ensuring that longer-term inflation expectations remained well anchored and agreed that the current stance of monetary policy positioned the Committee well to respond in a timely way to potential economic developments."

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal leans toward easing but is heavily conditioned on incoming data. "Most" expect cuts but with patience and data dependence emphasized. This is an easing lean with strong data-dependence qualifier.

**Minority views**: Some see no cuts this year; a couple are ready to cut soon. The ±8 band reflects this range.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 62 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants judged that uncertainty about the outlook was elevated amid evolving developments in trade policy, other government policies, and geopolitical risks, but that overall uncertainty had diminished since the previous meeting."
- "There was considerable uncertainty, however, about the timing, size, and duration of these effects" (regarding tariffs).
- "It remained appropriate to take a careful approach in adjusting monetary policy."
- "Participants noted that the Committee might face difficult tradeoffs if elevated inflation proved to be more persistent while the outlook for employment weakened."

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is elevated but has diminished from the prior meeting. Multiple participants emphasize trade policy uncertainty, tariff pass-through uncertainty, and potential tradeoffs. This is above-normal uncertainty but not extreme or "uncharted territory." Fits the "elevated" band (46-60) to "high" band (61-75) — settling at 62 given the repeated emphasis on "elevated" uncertainty with the caveat it has diminished somewhat.
