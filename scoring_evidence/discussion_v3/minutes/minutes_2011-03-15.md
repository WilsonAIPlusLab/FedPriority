# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2011-03-15 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Post-2009 segmented format with clear participant attribution cues ("participants agreed," "several participants noted," "a few participants").

**Era context**: March 2011. The economy is about two years into recovery from the GFC. QE2 (the second round of asset purchases) is underway. Unemployment has been declining but remains elevated. Commodity prices (oil, food) are rising, driven by Middle East/North Africa unrest and global demand. The Japan earthquake/tsunami just occurred. The fed funds rate is at 0-0.25%.

**Quantifier distribution**: "participants agreed" (broad consensus on recovery firming, no policy change), "a number of participants" (commodity pass-through, slack restraining inflation), "several participants" (unemployment drop due to labor force withdrawal, upside inflation risks), "some participants" (consumer spending shortfall, slack useful for predicting inflation), "a few participants" (excessive credit easing concern, explicit inflation objective, less-accommodative policy this year, more accommodation beyond 2011).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see headline inflation as being boosted by commodity/energy prices, but underlying/core inflation "remained subdued" and had "bottomed out." "A number of participants noted that, with significant slack in resource utilization and with longer-term inflation expectations stable, underlying inflation likely would remain subdued for some time." However, commodity price pass-through was occurring ("passing on at least a portion of these higher costs"), and "several" indicated upside inflation risks had shifted. The center of gravity: headline rising, core subdued but bottoming. This is modestly below the midpoint but with upside risks recognized. Direction: slightly below target on underlying, above on headline. Net: around 45-48 range. The "subdued" language for core pulls below 50, but headline pressure and commodity pass-through push toward 50. Score ~47.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Recovery was on a firmer footing." "Substantial slack in the labor market." "Significant slack in resource utilization." Bright-line rule: "slack remains" language requires score below 50. The economy is recovering but clearly below potential. Consistent with the 2010-14 recovery anchor of 35-42. Score ~38.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Participants generally judged that there was still substantial slack in the labor market." Unemployment had declined but "several participants noted that the drop in unemployment was attributable more to people withdrawing from the labor force and to fewer layoffs than to increased hiring." Scattered reports of difficulty hiring skilled workers, but overall substantial slack. Anchor for 2011-14 is 35-40. Score ~35.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumer spending...rose further during the early part of 2011, with auto sales showing particular strength." But "growth in consumer spending so far this year had not been as vigorous as they had anticipated." Business investment: "indicators of current and planned business investment in equipment and software continued to rise," contacts "increasing capital budgets," manufacturing "particularly upbeat." Housing "remained depressed." Net: moderate expansion, some strength in business investment offset by tepid consumer spending and depressed housing. Anchor for 2011-13 "moderate" is 35-42. The business side is stronger than "sluggish" but consumer side is tepid. Score ~42.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Credit conditions remained uneven." Large firms find credit on "relatively attractive terms," but "credit conditions reportedly remained tight for smaller, bank-dependent firms." Auto loans returning to "historical norms." CRE loans "remained tight." "A few participants expressed concern that the easing of credit conditions in some sectors was becoming or might become excessive." Mixed picture: loosening in some areas, still tight in others. Net: somewhat loose to neutral for larger economy, tight for smaller firms and CRE. Score ~45 (slightly loose of neutral, reflecting the uneven conditions tilting slightly toward still-tight side).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Committee agreed to continue QE2 asset purchases and maintain 0-0.25% range. "A few participants indicated that economic conditions might warrant a move toward less-accommodative monetary policy this year; a few others noted that exceptional policy accommodation could be appropriate beyond 2011." The center is continuing QE2 as planned. This is squarely in the QE-era dovish range. Per rubric, QE discussion IS the dovish signal (15-25 range). But they are continuing an existing program, not expanding. And there's growing discussion of exit strategies. Score ~25 -- still accommodative but with exit planning beginning.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: The text mentions "a few" wanting less accommodation this year and "a few" wanting more beyond 2011 -- so there is some spread. But the Committee "agreed" on no changes. No formal dissents mentioned. The differences are about timing of future moves, not the current action. Score ~30 (broad agreement with minor differences about forward path).

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Credit conditions discussed at moderate length (one full paragraph). "A few participants expressed concern that the easing of credit conditions in some sectors was becoming or might become excessive." Some mention of European banking/fiscal strains. But not a dominant topic. Score ~30 (limited but present discussion).

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "A few participants indicated that economic conditions might warrant a move toward less-accommodative monetary policy this year; a few others noted that exceptional policy accommodation could be appropriate beyond 2011." Exit planning discussed. The Committee continued QE2 with "extended period" language presumably still in place. The forward signal is still accommodative but with growing attention to exit. Score ~20 (mild easing bias, low-for-long but exit planning underway).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Unfolding events in the Middle East and North Africa, along with the recent earthquake, tsunami, and subsequent developments in Japan, had further increased uncertainty about the economic outlook." "The pace of expansion was uncertain." Growth risks "roughly balanced" but with geopolitical and supply chain concerns. "In light of uncertainty about the economic outlook, it was seen as prudent to consider possible exit strategies for a range of potential economic outcomes." Somewhat elevated uncertainty. Score ~45.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (38) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (35): Both below 50, consistent with substantial slack. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (25) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (20): Both dovish, with forward guidance slightly more dovish (still in QE mode). Coherent.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (47): Higher = more pressure. Evidence: headline up from commodities, core subdued. Net slightly below midpoint. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP (38): Higher = more above potential. Evidence: substantial slack, below potential. Below 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (45): Higher = tighter. Evidence: mixed, some easing but CRE/small firms tight. Slightly below neutral. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 47 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Measures of underlying inflation remained subdued" and "core inflation and other measures of underlying inflation remained subdued, though they appeared to have bottomed out."
- "A number of business contacts indicated that they were passing on at least a portion of these higher costs to their customers."
- "Several of them indicated, in light of recent developments, that the risks to their forecasts of inflation had shifted somewhat to the upside."

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants see underlying inflation as subdued but bottoming, with headline boosted by commodities. The net assessment is slightly below the 50 midpoint, with upside risk from commodity pass-through.

**Minority views**: "A few" worried about inflation expectations becoming unanchored if balance sheet size raises doubts; some debated whether slack meaningfully restrains inflation, citing the 2003-04 episode. These widen the band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The economic recovery was on a firmer footing" and "the recovery was gaining traction."
- "Significant slack in resource utilization" and "there was still substantial slack in the labor market."

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy recovering but clearly below potential, with substantial slack acknowledged broadly.

**Minority views**: Narrow range of views -- all agree slack is substantial. Uncertainty about degree of slack noted.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants generally judged that there was still substantial slack in the labor market."
- "Several participants noted that the drop in unemployment was attributable more to people withdrawing from the labor force and to fewer layoffs than to increased hiring."
- "Scattered reports indicated that firms in some regions were having difficulty hiring some types of highly skilled workers."

**Center-of-gravity**: Substantial labor market slack despite declining unemployment rate. The unemployment decline overstates improvement.

**Minority views**: Scattered skilled-worker shortages noted in some regions, but these are isolated and do not shift the center of gravity.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 42 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Spending by households...rose further during the early part of 2011, with auto sales showing particular strength."
- "Some participants noted that growth in consumer spending so far this year had not been as vigorous as they had anticipated."
- "Manufacturing firms were particularly upbeat. Some contacts reported they were increasing capital budgets."

**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate expansion with business investment firming, consumer spending growing but below expectations, and housing depressed. Below trend but improving.

**Minority views**: Business contacts more optimistic than macro data; consumer side weighed down by gasoline prices and housing weakness.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 45 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Credit conditions remained uneven. ...Large firms...continued to find credit...available on relatively attractive terms; however, credit conditions reportedly remained tight for smaller, bank-dependent firms."
- "Terms and conditions for auto loans had returned to historical norms."
- "A few participants expressed concern that the easing of credit conditions in some sectors was becoming or might become excessive."

**Center-of-gravity**: Mixed conditions -- loosening for large firms and consumer credit, still tight for small business and CRE. Net slightly below neutral (slightly loose side given the easing trend).

**Minority views**: A few concerned about excessive easing in some sectors, widening the band.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 25 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Committee members agreed that no changes to the Committee's asset purchase program or to its target range for the federal funds rate were warranted at this meeting."
- "A few participants indicated that economic conditions might warrant a move toward less-accommodative monetary policy this year; a few others noted that exceptional policy accommodation could be appropriate beyond 2011."
- Committee continued QE2 and exit planning.

**Center-of-gravity**: Firmly in the accommodative/dovish camp -- continuing QE2, maintaining near-zero rates. But the center is beginning to shift as exit planning is discussed, placing this at the upper end of the QE-era dovish range.

**Minority views**: "A few" on each side -- some wanting tightening this year, some wanting accommodation beyond 2011.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Committee members agreed that no changes...were warranted" -- consensus on current action.
- "A few participants indicated that economic conditions might warrant a move toward less-accommodative monetary policy this year; a few others noted that exceptional policy accommodation could be appropriate beyond 2011."
- Debate over whether slack restrains inflation ("the importance of resource slack as a factor influencing inflation was debated").

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on current action with notable differences about forward timing and some intellectual debate about inflation dynamics.

**Minority views**: Small factions on both sides of the timing question, plus analytical disagreement about the role of slack.

**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal remains accommodative -- QE2 still running, exit is being planned but not imminent. Mild easing bias with growing data dependence about timing.

**Minority views**: The "a few" wanting tightening this year pull the forward signal slightly less dovish than it might otherwise be.
