# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2018-08-01 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Post-2009 segmented format with clear participant attribution language.

**Era context**: August 2018. The Fed was in the middle of a gradual rate hiking cycle that began in December 2015. Tax cuts had been enacted in late 2017, providing fiscal stimulus. Trade tensions with China were escalating. The economy was growing above potential with unemployment low. Inflation had recently moved back toward the 2% target after years of undershooting.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all" (trade disputes as uncertainty source), "most" (trade escalation as downside risk; inflation near expectations), "many" (wage growth modest; further gradual increases appropriate; revise "accommodative" language; data dependence), "several" (transitory GDP boost; tariff price effects; yield curve inversion precedent; business investment strength), "some" (upside from momentum; fiscal upside risk; trade policy challenge; labor slack remaining; inflation weak connection to resources), "a few" (fiscal fading downside risk; labor slack via participation; increased confidence inflation sustained; Dallas trimmed mean below 2%; financial stability capital buffers).

**Core dimension reasoning**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as at/near the 2% target. "Both overall inflation and core inflation...had remained near 2 percent." "Many participants anticipated that...high levels of resource utilization and stable inflation expectations would keep inflation near 2 percent." Tariffs noted as short-term upward pressure. A few noted Dallas trimmed mean still below 2%. Several noted firms had greater scope to raise prices. The center of gravity is inflation right at target with balanced risks, perhaps a very slight upward tilt from tariffs and pricing power. Direction: at target. Score should be near 50, perhaps 51-53 given the tariff/pricing-power upside flavoring.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic activity had been rising at a strong rate." "Above-trend GDP growth." "Economy was projected to move further above its potential." "Continued momentum in spending." Bright-line rule: "above potential" language means score MUST be >50. "Further above its potential" suggests clearly above. Direction: clearly above potential. Score around 57-62.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor market had continued to strengthen." "Unemployment rate had stayed low." "Labor market tightness was reflected in recent readings on rates of private-sector job openings and quits." "Labor shortages" cited by business contacts. But "a few participants suggested there could still be some labor market slack" citing participation rates. Wage growth only "modestly" picked up. Center of gravity: tight labor market with minor dissent. Direction: above neutral, tight. Score around 60-65.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Household spending and business fixed investment had grown strongly." "Considerable momentum in spending by households and businesses." "Consumer spending had been quite strong in the second quarter." Business fixed investment continued strength. But residential investment weakened. Agricultural sector adversely affected. Direction: above trend, strong. Score around 62-66.

5. **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Accommodative financial conditions" supporting growth. "Financial conditions remained supportive of economic expansion." "Only a small change in overall financial conditions." "Corporate borrowing terms remained easy." "Asset valuations remained elevated." Direction: conditions are loose/accommodative. Higher = tighter, so score below 50. Score around 28-33.

6. **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: No rate change at this meeting. "Many participants suggested that if incoming data continued to support their current economic outlook, it would likely soon be appropriate to take another step in removing policy accommodation." "Further gradual increases" expected. "Current stance...remained accommodative." They agreed to hold at this meeting but signaled further hikes soon. This is a lean hawkish posture -- holding with a clear tightening bias. Anchors: 70-75 for 2017-18 hiking. Score around 67-70.

7. **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Broad agreement on the assessment and hold decision. "Participants agreed" on key assessments. Minor differences: "a few" saw possible labor slack vs others who saw tightness; some debate on yield curve significance; "some" questioned inflation-resource link. But no formal dissents mentioned. Direction: broad agreement with minor nuance. Score around 22-28.

8. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Moderate discussion. One paragraph on financial stability: "Participants who commented on financial stability noted that asset valuations remained elevated and corporate borrowing terms remained easy." Discussion of bank capital, countercyclical capital buffer. "Some participants raised the concern that a prolonged period...beyond potential could give rise to...financial imbalances." Also yield curve discussion. Direction: moderate attention. Score around 35-40.

9. **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Many participants suggested that...it would likely soon be appropriate to take another step in removing policy accommodation." "Further gradual increases in the target range." Discussion of dropping "accommodative" language because funds rate "moving closer to the range of estimates of its neutral level." This is clearly a tightening forward signal. Score around 70-73.

10. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "All participants pointed to ongoing trade disagreements and proposed trade measures as an important source of uncertainty and risks." Trade uncertainty is prominent. "Considerable uncertainty in estimates of the neutral rate." But "risks to the economic outlook as roughly balanced." Standard elevated uncertainty primarily around trade. Direction: somewhat elevated. Score around 40-47.

**Polarity checks**:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence = at target with slight upward tilt. Score 52. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence = "further above potential." Score 59. Matches.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence = tight, low unemployment, shortages. Score 62. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence = loose/accommodative. Score 30. Matches (low score = loose).
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence = further gradual hikes signaled. Score 68. Matches.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence = "soon appropriate to take another step." Score 71. Matches.

**Cross-dimension coherence**:
- OUTPUT_GAP (59) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (62): Both above 50, coherent with above-potential, tight labor economy.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (68) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (71): Both hawkish-leaning, coherent. Forward guidance slightly more hawkish because the explicit "soon" signal for next hike.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 52 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Both overall inflation and core inflation...had remained near 2 percent on a 12-month basis."
- "Many participants anticipated that, over the medium term, high levels of resource utilization and stable inflation expectations would keep inflation near 2 percent."
- "Reports from several Districts suggested that firms had greater scope than in the recent past to raise prices in response to strong demand or increases in input costs, including those associated with tariff increases."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants see inflation as essentially at the 2% target with modestly balanced upside risks from tariffs and pricing power, offset by some noting weak pass-through from resource pressures and trimmed-mean still slightly below 2%.

**Minority views**: "A few participants observed that the trimmed mean measure of inflation...was still below 2 percent." "Some participants observed that inflation in recent years had shown only a weak connection to measures of resource pressures."

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 59 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity had been rising at a strong rate."
- "Participants generally expected that GDP growth would...still exceed that of potential output" and "above-trend GDP growth."
- "The economy was projected to move further above its potential."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants clearly view the economy as operating above potential and continuing to do so, with strong GDP growth exceeding potential output growth. The "further above" language indicates a meaningful positive gap.

**Minority views**: "Several participants stressed the possibility that real GDP growth in the second quarter may have been boosted by transitory factors."

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 62 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Labor market had continued to strengthen" and "the unemployment rate had stayed low."
- "Labor market tightness was reflected in recent readings on rates of private-sector job openings and quits."
- "Business contacts in various sectors had cited labor shortages and other supply constraints as impediments to production."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The labor market is clearly tight with low unemployment, high job openings and quits, and reports of labor shortages from business contacts, though wages have only modestly picked up.

**Minority views**: "A few participants suggested there could still be some labor market slack, citing recent increases in labor force participation rates." "Measures of aggregate nominal wage growth had so far picked up only modestly."

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 64 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Household spending and business fixed investment had grown strongly."
- "Incoming data indicated considerable momentum in spending by households and businesses."
- "Consumer spending had been quite strong in the second quarter" and "continued strength in business fixed investment."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Both consumer spending and business investment are characterized as strong and above trend, with "considerable momentum." Residential investment is a notable drag but the net assessment is firmly above trend.

**Minority views**: "Persistent weakness and the risk of a further slowdown in residential investment were also noted." "Agricultural sector had been adversely affected."

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Accommodative financial conditions" and "financial conditions remained supportive of economic expansion."
- "Only a small change in overall financial conditions occurred."
- "Asset valuations remained elevated and corporate borrowing terms remained easy."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions are clearly loose/accommodative, supporting economic expansion. Credit terms are easy, asset valuations elevated, and the overall environment is supportive rather than restrictive.

**Minority views**: None notable -- broad agreement on this characterization.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 68 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Many participants suggested that if incoming data continued to support their current economic outlook, it would likely soon be appropriate to take another step in removing policy accommodation."
- "Participants generally expected that further gradual increases in the target range for the federal funds rate would be consistent with a sustained expansion."
- "Participants agreed that it would be appropriate for the Committee to leave the target range...unchanged at this meeting."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants hold at this meeting but have a clear tightening bias with "many" signaling another hike "soon." The consensus is for continued gradual removal of accommodation. This is moderate hawkish -- active hiking cycle with data-dependent forward lean.

**Minority views**: "Some participants suggested that, in the event of a major escalation in trade disputes...presented a challenge in determining the appropriate monetary policy response." Trade tensions introduce some caution.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 22 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants agreed that information received since the FOMC met in June indicated that the labor market had continued to strengthen."
- "Participants agreed that it would be appropriate...to leave the target range...unchanged at this meeting."
- Minor disagreements: some on labor slack, yield curve interpretation, inflation dynamics.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Broad agreement on both the economic assessment and the policy action. Differences are nuanced (pace/timing, specific indicator interpretations) rather than fundamental, with no formal dissents evident.

**Minority views**: Differences on labor slack, yield curve significance, and inflation-resource utilization link, but these are minor emphasis differences rather than policy disagreements.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 37 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants who commented on financial stability noted that asset valuations remained elevated and corporate borrowing terms remained easy."
- Discussion of countercyclical capital buffer and bank capital positions.
- "Some participants raised the concern that a prolonged period in which the economy operated beyond potential could give rise to...financial imbalances."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial stability receives a limited but substantive discussion. One dedicated paragraph covers asset valuations, borrowing terms, bank capital, and the countercyclical buffer. Additional mentions of financial imbalances and yield curve. Not a dominant topic but more than passing references.

**Minority views**: "A few participants emphasized that financial stability risks could be reduced if these institutions further boosted their capital cushions."

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 71 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Many participants suggested that...it would likely soon be appropriate to take another step in removing policy accommodation."
- "Participants generally expected that further gradual increases in the target range for the federal funds rate would be consistent with a sustained expansion."
- "Many participants noted that it would likely be appropriate...to revise the Committee's characterization of the stance of monetary policy" away from "accommodative" language, signaling rates approaching neutral.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal is clearly tilted toward further tightening, with "many" signaling a hike "soon" and ongoing gradual increases expected. The discussion about dropping "accommodative" language reinforces that rates are approaching neutral, but the path still points to further hikes.

**Minority views**: Trade uncertainty could alter the path; "most expressed the view that an escalation in international trade disputes was a potentially consequential downside risk."

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 43 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All participants pointed to ongoing trade disagreements and proposed trade measures as an important source of uncertainty and risks."
- "A number of participants emphasized the considerable uncertainty in estimates of the neutral rate of interest."
- "Participants generally viewed the risks to the economic outlook as roughly balanced."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Uncertainty is somewhat elevated, primarily driven by trade policy concerns (flagged by "all" participants) and neutral rate estimation. However, the overall risk assessment is "roughly balanced" and the economic outlook is fairly clear, keeping uncertainty from being highly elevated.

**Minority views**: None -- the trade uncertainty concern is broadly shared.
