# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2000-03-21

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Unsegmented — staff and participant content are mixed, but participant language cues are clear ("members commented," "members expressed concern," "all the members endorsed," etc.).

**Era context**: March 2000 — the peak of the late-1990s/early-2000s boom. The Fed was in the midst of a tightening cycle that began mid-1999. The dot-com bubble was at or near its peak. The economy was running hot with exceptional productivity growth. This meeting raised the fed funds rate 25bp to 6%.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all the members endorsed" (policy action), "members" used broadly (suggesting consensus views on most topics), "some members" (concern about high-tech valuations; preference for 50bp at some point), "a few" is not used. The language suggests broad consensus with minor tactical differences.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly above the midpoint. They see "few signs to date of significant acceleration in inflation" and "little evidence to date of any acceleration in core inflation." However, they express concern about "a less benign inflation climate," rising fuel prices, import prices, medical costs, and "some deterioration in surveys of inflation expectations." They believe demand growth will "at some point foster inflationary imbalances." Direction: above neutral — inflation is not yet accelerating but the risks are clearly tilted upward. The language is forward-looking concern rather than current observation. Score ~58. Higher = more pressure perceived. Evidence says moderate current pressure but rising risk. Matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants clearly describe the economy as above potential. "Aggregate demand could continue to grow faster than potential aggregate supply." "Persisting strength in aggregate domestic demand." "Substantial risks of rising pressures on labor and other resources." The economy is described as having "rapid expansion" with demand exceeding supply. Per the bright-line rule, above-potential language demands >50. This is clearly above potential. Score ~63.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Very tight conditions in labor markets." "Rising pressures on labor and other resources." Shortages of labor are retarding construction. "Draw on the pool of available workers" as a "safety valve" that "could not be counted on to work indefinitely." This is clearly tight — late-1990s anchor is 65-75. Score ~70.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumer spending had remained particularly robust." "Business fixed investment again appeared to be expanding at a vigorous pace." "Remarkable vigor" in aggregate demand. "High levels of consumer and business confidence." This is well above trend. Score ~68.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Accommodative financial markets." "Ready availability of both debt and equity financing." "Somewhat unsettled financial markets" is mentioned in the policy context. Stock market has posted "further gains." Overall, conditions are described as loose/accommodative with some recent unsettledness. Score ~30 (loose conditions, higher = tighter, so low score).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All the members endorsed a proposal to tighten reserve conditions" (25bp hike). They see "substantial risks of rising pressures" calling for "some further firming." "Additional tightening might well be needed." Risks remain "tilted toward heightened inflation pressures." Some wanted 50bp at some point. This is clearly hawkish — consensus for tightening with forward bias for more. Score ~72.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All the members endorsed" the 25bp hike. There are "some members" who thought 50bp might be needed at some point, but they agreed the gradual approach was appropriate now. This is near-consensus with minor tactical differences. Score ~18.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Stock market valuations discussed — "historically elevated valuations of many high-tech stocks" with risk of "sizable market adjustment." "Increased volatility of the stock market." "Somewhat unsettled financial markets." Equity market discussion is meaningful but not the dominant topic. Score ~35.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Committee would continue to assess the need for further tightening." "Additional tightening might well be needed." "Risks would remain tilted toward heightened inflation pressures." Clear hawkish forward signal. Score ~75.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Subject to a wide range of uncertainty as to both degree and timing." "Uncertainties continued to surround the economic outlook." But the overall tone is fairly confident about direction — they know demand is exceeding supply, they just can't pinpoint the degree or timing. Moderate uncertainty. Score ~40.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (63) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70): Both above 50, consistent with overheating economy. Labor slightly higher because "very tight" language is more emphatic than the output gap discussion. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (72) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (75): Both hawkish, coherent. Forward guidance slightly higher because the statement explicitly flags further tightening ahead.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 58 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "they also saw few signs to date of significant acceleration in inflation" / "little evidence to date of any acceleration in core inflation"
- "members expressed concern about indications of a less benign inflation climate. The direct and indirect effects of higher fuel prices, the rise in other import prices, increasing medical costs, and some deterioration in surveys of inflation expectations could begin to show through to higher underlying inflation"
- "the members continued to be concerned about the risks of rising inflation"

**Center-of-gravity**: Members perceive inflation as currently contained but with clear upside risks from multiple channels (energy, imports, tight labor). The concern is forward-looking rather than realized, placing this modestly above neutral.

**Minority views**: None noted — members appear broadly aligned on this assessment.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 63 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "they saw little evidence of any slowing in the rapid expansion of domestic economic activity"
- "aggregate demand could continue to grow faster than potential aggregate supply, even under optimistic assumptions regarding future productivity gains"
- "persistence of rapid growth in aggregate demand beyond growth in aggregate supply"

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is clearly running above potential, with demand outpacing supply even after accounting for strong productivity gains. This is a consensus view.

**Minority views**: None — this is a unanimous assessment.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 70 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "very tight conditions in labor markets"
- "substantial risks of rising pressures on labor and other resources"
- "housing and other construction activity reportedly was being retarded by shortages of labor"
- "the economy's ability to draw on the pool of available workers" described as a safety valve running low

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor markets are described as very tight with active shortages in some sectors. Consistent with late-1990s peak tightness.

**Minority views**: None noted.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 68 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The growth in aggregate demand continued to display remarkable vigor"
- "consumer spending had remained particularly robust thus far this year"
- "business fixed investment again appeared to be expanding at a vigorous pace"
- "brisk spending in a number of other areas as well"

**Center-of-gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment are described as strong to very strong, well above historical trend. Some moderation in consumer spending is expected but not yet evident.

**Minority views**: Some moderation expected ("a reasonable expectation"), but current conditions are uniformly characterized as very strong.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 30 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "accommodative financial markets"
- "ready availability of both debt and equity financing"
- "somewhat unsettled financial markets" (recent development)
- "increased volatility of the stock market"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are broadly described as accommodative/loose, though with some recent unsettledness and increased volatility. The overall picture remains one of easy financial conditions.

**Minority views**: The "somewhat unsettled" and "increased volatility" language suggests some tightening at the margin, but not enough to change the overall characterization.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 72 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all the members endorsed a proposal to tighten reserve conditions" (25bp hike to 6%)
- "the members saw substantial risks of rising pressures on labor and other resources and of higher inflation that called for some further firming of monetary policy"
- "Some members commented that, although a more forceful policy move of 50 basis points might be needed at some point"
- "additional tightening might well be needed"

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous support for tightening with clear forward bias for more. Some members open to 50bp moves. This is solidly hawkish.

**Minority views**: The debate is about pace (25bp vs potentially 50bp), not direction. All agree on tightening.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 18 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all the members endorsed a proposal to tighten reserve conditions"
- Some wanted 50bp potentially "at some point" but agreed 25bp was right for now
- "a gradual approach to policy adjustments was warranted" — shared view

**Center-of-gravity**: Near-consensus. The only variation is tactical (some open to faster pace later), not directional.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 35 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "some members expressed concern that the historically elevated valuations of many high-tech stocks were subject to a sizable market adjustment at some point"
- "That risk was underscored by the increased volatility of the stock market"
- "somewhat unsettled financial markets"
- Stock market wealth effects discussed as driver of consumer spending

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability receives limited but meaningful discussion, primarily focused on equity market valuations and their potential to correct. Not a dominant topic but clearly present.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 75 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the Committee would continue to assess the need for further tightening to contain inflation"
- "additional tightening might well be needed to ensure that financial conditions would adjust sufficiently"
- "the press statement...should continue to highlight their view that even after today's tightening move the risks would remain tilted toward heightened inflation pressures"

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear hawkish forward signal — further tightening expected, risks tilted toward inflation. The language is not tentative; it is a clear directional signal.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 40 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "subject to a wide range of uncertainty as to both degree and timing"
- "uncertainties continued to surround the economic outlook"
- "a significant acceleration in inflation did not appear to be imminent" — some confidence about near-term

**Center-of-gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty. Members are confident about direction (demand exceeding supply, tight labor) but uncertain about the degree and timing of inflation consequences. The uncertainty is genuine but bounded.
