# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1999-11-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format -- staff and participant content mixed. I will score only participant-attributable language ("members commented," "members noted," "members saw," etc.).

**Era context**: Late 1999, tail end of the late-90s boom. Economy running hot, labor markets very tight, productivity acceleration ongoing, Y2K concerns in background. The Fed was in a tightening cycle (this meeting raised rates 25bp). Inflation was moderate but risks tilted up.

**Quantifier distribution**: Heavy use of "all the members" (supported rate hike, accepted symmetric directive), "members" (general), "a number of members" (uncertain about further tightening), "other members" (thought further firming probably needed), "many members" (prospective demand pressures). No "most/majority" vs "several/some/a few" splits -- the document mostly uses "members" collectively, with a split at the end between "a number" and "other members" on the forward outlook.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as currently moderate but with significant upside risks. "Consumer price inflation had remained moderate, albeit above year-earlier levels." BUT "members nonetheless remained concerned about the outlook for inflation" -- reversals in energy, import prices, healthcare costs, possible labor cost pickup. "Significant risk that inflation would strengthen over time." Current perception: slightly above neutral with clear upside tilt. Score should be in the 51-60 range -- "modestly above" with forward concern. Direction: above midpoint.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Economy clearly above potential. "Robust growth," "unsustainable pace," "growth at an unsustainable pace continued to ratchet up pressures," "tightening resource constraints indicating unsustainable growth." The bright-line rule: "unsustainable pace" = above potential, must be > 50. Language is strong: "unsustainable" used multiple times. Score: 59-65 range ("clearly above potential; running hot").

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Very tight. "Long duration of very tight labor markets across the nation," "pool of unemployed workers willing to take a job had continued to be drawn down," "unusually high level of labor resource utilization," "added pressures on already tight labor markets." This is strong language. Score: 68-73 range ("very tight; severe shortages").

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Strong but expected to moderate. Consumer spending robust, "extraordinarily high" motor vehicle sales with "some moderation," retail contacts "uniformly optimistic," "very high level of consumer confidence." Business equipment spending strong uptrend continuing. Housing softening but from elevated levels. Exports picking up. Overall: above trend but with some early moderation signs. Score: 60-65 ("above trend; robust").

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Members note "real interest rates had risen to some extent" and "rise that had occurred in long-term interest rates, including mortgage rates" were having effects. Long-term rates rose, mortgage rates rose, housing softening. But no discussion of credit stress or spreads widening. Conditions tightening somewhat from policy actions. Score: 50-55 (neutral to somewhat tight from rate rises).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All the members supported raising the Committee's target for the federal funds rate by 25 basis points." Unanimous hike. Forward: split -- "a number of members" uncertain about further tightening, "other members" thought "additional firming probably would be necessary." Symmetric directive adopted (signals no imminent further action), but partly tactical (Y2K concerns). The action is hawkish (hike), forward lean is mixed but tilted hawkish. Score: 63-68 ("moderate hawkish: conditions warrant increase").

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All the members supported" the rate hike. "All the members accepted" symmetric directive. But "views differed to an extent on the outlook for inflation and policy going forward" -- a split between "a number" uncertain about further tightening and "other members" who thought more was needed. No formal dissents. Broad agreement on action, mild differences on forward path. Score: 20-28 ("broad agreement, minor emphasis differences").

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Limited discussion. Stock market mentioned as context for wealth effects and consumption outlook, not as stability concern. Y2K "financial uncertainties" mentioned briefly. No substantive discussion of financial stability risks, credit conditions, or systemic concerns. Score: 15-22 (brief/passing mentions).

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Symmetric directive adopted -- "no further policy move was likely to be considered before the Committee's meeting in February." But "other members" suggested "additional firming probably would be necessary." The symmetric directive is partly tactical (Y2K), not a genuine signal of neutrality. On balance, mild tightening bias forward but officially neutral near-term. Score: 58-64 (mild tightening bias).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Members acknowledged that their forecasts were subject to a substantial degree of uncertainty." "Considerable uncertainty" regarding housing. "Questions surrounding the outlook." "Quite uncertain about the possible need for further tightening." Multiple references to uncertainty about productivity's implications. Score: 42-50 ("somewhat elevated to elevated").

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 55: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says moderate with upside risks. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP 62: Higher = more above potential. "Unsustainable" growth. Matches.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS 70: Higher = tighter. "Very tight labor markets." Matches.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 65: Higher = more hawkish. Unanimous hike. Matches.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (62) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70): Both above 50, labor tighter than output gap -- consistent with late-90s where labor was the binding constraint while productivity kept output growing.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (65) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (60): Both lean hawkish, forward slightly less due to symmetric directive. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 55 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "consumer price inflation had remained moderate, albeit above year-earlier levels owing to a sharp rise in energy prices"
- "members nonetheless remained concerned about the outlook for inflation" -- reversal of favorable factors (energy, import prices, healthcare costs)
- "significant risk that inflation would strengthen over time given prevailing financial conditions"
- "any pickup in inflation was likely to be gradual, with cost pressures quite possibly continuing to be held largely in check for some time by improving productivity trends"

**Center-of-gravity**: Members perceive current inflation as moderate but modestly above earlier levels, with significant concern about future inflation pressures from reversing favorable factors. The "gradual" qualifier keeps this from scoring higher.

**Minority views**: None clearly identified -- members broadly shared this assessment.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 62 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "robust growth in overall economic activity"
- "growth at an unsustainable pace continued to ratchet up pressures in labor markets"
- "tightening resource constraints indicating unsustainable growth"
- "domestic demand would need to decelerate considerably for growth to proceed at a sustainable pace"

**Center-of-gravity**: Members unanimously see the economy as operating above potential, with growth at an unsustainable pace. Strong language ("unsustainable" used repeatedly) places this clearly above the midpoint.

**Minority views**: None -- this was a consensus assessment.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 70 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "long duration of very tight labor markets across the nation"
- "pool of unemployed workers willing to take a job had continued to be drawn down"
- "unusually high level of labor resource utilization"
- "risks on balance were seen as tilted toward growth strong enough to put added pressures on already tight labor markets"

**Center-of-gravity**: Members describe labor markets as very tight by any measure, with the pool of available workers continuing to shrink. This is consistent with the late-1999 anchor range (65-75).

**Minority views**: None -- universally acknowledged tightness.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 62 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "extraordinarily high levels" of motor vehicle sales (with some moderation)
- "contacts in the retail industry were uniformly optimistic about the outlook for sales during the holiday season"
- "no significant evidence that the strong uptrend in spending on capital equipment might otherwise be weakening"
- Housing softening from "recent peaks" but "at a fairly elevated level"

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending and business investment are both running above trend. Some early signs of moderation in housing and autos, but overall spending remains robust. Exports strengthening adds further momentum.

**Minority views**: None significant -- broad consensus on strong spending.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 52 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "real interest rates had risen to some extent to restore balance between supply and demand"
- "lagged and to some extent already evident effects of the rise that had occurred in long-term interest rates, including mortgage rates"
- "a less buoyant stock market, should the latter persist"
- Y2K concerns about "potential financial uncertainties and unsettlement"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are near neutral, with some tightening from higher long-term rates and mortgage rates. No discussion of credit stress or spreads. Conditions are slightly tighter than neutral but not restrictively so.

**Minority views**: Not explicitly discussed as a point of disagreement.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 65 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "all the members supported raising the Committee's target for the federal funds rate by 25 basis points"
- "tightening resource constraints indicating unsustainable growth, only tentative signs that growth might be slowing, and various factors that had been damping prices now turning around"
- Split on forward: "a number of members indicated that they were quite uncertain about the possible need for further tightening" vs "other members... suggested that additional firming of the stance of policy probably would be necessary"

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimously hawkish on the current action (25bp hike). Forward lean is mixed but tilted toward further tightening. The symmetric directive is partly tactical (Y2K) rather than a genuine dovish signal.

**Minority views**: "A number of members" were uncertain about further tightening and wanted to wait for data, keeping the score below 70.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 23 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "all the members supported raising the Committee's target for the federal funds rate by 25 basis points"
- "all the members accepted a proposal to adopt a symmetric directive"
- "views differed to an extent on the outlook for inflation and policy going forward"

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous on both the rate action and the directive. The only disagreement is about the forward outlook -- some uncertain about further tightening, others expecting it. This is minor emphasis differences, not substantive policy disagreement at this meeting.

**Minority views**: The forward-looking split is noted but does not rise to dissent.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 18 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Stock market mentioned only as context for consumption/wealth effects, not as stability risk
- Y2K: "unsettling effect on financial markets at a time when concerns relating to the century date change might be adding to normal year-end pressures"
- No discussion of systemic risk, credit conditions, or financial imbalances per se

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability is not a prominent topic. Stock market and Y2K are mentioned but in passing and primarily as macroeconomic or tactical considerations, not as financial stability concerns.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 60 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Symmetric directive: "consistent with the Committee's current expectation that no further policy move was likely to be considered before the Committee's meeting in February"
- "other members... suggested that additional firming of the stance of policy probably would be necessary to keep inflation in check"
- "a number of members indicated that they were quite uncertain about the possible need for further tightening"

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is officially neutral near-term (symmetric directive, no action expected before February). But the underlying tilt is toward further tightening, with part of the neutral stance driven by Y2K tactical concerns rather than genuine policy neutrality. Net: mild tightening bias.

**Minority views**: "A number of members" genuinely uncertain, widening the band.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 45 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "members acknowledged that their forecasts were subject to a substantial degree of uncertainty"
- "outlook for housing was subject to considerable uncertainty"
- "a number of members indicated that they were quite uncertain about the possible need for further tightening"
- "they recognized that forecasts of rising inflation had failed to materialize in recent years, raising questions about their understanding of the empirical specification"

**Center-of-gravity**: Members express elevated uncertainty on multiple fronts: the macro outlook, the inflation process itself, housing, and the appropriate policy path. "Substantial degree of uncertainty" is meaningful language. The admission about inflation forecasting models is notable.
