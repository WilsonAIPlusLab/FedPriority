# FOMC Scoring — 2021-01-27 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented — clear "Participants' Views" section distinct from staff content.

**Era context**: January 2021 — roughly 10 months after the COVID-19 emergency. Vaccines rolling out but slowly; winter COVID surge; economy recovering unevenly with service sectors still depressed, goods spending strong. Fed funds rate at 0-0.25%, QE running at $120B/month. New AIT framework in place. CAA fiscal stimulus just passed; more fiscal support anticipated (what became the March 2021 ARPA).

**Quantifier distribution**: Heavy use of "participants" (collective), "most," "many," "several," "some," "a few," "a couple." "All participants" used for supporting current policy settings. "Most" used for fiscal boost expectations and inflation risks weighted to downside.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW the midpoint. "Headline PCE price inflation...was poised to come in well below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective." "Weaker demand and earlier declines in oil prices were holding down consumer price inflation." Some noted possible near-term relative price increases from supply constraints and base effects, but "many participants stressed the importance of distinguishing between such one-time changes in relative prices and changes in the underlying trend for inflation." "Most still viewed the risks as weighted to the downside." This is clearly below-target inflation with some nascent supply-side price pressures noted. Direction: below 50. Magnitude: "well below" 2% target → roughly 26-35 range. The discussion of supply constraints nudges it slightly higher. Score ~30.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says low inflation, well below target. Score below 50. Correct.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as BELOW potential. "Economic activity and employment were currently well below levels consistent with achieving maximum employment." "The economy remained far from the Committee's longer-run goals." "The pace of the recovery in economic activity and employment had moderated in recent months." But medium-term outlook improved with vaccines and fiscal. The current assessment is clearly below potential.
- Bright-line rule: "well below" → must be < 50. "Far from" goals reinforces this.
- Magnitude: "well below" and "far from" = significant distance. But recovery underway, some sectors strong (goods, housing, manufacturing). Score ~32.
- Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Economy well below potential → below 50. Correct.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see the labor market as LOOSE. "The economy was far from achieving the Committee's broad-based and inclusive goal of maximum employment." "Payroll employment fell in December." "If the sizable number of workers who reported having left the labor force since the beginning of the pandemic were to be counted as unemployed, the unemployment rate would be substantially higher." "Even with a brisk pace of improvement in the labor market, achieving this goal would take some time."
- Direction: well below full employment → below 50. "Far from maximum employment" = substantial slack. Score ~28.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says very loose. Below 50. Correct.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. "Strong spending on many goods, especially durables" but "weakness in spending on some services, especially in travel and in leisure and hospitality." "Business equipment investment had continued to show strength while nonresidential construction remained weak." "Residential investment and home sales remained robust." Overall a mixed-but-below-trend picture due to the service sector drag. "Improved prospects for household spending over the course of the year" but currently constrained. Net: below trend but with pockets of strength. Score ~40.
- Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Mixed with significant service-sector weakness dragging it down. Below 50 but not deeply so because of goods/housing/equipment strength. Correct.

**5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Overall financial conditions remained highly accommodative." "A few participants remarked that credit conditions were relatively tight for borrowers with low credit scores and for some small and medium-sized businesses." The center of gravity is clearly loose/accommodative. Score ~20.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Conditions "highly accommodative" = low score. Correct.

**6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All participants supported maintaining the Committee's current settings and outcome-based guidance." "Maintaining a highly accommodative stance of policy was essential." "The stance for policy would need to remain accommodative until those goals were achieved." QE continuing at $120B/month. Outcome-based guidance in place. This is firmly dovish — maximum accommodation with no hint of tapering discussion. Score ~15.
- Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Full accommodation, no dissent, QE ongoing, economy "far from goals" → very dovish. Low score. Correct.
- Note: QE is running plus near-zero rates with outcome-based guidance. This is strong dovish territory (11-25 range per anchors for QE era).

**7. DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All participants supported maintaining the Committee's current settings." No dissents mentioned. Some variation in views on inflation risks and financial stability topics, but broad agreement on policy. Score ~12.
- Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Near-consensus. Low score. Correct.

**8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: "A number of participants commented on issues related to financial stability." Discussion covers banking resilience, structural vulnerabilities (run-prone funds, Treasury market fragilities), elevated equity valuations, IPO activity, retail trading, low risk spreads, CRE stress. This is a moderately substantive paragraph — "several" and "some" and "a few" participating. Not dominant but meaningful. Score ~40.
- Polarity check: Higher = more attention. Moderate discussion. Correct.

**9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is firmly dovish. "Likely to take some time for substantial further progress to be achieved." Outcome-based guidance promising rates at zero until maximum employment and inflation targets met. Asset purchases continuing until "substantial further progress." "Various participants noted the importance of the Committee clearly communicating its assessment of progress toward its longer-run goals well in advance" — signaling no change imminent. Score ~8.
- Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Forward guidance is extremely dovish with outcome-based thresholds. Very low score. Correct.
- Anchors: 2-10 for 2020 emergency guidance. January 2021 still firmly in this zone.

**10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants continued to see the uncertainty surrounding that outlook as elevated." "The path ahead remained highly uncertain, with the pandemic continuing to pose considerable risks." "The path of the economy would depend significantly on the course of the virus." "Many participants remarked that the pandemic continued to pose considerable risks." Multiple sources of uncertainty: virus strains, vaccine distribution, fiscal. Score ~65.
- Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. "Elevated" and "highly uncertain" and "considerable risks" = well above normal. Correct.
- Magnitude: "elevated" and "highly uncertain" → 60-75 range.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Checks

- **OUTPUT_GAP (32) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (28)**: Both well below 50, consistent. Labor slightly lower because of hidden unemployment (labor force exits) making true slack even greater.
- **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (15) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (8)**: Both very dovish. Forward guidance slightly more dovish because the outcome-based thresholds lock in accommodation for a long time. Consistent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 30 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "headline PCE price inflation in December, measured on a 12-month basis, was poised to come in well below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective"
- "Weaker demand and earlier declines in oil prices were holding down consumer price inflation"
- "a number of participants suggested that there could be increases in the prices of some goods whose production has been subject to supply chain constraints"

**Center of gravity**: Inflation well below target with nascent supply-side pressures acknowledged but characterized as temporary relative-price shifts rather than underlying trend inflation. Most participants still viewed inflation risks as weighted to the downside.

**Minority views**: Several participants flagged supply constraints as an upside risk; some noted rising market-based inflation compensation. This widens the band modestly.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 32 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "economic activity and employment were currently well below levels consistent with achieving maximum employment"
- "the economy remained far from the Committee's longer-run goals"
- "Most participants expected that the stimulus provided by the passage of the CAA...would lead to a sizable boost in economic activity"

**Center of gravity**: Economy well below potential with recovery underway but moderated by the winter COVID surge. Medium-term outlook improved but current position firmly below potential.

**Minority views**: Some upside risks noted (fiscal more expansionary, savings drawdown), but no one argued economy was near potential.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 28 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the economy was far from achieving the Committee's broad-based and inclusive goal of maximum employment and...even with a brisk pace of improvement in the labor market, achieving this goal would take some time"
- "Payroll employment fell in December"
- "if the sizable number of workers who reported having left the labor force...were to be counted as unemployed, the unemployment rate would be substantially higher"

**Center of gravity**: Labor market far from full employment with hidden slack from labor force exits. Improvement stalled in December with net job losses. Very loose conditions.

**Minority views**: Some noted medium-term expectation of strong employment growth, but current level assessment uniformly shows substantial slack.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 40 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "strong spending on many goods, especially durables, and weakness in spending on some services, especially in travel and in leisure and hospitality"
- "business equipment investment had continued to show strength while nonresidential construction remained weak"
- "residential investment and home sales remained robust"

**Center of gravity**: Bifurcated picture — goods, equipment, and housing strong but services deeply depressed by pandemic. Net assessment is below trend given the large service-sector drag.

**Minority views**: Some business contacts were optimistic about improvement; the split between goods strength and services weakness creates moderate uncertainty.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 20 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "overall financial conditions remained highly accommodative, in part reflecting investors' optimism about the economic outlook along with the accommodative stance of monetary policy"
- "a few participants remarked that credit conditions were relatively tight for borrowers with low credit scores and for some small and medium-sized businesses"

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions clearly loose/accommodative for most borrowers. Equity valuations elevated, risk spreads low. Tight conditions limited to a narrow slice of lower-credit borrowers.

**Minority views**: A few noted tightness for low-credit and small business borrowers, preventing the score from going even lower.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 15 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all participants supported maintaining the Committee's current settings and outcome-based guidance for the federal funds rate and the pace of asset purchases"
- "maintaining a highly accommodative stance of policy was essential to foster further economic recovery"
- "the stance for policy would need to remain accommodative until those goals were achieved"

**Center of gravity**: Unanimous support for maximum accommodation — near-zero rates plus $120B/month QE. No discussion of tapering or any tightening. Firmly in the strong dovish range.

**Minority views**: None. Complete agreement on maintaining accommodation.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 12 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all participants supported maintaining the Committee's current settings"
- No dissents or reservations mentioned regarding the policy decision

**Center of gravity**: Near-consensus on policy. Some variation in views on inflation risks and financial stability, but no policy disagreement.

**Minority views**: Minor differences in emphasis on supply-side inflation risks and financial stability vulnerabilities, but these do not translate into policy disagreement.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 40 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "A number of participants commented on issues related to financial stability"
- Discussion covered banking resilience, structural vulnerabilities (run-prone funds, Treasury fragilities), equity valuations, IPO activity, retail trading, low risk spreads, CRE stress
- "several participants noted that the pandemic had highlighted structural vulnerabilities in other parts of the financial system"

**Center of gravity**: Meaningful but not dominant discussion. A dedicated paragraph covers multiple financial stability topics with several/some/a few participants contributing. Moderate attention level.

**Minority views**: Range from "banking system resilient" to concerns about equity valuations and structural vulnerabilities.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 8 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "it was likely to take some time for substantial further progress to be achieved"
- outcome-based guidance: rate at zero "until labor market conditions had reached levels consistent with...maximum employment and inflation had risen to 2 percent and was on track to moderately exceed 2 percent for some time"
- asset purchases continue "until substantial further progress toward its employment and inflation goals had been achieved"

**Center of gravity**: Strong easing commitment via outcome-based thresholds. Forward signal is firmly dovish — no hint of approaching the conditions for tapering or rate increases.

**Minority views**: "Various participants noted the importance of clearly communicating assessment of progress well in advance" — this is procedural, not hawkish, but shows awareness of eventual transition.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 65 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "participants continued to see the uncertainty surrounding that outlook as elevated"
- "the path ahead remained highly uncertain, with the pandemic continuing to pose considerable risks to the outlook"
- "Many participants remarked that the pandemic continued to pose considerable risks to the economic outlook, including risks associated with new virus strains, potential public resistance to vaccination, and potential difficulties in the production and distribution of vaccines"

**Center of gravity**: Uncertainty clearly elevated with multiple identified risk factors (virus variants, vaccine rollout, fiscal outcomes). Both downside and upside risks articulated. "Highly uncertain" language places this well above normal.

**Minority views**: Some upside risks noted (fiscal more expansionary, savings drawdown, rapid vaccine boost), contributing to the elevated uncertainty in both directions.
