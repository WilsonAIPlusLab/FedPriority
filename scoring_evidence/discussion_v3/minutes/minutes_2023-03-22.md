# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2023-03-22 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented -- participant views section is clearly separated from staff review.

**Era context**: March 2023. The Fed is deep into its tightening cycle (rate at 4.50-4.75% entering meeting, hiked to 4.75-5.00%). Inflation remains well above 2% target but has been declining from 2022 peaks. The critical backdrop is the Silicon Valley Bank and Signature Bank failures in early March 2023, which introduced a major banking stress shock in the intermeeting period. This meeting sits at the intersection of still-high inflation and new financial stability concerns.

**Quantifier distribution in the text**:
- "All participants" / "participants agreed" -- used for the 25bp hike, banking system soundness, inflation being unacceptably high, labor market very tight
- "Many participants" -- incoming data before banking stress led them to see higher path; effects of banking led to lower rate path assessments
- "Several participants" -- considered holding steady; regional/community bank importance; need for flexibility/optionality; inflation expectations anchoring risk
- "Some participants" -- would have considered 50bp absent banking stress; downside risks to growth increased; upside risks to inflation remain key
- "A few participants" -- credit card delinquencies; nonbank contacts not yet affected
- "A couple of participants" -- high-frequency sentiment not yet changed

**Core dimension reasoning**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as well above target. "Inflation was unacceptably high," "recent data on inflation provided few signs that inflation pressures were abating at a pace sufficient to return inflation to 2 percent." Revisions showed "less disinflation" than thought. Core services ex-housing showed "little evidence pointing to disinflation." This is clearly elevated -- language like "unacceptably high," "well above," "still quite elevated" puts this solidly in the 70-80 range. Not at the extreme 2022 peak language (no "highest in decades" or "accelerating"), but persistent and concerning. Score ~73.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: "Participants generally expected real GDP to grow this year at a pace well below its long-run trend rate." Also: "a period of below-trend growth in real GDP would be needed." Data came in "stronger than expected" before banking stress. But with banking developments expected to weigh on activity, the expectation is below-trend growth going forward. However, the CURRENT state: "recent indicators pointed to modest growth in spending and production" and data was "stronger than expected." The economy is still operating -- not in recession. The language is about growth slowing TO below trend, not that it IS deeply below potential. Combined with very tight labor market and strong job gains, the current output gap is near or slightly above potential, being pulled down by expected credit tightening. Score ~50 -- near potential with modest growth, pulled between strong recent data and expected slowdown from banking stress.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Participants agreed that the labor market remained very tight." "Job gains had picked up to a robust pace." "Unemployment rate remained low." "Labor demand continued to substantially exceed labor supply." Wage growth "still well above" consistent rates. Some signs of easing (quits rate declining, participation rising) but still clearly very tight. "Very tight" with demand "substantially" exceeding supply is strong language. Anchors suggest 65-75 for 2022-23. Score ~68.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending: "pickup in spending in January and February" driven by strong job gains and rising real income. But expected to weigh down from banking stress and uncertainty. Business investment: "being restrained by tighter financial conditions" and expected to be further weighed on by banking developments. Net: some near-term strength in consumption offset by business investment weakness and expected drag. "Modest growth in spending and production" is the headline. Score ~47 -- slightly below trend given the mixed signals and expected drag.

5. **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: This is the dominant story. Banking sector stress has caused credit conditions to tighten. "Recent developments in the banking sector were likely to result in tighter credit conditions." "Banks were likely to tighten lending standards amid rising funding costs and increased concerns about liquidity." Cumulative policy firming already tightened conditions, and banking stress adds on top. But participants also say actions "helped calm conditions." Not a full crisis -- "banking system remained sound and resilient" and problems "limited to a small number of banks." This is tighter than normal but not crisis-level. Score ~67 -- tight conditions reflecting both cumulative policy firming and banking stress, but not frozen markets.

6. **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All agreed on 25bp hike. Some would have done 50bp absent banking stress. Several considered holding steady. Forward: "some additional policy firming may be appropriate." Many lowered their rate path assessments due to banking. The action was hawkish (hiking into stress), but banking stress moderated the hawkishness significantly. Net rate path "about unchanged from December." This is moderate hawkish -- still hiking, still committed to fighting inflation, but clearly tempered. Score ~65.

7. **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: All agreed on 25bp. But there was genuine debate: several considered pausing, some would have preferred 50bp absent banking stress. So there's a range from pause to 50bp, but all converged on 25bp. No formal dissents. Moderate differences about the right course. Score ~35.

8. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Banking sector developments dominate this meeting's discussion. Nearly every section references banking stress, credit conditions, financial stability. Participants discussed lender-of-last-resort tools, microprudential/macroprudential tools. "Participants emphasized that the Federal Reserve should use its liquidity and lender-of-last-resort tools, as well as its microprudential and macroprudential regulatory and supervisory tools." This is a very prominent topic -- perhaps the dominant new information. Score ~72.

9. **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Some additional policy firming may be appropriate" -- notably softer than previous "ongoing increases." Many lowered rate path. Emphasis on data dependence, flexibility, optionality. "Several participants emphasized the need to retain flexibility and optionality." Forward signal is mildly hawkish -- still leaning toward more tightening, but much more conditional and uncertain. Score ~62.

10. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Very high uncertainty expressed. "Significant uncertainty," "highly uncertain economic outlook" (used multiple times), "too early to assess with confidence," "the extent of these effects was uncertain/highly uncertain." Banking developments "further increased the already-high level of uncertainty." Multiple calls for flexibility and monitoring. Score ~72.

**Polarity checks**:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 73: Higher = more pressure. "Unacceptably high" = yes, matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP 50: Near potential. Strong recent data but expected slowdown. Reasonable.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS 68: Higher = tighter. "Very tight," demand "substantially" exceeds supply. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS 67: Higher = tighter. Banking stress + cumulative firming = tight. Matches.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 65: Higher = more hawkish. Hiking 25bp, committed to inflation fight, but moderated. Matches.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 62: Higher = more hawkish forward. "Some additional firming may be appropriate" but conditional. Matches.

**Cross-dimension coherence**:
- OUTPUT_GAP (50) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (68) diverge somewhat. This is explained by the text: the labor market is a lagging indicator and remains very tight from prior strength, while the output gap reflects the expected near-term drag from banking stress pulling growth below trend. The text itself notes this tension -- strong recent data but expected weakening.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (65) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (62) are aligned -- both moderate hawkish, with forward tone slightly softer reflecting increased conditionality.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 73 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants agreed that inflation was unacceptably high."
- "Recent inflation data indicated slower-than-expected progress on disinflation."
- "Regarding prices for core services excluding housing, participants agreed that there was little evidence pointing to disinflation in this component."

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants see inflation as well above the 2% target with disappointing progress on disinflation, though past the peak levels of 2022.

**Minority views**: None significantly divergent on the inflation assessment; uncertainty band reflects the unknown impact of banking-related credit tightening on future inflation.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 50 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Recent indicators pointed to modest growth in spending and production."
- "Data on inflation, employment, and economic activity generally came in stronger than expected."
- "Participants generally expected real GDP to grow this year at a pace well below its long-run trend rate."

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is currently operating near potential -- recent data was strong, but banking developments are expected to pull growth below trend. The score reflects the current state straddling between recent strength and anticipated weakening.

**Minority views**: Wide band reflects tension between strong incoming data and expected credit-driven slowdown.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 68 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants agreed that the labor market remained very tight."
- "Labor demand continued to substantially exceed labor supply."
- "Wage growth was still well above the rates that would be consistent over the longer run with the 2 percent inflation objective."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is very tight with demand substantially exceeding supply, though some nascent signs of easing (declining quits, rising participation).

**Minority views**: Broad agreement on tightness; some noted early signs of rebalancing, keeping uncertainty modest.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 47 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Recent indicators pointed to modest growth in spending and production."
- "Incoming data on real consumer expenditures showed a pickup in spending in January and February."
- "Growth in business fixed investment was being restrained by tighter financial conditions."

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending showed recent strength, but business investment was restrained, and banking developments were expected to weigh further on both. Net assessment is near trend but slightly soft.

**Minority views**: A few noted nonbank contacts had not yet changed plans; overall, uncertainty about credit effects widens the band.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 67 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Recent developments in the banking sector were likely to result in tighter credit conditions for households and businesses."
- "Some banks were likely to tighten lending standards amid rising funding costs and increased concerns about liquidity."
- "Growth in business fixed investment was being restrained by tighter financial conditions that reflected cumulative policy firming to date."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are tight and tightening further, driven by both cumulative policy firming and the new banking stress channel. Not at crisis levels -- the system is "sound and resilient" -- but clearly restrictive.

**Minority views**: Uncertainty about the magnitude of credit tightening widens the band.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 65 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All participants agreed that it was appropriate to raise the target range for the federal funds rate 25 basis points."
- "Some participants noted that given persistently high inflation and the strength of the recent economic data, they would have considered a 50 basis point increase... in the absence of the recent developments in the banking sector."
- "Several participants noted that, in their policy deliberations, they considered whether it would be appropriate to hold the target range steady at this meeting."

**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate hawkish -- unanimous 25bp hike with strong commitment to fighting inflation, but banking stress clearly moderated the hawkish impulse. Range of views from pause to 50bp converged on 25bp.

**Minority views**: Several considered pausing (dovish tail); some would have preferred 50bp (hawkish tail). Wide band reflects this debate.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 35 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All participants agreed that it was appropriate to raise the target range for the federal funds rate 25 basis points."
- "Several participants noted that... they considered whether it would be appropriate to hold the target range steady."
- "Some participants noted that... they would have considered a 50 basis point increase."

**Center-of-gravity**: No formal dissent and all agreed on 25bp, but substantive debate existed about whether to pause or hike more aggressively. Moderate-level differences resolved through compromise.

**Minority views**: The range from pause to 50bp represents meaningful disagreement about appropriate action, elevated beyond routine.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 72 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Banking sector developments are discussed in nearly every paragraph of the participant discussion.
- "Participants emphasized that the Federal Reserve should use its liquidity and lender-of-last-resort tools, as well as its microprudential and macroprudential regulatory and supervisory tools, to address stress in the banking sector."
- "Several participants noted that regional and community banks... were important in small business and middle-market lending."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability is a prominent topic rivaling the inflation discussion in emphasis. The banking crisis is the defining feature of this meeting, shaping both the economic outlook and policy deliberations.

**Minority views**: Broad agreement that this is important; narrow band.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 62 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "They anticipated that some additional policy firming may be appropriate to attain a sufficiently restrictive policy stance."
- "Many participants noted that the likely effects of recent banking-sector developments on economic activity and inflation had led them to lower their assessments of the federal funds rate target range that would be sufficiently restrictive."
- "Several participants emphasized the need to retain flexibility and optionality given the highly uncertain economic outlook."

**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal leans toward further tightening but is heavily conditioned on data and banking developments. The shift from "ongoing increases" to "some additional firming may be appropriate" marks a meaningful softening.

**Minority views**: Tension between those focused on still-high inflation (more hawkish forward lean) and those focused on banking risks (more cautious forward lean).

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 72 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The extent of these effects was highly uncertain" (repeated multiple times in different forms).
- "Participants generally observed that the recent developments in the banking sector had further increased the already-high level of uncertainty associated with their outlooks."
- "In light of the highly uncertain economic outlook, participants underscored the importance of closely monitoring incoming information."

**Center-of-gravity**: Very elevated uncertainty driven by banking sector developments layered on top of already-elevated baseline uncertainty about the inflation/growth outlook.

**Minority views**: Broad consensus on high uncertainty; narrow band.
