# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2011-09-21

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Post-2009 segmented format with clear "Participants' Views" section separated from staff content.

**Era context**: September 2011 — two years into the recovery from the GFC. Growth slow, unemployment still elevated (~9%). European sovereign debt crisis intensifying. The Committee is debating Operation Twist (maturity extension program). QE2 had just ended in June 2011. Inflation had temporarily risen due to commodity prices earlier in the year but was expected to moderate.

**Quantifier distribution**: "most" (several uses — policy action, inflation moderation), "many" (weak demand as primary driver, downside risks to growth, balanced inflation risks), "several" (financial conditions less accommodative, downside risks, long-duration unemployment concerns), "some" (core inflation moved up, contacts reported contingency plans), "a few" (structural mismatches, higher core inflation implying less slack), "one/two" (specific data points).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as having moderated from earlier in the year but "not as much as expected." "Most" anticipated inflation settling "at or below" mandate-consistent levels given slack, stable expectations, slow wage growth. However, "some noted that core as well as headline inflation had moved up, on balance, since last fall." "Many" judged inflation risks roughly balanced; "some" saw downside risk from slack; "some others" saw upside from accommodative policy. Direction: modestly below target but not dramatically so. The "not as much as expected" moderation and the rise in core temper what would otherwise be a lower score. Score around 42-45. Higher = more pressure. Evidence says moderate and expected to decline but not yet at comfortable levels. Score: 43.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic growth remained slow" but "did not suggest a contraction." Unemployment rate "remained elevated." "Considerable uncertainty" and "significant downside risks." "Below potential" is clearly implied — "significant slack in labor and product markets," "low rates of resource utilization." This is firmly below 50. The economy is growing but slowly, with substantial slack. Anchor: 35-42 for 2010-14 recovery. Score: 34. The language of "slow growth," "vulnerability to shocks," and persistent slack suggests the lower end of the recovery range.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Overall labor market conditions had shown no improvement or had deteriorated in recent months." "Unemployment rate remained elevated." "Weak job gains." "Average workweek and aggregate hours worked declined." Contacts reported restraint on hiring. Long-duration unemployment concerns. Direction: clearly loose/slack labor market. Anchor: 35-40 for 2011-14. Given that conditions "deteriorated," score at the lower end. Score: 32.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Modest growth in consumer spending." "Consumer confidence had dropped to historically low levels." Several pointed to "depressed home prices and financial constraints." Business sentiment "worsened." Business investment in equipment/software "continued to expand" but concern about cuts if sales slowed. Housing "remained depressed." Exports were a "bright spot." Direction: below trend, sluggish. Anchor: 35-42 for 2011-13. Score: 35.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Financial conditions were strained at times." "Several participants argued that broader financial conditions had become less accommodative." "Risk spreads had widened appreciably." Bank funding markets "tightened, particularly for European banks." Mortgage spreads "unusually wide." "Still-tight credit standards." On the positive side, U.S. banks "remained willing to lend" and their capital/liquidity had "strengthened." Direction: somewhat tight to tight, with European spillover risk. Score: 63.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Most members agreed that the revisions to the economic outlook warranted some additional monetary policy accommodation." The Committee launched Operation Twist ($400B maturity extension). "Two members said that current conditions and the outlook could justify stronger policy action." "Three members concluded that additional accommodation was not appropriate at this time." This is clearly dovish — actively easing. Anchor: 15-25 for QE era 2010-12. Operation Twist is a form of easing, though more limited than outright QE. The majority favored it, two wanted more, three opposed. Score: 22.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Three members opposed additional accommodation. Two members wanted stronger action. This is significant disagreement — effectively three camps. Three dissents is high historically. Anchor: 70+ for 3+ dissents. However, this is minutes language about "members," and the three who opposed didn't necessarily all formally dissent (need to consider). The text says "Three members concluded that additional accommodation was not appropriate" — this strongly suggests 3 dissents. Also one member opposed reinvestment policy change. Score: 68.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: European sovereign debt and banking problems discussed at length. Financial market volatility, risk spreads, bank CDS spreads, bank stock prices, European spillover risks all discussed prominently. Mortgage market conditions discussed. This is a significant topic but not the dominant one — growth and employment still dominate. Anchor: 40-55 for 2006-07 pre-crisis; 60-75 for 2008 early crisis. This is moderate-to-prominent. Score: 55.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Committee reaffirmed "exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate at least through mid-2013." A couple of members wanted to change forward guidance to provide "greater clarity about economic conditions" (i.e., state-based rather than calendar-based — even more dovish). The forward signal is strongly accommodative. Anchor: 3-8 for Evans Rule/QE3. The mid-2013 date-based guidance is a strong easing commitment, though not as explicit as later Evans Rule. Score: 8.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants continued to see the outlook for growth and inflation as more uncertain than usual." "Considerable uncertainty surrounding the outlook." "The reasons for the weakness were unclear, contributing to greater uncertainty." "Heightened uncertainty about economic prospects." Many saw "significant downside risks." Direction: elevated to high. Score: 62.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (34) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (32): Both well below 50, consistent — slack economy with weak labor market. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (22) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (8): Both dovish. The forward guidance is more dovish than current action because the date-based commitment through mid-2013 is a very strong signal. The current action (Operation Twist) is moderate easing. The divergence is reasonable — forward guidance is the strongest dovish signal.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (43): Higher = more pressure. Evidence says moderate and expected to decline. 43 is slightly below midpoint. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP (34): Higher = more above potential. Evidence says well below potential. 34 is below midpoint. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (63): Higher = tighter. Evidence says conditions strained, spreads widened. 63 is above midpoint. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (22): Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says majority favors easing. 22 is well below midpoint. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 43 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Inflation appeared to have moderated since earlier in the year as prices of energy and some commodities declined from their peaks, but inflation had not yet come down as much as participants had expected earlier this year."
- "Most participants anticipated that, with stable inflation expectations, significant slack in labor and product markets, slow wage growth, and little evidence of pricing power among firms, inflation was likely to decline moderately over time."
- "Some noted that core as well as headline inflation had moved up, on balance, since last fall."

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation moderating but not as fast as expected; most see it settling at or below mandate-consistent levels, but the upward surprise in core tempers the assessment. Slightly below the neutral midpoint.

**Minority views**: "Some" saw upside inflation risks from accommodative policy. "A few" suggested less slack than thought, given juxtaposition of higher core inflation and lower unemployment. These widen the band modestly.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 34 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Economic growth remained slow but did not suggest a contraction in activity."
- "Significant slack in labor and product markets" and "low rates of resource utilization."
- "Many participants saw significant downside risks to economic growth."

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy well below potential with persistent slack, slow growth, and significant downside risks. Consistent with 2011 recovery-era slack.

**Minority views**: Limited disagreement on the direction — all see slack. Some variation in magnitude (reasons for weakness "unclear").

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 32 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Overall labor market conditions had shown no improvement or had deteriorated in recent months and the unemployment rate remained elevated."
- "The employment report for August showed weak job gains. Moreover, both the average workweek and aggregate hours worked declined."
- "Many participants judged that weak demand was of most importance" for explaining labor slack, while "a few argued that structural and geographic mismatches were key."

**Center-of-gravity**: Clearly slack labor market with elevated unemployment, weak hiring, and deteriorating indicators. Some debate about cyclical vs. structural causes but unanimous view that conditions are weak.

**Minority views**: "A few" emphasized structural mismatches (which could imply tighter effective labor market than headline unemployment suggests), but this was the minority.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 35 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants noted modest growth in consumer spending on average in recent months." "Consumer confidence had dropped to historically low levels."
- "Business sentiment had worsened, seemingly in response to weaker economic prospects." "Business investment in equipment and software had continued to expand in recent months, but some contacts expressed concern that firms would cut capital spending."
- "The housing sector remained depressed, with construction at very low levels."

**Center-of-gravity**: Below-trend spending. Consumer spending modest at best with terrible confidence. Business investment still expanding but threatened. Housing deeply depressed. Exports a bright spot but insufficient to offset weakness elsewhere.

**Minority views**: Minimal divergence — broad agreement on weakness.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 63 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Several participants argued that broader financial conditions had become less accommodative over the intermeeting period: Risk spreads had widened appreciably."
- "Conditions in bank funding markets had tightened, particularly for European banks." "Some large U.S. banks had seen further pressure on their stock prices and CDS spreads."
- "Still-tight credit standards meant that many households were unable to qualify for loans." "Spreads between mortgage rates and yields on Treasury securities were unusually wide."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions have tightened notably, driven by European crisis spillovers, wider risk spreads, and tight credit standards. U.S. banking system somewhat insulated by stronger capital positions, tempering the score.

**Minority views**: "Some participants noted that the reduction in leverage and increase in financial firms' liquidity cushions" had attenuated effects, and banks "remained willing to lend" — this moderates the tightness somewhat.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 22 ±10 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Most members agreed that the revisions to the economic outlook warranted some additional monetary policy accommodation to support a stronger recovery."
- "Those viewing greater policy accommodation as appropriate at this meeting generally supported a maturity extension program" — Operation Twist ($400B).
- "Two members said that current conditions and the outlook could justify stronger policy action." "Three members concluded that additional accommodation was not appropriate at this time."

**Center-of-gravity**: Majority favors easing via Operation Twist, which is an active accommodation measure during the QE era. Firmly dovish, though not as aggressive as outright QE expansion.

**Minority views**: Three members opposed any additional accommodation (hawkish minority); two wanted even more action (dovish minority). This wide spread justifies ±10.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 68 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Three members concluded that additional accommodation was not appropriate at this time." (3 dissents)
- "Two members said that current conditions and the outlook could justify stronger policy action." (dovish wing)
- "One member who opposed the maturity extension program also opposed the change in reinvestment policy."

**Center-of-gravity**: Three dissents against accommodation plus two members wanting stronger action creates a three-camp dynamic. This is well above typical disagreement. Three formal dissents is historically very unusual.

**Minority views**: N/A — the dissent IS the story here.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 55 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Extensive discussion of European sovereign debt/banking problems, potential "serious spillovers to the U.S. economy."
- Multiple paragraphs on financial market volatility, risk spreads, bank funding markets, CDS spreads, bank stock prices.
- Mortgage market dysfunction discussed (wide spreads, tight credit).

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability is a prominent topic with several paragraphs devoted to it, but the meeting is primarily about growth/employment concerns and the policy response. Financial conditions are discussed as a channel of risk rather than the sole focus.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 8 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The Committee also decided to keep the target range for the federal funds rate at 0 to 1/4 percent and to reaffirm its anticipation that economic conditions...are likely to warrant exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate at least through mid-2013."
- "A couple of members noted that they would prefer to change the Committee's forward guidance to provide greater clarity about the economic conditions that would be likely to warrant maintaining exceptionally low levels" — suggesting even more explicit dovish guidance.
- Operation Twist itself is a forward commitment to purchases through June 2012.

**Center-of-gravity**: Date-based forward guidance committing to near-zero rates through mid-2013 is among the strongest dovish forward signals, just short of explicit threshold-based guidance. Additional accommodation program reinforces the easing signal.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 62 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants continued to see the outlook for growth and inflation as more uncertain than usual."
- "Considerable uncertainty surrounding the outlook for a gradual pickup in economic growth."
- "The reasons for the weakness were unclear, contributing to greater uncertainty about the economic outlook."
- "Many participants saw significant downside risks to economic growth."

**Center-of-gravity**: Elevated uncertainty with specific emphasis on downside risks, unclear reasons for recovery weakness, and European spillover potential. Multiple participants address uncertainty as a theme, not just as a hedge.
