# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2021-12-15

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented format — participant discussion is clearly separated from staff review sections.

**Era context**: December 2021. Inflation had surged well above target (PCE at 5.0%, CPI at 6.8%). The economy was recovering strongly from COVID but supply chain bottlenecks persisted. The labor market was tightening rapidly. The Fed was accelerating its taper and pivoting toward rate hikes. Omicron variant had just emerged.

**Quantifier distribution**: Frequent use of "participants" (near-universal), "many," "several," "some," "a few," and "a couple." "All participants" used for inflation exceeding 2%. "Almost all" used for revised-up inflation forecasts. "Most" agreed upside inflation risks. Strong consensus language on policy actions.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as clearly above target. "Inflation readings had been higher and were more persistent and widespread than previously anticipated." "Almost all stated that they had revised up their forecasts of inflation for 2022 notably." "All participants remarked that inflation had continued to run notably above 2 percent." Trimmed mean measures at decade highs. Businesses passing on costs. This is strong, persistent, broadening inflation — but participants still generally expected decline in 2022. The current perception is firmly elevated. Per the rubric's gotcha, score the perceived level now, not the forecast. Anchor: 65-72 for 2021 Q3-Q4. This meeting has stronger language than earlier Q4 meetings — "more persistent and widespread than previously anticipated," revised up forecasts, removal of "transitory." Score: 72. Polarity check: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says more pressure. Matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Growth of economic activity appeared to have been strong in the fourth quarter." "Generally expected robust growth to continue in 2022." Supply and demand imbalances persisting. Strong consumer demand. The economy is clearly running above trend given supply constraints and demand pressures. Labor market "very tight" and "making rapid progress toward maximum employment." Several viewed conditions as "already largely consistent with maximum employment." This implies above potential. Bright-line rule: above potential language -> must be > 50. Score: 58. Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says above potential. Matches.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Participants pointed to a number of signs that the U.S. labor market was very tight, including near-record rates of quits and job vacancies, as well as a notable pickup in wage growth." "Many participants reported District business contacts either planning or having implemented larger wage increases." "Several participants viewed labor market conditions as already largely consistent with maximum employment." Businesses experiencing "difficulty hiring workers across all skill levels." However, labor force participation still depressed, and unemployment rate at 4.2% — tight but not at the extreme levels of 2022. Score: 63. Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says tight. Matches.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Demand for consumer goods had remained strong" supported by accommodative policies, increased household income, net worth, and savings. "Growth of economic activity appeared to have been strong in the fourth quarter." Business fixed investment was rising at a slow pace due to supply bottlenecks. So consumer spending is strong/above trend, but investment is constrained. Net position: above trend but not booming uniformly. Anchor: 62-68 for 2021 Q3-2022 Q1 surge. Investment dragging slightly. Score: 60. Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Evidence says strong consumer, constrained investment. Matches.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Overall financial conditions remained accommodative, in part reflecting policy measures to support the economy and the flow of credit." This is stated multiple times. Corporate bond spreads widened "moderately" but financing conditions for most borrowers remained accommodative. Credit freely available for most. Score: 25. Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says accommodative (loose). Score below 50. Matches.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: This is a pivotal meeting. Participants doubled the taper pace (ending purchases by mid-March instead of June). "Participants generally noted that... it may become warranted to increase the federal funds rate sooner or at a faster pace than participants had earlier anticipated." "Some participants judged that a less accommodative future stance of policy would likely be warranted and that the Committee should convey a strong commitment to address elevated inflation pressures." Members agreed to remove "transitory." Rate at zero still, but clear pivot toward tightening. They are at the ELB but signaling imminent liftoff and faster normalization. This is lean hawkish transitioning to moderate hawkish. Anchor: 40-50 for pre-liftoff 2014-15, 60-65 for Dec 2015 liftoff. This meeting is more hawkish than pre-liftoff patience — they're actively accelerating taper and signaling imminent hikes. Score: 62. Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says hawkish pivot. Matches.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: The decision to double taper pace was unanimous. Agreement on removing "transitory." "All members reaffirmed their commitment." Broad agreement on direction. Some variation on timing of balance sheet runoff and rate hikes, but no formal dissents. Differences are about pace/sequencing, not direction. Score: 22. Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Evidence shows broad consensus with minor differences. Matches.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: "Several participants raised concerns about vulnerabilities in the Treasury market." "A few of these participants raised concerns that a relatively flat yield curve could adversely affect interest margins for some financial intermediaries, which may raise financial stability risks." Financial conditions discussed as accommodative. Some discussion of balance sheet normalization impacts. But financial stability is not a major focus — it is secondary to inflation and labor. Score: 30. Polarity check: Higher = more attention. Evidence shows limited/secondary discussion. Matches.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Very hawkish forward signal. "It may become warranted to increase the federal funds rate sooner or at a faster pace than participants had earlier anticipated." "Almost all participants agreed that it would likely be appropriate to initiate balance sheet runoff at some point after the first increase." Balance sheet runoff discussed as being closer to liftoff than previously. Taper accelerated. "Many participants judged that... labor markets would fast approach maximum employment" — removing the last barrier to rate hikes. Forward signal clearly points toward tightening. Score: 70. Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence says tightening ahead. Matches.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants observed that uncertainty about the economic outlook remained high." "Many participants noted that the emergence of the Omicron variant made the economic outlook more uncertain." "Participants generally continued to stress uncertainties associated with the labor market—in particular, the evolution of labor force participation—and with the length of time required to resolve the supply chain situation." "Maintaining flexibility to implement appropriate policy adjustments... in the current highly uncertain environment." Multiple sources of genuine uncertainty: Omicron, supply chains, labor participation, inflation persistence. Score: 58. Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. Evidence says elevated uncertainty. Matches.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (58) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (63): Both above 50, coherent. Labor slightly higher because explicit "very tight" language while output gap language is more about strong growth than overheating.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (62) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (70): Forward guidance more hawkish than current action, which makes sense — they're still at zero rates with QE winding down, but the forward signal is clearly pointing to imminent tightening. Coherent divergence.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 72 ±7 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Inflation readings had been higher and were more persistent and widespread than previously anticipated." "Almost all stated that they had revised up their forecasts of inflation for 2022 notably, and many did so for 2023 as well." "All participants remarked that inflation had continued to run notably above 2 percent." "Trimmed mean measures of inflation had reached decade-high levels."
- **Center of gravity**: Near-universal agreement that inflation is elevated, persistent, broadening, and worse than expected. Removal of "transitory" language signals a recognition that this is not fleeting.
- **Minority views**: "Several participants pointed to the possibility that structural factors that kept inflation low in the previous decade... may reemerge when the effects of the pandemic abate" — this is a modest downside voice but clearly a minority.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 58 ±6 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Growth of economic activity appeared to have been strong in the fourth quarter." "Generally expected robust growth to continue in 2022." Supply-demand imbalances persisting with strong demand outrunning constrained supply.
- **Center of gravity**: Economy running above potential — strong demand, tight labor markets, supply constraints binding. Not extreme overheating but clearly above trend.
- **Minority views**: Omicron variant introduced downside uncertainty, with "many participants" noting it made the outlook more uncertain, but "several remarked that they did not yet see the new variant as fundamentally altering the path of economic recovery."

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 63 ±6 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Participants pointed to a number of signs that the U.S. labor market was very tight, including near-record rates of quits and job vacancies, as well as a notable pickup in wage growth." "Several participants viewed labor market conditions as already largely consistent with maximum employment." "Many business contacts continued to experience difficulty hiring workers across all skill levels."
- **Center of gravity**: Labor market is very tight by multiple measures — vacancies, quits, wages, hiring difficulty. Near or at maximum employment.
- **Minority views**: Labor force participation still depressed; "a number of participants judged that a substantial improvement in labor force participation would take longer than previously expected," suggesting some remaining slack in participation.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 60 ±6 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Demand for consumer goods had remained strong." "Growth of economic activity appeared to have been strong in the fourth quarter." However, "growth in business fixed investment appeared to be rising at a slow pace again in the fourth quarter, as supply bottlenecks continued to weigh."
- **Center of gravity**: Consumer spending above trend, supported by strong balance sheets and policy. Business investment constrained by supply issues. Net: above trend but not uniformly booming.
- **Minority views**: Limited disagreement on the demand picture; differences mainly about how persistent supply constraints would be.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 25 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Overall financial conditions remained accommodative, in part reflecting policy measures to support the economy and the flow of credit to U.S. households and businesses." Repeated in both participant views and committee action sections. Corporate bond issuance "solid," leveraged loan issuance "robust."
- **Center of gravity**: Financial conditions clearly accommodative/loose. Multiple references confirm this assessment.
- **Minority views**: "Spreads on corporate bonds widened moderately" and small business conditions "remained stable but tighter than before the pandemic" — minor tightening at the margins but overall accommodative.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 62 ±7 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Participants generally noted that... it may become warranted to increase the federal funds rate sooner or at a faster pace than participants had earlier anticipated." Doubling of taper pace agreed unanimously. "Some participants judged that a less accommodative future stance of policy would likely be warranted and that the Committee should convey a strong commitment to address elevated inflation pressures."
- **Center of gravity**: Clear hawkish pivot. Accelerated taper, imminent rate liftoff signaled, balance sheet normalization discussed. But still at zero rates — action is forward-looking rather than currently tight.
- **Minority views**: "A measured approach to tightening policy" emphasized by some — hawkish but wanting gradualism. "A few participants" still emphasizing maximum employment conditions not fully met.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 22 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: Unanimous agreement on accelerated taper. "All members reaffirmed their commitment." "Members agreed" used repeatedly for all key decisions. No formal dissents noted.
- **Center of gravity**: Broad consensus on direction and pace of policy adjustment. Differences limited to pace of future normalization and sequencing of balance sheet vs rate actions.
- **Minority views**: Some differences on timing of balance sheet runoff relative to rate liftoff, and on whether to raise rates before maximum employment fully achieved, but these are tactical not directional.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 30 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Several participants raised concerns about vulnerabilities in the Treasury market and how those vulnerabilities could affect the appropriate pace of balance sheet normalization." "A few of these participants raised concerns that a relatively flat yield curve could adversely affect interest margins for some financial intermediaries, which may raise financial stability risks."
- **Center of gravity**: Financial stability mentioned in the context of balance sheet normalization and yield curve effects, but not a primary focus of the meeting. Secondary consideration.
- **Minority views**: None significant — this was a contextual concern, not a debated topic.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 70 ±7 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "It may become warranted to increase the federal funds rate sooner or at a faster pace than participants had earlier anticipated." "Almost all participants agreed that it would likely be appropriate to initiate balance sheet runoff at some point after the first increase in the target range." "The appropriate timing of balance sheet runoff would likely be closer to that of policy rate liftoff than in the Committee's previous experience."
- **Center of gravity**: Strong tightening signal ahead — faster taper completion, imminent rate liftoff, earlier balance sheet runoff. Forward lean is clearly hawkish.
- **Minority views**: Emphasis on data dependence and flexibility from multiple participants tempers the forward signal somewhat.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 58 ±7 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Participants observed that uncertainty about the economic outlook remained high." "Many participants noted that the emergence of the Omicron variant made the economic outlook more uncertain." "Participants generally continued to stress uncertainties associated with the labor market—in particular, the evolution of labor force participation—and with the length of time required to resolve the supply chain situation."
- **Center of gravity**: Elevated uncertainty from multiple sources (Omicron, supply chains, labor force participation, inflation persistence). Not extreme/Knightian but genuinely elevated.
- **Minority views**: "Several remarked that they did not yet see the new variant as fundamentally altering the path of economic recovery" — some less concerned about Omicron specifically.
