# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2021-11-03 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is **minutes** (synthesized summary of participant views). It is segmented, with a clear "Participants' Views on Current Economic Conditions and the Economic Outlook" section beginning on page 7. Staff content appears earlier and is excluded from scoring.

### Era Context
November 2021: The U.S. economy is recovering from the COVID-19 pandemic. Delta variant has slowed recovery. Inflation is elevated — running well above 2% — with supply chain disruptions and strong demand. The labor market has improved substantially but labor supply remains constrained. The Fed is beginning to taper asset purchases at this meeting. Policy rates remain at the zero lower bound.

### Quantifier Distribution
- "all participants" — judged it appropriate to announce tapering
- "participants generally" — saw inflation as transitory but lasting longer; supported taper plan; judged substantial further progress met on inflation
- "many" — pointed to considerations suggesting elevated inflation could prove more persistent
- "some" — preferred faster pace of reductions; highlighted price increases becoming more widespread; suggested faster taper to be in better position to raise rates
- "a number of" — commented on financial stability; observed LFPR remained below pre-pandemic; noted fiscal drag
- "several" — judged LFPR structurally lower; commented on stablecoin risks
- "a few" — cited retirements; mentioned COVID winter upsurge risks; cited financial vulnerabilities; stressed cybersecurity
- "a couple of" — pointed to inflation expectations possibly becoming less anchored

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as clearly above the midpoint. "Inflation was elevated" is the opening framing. "Generally saw the current elevated level of inflation as largely reflecting factors that were likely to be transitory but judged that inflation pressures could take longer to subside than they had previously assessed." Many pointed to factors suggesting persistence. Some highlighted broadening. Per the gotcha rule, "transitory" does not reduce the score — score the elevated part. The anchor for 2021 Q3-Q4 is 65-72. This meeting shows elevated inflation with growing concern about persistence but still a general belief in eventual transitoriness. Score: 68.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Growth slowed in Q3 "significantly below the robust pace seen in the first half." But the staff projection notes "the level of real GDP was expected to remain well above potential throughout the projection period." Participants noted strong underlying demand conditions, robust sales, and expected a Q4 pickup. The economy is above potential with supply constraints binding. The bright-line rule: language points to above potential. Score: 56.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants noted continued labor market improvement. Unemployment declined to 4.8%. "Some participants cited a number of signs that the U.S. labor market was very tight" including quits, job availability, wage growth. Contacts "continued to report difficulties in finding and retaining workers." But LFPR remained "well below" pre-pandemic levels. The center of gravity is a tight labor market with remaining structural issues. Score: 60.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Demand for most consumer goods had remained strong." "Businesses had generally recorded robust sales." Households had "strong balance sheets." But supply constraints were limiting activity, and Delta had slowed the shift to services. Manufacturing constrained by supply chain challenges. Demand strong but supply-constrained. Net position is above trend but not booming. Score: 60.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Overall financial conditions remained accommodative, in part reflecting policy measures." Participants noted accommodative financial conditions as supporting consumer spending and potentially contributing to persistent inflation. This is clearly loose/accommodative. Score: 22.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All participants agreed to begin tapering ($15B/month reduction). "Some participants preferred a somewhat faster pace of reductions." "Some participants suggested that reducing the pace of net asset purchases by more than $15 billion each month could be warranted so that the Committee would be in a better position to make adjustments to the target range for the federal funds rate." But "a number of participants stressed that a patient attitude toward incoming data remained appropriate." The center of gravity is: begin tapering, maintain flexibility, be prepared to adjust faster if inflation persists. This is a lean-hawkish posture — moving away from max accommodation but still at the ZLB. The anchor for 2014-15 pre-liftoff is 40-50; this meeting is beginning taper with some wanting faster, plus rate hike conditionality. Score: 53.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: All agreed on the taper decision. But there's notable spread: some wanted faster taper; a number wanted patience. Views on inflation persistence were split between "many" seeing persistence risks and "some other participants" seeing transitory. No formal dissents. This is broad agreement on action with meaningful differences on pace and outlook. Score: 32.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: "A number of participants commented on issues related to financial stability." Discussion covered bank capital, asset valuations, nonbank exposures, stablecoins, cybersecurity, and climate risks. This is a moderate-length paragraph with several sub-topics but it's not a dominant theme of the meeting. Score: 35.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Participants noted taper "was not intended to convey any direct signal regarding adjustments to the target range." They "highlighted the more stringent criteria for raising the target range." But "various participants noted that the Committee should be prepared to adjust the pace of asset purchases and raise the target range for the federal funds rate sooner than participants currently anticipated if inflation continued to run higher." Some wanted faster taper to be positioned for rate hikes. This is a mild tightening lean in forward guidance — taper underway, rate hikes conditional on data. Score: 55.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants observed that uncertainty about the economic outlook remained high." "They indicated that their uncertainty regarding this assessment had increased." Specific uncertainties: labor force participation, supply chain duration, virus course. "Continuing considerable uncertainty about developments in supply chains, production logistics, and the course of the virus." This is elevated uncertainty. Score: 60.

### Polarity Checks
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says elevated and potentially persistent. 68 = correct direction.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Economy above potential but growth temporarily slowed. 56 = correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Very tight with worker shortages but LFPR depressed. 60 = correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Conditions described as accommodative. 22 = correct (loose).
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Beginning taper, some want faster. 53 = correct.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Taper beginning, conditional rate hike readiness. 55 = correct.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (56) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (60): Both above 50, consistent. Labor slightly higher because of specific "very tight" language and worker shortage reports.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (53) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (55): Closely aligned. Both lean hawkish but modestly — the Committee is beginning normalization while maintaining optionality.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 68 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants generally saw the current elevated level of inflation as largely reflecting factors that were likely to be transitory but judged that inflation pressures could take longer to subside than they had previously assessed."
- "Many participants pointed to considerations that might suggest that elevated inflation could prove more persistent."
- "Some participants highlighted the fact that price increases had become more widespread."

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants see inflation as clearly elevated and broadening, with growing concerns about persistence, though the general view still expects eventual decline. This places the score solidly in the "clearly above target" range.

**Minority views**: "Some other participants" argued pressures continued to reflect pandemic imbalances and would abate. This informs the downward portion of the uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 56 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Growth in economic activity had slowed in the third quarter to a rate significantly below the robust pace seen in the first half of the year."
- "The underlying conditions supporting growth in demand remained strong and that... growth in economic activity would likely show a pickup in the fourth quarter."
- Staff projection (context): "the level of real GDP was expected to remain well above potential throughout the projection period."

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is operating above potential despite a temporary Q3 growth slowdown from Delta. The level remains above potential with strong demand fundamentals.

**Minority views**: The Q3 slowdown and supply constraints create some downward pull, reflected in the uncertainty band.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 60 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Some participants cited a number of signs that the U.S. labor market was very tight: These included data on quits, job availability, and stronger rates of nominal wage growth."
- "District contacts continued to report difficulties in finding and retaining workers."
- "A number of participants observed that the labor force participation rate remained well below the level reached before the pandemic."

**Center-of-gravity**: The labor market is tight with significant worker shortages and rising wages, but depressed LFPR and a 4.8% unemployment rate (still above pre-pandemic) moderate the degree of tightness.

**Minority views**: Several participants judged LFPR would be structurally lower (implying the market is tighter than it appears); several others saw pandemic factors as temporary (implying slack would return).

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 60 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Demand for most consumer goods had remained strong" and "businesses had generally recorded robust sales."
- "Households had strong balance sheets and that consumer spending would also be supported by accommodative financial conditions."
- "Supply chain challenges and limited labor availability continued to be major constraints on manufacturing activity."

**Center-of-gravity**: Demand is strong and above trend, supported by household balance sheets and accommodative conditions. Supply constraints are limiting the translation of demand into output. Net position is above trend.

**Minority views**: "A number of participants noted that there was likely to be a drag on household spending as previous fiscal support faded."

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 22 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Overall financial conditions remained accommodative, in part reflecting policy measures to support the economy and the flow of credit to U.S. households and businesses."
- Participants noted "accommodative financial conditions" as a factor potentially sustaining elevated inflation.

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly loose/accommodative. No mention of tightening conditions, stress, or difficulty accessing credit.

**Minority views**: None noted.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 53 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All participants judged that... it would be appropriate to announce at this meeting a reduction in the pace of net asset purchases."
- "Some participants preferred a somewhat faster pace of reductions" and "some participants suggested that reducing the pace of net asset purchases by more than $15 billion each month could be warranted."
- "A number of participants stressed that a patient attitude toward incoming data remained appropriate."

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous agreement to begin tapering signals a shift away from maximum accommodation. Some want faster normalization, while others counsel patience. The center is a lean-hawkish posture — actively withdrawing accommodation but gradually.

**Minority views**: The "some" wanting faster taper and the "number" wanting patience define the spread.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 32 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- All agreed on taper decision — no formal dissents.
- But "some participants preferred a somewhat faster pace of reductions" vs. "a number of participants stressed that a patient attitude toward incoming data remained appropriate."
- On inflation: "many" saw persistence risks vs. "some other participants" who maintained transitory view.

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on the taper action with notable differences on pace and on the inflation outlook. No dissents but meaningful dispersion in views on both the economic assessment and appropriate forward posture.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 35 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "A number of participants commented on issues related to financial stability."
- Topics covered: bank capital/liquidity, elevated asset valuations, nonbank exposure, stablecoins, cybersecurity, climate risks.
- "A few participants also cited a number of factors representing potential vulnerabilities to the financial system: These included elevated asset valuations prevailing widely across asset classes."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability receives a dedicated paragraph with several sub-topics, but it is not a dominant theme of the meeting. The discussion is limited to "a number of" / "a few" / "a couple of" participants — not "most" or "many."

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 55 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Beginning to scale back the pace of net asset purchases was not intended to convey any direct signal regarding adjustments to the target range for the federal funds rate."
- "Various participants noted that the Committee should be prepared to adjust the pace of asset purchases and raise the target range for the federal funds rate sooner than participants currently anticipated if inflation continued to run higher."
- "The Committee would not hesitate to take appropriate actions to address inflation pressures."

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is mildly hawkish — taper has begun, and participants are conditioning future rate hikes on inflation data. But no explicit tightening commitment is made; the forward signal is conditional and data-dependent.

**Minority views**: Some wanted to be positioned for earlier rate hikes; others stressed patience.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 60 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants observed that uncertainty about the economic outlook remained high."
- "They indicated that their uncertainty regarding this assessment had increased."
- "Continuing considerable uncertainty about developments in supply chains, production logistics, and the course of the virus."

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants explicitly state uncertainty is high and increasing. Multiple sources of uncertainty are identified (supply chains, labor force participation, virus). This is elevated but not "uncharted territory."
