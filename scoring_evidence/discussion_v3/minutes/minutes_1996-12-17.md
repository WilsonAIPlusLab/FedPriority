# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1996-12-17

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format, but participant language is reasonably distinct ("members commented," "members observed," "members noted," "all the members supported"). Some staff-attributable content appears in the directive section (lines 191-261), which I will ignore for scoring.

**Era context**: December 1996 -- mid-expansion, economy near potential, unemployment around 5.4%, inflation subdued but with upside risks from tight labor markets. Greenspan's "irrational exuberance" speech was just days before (December 5, 1996). The economy was in a sustained moderate growth phase.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all the members" (supported unchanged policy); "members" (broadly, meaning most/all); "some members" (fiscal optimism, emphasized prompt action on inflation, housing stabilization view). No "a few" or "one or two" language suggesting minority camps on policy.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as currently subdued but with upside risks. "Inflation had remained subdued, but the members continued to view the risks as tilted toward increases in the future." "Labor compensation costs clearly were rising at a faster pace in the context of persistently tight labor markets." Core inflation showed "little trend and even some decline over the past year." The current level is below concerning, but the risk tilt is upward. This is below-target-currently with acknowledged upside risk -- around 44-48. The tension is between benign current readings and rising wage costs that "at some point" could spill over. Score: 46.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "the economy was performing quite favorably" and "the economy operating in the neighborhood of its sustainable potential." Growth expected "near the economy's potential." This is textbook "at potential" language. Bright-line rule: "at potential" = 48-52. Score: 51. Slightly above because "fairly solid underpinnings" and "performing quite favorably" suggest operating just at or fractionally above.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "persistently tight labor markets," "labor markets might be tighter than could be sustained over the long term," "wage increases had moved higher." Clear tight labor market language. Anchors: 50 for 2016-17, 65-75 for 1999-2000. Late 1996 is the early phase of late-90s tightness. Score: 60. Tight but not yet at peak late-90s levels.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "picked up as expected" with "robust retail sales" during holiday season, but offset by factors inhibiting growth (debt burdens, tightening credit standards, job security concerns). Business investment expected "smaller though still sizable gains." Housing mixed. Net: moderate, slightly above trend. "appreciable increases in consumer spending and business investment" expected. Score: 55.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "generally supportive financial conditions," "readily available financing" for business equipment, "tightening consumer credit standards" mentioned as one factor but overall conditions described as supportive/accommodative. Score: 35 (somewhat loose to neutral, leaning loose given "generally supportive" and "readily available financing").

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "all the members supported a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance while also retaining a bias toward restraint." "the next policy move as more likely to be in the direction of some firming than toward easing." But "anticipatory tightening was not yet called for" and "watchful waiting remained the prudent course." This is a hold with tightening bias -- lean hawkish. Score: 55. They are holding but leaning toward the next move being a hike.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Unanimous vote (0 dissents). "all the members" supported both the policy and the asymmetric directive. Very little evidence of disagreement. "some members emphasized" prompt action on inflation, but this is emphasis variation, not dissent. Score: 10.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Stock market valuations discussed: "The rise over recent years had been extraordinary and had brought market valuations to fairly high levels relative to earnings and dividends." Members "recognized the need to monitor with special care price movements in the stock market and asset markets more generally." This is notable but not dominant. Score: 25. A meaningful paragraph devoted to stock market concerns, but it's context rather than driving the meeting.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Asymmetric directive toward tightening retained. "the next policy move as more likely to be in the direction of some firming than toward easing." "some members emphasized that it would be especially important for the Committee to act promptly to counter any tendency for price inflation to rise." But "anticipatory tightening was not yet called for" and "risks of waiting did not appear to be substantial." Mild tightening bias with patience. Score: 58.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "a great deal of uncertainty" about inflation outlook, "good deal of uncertainty regarding the outlook for inflation," stock market "injected an additional note of uncertainty." Multiple references to uncertainty. Score: 45. Somewhat elevated -- genuine uncertainty about wage-price dynamics and inflation transmission.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (51) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (60): Labor is tighter than output gap suggests, which is consistent with the text -- economy at potential but labor markets specifically described as "persistently tight." The divergence is explained by the document itself.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (55) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (58): Aligned -- both lean mildly hawkish. Current action is hold, forward signal is tightening bias.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Current inflation subdued but risks tilted up. 46 = slightly below midpoint. Matches: inflation not yet a problem but risk acknowledged.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Conditions described as "generally supportive" = loose. 35 = somewhat loose. Correct.
