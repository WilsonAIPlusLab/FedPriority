# FOMC Scoring: 2002-11-06 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format — staff and participant content mixed. I will score only participant-attributed language ("members commented," "members noted," "members anticipated," etc.).

**Era context**: November 2002. The economy is in the aftermath of the 2001 recession and the post-9/11 shocks. The fed funds rate had been cut aggressively through 2001 and held at 1.75% through 2002. This meeting features a 50bp cut to 1.25%. Growth has disappointed, geopolitical uncertainty (Iraq) looms, business investment remains weak, and deflation risk is beginning to enter the conversation.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all the members" (favored 50bp cut, supported balanced risk shift), "many members" (risk of market overreaction), "some members" (various views on recovery, deflation risk, reservations about balanced tilt), "most regional reports" (housing strength).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target and subdued. Evidence: "inflation was likely to remain subdued and perhaps even to edge a little lower," "further disinflation," "some members referred to the possibility...of a period of deflation." The center of gravity is low inflation with disinflation risk. The deflation mention is by "some" and described as "remote," so this is not a deflation alarm — more a subdued/below-target reading. Direction: well below midpoint. Score ~22. Higher = more pressure; evidence says very little pressure. Matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as clearly below potential. Evidence: "growth had slowed to a pace well below that experienced earlier in the year," "slack in labor and product markets at elevated levels," "longer-lasting spell of subpar economic performance." Bright-line: "slack" language = must be < 50. The slack is "elevated" and the growth path is "lower-than-anticipated." This is a clear negative output gap. Score ~30. Not catastrophic (not "worst since Depression") but substantial slack persists.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Evidence: "slack in labor...markets at elevated levels," "pressures on labor and other resources expected to be limited over coming quarters." This indicates significant labor market slack. Score ~28. Coherent with OUTPUT_GAP being ~30.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "decelerated since midsummer," business investment "not yet materialized" in recovery, "high and persisting uncertainty" holding down business investment. Some positive factors mentioned for longer term, but near-term picture is weak. Housing is "elevated" providing support. Net: below trend but not collapsing. Score ~33. "Sluggish" consumer spending, investment constrained, but housing holding up.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Members noted "overall financial conditions, including bank lending terms, had become more restrictive this year," "substantial decreases in equity wealth," and "diminishing stimulus from earlier policy easing actions." Financial conditions are tightening despite low rates. Score ~60. Somewhat tight — conditions have tightened, credit terms more restrictive.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All members favored a 50bp cut — a "relatively aggressive easing action." Members emphasized asymmetric risk: costs of over-easing "quite limited" vs. costs of under-easing could lead to "cumulatively weakening economy and possibly even attendant deflation." This is clearly dovish. All supported shifting risk assessment to balanced AFTER the cut, suggesting they see the 50bp as sufficient for now. Score ~22. Strong dovish: unanimous support for a sizable cut, risk management framework heavily weighted toward easing.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All the members favored" the 50bp cut. "All the members indicated" they could support the balanced risk shift, "although some voiced reservations." Zero dissents, minor reservations about the risk assessment language. Score ~18. Broad agreement with minor nuance differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial conditions are mentioned in context of equity wealth effects, bank lending terms, mortgage rates, and market reaction concerns. Discussion is moderate but not a dominant topic — it serves as supporting evidence for the policy decision rather than a standalone concern. Score ~30. Limited discussion; mentioned as context.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The Committee shifted from "tilted toward weakness" to "balanced" risk assessment. This signals they see the 50bp cut as sufficient and do NOT currently see a need for further easing. "Many members" worried that retaining the weakness tilt would cause markets to expect more easing. The forward signal is: we've done a big move, now we're balanced/waiting. This is a mild easing lean with data dependence — they're not signaling more cuts but the bias is still toward accommodation. Score ~32.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "High and persisting uncertainty" is a recurring theme. "Business investment expenditures continued to be constrained by a high degree of uncertainty," geopolitical risks mentioned repeatedly, "uncertainties that surrounded prospective market reactions." Members see uncertainty as elevated and it is a key factor in their assessment. Score ~58. Elevated uncertainty — multiple sources discussed meaningfully.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (30) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (28): Both below 50, coherent — economy well below potential with labor slack.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (22) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (32): Both dovish. The lean is more dovish than the forward signal because the Committee just acted aggressively but signaled "balanced" going forward. This divergence is explained by the large current action combined with a deliberate pause signal.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 22 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "inflation was likely to remain subdued and perhaps even to edge a little lower"; "the prospect of some persisting slack in resource use over coming quarters pointed to further disinflation"; "some members referred to the possibility, which they viewed as remote, of a period of deflation"
- **Center of gravity**: Participants see inflation as subdued and declining, with disinflation expected and deflation acknowledged as a remote tail risk. Well below target.
- **Minority views**: "Some" raised deflation possibility but characterized it as "remote" — not a central concern.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 30 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "economic growth had slowed to a pace well below that experienced earlier in the year"; "slack in labor and product markets at elevated levels"; "a longer-lasting spell of subpar economic performance than they had anticipated earlier"
- **Center of gravity**: Economy clearly below potential with elevated slack. Recovery anticipated but delayed.
- **Minority views**: "Some members commented that an even more robust recovery could not be ruled out" — upside scenario acknowledged but not central.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 28 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "slack in labor...markets at elevated levels"; "pressures on labor and other resources expected to be limited over coming quarters"
- **Center of gravity**: Labor market has significant slack. Not the primary focus of discussion but consistently referenced.
- **Minority views**: None noted.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 33 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "consumer spending appeared to have decelerated since midsummer"; "an anticipated and hopefully compensating strengthening in business investment had not yet materialized"; "High and persisting uncertainty...continued to hold down business investment spending"
- **Center of gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment are below trend. Housing provides some offset. Overall picture is sluggish.
- **Minority views**: Longer-term optimism about productivity-driven recovery, but near-term weakness dominates.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 60 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "overall financial conditions, including bank lending terms, had become more restrictive this year even though the nominal federal funds rate target had not been changed since late 2001"; "substantial decreases in equity wealth"; "diminishing stimulus from earlier policy easing actions"
- **Center of gravity**: Financial conditions have tightened despite accommodative policy rates. Bank lending terms more restrictive, equity wealth diminished.
- **Minority views**: Low mortgage rates and housing support provide some offset.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 22 ±4 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "all the members favored a proposal to reduce the target...by 50 basis points"; "a relatively aggressive easing action could help to ensure that the current soft spot...would prove to be temporary"; "the potential costs of a policy easing action that later proved not to have been needed were quite limited"
- **Center of gravity**: Strong dovish consensus for aggressive easing. Risk management framework explicitly weighted toward action over inaction.
- **Minority views**: None on the direction; "some voiced reservations" only about the balanced risk language, not the cut itself.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 18 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "all the members favored" the 50bp cut; "All the members indicated that...they could support a shift...to balanced...although some voiced reservations about the need for such a shift"
- **Center of gravity**: Near-unanimous on policy action; minor differences only on risk assessment language.
- **Minority views**: "Some" had "at least a marginal preference for retaining the current tilt toward weakness" but accepted balanced.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 30 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Discussion of equity wealth effects, bank lending terms, mortgage rates, potential market overreaction to the decision. Financial conditions mentioned as context for policy rather than as standalone stability concern.
- **Center of gravity**: Limited but present discussion of financial conditions; serves policy rationale rather than representing a stability focus.
- **Minority views**: None.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 32 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: All supported shifting risk assessment to "balanced for the foreseeable future"; "many members" worried retaining weakness tilt would cause market expectations of further easing; but underlying stance remains accommodative
- **Center of gravity**: The forward signal is "we've acted aggressively, now balanced/wait-and-see" — an easing lean with data dependence. Not signaling further cuts but not signaling tightening either.
- **Minority views**: "Some" preferred keeping the weakness tilt, implying they saw potential need for further easing.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 58 ±6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "a high degree of uncertainty and related caution" constraining business investment; "geopolitical and other uncertainties" affecting confidence; "uncertainties that surrounded prospective market reactions"; "dissipation of prevailing uncertainties, including those associated with geopolitical risks"
- **Center of gravity**: Elevated uncertainty is a pervasive theme — geopolitical risks, business caution, and economic outlook all flagged as unusually uncertain.
- **Minority views**: None — uncertainty is broadly shared.
