**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Unsegmented (pre-2009), so I must distinguish participant language from staff content. The final paragraphs (lines 226-297) are the formal directive/staff review language -- I will score only from participant discussion sections.

**Era context**: May 1998 -- late 1990s expansion. Asian financial crisis ongoing, strong domestic demand, tight labor markets, low measured inflation but concerns about future inflation. Fed funds rate at 5.5%.

**Quantifier distribution**: "members" (general consensus), "a majority" (for hold), "many" (costs of delay are small), "some members" (concern about risk complacency; reservations about deceleration), "several" (concern about rapid monetary growth), "a number of members" (close call; dollar/equity/financial conditions), "two members" (dissented for tightening), "a few" (not used frequently).

**Dimension-by-dimension reasoning**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Members see inflation as currently low/contained but with rising risks. "Price inflation has remained subdued" but "members generally were concerned that inflation might begin to rise over the intermediate term." Current readings are low, but the forward risk is clearly tilted up. One-time factors (dollar appreciation, oil, benefit costs) are fading. This is a case where current inflation is low but concern about future inflation is notable. Score should reflect the NET perception: below target currently but with upward risk. Around 42-48. The text says inflation is "lower than those seen in several decades" but concern about future rise is general. I'll score 45 -- slightly below midpoint (current low) with upward risk reflected in uncertainty band.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: Members note "exceptional strength in domestic final demand," economy "producing beyond its long-run potential" (line 156), "above trend" demand growth. The bright-line rule: "a number of members suspected, the economy was now producing beyond its long-run potential" --> MUST be > 50. Given "exceptional strength" and "unsustainable pace," this is clearly above potential. Score 60. The late 90s boom anchor is 57-63.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Decline in the unemployment rate to its lowest level in nearly three decades," "further tightening in labor markets," "tight labor markets," "pressures on labor resources continued to intensify." This is very tight. Anchor for 1999-2000 is 65-75. This is May 1998, slightly before peak. Score 68.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Exceptional strength in domestic final demand," "robust further expansion," "rapid growth in consumer spending," "continuing robust growth of business investment," "rebounded sharply." This is well above trend. Score 68 -- fits the "well above trend; unsustainably rapid" range (66-80).

5. **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Members explicitly call conditions "accommodative" and "very accommodative." "Ample availability of loans," "ready availability of equity and debt financing on favorable terms," "credit was cheap and plentiful" (Jordan dissent). Equity prices rising. This is clearly loose. Score 18 -- loose/accommodative conditions. Anchor for pre-crisis ease is 10-20.

6. **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: A majority held steady, but with asymmetric bias toward tightening. Two formal dissents for tightening. "A number of members indicated that the decision was a close call." The majority preferred hold but acknowledged "risks were tilted in the direction of rising inflation" and "a policy tightening move, possibly in the near future, was a likely though not an inevitable prospect." This is a lean-hawkish hold. Score 58 -- hold with clear tightening bias, two hawkish dissents.

7. **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Two formal dissents (Jordan and Poole), both for tightening. "A number of members indicated that the decision was a close call." Several others expressed discomfort. This is moderate-to-notable disagreement. Score 42 -- 2 dissents with multiple members on the fence.

8. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Significant discussion of equity prices "beyond levels that seemed justified by fundamentals," "reduced risk or complacency" spreading to other markets including commercial/residential properties. Asian financial turmoil discussed at length. Concern about "outsized effects on the already very sensitive financial markets in Asia." Jordan's dissent on asset price inflation. This is a moderate-to-prominent topic. Score 50.

9. **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Asymmetric directive biased toward tightening. All hold-voters supported this bias. "A policy tightening move, possibly in the near future, was a likely though not an inevitable prospect." This signals mild tightening bias. Score 62.

10. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Uncertainties in the outlook for economic expansion and inflation remained sufficiently great," "substantial uncertainty" about Asia effects, "considerable questions remained about the relationship of labor market pressures to inflation," "forecasts of rising inflation had proved unreliable." Multiple sources of genuine uncertainty. Score 52 -- elevated but not extreme.

**Polarity checks**:

- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS 18: Higher = tighter. Evidence says very loose/accommodative. Low score correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 58: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says hold with tightening bias + 2 hawkish dissents. Correct.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 62: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Asymmetric directive toward tightening. Correct.

**Cross-dimension coherence**:
- OUTPUT_GAP (60) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (68): Both above 50, consistent with late-cycle boom. Labor somewhat higher because labor market is described in more extreme terms than overall output gap.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (58) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (62): Generally aligned. Forward guidance slightly more hawkish because the asymmetric directive is an explicit signal, while current action is a hold. Makes sense.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 45 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "developments in business costs had been relatively benign...contained inflation at rates that were lower than those seen in several decades"
- "the members generally were concerned that inflation might begin to rise over the intermediate term, especially if labor markets tightened further"
- "By some measures, inflation had continued to drop in the first quarter"

**Center of gravity**: Current inflation is low/subdued, but members broadly anticipate upward pressure if labor markets tighten further. The score reflects currently contained inflation with recognized upward risks.

**Minority views**: Two dissenters (Poole, Jordan) saw inflation as more imminent. Some members noted one-time disinflationary factors were fading.

---

### OUTPUT_GAP: 60 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "members noted the exceptional strength in domestic final demand and viewed robust further expansion in such demand as highly likely"
- "a number of members suspected, the economy was now producing beyond its long-run potential"
- "the growth rate of aggregate demand over the past two years clearly had exceeded the economy's long-run growth potential" (Poole dissent)

**Center of gravity**: Economy clearly operating above potential, with exceptional demand growth, though some moderation expected ahead from inventories and net exports.

**Minority views**: Some expected substantial moderation from inventory adjustment and weaker exports, but current position is clearly above potential.

---

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 68 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The decline in the unemployment rate to its lowest level in nearly three decades underscored anecdotal reports of further tightening in labor markets"
- "if pressures on labor resources continued to intensify, the associated increases in labor compensation would at some point significantly exceed the gains in productivity"
- "tight labor markets" referenced multiple times

**Center of gravity**: Labor markets are very tight, with unemployment at multi-decade lows and labor compensation trending higher. This is broadly recognized by members.

**Minority views**: Narrow range of views on the level of tightness; disagreement is about the inflation implications of that tightness.

---

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 68 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "members noted the exceptional strength in domestic final demand"
- "The rapid growth in consumer spending was being bolstered by large gains in stock market wealth"
- "the ready availability of equity and debt financing on favorable terms was a key factor in the continuing robust growth of business investment"

**Center of gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment are very strong, well above trend, driven by accommodative financial conditions and wealth effects.

**Minority views**: "Limited indications of weakness in some sectors of the economy--such as manufacturing, energy, and agriculture" but these are minor relative to the dominant strength.

---

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 18 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "accommodative financial conditions" noted by members as a key driver of demand
- "ample availability of loans," "ready availability of equity and debt financing on favorable terms"
- "financial conditions were very accommodative in terms of the ample availability of financing to most borrowers on very attractive terms" (some members)
- "credit was cheap and plentiful" (Jordan dissent)

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are unambiguously loose/accommodative, with easy credit, rising equity prices, and favorable financing terms. Broad consensus on this characterization.

**Minority views**: None in the opposite direction; if anything, dissenters viewed conditions as even more excessively loose.

---

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 58 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "a majority of the members indicated that they preferred or could accept an unchanged policy" with asymmetric bias toward tightening
- "A number of members indicated that the decision was a close call"
- "two members...indicated a strong preference for tightening the stance of policy at this meeting"
- "a policy tightening move, possibly in the near future, was a likely though not an inevitable prospect"

**Center of gravity**: Majority holds but with clear hawkish lean. Two dissents for tightening, multiple others on the fence. The overall sentiment is that tightening is likely needed soon.

**Minority views**: Two formal hawkish dissents (Jordan, Poole). No dovish minority.

---

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 42 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Two formal dissents (Jordan and Poole) -- both for tightening
- "A number of members indicated that the decision was a close call"
- "some of these members could accept postponing action for the present"

**Center of gravity**: Moderate disagreement. Two dissents in the same direction, plus several members describing it as a "close call," indicating a significant hawkish contingent beyond just the two dissenters.

**Minority views**: The disagreement is about timing (now vs. soon), not about the direction of risk. This somewhat reduces the intensity.

---

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 50 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "some members expressed concern that the widespread perceptions of reduced risk or complacency that had bolstered equity prices beyond levels that seemed justified by fundamentals were beginning to be felt in a variety of other markets as well, including commercial and residential properties, business ventures, and land"
- Extensive discussion of Asian financial turmoil and its potential to destabilize global markets
- Jordan's dissent focused heavily on asset price inflation and unsustainable trends

**Center of gravity**: Financial stability receives moderate-to-substantial attention. Equity overvaluation concerns, Asian contagion risks, and asset price spillovers are all discussed meaningfully by multiple members.

---

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 62 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Asymmetric directive biased toward tightening: "a somewhat higher federal funds rate would be acceptable"
- "All the members who intended to vote for an unchanged policy at this meeting supported the retention of a directive that was biased toward restraint"
- "a policy tightening move, possibly in the near future, was a likely though not an inevitable prospect"

**Center of gravity**: Clear forward signal toward tightening, with the asymmetric directive and explicit discussion of near-term tightening as a likely prospect.

---

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the uncertainties in the outlook for economic expansion and inflation remained sufficiently great to warrant a continued wait-and-see policy stance"
- "forecasts of rising inflation had proved unreliable and needed to be viewed in light of the considerable uncertainties surrounding them"
- "substantial uncertainty, with the risks tilted toward a greater effect on the U.S. economy than had been anticipated" (re: Asia)
- "the nexus between labor market tightness, accelerating labor costs, and the effects on price inflation was very difficult to ascertain"

**Center of gravity**: Elevated uncertainty from multiple sources -- Asia, inflation dynamics, inventory adjustment. Uncertainty is genuine and cited as a reason for holding rather than tightening.
