# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1997-12-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented (pre-2009), so I must focus on participant language cues ("members commented," "members emphasized," "many members," etc.) and ignore staff-attributable content.

**Era context**: Late 1997 -- late-cycle expansion, Asian Financial Crisis unfolding. Unemployment at 4.6%, strong GDP growth, inflation surprisingly subdued. Fed funds rate at 5.5%.

**Quantifier distribution**: "nearly all" (hold policy), "many members" (risks tilted to inflation, next move likely tightening), "most members" (no imminent need for adjustment), "a majority" (symmetric directive), "a number of members" (consumer spending resilient), "several" (worker scarcity limiting growth), "some members" (retained tightening tilt preference), "one member" (dissent for immediate tightening -- Broaddus).

**Dimension-by-dimension directional reasoning**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Members note inflation has been "surprisingly low," "subdued," with "some key price measures indicating declining inflation." However, there is clear concern about FUTURE inflation from tight labor markets -- "multiplying indications of faster wage increases might presage rising price inflation." The Asian crisis is expected to dampen prices further. The current perception is low inflation but with building wage pressures that have not yet translated into prices. Direction: below neutral currently but with upward risk. Score should be in the 38-45 range -- inflation is low now, but wage pressures are mounting, placing it slightly below midpoint.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: Members describe "robust economic growth," "high levels of resource use," "the economy possibly producing beyond its potential," and domestic demand "quite strong." One member explicitly says "unsustainable pace." The bright-line rule: "above potential" language requires score > 50. Direction: clearly above potential. Score in 55-60 range. Late 90s boom anchor is 57-63.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Members emphasize "very limited supply of new workers," "extraordinary tightness prevailing in markets throughout the nation," "scarcity of available workers was limiting the growth of economic activity." Several report novel approaches to entice non-participants into the labor force. Wage increases "gradually becoming more pervasive." Unemployment at 4.6%. Direction: very tight. Anchor for 1999-2000 is 65-75. This is 1997 and the language is quite strong -- "extraordinary tightness." Score around 68-72.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Domestic demand "quite strong," consumer spending "brisk," tourism "booming," business contacts "optimistic about holiday sales," housing demand at "relatively high level," business investment outlook "favorable" with "strong support for capital spending." Some expected moderation from Asia effects and investment slowing from "unusually rapid pace." Direction: above trend but with expected moderation. Score 58-63.

5. **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Current conditions in domestic financial markets clearly remained supportive of spending," "low cost of capital," "ready availability of finance on attractive terms," "very large increases in household wealth." The real federal funds rate was "relatively high" but credit conditions are loose. Direction: loose/accommodative conditions. Score around 25-30.

6. **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Nearly all favored holding steady. Many saw next move as likely tightening. Most agreed no imminent need. One dissent for tightening (Broaddus). Majority moved to symmetric directive (from tightening tilt), meaning a slight dovish shift in stance. The center of gravity is: hold with a mild tightening bias, but Asia uncertainty warrants patience. This is a "lean hawkish to balanced" posture. Score around 52-55 -- hold with tightening bias but no urgency.

7. **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal dissent (Broaddus). "Views were somewhat more divided" on the directive language -- majority wanted symmetric, others wanted tightening tilt. There is genuine disagreement about the directive but broad agreement on the hold decision. Score around 30-35 (one dissent, some division on directive).

8. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Asian financial turmoil is discussed extensively as a key factor influencing the outlook and policy. Members discuss equity market volatility, foreign exchange effects, potential spillovers. However, the discussion is primarily about macroeconomic transmission effects rather than financial stability per se. Still, the attention to financial conditions/markets is moderate to significant. Score around 40-48.

9. **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Many members commented that... they viewed the risks on balance as pointing to rising price inflation and the next policy move as likely to be in the direction of some tightening." But "most members agreed that the need for such a policy adjustment did not appear to be imminent." The directive was shifted to symmetric. Forward signal: mild tightening bias but very patient. Score around 55-60.

10. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "the extent of this effect was difficult to judge," "the ultimate extent of the adjustment in Asian economies remained unknown," members note "prevailing near-term uncertainties," "crosscurrents that were generating the present uncertainties," "increased uncertainty about financial asset values." Asia is the dominant source of uncertainty. This is somewhat elevated. Score around 45-52.

**Polarity checks**:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says low current inflation but building wage pressures. Score ~42 checks out -- slightly below midpoint.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. "Possibly producing beyond potential." Score ~58 checks out.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. "Extraordinary tightness." Score ~70 checks out.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Conditions are "supportive" / loose. Score ~27 checks out.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Hold with tightening bias. Score ~53 checks out.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward. Next move likely tightening but not imminent, symmetric directive. Score ~57 checks out.

**Cross-dimension coherence**:
- OUTPUT_GAP (58) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70): Both above 50, consistent with late-90s boom. Labor tightness is higher because it is more emphasized and extreme in the text than general output gap language.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (53) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (57): Both lean mildly hawkish, consistent. The forward tone is slightly more hawkish because "many members" expect next move to be tightening even though current action is a hold with symmetric directive.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 42 +/-7 | Moderate confidence**

**Evidence**:
- "price inflation had remained subdued, indeed with some key price measures indicating declining inflation, despite the persistence of robust economic growth"
- "multiplying indications of faster wage increases might presage rising price inflation at some point"
- "developments associated with the turmoil in Asia along with the partly related appreciation of the dollar would tend to intensify import competition and damp the prices of goods"

**Center-of-gravity**: Members perceive current inflation as clearly subdued and below target, but mounting wage pressures create upside risk. The Asian crisis adds disinflationary force. Net assessment is slightly below neutral.

**Minority views**: One member (Broaddus) saw inflation risk as more imminent, warranting immediate action. Some members emphasized the favorable price performance more; others stressed wage acceleration. Uncertainty band reflects this range.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 58 +/-5 | High confidence**

**Evidence**:
- "growth in economic activity had remained solid"
- "the economy possibly producing beyond its potential"
- "domestic demand for goods and services had been quite strong"

**Center-of-gravity**: Members view the economy as operating above potential, with robust domestic demand, though Asian effects are expected to moderate growth going forward.

**Minority views**: While broad agreement on current above-potential status, views differ on how much Asian spillovers will slow growth. Band is moderate.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 70 +/-5 | High confidence**

**Evidence**:
- "members emphasized the very limited supply of new workers and the extraordinary tightness prevailing in markets throughout the nation"
- "Several reported that the scarcity of available workers was limiting the growth of economic activity in some parts of the country"
- "larger increases were gradually becoming more pervasive as labor markets tightened"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants unanimously view labor markets as very tight, with shortages constraining growth and wage pressures building broadly. This is a prominent topic with strong language.

**Minority views**: Little disagreement on the tightness assessment itself; some variation on how quickly wages would translate to prices.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 60 +/-7 | High confidence**

**Evidence**:
- "Domestic demand for goods and services had been quite strong and was likely to remain reasonably robust"
- "business contacts were optimistic about holiday sales, tourism was booming in some parts of the country, and spending for services had been brisk"
- "The outlook for business fixed investment remained favorable... low cost of capital, the ready availability of finance on attractive terms... were providing strong support for capital spending"

**Center-of-gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment are described as strong to robust, above trend but with expected moderation from Asia effects and natural deceleration. Net: clearly above trend.

**Minority views**: Some members saw risk of weaker consumer spending if Asia effects deepen; "a number of members" expected consumer spending to hold up well.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 27 +/-6 | Moderate confidence**

**Evidence**:
- "Current conditions in domestic financial markets clearly remained supportive of spending"
- "the low cost of capital, the ready availability of finance on attractive terms"
- "the cumulative effects of very large increases in household wealth in recent years"

**Center-of-gravity**: Domestic financial conditions are clearly loose and supportive. Credit is freely available, equity wealth is high, and financing terms are attractive. Some note the real funds rate is "relatively high," but overall conditions are accommodative.

**Minority views**: One member noted the real federal funds rate was relatively high, and money growth was expected to moderate. The Asia turmoil created some external financial stress, but domestic conditions remain loose.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 53 +/-7 | High confidence**

**Evidence**:
- "nearly all the members favored a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance"
- "many members commented that... they viewed the risks on balance as pointing to rising price inflation and the next policy move as likely to be in the direction of some tightening"
- "most members agreed that the need for such a policy adjustment did not appear to be imminent"
- One dissent (Broaddus) for immediate tightening

**Center-of-gravity**: Hold steady with a mild tightening bias. The center of gravity is just above neutral -- members expect the next move to be a hike but see no urgency. The shift to a symmetric directive tempers the hawkish lean.

**Minority views**: Broaddus dissented for immediate tightening. "Other members" preferred retaining a tightening-tilted directive. The range spans from those emphasizing Asia downside risks to Broaddus wanting action now.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 33 +/-5 | High confidence**

**Evidence**:
- One formal dissent: "Mr. Broaddus dissented because he continued to believe that a modest tightening of policy would be prudent"
- "Views were somewhat more divided with regard to the instruction in the directive relating to the possible adjustment of policy during the intermeeting period"
- "A majority of the members indicated a preference for a shift to a symmetrical directive" vs. "Other members expressed a slight preference for retaining a directive that was tilted toward tightening"

**Center-of-gravity**: One formal dissent plus notable disagreement on directive language. Moderate but not severe disagreement. The main policy action (hold) had near-consensus.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 45 +/-8 | Moderate confidence**

**Evidence**:
- Extensive discussion of Asian financial turmoil and its potential U.S. effects
- "increased uncertainty about financial asset values, possibly related in part to further difficulties in Asia, could lead to greater caution in spending"
- "a substantial decline in equity values, should it occur, would have a more pronounced effect by reducing household wealth"
- "a modest firming action might well have outsized effects in financial markets, especially the foreign exchange markets"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions and the Asian crisis are a significant part of the discussion. Members address equity markets, foreign exchange, and potential spillover effects. This goes beyond passing mention but is discussed primarily through the lens of macroeconomic impact rather than systemic stability per se.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 57 +/-8 | Moderate confidence**

**Evidence**:
- "many members commented that... the next policy move as likely to be in the direction of some tightening"
- "most members agreed that the need for such a policy adjustment did not appear to be imminent"
- Directive shifted to symmetric: "a slightly higher or a slightly lower federal funds rate might be acceptable"

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is mildly hawkish -- members expect the next move to be tightening but are in no hurry and shifted to a symmetric directive, acknowledging the possibility that the next move could even be easing if Asia deteriorates sharply.

**Minority views**: "Other members" preferred retaining a tightening-tilted directive, which would have been a somewhat more hawkish forward signal.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 50 +/-7 | High confidence**

**Evidence**:
- "the ultimate extent of the adjustment in Asian economies remained unknown, and more substantial downward pressure on the economies of the United States and its trading partners could not be ruled out"
- "the crosscurrents that were generating the present uncertainties in the outlook for economic activity and inflation made a flexible approach to monetary policy particularly desirable"
- "prevailing near-term uncertainties warranted a cautious wait-and-see policy posture"

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is elevated above normal, driven primarily by the Asian crisis and its unknown spillover effects. Members explicitly cite uncertainty as justification for patience. This is moderately elevated -- more than standard caveats but not extreme.
