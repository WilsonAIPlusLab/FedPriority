# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2000-05-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format, but the provided text is already participant-focused content (member discussion sections). Quantifier language present: "all," "members," "a number of," "some members."

**Era context**: May 2000 — late-stage dot-com boom. The Fed had been hiking in 25bp increments since mid-1999 and at this meeting escalated to a 50bp hike to 6.5%. Economy was running hot with very tight labor markets, strong productivity growth, and early signs of rising inflation pressure.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all the members" (endorsed 50bp hike), "members believed" (risks appreciably higher), "a number of the members" (don't want to prejudge future pace), "some members" (inflation expectations worsening), "widespread view." Predominantly consensus language with minor nuances.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as above the midpoint and rising. Evidence: "acceleration in some measures of labor compensation," "early signs of a possible upturn in underlying price inflation," "risks of acceleration in core prices were now appreciably higher." However, actual core inflation had not yet broken out — "examples of successful efforts to mark up prices... were still the exception" and "strong competition" constrained pricing. This is elevated risk/pressure, not yet realized broad inflation. Direction: above 50. Magnitude: "appreciably higher" risks but still mostly prospective — around 58-62. Score: 60.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as clearly above potential. "Extraordinary and persisting strength of overall demand, exceeding even the increasingly rapid growth of potential supply." "Rapidly rising demand was pressing on limited supply resources." This is unambiguously above potential. Magnitude: "extraordinary" strength, "exceeding" potential — this is strong language. Score: 65.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Extremely tight labor markets, which at least in some parts of the country appeared to have tightened further." "Unusually high level of labor resource utilization." "Spending a great deal of time and expense to attract and retain workers." Direction: well above midpoint. Magnitude: "extremely tight" is very strong language. Score: 73.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Extraordinary and persisting strength of overall demand," "burgeoning growth in demand," "strong momentum of the expansion." All language points to demand well above trend. "Profitable investment opportunities" also mentioned. Score: 68.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Limited direct discussion of financial conditions. Markets "seemed to have recognized the need for real interest rates to rise further" — suggesting markets were pricing in tightening. "Widespread market expectations" of the 50bp move. Equity prices mentioned positively (higher equity prices from productivity). No mention of credit stress, spreads widening, or tight conditions. Financial conditions appear still relatively accommodative despite rate hikes. Score: 42.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All the members endorsed" a 50bp hike — the first such escalation. "A more forceful policy move... was desirable." "The greater risk... was for policy to be too sluggish." "Little risk in a relatively aggressive policy move." Balance of risks tilted toward rising inflation. Forward: "meeting-by-meeting basis" but risks still tilted hawkish. This is strong hawkish consensus for aggressive action. Score: 75.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All the members endorsed" the 50bp hike. The balance of risks statement was agreed upon. "A number of the members" had nuances about not prejudging future pace, but no dissent on action. Near-consensus. Score: 12.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Equity prices mentioned briefly as part of demand feedback mechanism, not as stability concern. No discussion of bubbles, credit stress, systemic risk, or financial imbalances as a topic. Very limited attention. Score: 12.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Balance of risks "tilted toward rising inflation" — signals further tightening likely. But "a number of the members... did not want to prejudge the potential extent or pace of future policy tightening" and emphasized "meeting-by-meeting basis." Hawkish bias but with data-dependence caveat. Score: 70.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Limited uncertainty language. Members seem fairly confident in their assessment. "Some members" note inflation expectations "already showed signs of worsening" (some uncertainty about expectations). The meeting-by-meeting language reflects some uncertainty about pace but not about direction. Normal-to-slightly-elevated. Score: 30.

- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (75) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (70): Both strongly hawkish, aligned. Forward guidance slightly lower due to "meeting-by-meeting" qualification.

### Polarity verification:

- OUTPUT_GAP 65: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says "exceeding potential." Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS 73: Higher = tighter. Evidence says "extremely tight." Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 60 ±8 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "risks of acceleration in core prices were now appreciably higher given current trends in aggregate demand, pressures on resources"; "early signs of a possible upturn in underlying price inflation"; "rising wages and benefits and increasing costs of nonlabor inputs could no longer be fully offset by improvements in productivity"
- **Center of gravity**: Members see inflation pressure as meaningfully above neutral — not yet realized broad inflation, but risks clearly elevated with early signs of pass-through from costs to prices.
- **Minority views**: Offsetting factor noted: "strong competition across much of the economy" constraining price increases. This keeps the score from going higher.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 65 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "extraordinary and persisting strength of overall demand, exceeding even the increasingly rapid growth of potential supply"; "rapidly rising demand was pressing on limited supply resources"
- **Center of gravity**: Economy unambiguously above potential with demand exceeding even fast-growing supply capacity.
- **Minority views**: None expressed; unanimous assessment.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 73 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "extremely tight labor markets, which at least in some parts of the country appeared to have tightened further since early in the year"; "unusually high level of labor resource utilization"; "spending a great deal of time and expense to attract and retain workers"
- **Center of gravity**: Labor markets at extreme tightness with further tightening occurring. Consistent with late-1990s/2000 anchor range of 65-75.
- **Minority views**: None; uniform assessment.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 68 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "extraordinary and persisting strength of overall demand"; "burgeoning growth in demand"; "strong momentum of the expansion"; "profitable investment opportunities"
- **Center of gravity**: Demand growth well above trend, characterized as extraordinary and persistent. Both consumption (via equity wealth) and investment (via productivity/profit opportunities) strong.
- **Minority views**: None expressed.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 42 ±8 [Low confidence]
- **Evidence**: "ongoing acceleration of productivity... feeding back on demand through higher equity prices and profitable investment opportunities"; "financial markets seemed to have recognized the need for real interest rates to rise further"
- **Center of gravity**: Financial conditions not prominently discussed. Equity wealth effects still supporting demand suggests conditions not yet restrictive despite rate hikes. Slightly loose of neutral.
- **Minority views**: None clearly stated; limited discussion drives low confidence.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 75 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "all the members endorsed a proposal to tighten reserve conditions sufficiently to raise the federal funds rate by [50bp]"; "a more forceful policy move... was desirable"; "the greater risk to the economic expansion at this point was for policy to be too sluggish"; "the members saw little risk in a relatively aggressive policy move"
- **Center of gravity**: Unanimous support for escalation from 25bp to 50bp hike. Strong hawkish consensus with view that bigger risk is doing too little.
- **Minority views**: "A number of the members" cautioned against prejudging future pace — a mild moderating note that does not offset the strong hawkish action.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 12 ±5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "all the members endorsed" the 50bp hike; "the members agreed that the balance of risks sentence... should indicate... risks would remain tilted toward rising inflation"
- **Center of gravity**: Near-complete consensus on both the action and the risk assessment. Minor nuance from "a number" about future pace does not constitute meaningful disagreement.
- **Minority views**: The "number of members" who cautioned about not prejudging future moves represent emphasis differences, not dissent.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 12 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Equity prices mentioned once in passing as part of demand transmission mechanism ("higher equity prices"). No discussion of financial stability risks, asset bubbles, credit conditions, or systemic concerns.
- **Center of gravity**: Financial stability essentially absent as a discussion topic.
- **Minority views**: None.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 70 ±7 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "risks would remain tilted toward rising inflation"; "a number of the members commented that they did not want to prejudge the potential extent or pace of future policy tightening"; "the Committee should continue to assess the need for further policy moves... on a meeting-by-meeting basis"
- **Center of gravity**: Clear tightening bias in the forward signal — risks still tilted hawkish — but tempered by data-dependence and reluctance to commit to specific future path.
- **Minority views**: The meeting-by-meeting caveat modestly widens the band.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 30 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "the Committee should continue to assess the need for further policy moves in the light of evolving economic conditions"; the productivity-demand feedback loop described implies some uncertainty about underlying dynamics
- **Center of gravity**: Members express relatively normal levels of uncertainty. They are confident about the current assessment (tight labor, strong demand, rising inflation risk) but maintain standard data-dependence about the future path.
- **Minority views**: "Some members" note inflation expectations "already showed signs of worsening" — this reflects concern more than uncertainty.

### Optional Dimensions:

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 57 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "a 50 basis point adjustment was more likely to help forestall a rise in inflationary expectations that, at least in the opinion of some members, already showed signs of worsening"; "subdued long-run inflation expectations during an extended period"
- Members see expectations as still mostly anchored but with early signs of drift upward.

**PRODUCTIVITY: 70 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "increasingly rapid growth of potential supply"; "ongoing acceleration of productivity"; "improvements in productivity" by businesses trying to offset costs
- Members clearly perceive above-trend productivity growth, though it is insufficient to fully absorb demand.
