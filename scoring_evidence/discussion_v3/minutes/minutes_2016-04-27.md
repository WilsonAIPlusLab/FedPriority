# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2016-04-27 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Segmented — participant discussion section is clearly labeled ("Participants' Views on Current Conditions and the Economic Outlook") and separated from staff content. The "Committee Policy Action" section at the end uses "members" language which also counts.

**Era context**: April 2016 — post-liftoff (Dec 2015 first hike), rates at 0.25-0.50%. Economy growing moderately, labor market improving, inflation below 2%. Global risks (China, Brexit) still present but diminishing. First quarter GDP was weak (later revised up). Oil prices low but stabilizing.

**Quantifier distribution**: "participants agreed," "most participants," "many participants," "several," "some," "a few," "a couple," "one member" (dissent on hold). Good range of quantifiers providing rich center-of-gravity information.

### Dimension-by-dimension analysis:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target. "Inflation had continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective." Core PCE at 1.7%, up from 1.4%. Split views: "some participants continued to see progress toward the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective as likely to be gradual" while "many other participants, the recent developments provided greater confidence that inflation would rise to 2 percent over the medium term." Still below target currently; some firming noted. Energy/import drags. Market-based inflation compensation "remained low." Direction: below 50 but moving up. Score around 38-40. Higher = more pressure. Evidence says below target but firming slightly — matches a score in the upper 30s.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Growth in economic activity appeared to have slowed" in Q1 but "most participants judged that the slowdown in growth of domestic spending would be temporary." "Many participants judged that labor market conditions had reached or were quite close to those consistent with... maximum employment." Medium-term outlook for "moderate pace" of expansion. Some see "risk that a more persistent slowdown in economic growth might be under way." The language is mixed: economy near potential but Q1 soft. "Near potential" language plus "approaching" → score around 48-50. Bright-line rule: "reached or were quite close to" maximum employment suggests near potential. Score: 48.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Many participants judged that labor market conditions had reached or were quite close to those consistent with their interpretation of the Committee's objective of maximum employment." "Several of them reported... shortages of workers in selected occupations, or pressures to retain or train workers." But "many other participants continued to see scope for reducing labor market slack." Split between near-full-employment and some-slack-remaining. Center of gravity: near full employment but not tight. Score: 48-50. Anchors say 50 for 2016-17.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending slowed in Q1 but "most participants expected to see a pickup." Business fixed investment "declined in the fourth quarter of 2015 and appeared to have dropped further in early 2016." Energy sector drag, weak exports. "Several participants commented that their business contacts had expressed considerable caution about the economic outlook." But housing improved, consumer fundamentals solid. Net: below trend due to weak investment and Q1 consumer slowdown, but expected temporary. Score around 38-40. "Sluggish" to "below trend."

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "U.S. and global financial conditions improved significantly over the intermeeting period" — "rise in equity indexes, more positive risk sentiment, and a decline in financial market volatility." "Easing in financial conditions" noted multiple times. "Favorable credit conditions for household and business borrowers." Direction: conditions eased/loosened. Lower score = looser. Score: 32-35.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Held rates. "Participants generally saw maintaining the target range... at this meeting" as appropriate. "A few participants judged it appropriate to increase the target range... at this meeting." One formal dissent for hiking. "Most participants judged that if incoming data were consistent with economic growth picking up in the second quarter... then it likely would be appropriate for the Committee to increase the target range... in June." Lean: hold with tightening bias conditional on data. This is a mild hawkish lean — they're signaling June as live. Anchors: 40-50 for 2014-15 pre-liftoff. Post-liftoff but pausing, with conditional June signal. Score: 52-55. Hold with tightening bias.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal dissent (member preferred to hike). "Several" saw roughly balanced risks vs "many others" saw downside risks. Split on inflation interpretation (some gradual vs many gaining confidence). Split on labor slack remaining. Moderate spread of views but general agreement on hold decision. Score: 30-35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: "Several participants pointed out that U.S. firms and financial markets had come through the period of elevated financial market volatility earlier in the year looking relatively resilient." "Several noted the ongoing need to remain alert to vulnerabilities in the financial system." "A few cited concerns about rapidly rising prices of CRE." "Some participants saw limited costs to maintaining a patient posture... but noted the risks—including potential risks to financial stability—of waiting too long." Puerto Rico mentioned briefly. Moderate level of attention — several participants addressed it. Score: 30-35.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Most participants judged that if incoming data were consistent with economic growth picking up in the second quarter... then it likely would be appropriate for the Committee to increase the target range for the federal funds rate in June." "The Committee expected that economic conditions would evolve in a manner that would warrant only gradual increases." Forward signal: conditional tightening (June is live) but gradual. Score: 60-63. Mild tightening bias forward.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Some participants were concerned that transitory factors may not fully explain the softness in consumer spending." Uncertainty about productivity growth explicitly discussed: "acknowledging uncertainty about the reasons for the slowdown in productivity growth." Global risks from Brexit and China mentioned. "Several participants were concerned that the incoming information might not provide sufficiently clear signals to determine by mid-June whether an increase... would be warranted." Somewhat elevated uncertainty. Score: 40-45.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (48) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (49): Aligned, both near potential. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (53) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (62): Forward guidance slightly more hawkish than current lean — makes sense given hold now but conditional June hike signal. Divergence explained by data-dependent conditional forward guidance.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 39 ±7 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Inflation had continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective, partly reflecting earlier declines in energy prices and falling prices of non-energy imports."
- "The 12-month change in core PCE prices also continued to run below 2 percent, but it moved up to 1.7 percent in January and February from 1.4 percent at the end of 2015."
- "Several continued to see important downside risks to inflation in light of the still-low readings on market-based measures of inflation compensation." But "for many other participants, the recent developments provided greater confidence that inflation would rise to 2 percent over the medium term."

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation running below target (core PCE at 1.7%) with some firming but significant downside risks remain; split between those seeing gradual progress and those gaining confidence in return to 2%.

**Minority views**: "Some viewed the recent firming in core inflation as broadly based and unlikely to unwind" (upside); "several continued to see important downside risks" (downside). Uncertainty band reflects this split.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 48 ±6 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Growth in economic activity appeared to have slowed" but "most participants judged that the slowdown in growth of domestic spending would be temporary."
- "Many participants judged that labor market conditions had reached or were quite close to those consistent with their interpretation of the Committee's objective of maximum employment."
- "Some participants were concerned that... a more persistent slowdown in economic growth might be under way."

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy near potential; Q1 weakness seen as temporary by most; approaching maximum employment. Slightly below potential due to Q1 softness.

**Minority views**: "Some" saw risk of persistent slowdown (lower gap); "a few" ready to hike suggesting economy at/above potential.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 49 ±7 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Many participants judged that labor market conditions had reached or were quite close to those consistent with their interpretation of the Committee's objective of maximum employment."
- "Several of them reported that businesses in their Districts had seen a pickup in wages, shortages of workers in selected occupations, or pressures to retain or train workers for hard-to-fill jobs."
- "Many other participants continued to see scope for reducing labor market slack as labor demand continued to expand."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market near full employment, with a meaningful split between those seeing it as at maximum employment (with some tightness signals) and those still seeing slack. Center near NAIRU.

**Minority views**: "Several" report worker shortages (tighter); "many others" see remaining slack (looser).

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 38 ±7 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Growth in household spending had moderated" and "business fixed investment declined in the fourth quarter of 2015 and appeared to have dropped further in early 2016."
- "Several participants commented that their business contacts had expressed considerable caution about the economic outlook or had indicated that their firms were focused on cost-cutting measures."
- "Most participants expected to see a pickup in the growth rate of consumer spending in coming months" citing "still-solid fundamental determinants of household spending."

**Center-of-gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment were weak in Q1 2016, with investment hit by energy and export drags. Most expected consumer pickup but investment outlook was mixed. Below trend currently.

**Minority views**: "Some other participants were more positive about the outlook for business spending, pointing to the optimism reported in a number of business surveys."

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 33 ±5 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "U.S. and global financial conditions improved significantly over the intermeeting period, marked by a rise in equity indexes, more positive risk sentiment, and a decline in financial market volatility."
- "The easing in financial conditions in the United States would provide some support for consumer spending and business investment going forward."
- "A number of participants cited reports from business contacts in their Districts of favorable credit conditions for household and business borrowers."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions eased notably over the intermeeting period after earlier-in-year tightening. Conditions now somewhat accommodative with favorable credit availability, equity gains, and reduced volatility.

**Minority views**: Broad agreement on easing; narrow uncertainty band.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 53 ±8 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Participants generally saw maintaining the target range for the federal funds rate at ¼ to ½ percent at this meeting... as consistent with setting policy in a data-dependent manner and as leaving open the possibility of an increase in the federal funds rate at the June FOMC meeting."
- "A few participants judged it appropriate to increase the target range for the federal funds rate at this meeting." One member dissented for a hike.
- "A number of participants judged that it would be appropriate to proceed cautiously in removing policy accommodation."

**Center-of-gravity**: Hold decision with a mild tightening lean. Most participants saw June as a live meeting for hiking, but majority wanted more data first. One dissent for hiking. Overall lean is just barely hawkish of center — patient but with tightening bias.

**Minority views**: "A few" wanted to hike now (more hawkish); "some" emphasized downside risks and caution (more dovish).

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 32 ±6 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "One member, however, preferred to raise the target range for the federal funds rate at this meeting."
- "Several FOMC participants judged that the risks to the economic outlook were now roughly balanced. However, many others indicated that they continued to see downside risks."
- Split on inflation: "some participants continued to see progress... as likely to be gradual" vs. "for many other participants, the recent developments provided greater confidence."

**Center-of-gravity**: One formal dissent, with moderate disagreement about risk balance, inflation trajectory, and labor slack. Views spread but not sharply divided on the decision itself (broad agreement to hold).

**Minority views**: Disagreements extend to economic assessment (labor slack, inflation trajectory, risk balance) beyond just tactics.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 32 ±5 [Moderate confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Several noted the ongoing need to remain alert to vulnerabilities in the financial system. In that regard, a few cited concerns about rapidly rising prices of CRE, including multifamily properties, or about illiquidity of the assets of some mutual funds."
- "Some participants saw limited costs to maintaining a patient posture at this meeting but noted the risks—including potential risks to financial stability—of waiting too long to resume the process of removing policy accommodation."
- Puerto Rico debt situation mentioned briefly.

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability received limited but notable attention. Several participants raised specific concerns (CRE, mutual fund liquidity, risks of prolonged accommodation). Not a dominant topic but more than passing mentions.

**Minority views**: Narrow range of attention — mostly brief/moderate references.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 62 ±7 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Most participants judged that if incoming data were consistent with economic growth picking up in the second quarter, labor market conditions continuing to strengthen, and inflation making progress toward the Committee's 2 percent objective, then it likely would be appropriate for the Committee to increase the target range for the federal funds rate in June."
- "The Committee expected that economic conditions would evolve in a manner that would warrant only gradual increases in the federal funds rate."
- "Several participants were concerned that the incoming information might not provide sufficiently clear signals to determine by mid-June whether an increase... would be warranted."

**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal is mildly hawkish — June hike conditional on data, with "gradual" path framing. Clear signal that normalization is intended to continue but data-dependent.

**Minority views**: "Several" doubted data would be clear enough by June (less hawkish forward); "some" more confident June appropriate (more hawkish forward).

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 42 ±6 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "Acknowledging uncertainty about the reasons for the slowdown in productivity growth in recent years and whether it would persist, many participants commented on a range of possible outcomes."
- "Some participants noted that global financial markets could be sensitive to the upcoming British referendum on membership in the European Union or to unanticipated developments associated with China's management of its exchange rate."
- "Several participants were concerned that the incoming information might not provide sufficiently clear signals to determine by mid-June whether an increase in the target range... would be warranted."

**Center-of-gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty. Productivity growth trajectory genuinely uncertain; Brexit upcoming; Q1 GDP weakness ambiguous (temporary or persistent?). Multiple sources of uncertainty but none at crisis-level.

**Minority views**: Range of views on whether Q1 weakness was temporary or persistent adds to expressed uncertainty.
