# FOMC Meeting Scoring — 2002-06-26 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Unsegmented pre-2009 format — staff and participant content potentially mixed. I will score ONLY portions with participant language cues ("members commented," "members expected," "members cited," etc.) and ignore any staff-attributable content.

**Era context**: June 2002 — the economy is emerging from the 2001 recession but recovery is hesitant. The fed funds rate is at 1.75%, which is quite accommodative. Stock market weakness, accounting scandals (Enron/WorldCom), and geopolitical uncertainty are weighing on confidence. Inflation is low and trending down.

**Quantifier analysis**: The minutes use "all the members" (unanimous on holding rates), "a number of members" (policy too accommodative long-term), "some members" (concerned about fiscal discipline; concerned markets don't appreciate future tightening), "most Committee members" (structural models useful). Language is predominantly consensus-oriented with limited disagreement.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW target and benign. Key evidence: "Inflation was still edging down"; "inflation expectations appeared to be low and stable"; "unit costs and prices would remain subdued for some time"; PCE forecasts centered on 1.5-1.75% (below 2% target); "continuing absence of pricing power throughout the economy." Only minor upside mentions: steel prices, energy costs, dollar depreciation effects. Direction: clearly below midpoint. Magnitude: "subdued," "edging down," "low" = moderately below. Score ~30-35.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as BELOW potential with "continuing slack in labor and other markets." Recovery is underway but "uneven"; "the timing and strength of an upturn remained subject to considerable uncertainty." Members anticipated growth "in the vicinity of, and perhaps somewhat above, the rate of growth of the economy's potential" — meaning they're still below potential, hoping to close the gap. GDP forecasts of 3.5-4% suggest above-trend growth expected but gap persists. Bright-line rule: "slack remains" language → must be < 50. Score ~35-38.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Continuing slack in labor and other markets"; unemployment forecast 5.75-6% for Q4 2002, falling to 5.25-5.5% by Q4 2003. This is elevated unemployment with slack. "Moderate wage gains" — no tightness. Score ~30-33.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending "well maintained" and providing "moderate impetus" — positive but not strong. Housing "well maintained" at high level but not adding stimulus. Business investment is the key question: "preconditions for a robust advance appeared to be largely in place" but "improvement was still quite limited, unevenly distributed." Nonresidential construction "outlook remained bleak." Inventory rebuilding expected but "gradual." Net: below trend but not contracting — sluggish/uneven. Score ~38-42.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Ample availability of credit to most consumers"; "ready availability of mortgage financing at very attractive rates"; accommodative fiscal and monetary policy. But: "financial markets had been shaken by revelations of accounting irregularities"; stock market weakness; South American financial crisis concern. Credit is loose, but equity markets are stressed. Net: somewhat loose conditions overall, with specific equity market concerns. Score ~38-42 (leaning toward loose side).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All the members agreed... maintaining an unchanged policy stance" at 1.75%. "Retention of the currently accommodative policy stance was desirable." But "a number of members noted that the current policy stance was too accommodative to be consistent over time" and eventual tightening would be needed. Balanced risks assessment. This is a clear hold with acknowledgment of future tightening need but no urgency. Score ~45-48 (lean dovish/balanced hold).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All the members agreed" on unchanged rates. "They agreed to retain an assessment of balanced risks." Very broad consensus. Minor differences in emphasis (some worried about markets not pricing in tightening, others disagreed), but no formal dissents, no sharp disagreement. Score ~15-18.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Accounting irregularities mentioned as shaking markets and confidence. Stock price weakness discussed as risk to consumer spending. South American financial crisis mentioned. Dollar depreciation discussed. But these are context/risk mentions, not deep financial stability discussions dominating the meeting. Moderate attention. Score ~30-35.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Balanced risks statement. "A number of members" noted policy too accommodative long-term and tightening would eventually be needed. But "little risk of triggering an increase in inflation by waiting." Forward signal: hold with eventual tightening anticipated but no urgency. Mildly easing lean with data dependence transitioning to neutral. Score ~42-47.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Considerable uncertainty" about timing and strength of pickup (explicit language). "Questions about the timing and strength of such a pickup was a major source of uncertainty." Members "could see risks on both sides." "Widespread pessimism among business contacts." "Persistence of a high degree of caution." This is genuinely elevated uncertainty. Score ~55-60.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (37) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (32): Both below 50, consistent — slack economy.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (46) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (45): Both lean slightly dovish/neutral, consistent — hold with eventual tightening acknowledged.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (32) low with slack economy — coherent.

### Polarity verification:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says low/subdued. Low score. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says below potential. Low score. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says credit loose but some market stress. Below-midpoint score. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 32 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Inflation was still edging down, inflation expectations appeared to be low and stable, and going forward the members' forecasts for growth and productivity implied that unit costs and prices would remain subdued for some time."
- "The continuing absence of pricing power throughout the economy, evidently related in part to increased price competition in markets around the world stemming from globalization."
- PCE inflation forecasts centered on 1.5-1.75% for both 2002 and 2003.

**Center-of-gravity**: Members see inflation as below target, still edging down, with strong productivity holding down costs. Only minor upside risks from steel, energy, and dollar depreciation are noted but dismissed as insufficient to alter the outlook.

**Minority views**: Members cited "rising prices for a few products, notably steel" and potential dollar depreciation effects, but these were clearly minor caveats, not a significant camp.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 37 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Continuing slack in labor and other markets" — explicit below-potential language.
- "Economic activity would accelerate over coming months to a pace in the vicinity of, and perhaps somewhat above, the rate of growth of the economy's potential" — still below potential now, hoping to close the gap.
- "Some members observed...they had expected to see firmer indications of a strengthening recovery by the time of this meeting."

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is recovering from recession but still operating below potential with slack. Growth is expected to reach potential-rate but the gap has not yet closed. Consistent with the 31-40 range ("below potential; recovering but gap persists").

**Minority views**: None significant — broad agreement on this assessment.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 32 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Continuing slack in labor and other markets."
- Unemployment forecast: 5.75-6% for Q4 2002, falling to 5.25-5.5% by Q4 2003.
- "Rising productivity and moderate wage gains likely continuing to help hold down unit labor costs."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market has significant slack with elevated unemployment. Wage gains are moderate, consistent with below-full-employment conditions. Squarely in the 31-45 range.

**Minority views**: None — consensus view.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 40 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Consumer: "Consumer spending was seen as likely to provide some continuing, though moderate, impetus"; "a typical recovery-period surge in consumer spending was unlikely."
- Business investment: "Improvement was still quite limited, unevenly distributed across industries, and not yet firmly indicative of a sustained advance"; "widespread pessimism among their business contacts."
- Nonresidential construction: "The outlook for nonresidential construction activity remained bleak."

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending is moderate/sustained but not surging. Business investment shows early tentative signs of improvement but remains weak. Commercial real estate is contracting. Net assessment is below trend — "sluggish" to "moderate."

**Minority views**: Some optimism about business investment preconditions being in place, but offset by widespread caution reports. Uncertainty band reflects this range.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 40 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Ample availability of credit to most consumers"; "ready availability of mortgage financing to most borrowers at very attractive rates."
- "Financial markets, and perhaps business and household confidence, had been shaken by revelations of accounting irregularities."
- South American financial crisis potential and dollar depreciation concerns.

**Center-of-gravity**: Credit conditions are broadly loose — abundant consumer and mortgage credit at low rates. However, equity market stress from accounting scandals introduces some tightening element. Net: somewhat loose to neutral, pulled below 50 by the accommodative credit environment.

**Minority views**: The tension between loose credit and equity market stress widens the band.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 46 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All the members agreed that recent developments argued for maintaining an unchanged policy stance, with the target for the federal funds rate remaining at 1-3/4 percent."
- "Retention of the currently accommodative policy stance was desirable to counter the lingering effects of financial and other shocks."
- "A number of members noted that the current policy stance was too accommodative to be consistent over time with the Committee's objectives" — forward-looking hawkish acknowledgment.
- "There was little risk of triggering an increase in inflation by waiting for a better reading on the course of the economy."

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous hold with accommodative intent for now. The acknowledgment of eventual tightening need prevents a more dovish score, but the absence of urgency and comfort with waiting keeps it near balanced with a slight dovish lean.

**Minority views**: "Some members were concerned that markets might not fully appreciate the inevitability of eventual policy tightening" — a mildly hawkish faction, but still supporting the hold.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 15 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All the members agreed that recent developments argued for maintaining an unchanged policy stance."
- "They agreed to retain an assessment of balanced risks."
- Differences limited to emphasis: some vs. others on market expectations of tightening.

**Center-of-gravity**: Near-unanimous consensus on both the rate decision and the risk assessment. Minor emphasis differences do not rise to meaningful dissent.

**Minority views**: Mild disagreement on forward communication but not on the decision itself.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 32 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Financial markets, and perhaps business and household confidence, had been shaken by revelations of accounting irregularities."
- "Possible further shocks to confidence and household wealth should weakness in stock prices persist."
- "Severe problems being experienced by a number of large countries in South America raised the specter of a deepening financial crisis within that region and the possibility of more widespread contagion."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are discussed as context and risk factors — accounting scandals, equity market weakness, South American contagion — but these are not the dominant focus. They are woven into the economic assessment rather than treated as standalone financial stability concerns. Limited-to-moderate discussion.

**Minority views**: None distinct.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 45 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "They agreed to retain an assessment of balanced risks to their long-term objectives."
- "A number of members noted that the current policy stance was too accommodative to be consistent over time... economic performance in line with their current forecasts would at some point require an adjustment to policy toward a less accommodative stance."
- "In current circumstances, there was little risk of triggering an increase in inflation by waiting."

**Center-of-gravity**: The formal forward signal is balanced risks — neutral. However, the forward-looking discussion tilts mildly toward eventual tightening. The net effect is near-neutral with a very slight easing lean because the dominant message is patience/waiting. Not yet signaling tightening action.

**Minority views**: "Some members concerned markets might not fully appreciate eventual tightening" — but this is about communication, not an actual tightening signal.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 57 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The timing and strength of an upturn in the expansion remained subject to considerable uncertainty."
- "Questions about the timing and strength of such a pickup was a major source of uncertainty about the pace of the expansion."
- "Members could see risks on both sides of their forecasts."
- "Persistence of a high degree of caution" in the business community.

**Center-of-gravity**: "Considerable uncertainty" is used explicitly and repeatedly. Multiple downside risks enumerated (confidence shocks, stock market weakness, terrorism, South America). But members do have a baseline forecast they believe in. This is elevated but not extreme uncertainty. Consistent with the 46-60 range.

**Minority views**: None — uncertainty was broadly shared.
