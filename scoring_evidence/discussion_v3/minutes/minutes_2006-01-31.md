# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2006-01-31 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format — staff and participant content are mixed. I will score only portions with participant language cues ("participants noted," "members favored," "some members," etc.).

**Era context**: January 2006. The Fed is deep into the 2004-2006 tightening cycle, raising rates at each meeting in "measured" 25bp increments. This is Greenspan's final meeting. The economy is growing near potential, housing is beginning to slow, core inflation is near 2%, and the funds rate target is moving to 4.5%.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all members" (2 instances — favoring 25bp hike, agreeing future path depends on data), "most participants" (core inflation expected to move up slightly), "some" (housing wealth effects potentially sizable; additional policy moves reinforced by inflation readings; resource utilization nearly full), "several" (high-frequency indicators suggest underlying strength), "participants generally" (strong capex growth). The language is mostly consensus-oriented with some minority notes.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly above the midpoint. "Most participants expected core inflation to move up slightly in the near term, reflecting some pass-through of increased energy and other commodity prices." There are "continuing upside risks to inflation" from energy and commodities. However, pass-through "remained subdued," inflation expectations had "notched down," and firms' ability to pass through costs "remained limited." Some members found core inflation and inflation expectations "somewhat higher than was desirable over the long run." Net: slightly elevated with upside risks, but contained. Direction: modestly above 50. Score ~57.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Economy "expanding at a solid pace." GDP slowed in Q4 but "transitory factors." Economy "poised to expand at a moderate rate in the neighborhood of its sustainable pace." "Some members viewed [resource utilization] as nearly full at present." Capacity utilization was "tightening." This is essentially at-potential to slightly above. Score ~52.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Employment was expanding at a fairly good pace and labor compensation was rising moderately." Unemployment projections at 4.75-5%. "Uncertainties regarding the extent of remaining capacity in labor markets." This suggests near full employment but not extremely tight. Score ~53.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "solid over the holiday season and in recent weeks," consumer confidence "high." But housing slowing, risks from variable-rate mortgage repricing. Capital expenditures expected to be "fairly strong" / "reasonably robust." Net: solid but not booming, with housing headwinds. Score ~55.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Financial market conditions...suggested that investors were optimistic." "Low prevailing term premiums and bond spreads," "ample supply of liquidity," "recent strength in equity markets." This describes loose financial conditions. The possibility of spreads returning to typical settings is noted as a "downside risk." Score ~20 (loose conditions, consistent with 2005-06 anchor of 10-20).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All members favored 25bp hike. "Some further policy firming might be needed." "The stance of policy seemed close to where it needed to be given the current outlook." Future path "could no longer be prejudged with the previous degree of confidence" — suggesting approaching the end of the cycle. This is hawkish (hiking) but with growing sense they are near the end. Score ~67.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All members favored raising the target." All agreed on the data-dependent forward language. No dissents. Minor differences about emphasis on inflation risks ("some members" vs others). Very broad agreement. Score ~15.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: One substantial paragraph on financial market conditions — low spreads, term premiums, ample liquidity, equity market strength. The possibility of spreads normalizing noted as a risk. Housing sector risks discussed but more in macro context. Moderate attention level. Score ~40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Some further policy firming might be needed to keep inflation pressures contained." But "the stance of policy seemed close to where it needed to be" and "the future path for the funds rate would depend increasingly on economic developments and could no longer be prejudged." Mildly hawkish forward signal — further tightening possible but not certain. Score ~65.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The future path...could no longer be prejudged with the previous degree of confidence." "Uncertainties regarding the extent of remaining capacity in labor markets." Housing effects "difficult to predict." Some elevated uncertainty about the path forward, but not extreme. Score ~38.

### Cross-dimension coherence check:
- OUTPUT_GAP (52) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (53): Both slightly above 50, consistent — near potential/near full employment.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (67) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (65): Both moderately hawkish, consistent — hiking with signal of possible more to come.

### Polarity checks:
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says conditions are loose. Score 20 = loose. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. All favor hike, some further firming. Score 67. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 57 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Most participants expected core inflation to move up slightly in the near term, reflecting some pass-through of increased energy and other commodity prices."
- "Some members, the possibility of additional policy moves was reinforced by readings on core inflation and inflation expectations that were somewhat higher than was desirable over the long run."
- "The pass-through of higher energy and other commodity prices to prices of core goods and services had remained subdued."

**Center of gravity**: Participants perceive modest upside inflation risks — core inflation near target but with upward pressures from energy and commodities, partially offset by subdued pass-through and stable expectations.

**Minority views**: Some members see inflation readings as "somewhat higher than desirable"; others note pass-through remains limited. This creates moderate dispersion.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 52 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The economy seemed to be expanding at a solid pace."
- "The economy seemed poised to expand at a moderate rate in the neighborhood of its sustainable pace."
- "Some members viewed [resource utilization] as nearly full at present."

**Center of gravity**: Economy operating at or very slightly above potential — near sustainable pace with capacity utilization tightening.

**Minority views**: Narrow — broad agreement on near-potential growth.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 53 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Employment was expanding at a fairly good pace and labor compensation was rising moderately."
- "Uncertainties regarding the extent of remaining capacity in labor markets."
- Unemployment projections centered at 4.75-5%.

**Center of gravity**: Labor market near full employment; employment growing well but uncertainty about remaining slack. Slightly tight but not stressed.

**Minority views**: "Some participants remarked on the uncertainties regarding the extent of remaining capacity" — suggesting views ranged from nearly full to still some slack.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 55 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending had been solid over the holiday season and in recent weeks, while measures of consumer confidence remained high."
- "Participants generally anticipated fairly strong growth of capital expenditures."
- "Signs of slowing in the housing sector had become more evident" (partial offset).

**Center of gravity**: Net spending/investment modestly above trend — solid consumption and strong capex, partially offset by housing headwinds.

**Minority views**: "Some" saw housing wealth effects as "potentially sizable" risk to demand.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 20 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Financial market conditions...suggested that investors were optimistic about the economic outlook."
- "Low prevailing term premiums and bond spreads...strong demands for longer-term assets and an ample supply of liquidity."
- "The possibility that term premiums and credit spreads could return to more typical settings represented a downside risk."

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions clearly loose — low spreads, ample liquidity, equity strength, compressed risk premiums.

**Minority views**: Minimal — broad recognition of loose conditions.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 67 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All members favored raising the target federal funds rate 25 basis points to 4-1/2 percent."
- "Some further policy firming might be needed to keep inflation pressures contained."
- "The stance of policy seemed close to where it needed to be given the current outlook."

**Center of gravity**: Unanimously hawkish action (another 25bp hike) with signal that more may be needed, though nearing the end of the cycle.

**Minority views**: Some members see additional moves reinforced by inflation readings; others emphasize nearness to appropriate stance. Narrow band.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 15 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All members favored raising the target federal funds rate 25 basis points."
- "All members agreed that the future path for the funds rate would depend increasingly on economic developments."

**Center of gravity**: Near-consensus — unanimous vote, unanimous agreement on forward guidance language. Minor emphasis differences on inflation risks.

**Minority views**: None that rise to the level of dissent.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 40 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Full paragraph on financial market conditions: low term premiums, compressed spreads, ample liquidity, equity strength.
- Housing sector risks discussed as macro demand concern with stability undertones.
- "Economic risks were lower than usual" as market perception noted by participants.

**Center of gravity**: Moderate attention to financial conditions — one dedicated paragraph plus housing risk discussion. Consistent with 2006-07 pre-crisis anchor of 40-55.

**Minority views**: N/A — general observation rather than debated.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 65 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Some further policy firming might be needed to keep inflation pressures contained."
- "The stance of policy seemed close to where it needed to be given the current outlook."
- "The future path for the funds rate would depend increasingly on economic developments and could no longer be prejudged."

**Center of gravity**: Mildly hawkish forward signal — further tightening possible but approaching the end, increasingly data-dependent. Transition from pre-committed path to open posture.

**Minority views**: "Some members" see additional moves reinforced by inflation data, suggesting a slightly more hawkish tail.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 38 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The future path for the funds rate...could no longer be prejudged with the previous degree of confidence."
- "Uncertainties regarding the extent of remaining capacity in labor markets."

**Center of gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty — several sources of uncertainty noted (labor capacity, housing, policy path) but no pervasive alarm. Participants are generally confident in the outlook but acknowledge growing unknowns.

**Minority views**: "Some" see housing risks as "potentially sizable" — adding to tail uncertainty.
