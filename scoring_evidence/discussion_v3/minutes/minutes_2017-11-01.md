# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2017-11-01 (October 31 - November 1, 2017)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented -- participant discussion is clearly separated from staff content.

**Era context**: Late 2017, midway through the post-GFC gradual hiking cycle. Fed funds rate at 1-1.25%. Economy near or above full employment. Inflation persistently undershooting the 2% target (core PCE at 1.3%). Balance sheet normalization just started. Hurricane disruptions (Harvey, Irma, Maria) providing noise in data.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all" (1 -- hold decision), "nearly all" (1 -- gradual approach), "most" (3 -- cyclical pressures on inflation, outlook unchanged, inflation to stabilize), "many" (4 -- softness transitory, inflation might stay low, another hike warranted, low readings), "several" (4 -- financial imbalances, diminished inflation responsiveness, inflation confidence, near-term hike conditional), "some" (3 -- stable expectations, upside risks, inflation concerns), "a few" (5 -- tax cuts, secular inflation drags, defer firming, persistent below-2%, wage firming), "a couple" (2 -- alternative frameworks, wage/productivity).

### Core Dimension Reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target. Core PCE at 1.3%, well below 2%. "Many participants judged that much of the recent softness in core inflation reflected temporary or idiosyncratic factors." But "many participants observed that there was some likelihood that inflation might remain below 2 percent for longer." "Several participants expressed concern that the persistently weak inflation data could lead to a decline in longer-term inflation expectations." The center of gravity is clearly below target, with concern about the persistence. This is not just "slightly below" -- core at 1.3% is notably below, and participants are worried. Score should be in the 30-38 range. Polarity check: higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says low inflation pressure. Score below 50. Correct.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Many participants judged that the economy was operating at or above full employment." "Above-trend economic growth." GDP expected to grow "at a pace exceeding that of potential output." Unemployment at 4.2%, "falling further below participants' estimates of its longer-run normal level." This is clearly above potential language. Bright-line rule: must be > 50. The language "at or above" with "above-trend growth" suggests modestly above potential. Score 53-56.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor market had continued to strengthen." Unemployment 4.2%, "falling further below participants' estimates of its longer-run normal level." "Many participants judged that the economy was operating at or above full employment." "Some Districts pointed to difficulty attracting and retaining labor" but "other Districts suggested that workers with the requisite skills remained reasonably available." Wage growth "generally seen as modest." This is tight but not severely tight -- full employment with mixed tightness signals. Score 55-58.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Participants expected solid growth in consumer spending." "Robust gains in consumer spending in September." "Participants anticipated appreciable increases in business fixed investment." "Reports on business spending from District contacts were generally upbeat." "Several participants reported that business contacts appeared to be more confident." Activity rising "at a solid rate." This is above-trend but not booming. Score 57-60.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Participants generally judged that financial conditions remained accommodative despite the recent increases in the exchange value of the dollar and Treasury yields." "Elevated asset valuations and low financial market volatility." "Still very accommodative financial conditions." Clearly loose/accommodative. Score 25-30.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All held rates. "Nearly all participants reaffirmed the view that a gradual approach to increasing the target range was likely." "Many participants thought that another increase in the target range for the federal funds rate was likely to be warranted in the near term." But "several participants indicated that their decision about whether to increase the target range in the near term would depend importantly on whether the upcoming economic data boosted their confidence that inflation was headed toward the Committee's objective." "A few other participants thought that additional policy firming should be deferred." The center of gravity is leaning toward another hike soon (December), but with notable inflation caution. This is a gradual hiking stance -- lean hawkish but with hesitation from some. Score 62-66.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissents at this meeting (unanimous hold). But there are clear camps: "many" favor near-term hike vs. "several" want to see inflation data first vs. "a few" want to defer. Some worried about upside inflation risks vs. many worried about downside inflation. This is moderate disagreement without formal dissent. Score 30-38.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: "Several participants expressed concerns about a potential buildup of financial imbalances." Discussion of elevated asset valuations, low volatility, potential for sharp reversal. But also counterbalanced by noting stronger capital positions. Balance sheet normalization mentioned briefly. This is a limited but meaningful discussion. Score 30-38.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Many participants thought that another increase in the target range for the federal funds rate was likely to be warranted in the near term." "Members reaffirmed their expectation that economic conditions would evolve in a manner that would warrant gradual increases in the federal funds rate." "Federal funds rate was likely to remain, for some time, below levels that are expected to prevail in the longer run." This is a clear signal toward further gradual tightening, with a near-term hike likely (December 2017). Score 67-72.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Near-term risks to the economic outlook appeared to be roughly balanced." Hurricane disruptions "made it more difficult than usual to interpret some of this information." Inflation puzzlement is a key theme -- uncertainty about why inflation is soft. "Several participants pointed to a diminished responsiveness of inflation to resource utilization." Discussion of alternative monetary frameworks. But no "considerable uncertainty" or "unprecedented" language. Somewhat elevated uncertainty, mainly around inflation dynamics. Score 35-42.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (54) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (56): Both modestly above 50, consistent with economy at/above full employment. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (64) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (69): Both moderately hawkish, with forward guidance slightly more hawkish (signaling December hike). Coherent.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE low (35) but HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN moderately hawkish (64): This tension is real in the text -- participants are hiking despite low inflation, based on labor market tightness and expectation that inflation will return. The minutes explicitly document this tension.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 35 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "On a 12-month basis, PCE price inflation in September, at 1.6 percent, remained below the Committee's longer-run objective; core PCE price inflation...was only 1.3 percent."
- "Many participants judged that much of the recent softness in core inflation reflected temporary or idiosyncratic factors."
- "Many participants observed that there was some likelihood that inflation might remain below 2 percent for longer than they currently expected."
- "Several participants expressed concern that the persistently weak inflation data could lead to a decline in longer-term inflation expectations."

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants see inflation as clearly below the 2% target, with debate about whether the weakness is transitory or persistent, and notable concern about possible downward drift in expectations.

**Minority views**: "Most participants continued to think that the cyclical pressures associated with a tightening labor market were likely to show through to higher inflation over the medium term" -- this provides some upward pull but does not change the current assessment. "Some other participants" noted upside risks from full employment.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 54 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Many participants judged that the economy was operating at or above full employment."
- "Participants saw the incoming information on spending and the labor market as consistent with continued above-trend economic growth."
- "GDP was expected to grow at a pace exceeding that of potential output."
- Unemployment at 4.2%, "falling further below participants' estimates of its longer-run normal level."

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy modestly above potential -- "at or above full employment" with above-trend growth, but not running hot. The "at or above" qualifier suggests the center sees it as right around or slightly above.

**Minority views**: Uncertainty band reflects the "at or above" language -- some see it merely at potential, others clearly above.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 56 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The unemployment rate ticked down to 4.2 percent in September, falling further below participants' estimates of its longer-run normal level."
- "Many participants judged that the economy was operating at or above full employment and anticipated that the labor market would tighten somewhat further."
- "Reports from some Districts pointed to difficulty attracting and retaining labor, but anecdotal information from other Districts suggested that workers with the requisite skills remained reasonably available."
- "Overall, wage increases were generally seen as modest."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is tight -- at or slightly beyond full employment -- but the tightness is moderate, as evidenced by only modest wage gains and mixed reports of labor shortages.

**Minority views**: "A few participants interpreted recent data...as indicating some firming in wage growth; a few others, however, judged wage growth to have been little changed."

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 58 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants expected solid growth in consumer spending in the near term, supported by ongoing strength in the labor market, improved household balance sheets, and a high level of consumer sentiment."
- "Robust gains in consumer spending in September."
- "Participants anticipated appreciable increases in business fixed investment."
- "Reports on business spending from District contacts were generally upbeat."

**Center-of-gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment are characterized as solid to robust, above trend but not booming. "Solid rate" and "appreciable increases" push this moderately above 50.

**Minority views**: Minimal -- broad agreement that spending and investment are healthy. Some noted potential additional boost from tax legislation.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 27 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants generally judged that financial conditions remained accommodative despite the recent increases in the exchange value of the dollar and Treasury yields."
- "Elevated asset valuations and low financial market volatility."
- "Still very accommodative financial conditions" (from policy discussion).

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly accommodative/loose. "Generally" indicates broad agreement. The word "accommodative" is used multiple times, and "very accommodative" appears.

**Minority views**: None dissented on this characterization. The uncertainty band reflects the note that dollar and Treasury yields had risen somewhat.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 64 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All participants thought that it would be appropriate to maintain the current target range for the federal funds rate at this meeting."
- "Many participants thought that another increase in the target range for the federal funds rate was likely to be warranted in the near term if incoming information left the medium-term outlook broadly unchanged."
- "Several participants indicated that their decision...would depend importantly on whether the upcoming economic data boosted their confidence that inflation was headed toward the Committee's objective."
- "A few other participants thought that additional policy firming should be deferred until incoming information confirmed that inflation was clearly on a path toward the Committee's symmetric 2 percent objective."

**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate hawkish lean -- majority ready for another hike soon (December) but with notable caution from a meaningful minority focused on inflation shortfall. The hold-now-but-hike-soon stance places this in the 60-65 range.

**Minority views**: "Some other participants were concerned about upside risks to inflation" (more hawkish wing); "a few...thought that additional policy firming should be deferred" (more dovish wing). The 8-point uncertainty band captures this spread.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 35 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Unanimous hold decision (no formal dissents).
- Clear debate camps: "many" favor near-term hike vs. "several" conditional vs. "a few" favor deferral.
- "Some other participants were concerned about upside risks to inflation...They cautioned that waiting too long to remove accommodation...could result in a substantial overshoot" vs. "a number of these participants were worried that a decline in longer-term inflation expectations would make it more challenging."
- "A couple of participants discussed the possibility that potential alternative frameworks for the conduct of monetary policy could be helpful."

**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate differences in views, especially about inflation dynamics and the appropriate pace of further tightening. No formal dissent, but meaningful disagreement about the forward path and the interpretation of soft inflation data.

**Minority views**: The alternative-framework discussion by a couple of participants signals deeper conceptual divergence, but only involved a small number.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 33 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "In light of elevated asset valuations and low financial market volatility, several participants expressed concerns about a potential buildup of financial imbalances."
- "They worried that a sharp reversal in asset prices could have damaging effects on the economy."
- "It was noted, however, that elevated asset prices could be partly explained by a low neutral rate of interest."
- Balance sheet normalization mentioned but briefly ("limited reaction in financial markets").

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability received limited but meaningful discussion. "Several" participants raised concerns about asset valuations and potential imbalances, but it was not a dominant theme. About one paragraph of substantive discussion.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 69 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Many participants thought that another increase in the target range for the federal funds rate was likely to be warranted in the near term."
- "Members reaffirmed their expectation that economic conditions would evolve in a manner that would warrant gradual increases in the federal funds rate."
- "Several other members...were reasonably confident that the economy and inflation would evolve in coming months such that an additional firming would likely be appropriate in the near term."
- "Federal funds rate was likely to remain, for some time, below levels that are expected to prevail in the longer run."

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear forward signal toward further gradual tightening, with a near-term hike strongly expected. The "gradual increases" language and near-term hike signal place this solidly in the mild-to-moderate tightening bias range.

**Minority views**: "A few other participants thought that additional policy firming should be deferred" and "a few participants cautioned that further increases...while inflation remained persistently below 2 percent could unduly depress inflation expectations."

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 38 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Near-term risks to the economic outlook appeared to be roughly balanced."
- Hurricane disruptions "made it more difficult than usual to interpret some of this information."
- "Several participants pointed to a diminished responsiveness of inflation to resource utilization" -- suggesting standard models may not apply.
- "A couple of participants discussed the possibility that potential alternative frameworks for the conduct of monetary policy could be helpful" -- questioning the framework itself.

**Center-of-gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty, concentrated around inflation dynamics (why inflation is soft despite tight labor markets). Growth and employment outlook are relatively clear, but inflation puzzlement adds uncertainty. Risks characterized as "roughly balanced" keeps this from scoring higher.
