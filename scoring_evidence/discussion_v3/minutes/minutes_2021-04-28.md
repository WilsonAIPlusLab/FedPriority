# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2021-04-28 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Segmented -- participant views section is clearly separated from staff review.

**Era context**: April 2021. The economy is recovering from the COVID-19 pandemic. Vaccines are rolling out rapidly. The Fed is maintaining zero rates and $120B/month asset purchases. Inflation is beginning to rise but still mostly attributed to transitory factors. Labor market improving but far from pre-pandemic levels.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all" (unanimous on rate decision, AIT framework), "many" (spending factors, hiring difficulties, labor participation, inflation expectations anchored, communicating taper), "several" (housing, asset valuations), "some" (uneven recovery, upside/downside risks, inflation upside risks, outcome-based guidance), "a few" (debt vulnerabilities, hedge fund risks), "a couple" (inflation pressures building, agricultural sector, forbearance masking vulnerabilities), "a number of" (supply chain bottlenecks beyond this year, taper discussion).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as moving above 2% in near term due to base effects, energy prices, demand surge, and supply bottlenecks. However, "after the transitory effects of these factors fade, participants generally expected measured inflation to ease." "A number of participants remarked that supply chain bottlenecks and input shortages may not be resolved quickly." The center of gravity treats current inflation as temporarily above target but not alarming. "Many participants commented that various measures of longer-term inflation expectations remained well anchored." This is modestly above target with transitory framing but real current pressures -- I score the current perception of elevated inflation per the rubric's gotcha. Direction: above midpoint. Score ~58. The "transitory" language doesn't reduce it -- they perceive prices running "somewhat above 2 percent."

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Participants generally noted that the economy remained far from the Committee's maximum-employment and price-stability goals." "Economic activity had picked up sharply this year, with robust gains." The economy is recovering fast but still below potential -- "far from" goals. Bright-line rule: below potential language -> must be < 50. The sharp recovery suggests narrowing gap. Score ~40.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 6.0%, payroll employment 8.4 million below pre-pandemic. "Economy was far from achieving the Committee's broad-based and inclusive maximum-employment goal." BUT "many participants also remarked that business contacts in their Districts reported having trouble hiring workers" -- showing tightness pockets despite overall slack. Level is still well below full employment despite hiring difficulties. Score ~35. The hiring difficulties create upward uncertainty.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Indicators of consumer spending surged in March." "Business equipment investment continued to rise at a robust pace and manufacturing activity was expanding at a strong clip." "Robust gains in consumer spending, housing-sector activity, business equipment investment, and manufacturing production." This is clearly above trend -- strong momentum. Score ~62.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Overall financial conditions remained accommodative." "Various participants commented on the prolonged period of low interest rates and highly accommodative financial market conditions." Capital market financing "highly accommodative," bank lending easing. Very loose conditions. Score ~18.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All members agreed to maintain zero rates and $120B/month purchases. "Current stance of policy and policy guidance remained appropriate." "A number of participants suggested that if the economy continued to make rapid progress toward the Committee's goals, it might be appropriate at some point in upcoming meetings to begin discussing a plan for adjusting the pace of asset purchases." This is early taper talk but still firmly in accommodation mode. The center of gravity is maintaining max accommodation with nascent forward lean toward eventual taper discussion. Score ~25 -- strong dovish, maintaining QE, but beginning to hint at future adjustments.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: All members agreed on rate and purchase decisions. No dissents. But "a couple of participants commented on the risks of inflation pressures building up to unwelcome levels before they become sufficiently evident to induce a policy reaction" -- minority concern. "A number of participants suggested" beginning taper discussion. Moderate range of views on taper timing but broad agreement on current action. Score ~22.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Substantial paragraph dedicated to financial stability. "Participants who commented on financial stability" discussed: elevated equity valuations, IPO activity, corporate bond spreads at bottom of historical distribution, housing valuation pressures, hedge fund risks, run-prone investment funds, reach-for-yield behavior. Multiple specific concerns raised by "several," "some," "a few," "various" participants. This is moderate-to-prominent discussion. Score ~48.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Outcome-based guidance maintained -- rates stay until maximum employment reached and inflation at 2% and on track to exceed. Asset purchases continue until "substantial further progress." "Various participants noted that it would likely be some time until the economy had made substantial further progress." But "a number of participants suggested... it might be appropriate at some point in upcoming meetings to begin discussing a plan for adjusting the pace of asset purchases." Forward signal is still dovish (accommodation continuing) but with early hints of taper. Score ~20 -- easing bias with data dependence, early normalization whispers.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants judged that uncertainty was elevated and that the outlook was highly dependent on the course of the virus." "Risks to the outlook were no longer as elevated as in previous months." Elevated but declining uncertainty. Score ~52.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (40) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (35): Both below 50, consistent -- economy below potential with slack in labor market. Labor slightly lower because 8.4M jobs gap is large.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (25) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (20): Both dovish, consistent. Forward guidance slightly more dovish because it reflects the maintained commitment language, while current lean captures the nascent taper discussion nudging it slightly less dovish.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 58: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says above 2%, supply bottlenecks. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP 40: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says "far from goals," below potential. Below 50. Matches.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS 35: Higher = tighter. Evidence says far from max employment, 6% unemployment, 8.4M jobs gap. Below 50. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS 18: Higher = tighter. Evidence says "highly accommodative." Very low score. Matches.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 25: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says maintaining QE and zero rates. Low score. Matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 58 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants also noted that the expected surge in demand as the economy reopens further, along with some transitory supply chain bottlenecks, would contribute to PCE price inflation temporarily running somewhat above 2 percent."
- "A number of participants remarked that supply chain bottlenecks and input shortages may not be resolved quickly and, if so, these factors could put upward pressure on prices beyond this year."
- "Many participants commented that various measures of longer-term inflation expectations remained well anchored."

**Center of gravity**: Participants perceive inflation moving modestly above 2% from base effects, energy, demand, and supply disruptions, with most viewing it as transitory but some flagging persistence risks.

**Minority views**: "A couple of participants commented on the risks of inflation pressures building up to unwelcome levels before they become sufficiently evident to induce a policy reaction." This widens the band upward.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 40 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants generally noted that the economy remained far from the Committee's maximum-employment and price-stability goals."
- "Participants observed that economic activity had picked up sharply this year, with robust gains in consumer spending, housing-sector activity, business equipment investment, and manufacturing production."

**Center of gravity**: Economy is below potential but recovering rapidly. The "far from goals" language anchors it below 50, while the "picked up sharply" and robust activity narrows the gap from deeper recession levels.

**Minority views**: None significant -- broad agreement on the assessment.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 35 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The economy was far from achieving the Committee's broad-based and inclusive maximum-employment goal. Payroll employment was 8.4 million jobs below its pre-pandemic level."
- "Many participants also remarked that business contacts in their Districts reported having trouble hiring workers, likely reflecting factors such as early retirements, health concerns, childcare responsibilities, and expanded unemployment insurance benefits."

**Center of gravity**: Labor market has significant remaining slack (6% unemployment, 8.4M jobs gap) despite pockets of hiring difficulty driven by pandemic-specific frictions rather than genuine tightness.

**Minority views**: "Some participants noted that the step-up in demand for labor had started to put some upward pressure on wages." This creates upward uncertainty.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Indicators of consumer spending surged in March and expected that further gains in spending would contribute significantly to the economic recovery."
- "Business equipment investment continued to rise at a robust pace and manufacturing activity was expanding at a strong clip."
- "Many participants commented that fiscal stimulus, accommodative financial conditions, the release of pent-up demand, progress on widespread vaccination... were important factors supporting spending."

**Center of gravity**: Spending and investment are clearly above trend, driven by fiscal stimulus, pent-up demand, and reopening. "Surged" and "robust pace" language places this well above the midpoint.

**Minority views**: Supply chain bottlenecks as restraints noted by many, which moderates the score slightly.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 18 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Overall financial conditions remained accommodative, in part reflecting policy measures to support the economy and the flow of credit to U.S. households and businesses."
- "Various participants commented on the prolonged period of low interest rates and highly accommodative financial market conditions and the possibility for these conditions to lead to reach-for-yield behavior."
- "Several participants noted that risk appetite in capital markets was elevated, as equity valuations had risen further, IPO activity remained high, and risk spreads on corporate bonds were at the bottom of their historical distribution."

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are very loose by any measure -- accommodative lending, tight credit spreads, elevated equity valuations, reach-for-yield behavior.

**Minority views**: None -- unanimous view that conditions are accommodative.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 25 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All members agreed to keep the target range for the federal funds rate at 0 to 1/4 percent."
- "Members agreed that it would be appropriate for the Federal Reserve to continue to increase its holdings of Treasury securities by at least $80 billion per month and agency mortgage-backed securities by at least $40 billion per month."
- "A number of participants suggested that if the economy continued to make rapid progress toward the Committee's goals, it might be appropriate at some point in upcoming meetings to begin discussing a plan for adjusting the pace of asset purchases."

**Center of gravity**: Firmly dovish -- maintaining zero rates and full-pace QE. However, the nascent taper discussion signals this is the late stage of maximum accommodation, nudging it above the most dovish readings.

**Minority views**: "A couple of participants commented on the risks of inflation pressures building up to unwelcome levels before they become sufficiently evident to induce a policy reaction" -- mildly hawkish minority.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 22 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- All members agreed on rates and asset purchases -- no dissents.
- "A number of participants suggested... it might be appropriate at some point in upcoming meetings to begin discussing a plan for adjusting the pace of asset purchases" -- minor forward-looking divergence.
- "A couple of participants commented on the risks of inflation pressures building up" -- small minority with different risk assessment.

**Center of gravity**: Broad agreement on current policy with minor differences about timing of future adjustments. No formal dissents, modest variation in risk assessments.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 48 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Several participants noted that risk appetite in capital markets was elevated, as equity valuations had risen further, IPO activity remained high, and risk spreads on corporate bonds were at the bottom of their historical distribution."
- "Some participants mentioned the potential risks to the financial system stemming from the activities of hedge funds and other leveraged investors."
- "Various participants commented on the prolonged period of low interest rates and highly accommodative financial market conditions and the possibility for these conditions to lead to reach-for-yield behavior that could raise financial stability risks."

**Center of gravity**: Moderate and substantive discussion of financial stability across multiple angles (valuations, leverage, reach-for-yield, housing). Multiple participants engaged across "several," "some," "a few," and "various" quantifiers. Not dominant but clearly a meaningful topic.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 20 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "They would aim to achieve inflation moderately above 2 percent for some time so that inflation averages 2 percent over time and longer-term inflation expectations remain well anchored at 2 percent."
- "Various participants noted that it would likely be some time until the economy had made substantial further progress toward the Committee's maximum-employment and price-stability goals."
- "A number of participants suggested that if the economy continued to make rapid progress... it might be appropriate at some point in upcoming meetings to begin discussing a plan for adjusting the pace of asset purchases."

**Center of gravity**: Forward signal is decidedly dovish -- outcome-based guidance commits to accommodation until goals are met, with AIT framework. But early discussion of eventual taper planning lifts it above the most extreme dovish readings.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants judged that uncertainty was elevated and that the outlook was highly dependent on the course of the virus."
- "Risks to the outlook were no longer as elevated as in previous months."
- "Some participants remarked that the pandemic continued to pose downside risks to the economic outlook and noted the potential for an uneven recovery in light of new virus strains and potential hesitancy regarding vaccination."

**Center of gravity**: Uncertainty is elevated but declining from previous highs. "Elevated" and "highly dependent on the course of the virus" indicate above-normal uncertainty, while "no longer as elevated as in previous months" shows improvement.

**Minority views**: Some emphasize upside risks (pent-up demand), some downside (virus variants) -- both directions contribute to elevated uncertainty.
