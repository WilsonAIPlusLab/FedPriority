# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2013-09-18 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Segmented -- participant discussion section is clearly separated from staff content. Uses standard quantifier language throughout.

**Era context**: September 2013 -- the famous "taper tantrum" aftermath meeting. The Fed had been conducting QE3 (open-ended asset purchases since September 2012). Chairman Bernanke had discussed tapering conditions in June 2013, and markets widely expected tapering to begin at this meeting. The Committee surprised markets by NOT tapering. Economy was in moderate recovery, unemployment still elevated (~7.3%), inflation running below 2% target.

**Quantifier distribution**: "participants generally" (broad consensus language used frequently), "a number of" (moderate group), "several" (moderate), "a few" (small minority), "one participant" (individual), "some participants" (moderate group), "many" (large group), "most" (majority -- used for those leaning toward maintaining purchases), "all members but one" (near-consensus on not tapering).

**Dimension-by-dimension reasoning**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW the midpoint. "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's longer-run objective" and "recent readings continued to be below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent." Participants "generally expected inflation to pick up over the coming year... although to a rate still below the Committee's longer-run objective." Wage and price pressures described as "subdued." Members "recognized that inflation persistently below the Committee's 2 percent objective could pose risks to economic performance." This is clearly below-target territory. Direction: below 50. Magnitude: persistent below-target, "subdued" pressures = roughly 15-20 points below midpoint. Score ~32. Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says low/below-target inflation. Score below 50 -- correct.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see economy as BELOW potential. "economic activity had continued to expand at a moderate pace, albeit somewhat more slowly than earlier anticipated." "Slack in resource utilization" mentioned explicitly. Recovery ongoing but headwinds persist: "household-sector deleveraging, tight credit conditions for some households and businesses, and fiscal restraint." GDP growth only moderate. Direction: below 50. The economy is recovering but still has slack. Score ~38. Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says below potential with slack. Below 50 -- correct. Anchor check: 2013 recovery era, anchor 35-42 -- fits.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see labor market as still LOOSE. Unemployment "remained elevated." Gains in payrolls were "disappointing." The unemployment rate decline may "overstate the degree of improvement in broader labor market conditions" due to falling participation rate. "Employment-to-population ratio" still low. Risk of "lasting structural damage" from prolonged elevated unemployment. Direction: below 50. Score ~35. Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says still slack. Below 50 -- correct. Anchor: 2013 era, 35-40 fits.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Below trend. Consumer spending "continued to advance" but retail sales "somewhat weaker than expected." Auto sales strong. Restraints include "declines in consumer confidence, concerns about job security." Business contacts "cautiously optimistic" but "quite cautious about expanding their workforces." Firms focused on "improving their balance sheets" rather than expanding. Manufacturing showed "some rebound." Direction: below trend but not contracting. Score ~40. Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Evidence says moderate/sluggish. Below 50 -- correct. Anchor: 2011-13 "moderate" = 35-42. Fits.

5. **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: This is a major discussion topic. "Several participants judged that overall financial conditions had tightened notably over the past few months, as seen most importantly in the rise in mortgage rates." However, "a few others observed that the increase in longer-term yields... had not seemed to leave a meaningful imprint on other asset prices" and pointed to "higher equity prices, the further gradual loosening of terms in bank lending, and the continued availability of credit at inexpensive terms in corporate debt markets as signs that financial conditions more generally had not tightened materially." There's genuine disagreement. Center of gravity: somewhat tightened from very accommodative levels. Mortgage rates up significantly, but broader credit conditions still relatively supportive. Score ~48-50. The conditions were tightening FROM very loose levels, so the level is still not restrictive -- it moved from very loose toward neutral. I'll score 47. Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence shows mixed but some tightening from loose levels. 47 (roughly neutral, slightly loose side) seems right. Uncertainty band should be wide given the disagreement.

6. **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: This is the taper debate meeting. "All members but one judged that it would be appropriate for the Committee to await more evidence... before adjusting the pace of asset purchases." The Committee decided NOT to taper. The decision was to maintain QE3 at its current pace. This is firmly in accommodative/dovish territory -- continuing large-scale asset purchases. However, there was serious discussion about beginning to taper, and "the participants who spoke in favor of moderating the pace" presented a credible case. The center of gravity is: maintain maximum accommodation for now, but tapering is on the near horizon. During QE, the rubric says 15-25 range for "how much more to buy" debates. This meeting is about whether to START reducing purchases -- that's a step toward less dovish. Score ~28. Direction: dovish. The Committee actively chose to continue QE purchases. But the debate itself shows they're moving toward tapering. Anchor: 2014 taper era = 8-15. But this is pre-taper, and most wanted to keep buying. I'll score 25 -- moderate dovish, majority favors maintaining max accommodation but tapering is actively debated. Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says dovish (continuing QE). Below 50 -- correct.

7. **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All members but one" on the policy decision -- so 1 dissent favoring tapering. But the discussion reveals substantial debate: "participants debated the advantages and disadvantages of reducing the pace of... asset purchases." There were clear camps -- those wanting to maintain and those wanting to taper. The taper advocates made strong arguments about credibility and communications. Also disagreement on financial conditions assessment (several vs a few). Score ~40. 1 formal dissent + substantial debate with distinct camps but ultimately near-consensus decision. Anchor: 25-35 typical one-dissent. But the intensity of the debate pushes it higher. Score 38.

8. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Significant discussion. "Participants also touched on the implications for financial stability resulting from the increase in interest rates, focusing on the effects on securities held by banking and other nonbank institutions, the unwinding of leveraged trades, and the liquidity and functioning of a number of fixed-income markets." Discussion of community/regional bank risk exposures, municipal bond markets, EME financial conditions, and "complexities related to the interaction between the stance of monetary policy and the vulnerabilities in the financial system." This is a meaningful paragraph of discussion plus the broader financial conditions discussion. Score ~38. Anchor: 2013-19 typical = 20-35. This is on the higher end given the taper tantrum context. Score 35.

9. **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is dovish/easing. Participants discussed "clarifying or strengthening the Committee's forward guidance for the federal funds rate" -- including adding an inflation floor to the threshold guidance, providing more info about post-threshold intentions. "A couple of participants also remarked that they viewed their projections of a low federal funds rate in 2016 as reflecting a commitment to support the economy by maintaining a more accommodative policy for longer." The 6.5% unemployment threshold was still in place. The forward signal is: rates low for long, potentially strengthening easing guidance. Score ~12. Anchor: 2012-13 Evans Rule/QE3 thresholds = 3-8. But this is about potentially strengthening already-existing threshold guidance, not the initial launch. Score 12. Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence says easing forward guidance, low for long. Below 50 -- correct.

10. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Elevated. "A number of significant risks remained," "heightened uncertainty about the course of federal fiscal policy," "near-term risks and uncertainties," "ambiguous cast of recent readings," "considerable risks surrounding fiscal policy," fiscal debates including potential government shutdown and debt ceiling. Also uncertainty about effects of rate rises on housing. But this is 2013 -- not crisis-level uncertainty. Score ~45. "Considerable risks" and "heightened uncertainty" and "ambiguous" readings push this above baseline. Anchor: 2013-14 stable expansion = 25-35. But this meeting has more uncertainty language than typical 2013. Score 45.

**Cross-dimension coherence check**:
- OUTPUT_GAP (38) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (35): Both below 50, consistent -- economy below potential with labor slack. Close together. Good.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (25) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (12): Both dovish. Forward guidance more dovish because it's about threshold-based commitments and "low for long" while current policy lean includes active taper debate. Divergence explained: current action debate (taper vs not) is less dovish than the forward rate guidance (low for long, strengthening thresholds). This makes sense.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's longer-run objective, apart from fluctuations that largely reflected changes in energy prices"
- "recent readings continued to be below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent"
- "wage and price pressures were subdued"
- "inflation persistently below the Committee's 2 percent objective could pose risks to economic performance"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants broadly agreed that inflation was running persistently below the 2% target with subdued price and wage pressures, though they expected a gradual pickup over the medium term.

**Minority views**: "in one District, contacts pointed to rising wage pressures and labor shortages" -- a single-district outlier that does not shift the center of gravity.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "economic activity had continued to expand at a moderate pace, albeit somewhat more slowly than earlier anticipated"
- "slack in resource utilization" mentioned as keeping inflation low
- Headwinds cited: "household-sector deleveraging, tight credit conditions for some households and businesses, and fiscal restraint"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy was recovering at a moderate pace but remained below potential, with slack in resource utilization and multiple headwinds persisting.

**Minority views**: Some noted "growing underlying strength in the broader economy" suggesting the gap was narrowing, but no one suggested the economy was at or above potential.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the unemployment rate remained elevated"
- "gains in payrolls in the July and August employment reports were disappointing"
- "the continued low readings on the employment-to-population ratio... suggesting that overall labor market conditions had not improved as much as the unemployment rate would indicate"
- Concern about "lasting structural damage" from prolonged elevated unemployment

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Labor market remained significantly below full employment with elevated unemployment, disappointing payrolls, and low employment-to-population ratios, though conditions had improved meaningfully since QE3's inception.

**Minority views**: None pushing toward tightness; the debate was about how much improvement had occurred, not whether the market was tight.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 40 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "consumer spending continued to advance, but incoming data on retail sales were somewhat weaker than expected"
- "firms focused on improving their balance sheets and enhancing productivity and still quite cautious about expanding their workforces"
- "Business contacts in selected parts of the country were reported to be cautiously optimistic"
- Housing sector "continued to strengthen" but mortgage rate increases posed a risk

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumer spending and business investment were advancing but below trend, with weaker-than-expected retail sales, cautious business behavior, and headwinds from higher mortgage rates and fiscal drag.

**Minority views**: Auto sales remained strong; manufacturing showed "some rebound" -- pockets of strength within general sluggishness.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 47 +/-10 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Several participants judged that overall financial conditions had tightened notably over the past few months, as seen most importantly in the rise in mortgage rates"
- "a few others observed that the increase in longer-term yields in recent months had not seemed to leave a meaningful imprint on other asset prices"
- Those same participants pointed to "higher equity prices, the further gradual loosening of terms in bank lending, and the continued availability of credit at inexpensive terms in corporate debt markets as signs that financial conditions more generally had not tightened materially"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions had tightened from very accommodative levels due to the rise in mortgage and longer-term rates, but broader credit conditions remained supportive, leaving the net assessment near neutral with wide disagreement.

**Minority views**: Clear split -- "several" saw notable tightening vs. "a few" who saw conditions as not materially tighter. Wide uncertainty band reflects this.

**Score: 25 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "all members but one judged that it would be appropriate for the Committee to await more evidence that progress would be sustained before adjusting the pace of asset purchases"
- "a number of participants thought that risk-management considerations called for a cautious approach"
- "The participants who spoke in favor of moderating the pace of securities purchases at this meeting... viewed those data as broadly consistent with the Committee's outlook"
- "Most of the participants leaning toward a downward adjustment... favored a relatively small reduction"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The strong majority favored maintaining the full pace of asset purchases, reflecting a moderately dovish stance; however, a significant minority advocated for beginning to taper, and the overall trajectory was clearly toward eventual reduction.

**Minority views**: One formal dissent favoring tapering, plus a substantial group of participants who advocated beginning to reduce purchases at this meeting. The uncertainty band captures their influence.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: One formal dissent combined with vigorous two-camp debate about tapering and disagreements about financial conditions creates moderate disagreement intensity, above a typical one-dissent meeting.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward guidance signal was strongly dovish, with threshold-based guidance still active and active discussion of strengthening it further by adding an inflation floor, signaling extended accommodation.

**Minority views**: No one advocated for hawkish forward guidance; the debate was about how to strengthen easing guidance.

- "the ambiguous cast of recent readings on the economy"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants expressed elevated uncertainty driven by fiscal policy risks (government shutdown, debt ceiling), ambiguous economic data, and unknown effects of the rise in interest rates, above standard caveats but not extreme.

**Minority views**: "one participant noted that the effects of the federal sequestration appeared to be less pronounced than previously anticipated" -- a minority downplaying fiscal risk.
