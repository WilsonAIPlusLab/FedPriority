# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1996-11-13

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Unsegmented pre-2009 format -- staff and participant content are mixed, but participant language cues are clear ("members commented," "members emphasized," "several members," etc.). The directive/vote section at the end is formal boilerplate.

**Era context**: November 1996. The US economy is in a mid-cycle expansion. GDP growth had been strong earlier in 1996 but slowed in Q3. Unemployment is at 5.2%. Inflation has been generally contained despite tight labor markets. The fed funds rate is at 5.25% (unchanged since January 1996). This is the "Goldilocks" period -- growth near potential, low inflation, tight labor markets but no wage-price spiral.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all the members" (3 times -- on policy hold, asymmetric bias, final vote), "many" (2 times -- moderate expansion, slight inflation downtrend), "several" (3 times -- rebound prospects, risks tilted to inflation, asymmetric bias desirable), "some" (2 times -- overbuilding, favorable inflation factors), "other members" (2 times -- balanced risks, symmetric directive).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint but with upside risks. Key evidence: "no broad evidence of rising price inflation," "many major measures of inflation had exhibited a slight downtrend since 1993," "increases in such costs were still falling short of those that would have been anticipated." However, "several continued to view slightly higher inflation as a likely if not inevitable prospect" and "risks of rising inflation could not be dismissed." The center of gravity is: inflation currently benign/subdued, but labor market tightness creates asymmetric risk. This is a below-target-but-watchful picture. Score should be in the 41-49 range -- slightly below target but not concerning, with upside risk acknowledged. I'll score 44.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "appreciable slowing in the expansion to a sustainable pace close to the economy's potential." "growth near the economy's potential." This is textbook "at potential" language. Bright-line rule: "at potential" / "near potential" = 48-52. Score 50.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "widespread indications of tight labor markets," "tight labor markets and to a somewhat faster rise in labor compensation costs," "comparatively little slack" in labor markets. Unemployment at 5.2%. This is clearly tight. The "tight" language with "little slack" places this in the 53-65 range. Given that wages are rising but less than expected, and the tightness is persistent but not creating severe shortages, I'll score 60.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending was weak in Q3 but members "anticipated a pickup in consumer spending" to "a moderate pace." Business fixed investment "expected to provide further but diminished impetus." Housing "slowing somewhat." Trade deficit widening. The net picture: moderate pace expected, with Q3 weakness seen as temporary. Members "generally concluded that a pickup in the growth of consumer spending to a moderate pace was a likely prospect." This is at-trend to slightly below: 48.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "generally supportive financial conditions," "continued availability of debt and equity financing on favorable terms," "marked increases that had occurred in the value of stock holdings," "still-ample availability of credit to most households." BUT also "generally tightening credit standards for consumer loans" and consumer indebtedness straining liquidity. On net, conditions are somewhat loose to neutral. Score 35 -- "somewhat loose to neutral; generally supportive."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "all the members indicated that they could support an unchanged policy stance and the retention of a bias toward restraint." Unanimous hold with asymmetric tightening bias. Several wanted the bias because risks "tilted toward rising inflation." Others thought symmetric might be more appropriate but agreed shift would be "premature." No one advocated easing. This is a hold with mild hawkish lean. Score 55 -- lean hawkish, hold steady with tightening bias.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Unanimous vote (12-0). Some difference of opinion on whether directive should be symmetric vs asymmetric, but all agreed on asymmetric. "views differed to some extent regarding the most likely course for inflation." Low disagreement -- broad agreement with minor emphasis differences. Score 20.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Brief mentions of stock market valuations ("possibility of a correction in the stock market"), credit availability, consumer debt levels. These are mentioned as factors in the economic outlook, not as financial stability concerns per se. Very limited financial stability focus -- typical of the mid-1990s. Score 12.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The asymmetric directive tilted toward tightening signals a mild hawkish forward lean. "somewhat greater reserve restraint would be acceptable." But the dominant message is "pause and observe." The forward signal is mildly hawkish -- hold but ready to tighten. Score 57.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The outlook remained subject, as usual, to considerable uncertainty." "the outlook was far from certain." Fiscal policy outlook described as having "considerable uncertainty." Inventory behavior "subject to substantial uncertainties." This is somewhat elevated uncertainty language -- "considerable" and "far from certain" -- but partly rhetorical ("as usual"). Score 40.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (50) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (60): Labor is tighter than output gap suggests. This is coherent because the text explicitly notes labor markets have "comparatively little slack in relation to other producer resources" -- labor is the binding constraint even at potential output.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (55) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (57): Both mildly hawkish, consistent. The hold-with-tightening-bias posture matches.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says benign but with upside risk. 44 (slightly below midpoint) matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says "supportive" and "favorable terms" = loose. 35 matches.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Asymmetric bias toward tightening. 57 matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 44 +/-7 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "no broad evidence of rising price inflation"; "many major measures of inflation had exhibited a slight downtrend since 1993"; "increases in such costs were still falling short of those that would have been anticipated on the basis of historical experience"; "several continued to view slightly higher inflation as a likely if not inevitable prospect"
- **Center of gravity**: Current inflation is benign and below historical expectations given labor tightness, but members see asymmetric upside risk. The net assessment is slightly below neutral.
- **Minority views**: Several members see upside risk as material, tilting toward higher inflation eventually. This widens the band modestly.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 50 +/-5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "appreciable slowing in the expansion to a sustainable pace close to the economy's potential"; "growth near the economy's potential as they anticipated"
- **Center of gravity**: Economy is operating at or very near potential. Text uses "close to" and "near" potential repeatedly.
- **Minority views**: None diverge meaningfully on this dimension.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "widespread indications of tight labor markets"; "comparatively little slack in relation to other producer resources"; "tight labor markets and to a somewhat faster rise in labor compensation costs this year"
- **Center of gravity**: Labor markets are clearly tight with little slack, but wage pressures are surprisingly muted relative to historical patterns.
- **Minority views**: No disagreement that labor markets are tight; the debate is about whether this will generate inflation.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 48 +/-6 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "a pickup in the growth of consumer spending to a moderate pace was a likely prospect"; "Business fixed investment was expected to provide further but diminished impetus"; residential building "slowing somewhat"
- **Center of gravity**: After a weak Q3, members expect a return to moderate spending growth. Investment still supportive but slowing. Net picture is approximately at trend.
- **Minority views**: Some emphasis on consumer debt as a downside risk vs. stock wealth and sentiment as upside.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 35 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "generally supportive financial conditions"; "continued availability of debt and equity financing on favorable terms"; "still-ample availability of credit to most households"; "generally tightening credit standards for consumer loans"
- **Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are broadly supportive/accommodative, though some tightening in consumer credit standards noted.
- **Minority views**: The consumer credit tightening is a partial offset to the broadly loose conditions.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 55 +/-5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "all the members indicated that they could support an unchanged policy stance and the retention of a bias toward restraint"; "several viewed such a bias as desirable because they continued to believe that the risks remained tilted...toward rising inflation"; vote was unanimous 12-0 for hold with asymmetric tightening bias
- **Center of gravity**: Unanimous hold with tightening bias. The lean is mildly hawkish -- no one favored easing, and the dominant concern was inflation risk.
- **Minority views**: Some members thought a symmetric directive was more justified but agreed asymmetric was prudent.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 20 +/-5 [High confidence]
- **Evidence**: "Votes for this action: [12 members]. Vote against this action: None."; "views differed to some extent regarding the most likely course for inflation" but all agreed on action and directive
- **Center of gravity**: Near-consensus on both the decision and the directive. Minor differences in inflation outlook assessment but no operational disagreement.
- **Minority views**: The symmetric-vs-asymmetric debate was resolved without dissent.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 12 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: Brief mentions of "possibility of a correction in the stock market"; "marked increases that had occurred in the value of stock holdings"; consumer debt strain -- all in context of economic outlook, not financial stability per se
- **Center of gravity**: Financial stability is not a focus. References to markets and credit are embedded in consumption/investment assessment, not as standalone stability concerns.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 57 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "somewhat greater reserve restraint would be acceptable and slightly lesser reserve restraint might be acceptable" (asymmetric toward tightening); "a desirable opportunity to pause and observe further developments"
- **Center of gravity**: Mildly hawkish forward lean. The asymmetric directive signals readiness to tighten, but the dominant message is patience.
- **Minority views**: Some members would have preferred symmetric (neutral) forward posture.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 40 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
- **Evidence**: "The outlook remained subject, as usual, to considerable uncertainty"; "the outlook was far from certain"; "range of potential developments was especially wide" (re fiscal policy); "prospective behavior of inventories remained subject to substantial uncertainties"
- **Center of gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty. Multiple sources of uncertainty cited (fiscal, inventories, inflation trajectory), but partially discounted by "as usual" framing.
- **Minority views**: None -- uncertainty language is distributed across the discussion.
