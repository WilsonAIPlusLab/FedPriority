# FOMC Scoring: 2020-06-10 Meeting (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is **minutes** (synthesized summary of participant views). It uses quantifier language: "all," "participants agreed," "most," "many," "several," "some," "a few," "a couple," "a number of."

### Era Context
June 2020: Three months into the COVID-19 pandemic. The economy is in the deepest part of the contraction -- "largest decline in economic activity in post-WWII history." Unemployment at 13.3% (understated). The Fed cut rates to the ELB in March 2020 and launched massive asset purchases and lending facilities. This meeting features an extended discussion of forward guidance tools, YCT policies, and asset purchases. The economy is showing very early signs of stabilization but remains deeply depressed.

### Quantifier Distribution
- "all/participants agreed": used frequently for broad consensus on severity, ELB, policy stance
- "most": forward guidance communication
- "many": clarifying intentions, framework review
- "several": low drilling activity, lending facilities
- "some": inflation expectations risk, business challenges, rehiring difficulties
- "a few": calendar-based guidance, questioning desirability of large-scale asset purchases
- "a couple": preference for unemployment-rate-tied guidance, financial stability risks from low rates, YCT design

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as well below target. "12-month PCE inflation measure would likely run well below the Committee's 2 percent objective for some time." "The negative effect from the pandemic on aggregate demand was likely to more than offset any upward pressure from supply constraints so that the overall effect of the outbreak on prices was seen as disinflationary." "Inflation had been running somewhat below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective before the coronavirus outbreak." This is clearly in the 15-25 range -- meaningful disinflation with some risk of expectations deteriorating. Direction: well below midpoint. Score ~20.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy far below potential. "The largest decline in economic activity in post-WWII history." "Sharp declines in economic activity." "Nearly 20 million jobs had been lost." There are very early signs of stabilization ("April could turn out to be the trough") but the economy remains deeply depressed. The anchor for COVID Q2 2020 is 15-22. Score ~18.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Extremely loose labor market. Unemployment at 13.3% (understated by ~3 percentage points). "Nearly 20 million jobs had been lost, on net, since February." "Historically elevated" unemployment claims. "A full recovery in employment would take some time." The anchor for 2020 Q2 is 15-22. Score ~17.

Cross-check: OUTPUT_GAP (18) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (17) are closely aligned -- both deeply depressed. Coherent.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "may be stabilizing or rebounding modestly" from very low levels. "Real PCE slumped in April." Expected to "grow strongly in the second half" but from "very low levels." Business investment "continued to tumble." Capital expenditures restrained. Energy sector in difficulty. This is a picture of spending that has collapsed and is showing only the earliest hints of recovery. Anchor: 15-20 for 2020 Q2. Score ~20.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Financial conditions have improved substantially from March crisis levels but remain strained. "Financial conditions had improved, in part reflecting policy measures." "Strains in some financial markets" have eased. "Risk spreads across a range of fixed-income markets had narrowed." But "conditions remained moderately strained for lower-rated borrowers." "Financing conditions for small businesses tightened." "Liquidity in the municipal bond markets was still below pre-pandemic levels." Overall: conditions have moved from crisis-level tight to somewhat tight -- still above neutral. Anchor: March 2020 was 85-95; by June conditions have eased considerably. Score ~60.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Deeply dovish. "All participants judged that it would be appropriate to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 0 to 1/4 percent." Active discussion of forward guidance, asset purchases, and YCT as additional easing tools. "The economy is likely to need support from highly accommodative monetary policy for some time." "A highly accommodative stance of monetary policy would likely be needed for some time." The discussion is about how to provide MORE accommodation, not less. Only "a couple" mentioned financial stability risks from prolonged low rates. Anchor: 2-5 for March 2020 emergency; by June the acute emergency has slightly passed but the stance is full accommodation plus discussing additional tools. Score ~12.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Very dovish forward signal. "Most participants commented that the Committee should communicate a more explicit form of forward guidance." Discussion of outcome-based guidance tied to inflation or unemployment thresholds. "A number of participants judged that it was important for forward guidance and asset purchases to be structured in a way that provides the accommodation necessary to support rapid economic recovery." Rates expected at ELB "for at least the next couple of years." The signal is strongly dovish -- they are actively planning how to commit to extended accommodation. Anchor: 2-10 for 2020 emergency. Score ~8.

Cross-check: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (12) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (8) both deeply dovish. Forward guidance is slightly more dovish because the discussion is specifically about creating stronger commitment devices for prolonged accommodation. Coherent.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Broad consensus on the dire economic situation and need for accommodation. "All participants" agreed on many key points. The main differences are tactical: what form of forward guidance (outcome-based vs. calendar-based), whether YCT is useful, and whether extended low rates pose financial stability risks. "A few" vs. "a couple" on tactical questions. No formal dissents mentioned. This is mostly minor emphasis differences. Score ~20.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Moderate attention. Financial market conditions are discussed at length -- the improvement from March crisis, remaining strains in credit markets, lending facilities. "A couple of participants expressed the concern that policies that effectively committed the Committee to maintaining very low interest rates for a long time could ultimately pose significant risks to financial stability." "A few participants questioned the desirability of large-scale asset purchases" due to "potentially adverse implications for financial stability." Financial stability is mentioned but as a secondary concern, not a dominant topic. The main focus is on the economic collapse and how to provide accommodation. Score ~55.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Extremely high. "There remained an extraordinary amount of uncertainty and considerable risks to the economic outlook." "A great deal of uncertainty." "Substantial likelihood of additional waves of outbreaks." "In some scenarios, could result in further economic disruptions and possibly a protracted period of reduced economic activity." The staff judged "a more pessimistic projection was no less plausible than the baseline forecast." This is very near the top of the scale -- multiple participants emphasizing deep, genuine uncertainty about the pandemic trajectory. Anchor: 85-95 for March 2020. By June the acute panic has receded slightly but uncertainty remains "extraordinary." Score ~82.

### Optional Dimensions

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Some participants noted a risk that long-term inflation expectations might deteriorate." Survey measures "were little changed on balance" and "continued to be near their recent ranges." Discussed by several participants. Expectations anchored but with downside risk. Score ~40.

**CREDIT_STRESS**: Extensively discussed. Spreads narrowed but "remained at levels similar to those in other periods of notable economic or bond market stress." "Financing conditions remained tight for borrowers with relatively low credit scores." "Financing conditions for small businesses tightened." "Credit quality of nonfinancial corporations continued to deteriorate sharply." Defaults projected to increase. Score ~65.

**INFLATION_GOODS/SERVICES/HOUSING**: Not specifically broken out by participants in their discussion. N/A.

**EQUITY_STRESS**: Equity prices rose, implied volatility at 85th percentile -- but this is staff/market reporting, not participant concern about equity stress per se. N/A.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Not discussed as a distinct concern by participants. N/A.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Treasury market functioning "improved substantially since March" but some liquidity issues remain. Discussed in context of asset purchases. Score ~55.

**DOLLAR_CONCERN**: Not discussed by participants. N/A.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: "To the extent that transmission-mitigation procedures adopted by firms reduced their productivity" and "lasting reduction in business investment, the longer-run level of potential output could be reduced." Concern about productivity decline. Score ~38.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 20 +/-5, High confidence
**Evidence**:
- "The negative effect from the pandemic on aggregate demand was likely to more than offset any upward pressure from supply constraints so that the overall effect of the outbreak on prices was seen as disinflationary."
- "They anticipated that the 12-month PCE inflation measure would likely run well below the Committee's 2 percent objective for some time."
- "Some participants noted a risk that long-term inflation expectations might deteriorate."

**Center of gravity**: Participants unanimously see inflation as well below target, driven by the massive demand shock, with disinflationary forces dominant. Risk of further deterioration in expectations noted.
**Minority views**: None pushing the other direction; supply constraints acknowledged but assessed as weaker than demand effects.

---

### OUTPUT_GAP: 18 +/-5, High confidence
**Evidence**:
- "Participants agreed that the data for the second quarter would likely show the largest decline in economic activity in post-World War II history."
- "The coronavirus outbreak was causing tremendous human and economic hardship across the United States and around the world."
- Early signs of stabilization: "consumer expenditures may be stabilizing or rebounding modestly," but "April could turn out to be the trough."

**Center of gravity**: The economy is catastrophically below potential, with only the earliest signs of a possible bottom.
**Minority views**: None; consensus on the severity.

---

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 17 +/-5, High confidence
**Evidence**:
- "Nearly 20 million jobs had been lost, on net, since February."
- "The official unemployment rate for May likely understated the extent of unemployment" (13.3% official, potentially 16%+).
- "A full recovery in employment would take some time."

**Center of gravity**: Labor market in historic collapse. Very early signs of improvement (May payrolls positive surprise) but from an extremely depressed level.
**Minority views**: "Some participants also noted reports of firms stating that they have had some challenges in rehiring employees" -- suggesting pockets of friction but not tightness.

---

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 20 +/-5, High confidence
**Evidence**:
- "Consumer expenditures may be stabilizing or rebounding modestly" from very low levels; "personal consumption expenditures to grow strongly in the second half of the year, albeit from very low levels."
- "Levels of uncertainty and risks perceived by businesses remained high and that these factors continued to contribute to restraints on capital expenditures."
- "Some proportion of businesses would close permanently."

**Center of gravity**: Spending and investment have collapsed; very early signs of stabilization but levels remain far below pre-pandemic.
**Minority views**: None; broad agreement.

---

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 60 +/-8, High confidence
**Evidence**:
- "Financial conditions had improved, in part reflecting policy measures to support the economy."
- "Strains in some financial markets" eased; "risk spreads across a range of fixed-income markets had narrowed."
- "Conditions remained moderately strained for lower-rated borrowers and small businesses."

**Center of gravity**: Conditions have improved significantly from the March crisis but remain somewhat tight, particularly for lower-rated and small borrowers. Well below crisis levels but still above neutral.
**Minority views**: Some emphasis on remaining strains vs. improvement -- reflected in the uncertainty band.

---

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 12 +/-4, High confidence
**Evidence**:
- "All participants judged that it would be appropriate to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 0 to 1/4 percent."
- "Various participants noted that the economy is likely to need support from highly accommodative monetary policy for some time."
- Active discussion of additional accommodation tools (forward guidance, asset purchases, YCT).

**Center of gravity**: Deeply dovish consensus. The entire discussion is about how to provide more and better-communicated accommodation. No hawkish voices.
**Minority views**: "A couple of participants expressed the concern that policies that effectively committed the Committee to maintaining very low interest rates for a long time could ultimately pose significant risks to financial stability" -- but this is a cautionary note, not advocacy for tightening.

---

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 20 +/-5, High confidence
**Evidence**:
- "All participants" agreed on multiple key assessments (severity, ELB, forward guidance tools effective).
- Differences are tactical: "a few" preferred calendar-based guidance vs. "a number of" outcome-based; "a couple" concerned about financial stability risks.
- No formal dissents mentioned.

**Center of gravity**: Broad consensus on both the economic assessment and the policy response. Differences are about implementation details of additional tools, not about direction.
**Minority views**: Minor tactical disagreements about forward guidance design and YCT skepticism.

---

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 55 +/-8, Moderate confidence
**Evidence**:
- Extensive discussion of financial market functioning, lending facilities, credit conditions across multiple market segments.
- "A couple of participants expressed the concern that policies that effectively committed the Committee to maintaining very low interest rates for a long time could ultimately pose significant risks to financial stability."
- "A few participants questioned the desirability of large-scale asset purchases" due to "potentially adverse implications for financial stability."

**Center of gravity**: Financial stability/conditions receive substantial attention as context for policy tools and market functioning, though the primary focus remains on the economic collapse and recovery. A few participants raised longer-term financial stability concerns.
**Minority views**: Most discussion is about supporting markets, not about stability risks per se.

---

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 8 +/-4, High confidence
**Evidence**:
- "Most participants commented that the Committee should communicate a more explicit form of forward guidance for the path of the federal funds rate."
- "Participants generally indicated support for outcome-based forward guidance" tied to inflation/employment thresholds.
- "It was important for forward guidance and asset purchases to be structured in a way that provides the accommodation necessary to support rapid economic recovery."

**Center of gravity**: The forward signal is deeply dovish -- participants are actively designing stronger commitment devices to signal prolonged accommodation. The expected path is at the ELB for years.
**Minority views**: "A couple" favored unemployment-rate-based vs. inflation-based guidance, but all variants are dovish.

---

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 82 +/-6, High confidence
**Evidence**:
- "There remained an extraordinary amount of uncertainty and considerable risks to the economic outlook."
- "A number of participants judged that there was a substantial likelihood of additional waves of outbreaks, which, in some scenarios, could result in further economic disruptions and possibly a protracted period of reduced economic activity."
- "Participants judged there to be a great deal of uncertainty and expressed concerns about the possibility that an early reopening would contribute to a significant increase of infections."

**Center of gravity**: Uncertainty is at very high levels -- participants use "extraordinary" and stress genuine inability to forecast the pandemic trajectory. Slightly below the March 2020 emergency peak since some data has materialized but fundamental uncertainty remains.
**Minority views**: Some noted upside possibilities ("recover more quickly if sizable, widespread outbreaks could be avoided") but the emphasis is on downside risks.

---

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 40 +/-6, Moderate confidence
**Evidence**:
- "Some participants noted a risk that long-term inflation expectations might deteriorate."
- Survey measures "were little changed on balance" and "continued to be near their recent ranges."

**Center of gravity**: Expectations broadly anchored but with acknowledged downside risk given persistent below-target inflation.

---

### CREDIT_STRESS: 65 +/-8, High confidence
**Evidence**:
- Spreads "remained at levels similar to those in other periods of notable economic or bond market stress."
- "Credit quality of nonfinancial corporations continued to deteriorate sharply."
- "Financing conditions for small businesses tightened."

**Center of gravity**: Credit stress has eased from crisis levels but remains notably elevated, particularly for lower-rated and small borrowers.

---

### TREASURY_STRESS: 55 +/-8, Moderate confidence
**Evidence**:
- "Measures of market functioning in the markets for Treasury securities... had improved substantially since March."
- "Off-the-run Treasury securities of all tenors and longer-maturity on-the-run securities remained less liquid than before the onset of the pandemic."

**Center of gravity**: Treasury market functioning much improved from March but not fully normalized.

---

### PRODUCTIVITY: 38 +/-10, Low confidence
**Evidence**:
- "To the extent that transmission-mitigation procedures adopted by firms reduced their productivity, or if the reallocation of industry output resulted in a lasting reduction in business investment, the longer-run level of potential output could be reduced."

**Center of gravity**: Concern about productivity being reduced by pandemic-related adaptations, but limited discussion.
