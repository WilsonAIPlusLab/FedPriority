# FOMC Meeting Scoring: October 28-29, 2025

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is **minutes** (synthesized summary of participant views). It uses standard quantifier language: "most," "many," "several," "a few," "some," "various," "a couple of."

### Era Context
Late 2025. The U.S. economy is in a period of moderate growth with elevated uncertainty driven by tariff policy, a federal government shutdown limiting data availability, and inflation that remains somewhat above the 2% target. The labor market has gradually cooled but not sharply deteriorated. The Fed has been in a cutting cycle but is debating whether to continue.

### Quantifier Distribution
- "Participants generally" / "participants" (collective): used frequently for consensus views
- "Most participants": favored proportional SOMA composition; judged further rate cuts likely appropriate; noted gradual cooling wouldn't entrench inflation risk
- "Many participants": remarked overall inflation above target showing little sign of returning; continued to see upside risks to inflation; favored lowering target range; observed divergence between solid growth and weak job creation; agreed Committee should be deliberate; suggested keeping rates unchanged rest of year
- "Several participants": observed tariff-adjusted inflation near target; pointed to persistence in core nonhousing services; described lack of job turnover as downside risk; did not necessarily view another 25bp cut as likely in December; highlighted possibility of disorderly fall in equity prices
- "Some participants": noted divergence between subdued job growth and moderate GDP growth; suggested tech investments could boost productivity; supported lowering the rate but could also support maintaining; assessed policy still restrictive after a cut; pointed to resilience of activity as evidence policy not clearly restrictive
- "A few participants": suggested productivity gains could limit pass-through; noted softer labor market would keep inflation in check; suggested immigration policy changes would lessen housing disinflation; noted recent failures of nonbank credit firms; remarked trade tensions could disrupt supply chains; noted lower business taxes would support activity

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**1. INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants observed inflation "moved up since earlier in the year and remained somewhat above the Committee's 2 percent longer-run goal." Core inflation "remained elevated." Many noted inflation "had been above target for some time and had shown little sign of returning sustainably to the 2 percent objective in a timely manner." Many expected "some additional pickup in core goods inflation" from tariffs. This is clearly above target but not dramatically so -- "somewhat elevated" language. Direction: above midpoint. Magnitude: "somewhat elevated" and "remained above target" = 55-62 range. The upward movement and tariff concerns push toward the higher end. Score: 60.
- Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says elevated and persistent. Score matches.

**2. OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic activity appeared to have been expanding at a moderate pace." PDFP growth "appeared to have continued at a solid pace." No language about being "above potential" or "below potential" explicitly from participants. Growth had "moderated over the first half of the year" relative to 2024 pace. The staff projected GDP to "remain above potential until 2028." Participants note "moderate pace" which is roughly at-trend language. The divergence between solid GDP growth and weak job creation is notable. Direction: roughly at or slightly above potential. Score: 51.
- Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. "Moderate pace" with staff forecasting above potential = slightly above. Score matches.

**3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Job gains had slowed this year and that the unemployment rate had edged up but remained low." Layoffs and hiring both "remained low" -- a less dynamic market. Participants "generally expected conditions to soften gradually." Several described "lack of job turnover and hesitancy among businesses to add jobs as adding downside risks." The labor market has "gradually softened through September and October but had not sharply deteriorated." This is near full employment but softening -- still fairly balanced but cooling. Direction: near NAIRU but slightly below peak tightness. Score: 48.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Market is cooling, unemployment edging up, but still low. Near balanced, slight softening. Score matches.

**4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "had shown signs of firming in recent months after the slowdown observed early in the year." But many noted divergence across income groups -- higher-income households supporting growth, lower-income showing "increased price sensitivity." Strong technology/AI investment highlighted. Housing market weak with affordability challenges. Agricultural sector facing headwinds. Overall: moderate pace, somewhat mixed. Direction: roughly at trend. Score: 50.
- Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Mixed signals with consumer firming, strong tech investment, but housing weak and lower-income consumers strained. At-trend is right. Score: 50.

**5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Some participants remarked that financial conditions were supportive of economic activity." Credit "remained generally available but relatively tight for small businesses." Corporate bond/leveraged loan issuance was "robust." Equity prices increased moderately. Credit spreads "remained very low by historical standards." Bank lending standards eased. But borrowing costs remained "significantly lower than highs observed in 2023 but elevated relative to post-GFC levels." Some participants assessed policy as "restrictive" while others pointed to "resilience of economic activity, supportive financial conditions" as evidence policy was "not clearly restrictive." Direction: somewhat loose to neutral overall -- supportive conditions. Score: 38.
- Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Conditions described as "supportive," spreads very low, robust issuance = not tight. Score of 38 (somewhat loose) matches.

**6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: This is nuanced. "Many participants were in favor of lowering the target range" (dovish), "some supported such a decision but could have also supported maintaining" (moderate), and "several were against lowering the target range" (hawkish). One participant wanted 50bp cut. On forward guidance: "most participants judged that further downward adjustments would likely be appropriate" but "many participants suggested it would likely be appropriate to keep the target range unchanged for the rest of the year." Strong concern about inflation stalling progress. "Participants judged that a careful balancing of risks was required." The center of gravity is a mild dovish lean -- they appear to have cut 25bp with significant disagreement. Direction: lean dovish but cautious. Score: 42.
- Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. A 25bp cut with many wanting to pause and several opposing the cut = lean dovish but not strongly so. 42 fits.

**7. DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Participants expressed strongly differing views about what policy decision would most likely be appropriate at the Committee's December meeting." The split: many favored cutting, some could go either way, several opposed cutting. One wanted 50bp. On forward guidance, most vs. many vs. several had different views on December. This is notable disagreement. Direction: elevated. Score: 55.
- Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. "Strongly differing views" is clear. Multiple camps with genuine differences. 55 fits.

**8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: "A number of participants pointed to some recent failures of firms involved in nonbank credit activity." Discussion of private credit risks, stretched asset valuations, possibility of disorderly equity price falls, corporate borrowing risks. Staff assessment characterized vulnerabilities as "notable." This is a meaningful discussion but not dominant. Direction: moderate attention. Score: 42.
- Polarity check: Higher = more attention. Several participants addressed it meaningfully but it wasn't the dominant topic. 42 fits.

**9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Most participants judged that further downward adjustments to the target range for the federal funds rate would likely be appropriate" as Committee moved to more neutral stance. But "several of these participants indicated they did not necessarily view another 25bp reduction as likely in December." "Many participants suggested it would likely be appropriate to keep the target range unchanged for the rest of the year." "All participants agreed that monetary policy was not on a preset course." The forward signal is mixed -- leaning toward further cuts eventually but with significant caution and data dependence. Direction: mild easing bias with strong data dependence. Score: 38.
- Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Most see further cuts ahead but many see holding steady -- a cautious easing lean. 38 (easing lean with data dependence) fits.

**10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants generally judged that uncertainty about the economic outlook remained elevated." Government shutdown limiting data availability was a major concern. "Several participants expressed concern about the potential effect of a prolonged government shutdown." Tariff effects uncertain. "Divergence between solid economic growth and weak job creation created a particularly challenging environment." "Participants expressed strongly differing views." Direction: elevated. "Elevated" is genuine, not rhetorical -- multiple concrete sources of uncertainty. Score: 60.
- Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. "Remained elevated" with multiple specific uncertainty sources. 60 fits.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (51) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (48): Slight divergence is explicitly discussed by participants -- "divergence between solid economic growth and weak job creation." This is coherent with the text.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (42) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (38): Both lean dovish, with forward guidance slightly more so because "most" see further cuts as appropriate over time. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 60 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants observed that overall inflation had moved up since earlier in the year and remained somewhat above the Committee's 2 percent longer-run goal."
- "Many participants, however, remarked that overall inflation had been above target for some time and had shown little sign of returning sustainably to the 2 percent objective in a timely manner."
- "Many participants expected some additional pickup in core goods inflation over the next few quarters, driven in part by further pass-through of tariffs."

**Center of gravity**: Participants see inflation as somewhat elevated and persistent, with upside risks from tariffs, placing it clearly above neutral but not at alarming levels.

**Minority views**: Several participants observed that tariff-adjusted inflation was close to target; a few noted softer labor market would help keep pressures in check. These inform the lower end of the uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 51 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants noted that available indicators suggested that economic activity appeared to have been expanding at a moderate pace."
- "PDFP growth appeared to have continued at a solid pace in the third quarter."
- "Many participants observed that the divergence between solid economic growth and weak job creation created a particularly challenging environment."

**Center of gravity**: Economy expanding at a moderate-to-solid pace, roughly near potential with perhaps a slight above-potential lean given staff projections.

**Minority views**: A number of participants noted data limitations from the shutdown made it challenging to gauge recent strength, adding uncertainty.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 48 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Job gains had slowed this year and that the unemployment rate had edged up but remained low through August."
- "Participants generally expected conditions to soften gradually in coming months and the labor market to remain less dynamic than earlier in the year."
- "Several participants described the lack of job turnover and hesitancy among businesses to add jobs as adding downside risks."

**Center of gravity**: The labor market is near balance but has softened from previously tight conditions, with hiring and layoffs both low, creating a less dynamic but not weak market.

**Minority views**: A few participants viewed rising unemployment rates for cyclically sensitive groups as signaling potential broader weakness, pushing the lower bound down.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 50 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending had shown signs of firming in recent months after the slowdown observed early in the year."
- "Many participants highlighted strong investment in technology, particularly spending related to AI and data centers."
- "Many participants remarked on a divergence in spending patterns across income groups, noting that consumption growth appeared to be disproportionately supported by higher-income households."

**Center of gravity**: Spending is roughly at trend -- consumer firming and strong tech investment offset by housing weakness, agricultural headwinds, and lower-income consumer strain.

**Minority views**: A couple of participants expressed concern about the narrow base of consumption support; a couple mentioned continued housing market weakness.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 38 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Some participants remarked that financial conditions were supportive of economic activity."
- Credit spreads "remained very low by historical standards." Issuance of corporate bonds, leveraged loans, and private credit was "robust."
- "Some participants assessed that the Committee's policy stance would be restrictive even after a potential 1/4 percentage point reduction... By contrast, some participants pointed to the resilience of economic activity, supportive financial conditions... as indicating that the stance of monetary policy was not clearly restrictive."

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are broadly supportive/accommodative despite the elevated policy rate, with low credit spreads and robust issuance, though small businesses still face relatively tight conditions.

**Minority views**: Some participants viewed conditions as restrictive given elevated borrowing costs relative to post-GFC norms; credit was "relatively tight for small businesses."

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 42 +/-10 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Many participants were in favor of lowering the target range for the federal funds rate at this meeting, some supported such a decision but could have also supported maintaining the level of the target range, and several were against lowering the target range."
- "Those who preferred to keep the target range for the federal funds rate unchanged at this meeting expressed concern that progress toward the Committee's inflation objective had stalled this year."
- "Participants judged that a careful balancing of risks was required and agreed on the importance of well-anchored longer-term inflation expectations."

**Center of gravity**: The center of gravity is a cautious dovish lean -- a majority supported or could support a 25bp cut, but with significant hesitation driven by inflation concerns. This is not a confident easing posture.

**Minority views**: Several participants were against cutting; one wanted a 50bp cut. Wide spread of views informs the +/-10 band.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 55 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants expressed strongly differing views about what policy decision would most likely be appropriate at the Committee's December meeting."
- The three-way split on current action: many favored cutting, some ambivalent, several opposed.
- Forward guidance also split: most saw further cuts as appropriate, many suggested holding the rest of the year.

**Center of gravity**: Notable disagreement with distinct camps on both the current decision and forward path, though not rising to the level of fundamental framework disagreements.

**Minority views**: The range from one participant wanting 50bp cuts to several opposing any cut represents genuine policy divergence.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 42 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "A number of participants pointed to some recent failures of firms involved in nonbank credit activity... various reasons for concern about developments in the private credit sector."
- "Some participants commented on stretched asset valuations in financial markets, with several of these participants highlighting the possibility of a disorderly fall in equity prices."
- "A couple of participants cited risks associated with high levels of corporate borrowing."

**Center of gravity**: Moderate discussion -- a subset of participants engaged meaningfully with financial stability concerns (nonbank credit, valuations, leverage), but it was not the dominant theme of the meeting.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 38 +/-10 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Most participants judged that further downward adjustments to the target range for the federal funds rate would likely be appropriate as the Committee moved to a more neutral policy stance over time."
- "Several of these participants indicated that they did not necessarily view another 25 basis point reduction as likely to be appropriate at the December meeting."
- "Many participants suggested that, under their economic outlooks, it would likely be appropriate to keep the target range unchanged for the rest of the year."

**Center of gravity**: The forward signal leans toward further easing over time but is heavily data-dependent, with a significant faction favoring a pause. The forward tone is a cautious easing lean, not a committed easing signal.

**Minority views**: Many wanting to hold rates unchanged for the rest of the year pulls the signal toward neutral; most seeing eventual further cuts pulls it toward easing.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 60 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants generally judged that uncertainty about the economic outlook remained elevated."
- "Various participants expressed concern about the potential effect of a prolonged government shutdown, both on near-term economic activity and on the ability to accurately assess economic conditions."
- "Many participants observed that the divergence between solid economic growth and weak job creation created a particularly challenging environment for policy decisions."

**Center of gravity**: Participants express genuinely elevated uncertainty from multiple sources: government shutdown data gaps, tariff effects, and the puzzling growth-employment divergence. This is above normal but not extreme.
