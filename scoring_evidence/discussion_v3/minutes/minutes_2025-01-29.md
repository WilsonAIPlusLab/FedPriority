# FOMC Participant Discussion Scoring — January 28-29, 2025

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented format — participant discussion section is clearly separated from staff content.

**Era context**: January 2025. The Fed had cut rates at the previous three meetings (Sept, Nov, Dec 2024), bringing the fed funds rate to 4.25-4.50%. Inflation had been coming down from its 2022-23 peaks but remained somewhat above the 2% target. The labor market was solid but no longer overheating. The economy was expanding at a solid pace. Policy uncertainty was elevated due to potential trade/immigration policy changes under the new administration.

**Quantifier distribution**: The text uses "all participants" (hold decision), "vast majority" (risks roughly balanced; restrictive stance), "most" (month-over-month inflation progress), "many" (additional evidence needed for disinflation; financial conditions tightened; business optimism), "several" (various inflation subcategories, Treasury market vulnerabilities, labor monitoring), "some" (upside risks to inflation, market-based inflation expectations up), "a few" (neutral rate not far above current, housing inflation, financial strains on lower-income households), "a couple" (various minority views).

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants observed inflation "had eased significantly over the past two years" but "remained somewhat elevated" relative to 2%. Progress "had slowed over the past year." Core PCE at 2.8% over 12 months. Most noted month-over-month progress in Nov-Dec, but "many participants emphasized that additional evidence of continued disinflation would be needed." Several upside risk factors cited: potential trade/immigration policy changes, strong consumer demand, firms passing on tariff costs. Direction: above target but moderating. "Somewhat elevated" = 51-60 range. The 2.8% core PCE and slowing progress push this toward the upper end. Score ~58.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Economy "continued to expand at a solid pace" and data "stronger than anticipated." Favorable supply developments (labor supply, investment, productivity) "continued to support a solid expansion." Business contacts reported "substantial optimism." No language about overheating or above-potential. The phrase "solid pace" suggests at or modestly above trend. No "above potential" language; no "below potential" language. This reads as economy near potential with perhaps a slight above-trend tilt. Score ~53.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Labor market "remained solid" and "broadly consistent with the Committee's goal of maximum employment." Unemployment "stabilized at a relatively low level." Job vacancies, quits rate, labor turnover "generally consistent with stable labor market conditions." Supply and demand "roughly in balance." This is near-NAIRU language — balanced, full employment. Some noted labor supply increases and productivity gains making labor conditions unlikely to be inflationary. Score ~50-52. Near balance at full employment.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "stronger than anticipated," supported by "solid labor market, elevated household net worth, and rising real wages" plus "productivity gains." Investment in equipment and intangibles "strong over the past year, though it appeared to have slowed in the fourth quarter." Business optimism "substantial." However, low/moderate-income households facing strains; delinquencies on credit cards and auto loans rising. Net position: above trend on consumption, mixed on investment. Score ~58.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Many participants noted that certain financial conditions had tightened over the past several months" — longer-term Treasury yields, corporate bond yields, mortgage rates "risen notably." However, "a couple of participants commented that high equity valuations or low credit spreads were providing some support to economic activity." So a mixed picture: rates/yields tightened, but equity/credit conditions still supportive. The stance of policy is "significantly less restrictive than before" the easing. Net: somewhat tight on rates side, loose on asset prices. Score ~55 — somewhat tightened conditions.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All participants agreed to hold at 4.25-4.50% (pause after three cuts). The Committee is "well positioned to take time to assess the evolving outlook." They want to "see further progress on inflation before making additional adjustments." Many noted the Committee "could hold the policy rate at a restrictive level if the economy remained strong and inflation remained elevated." The vast majority pointed to a "still-restrictive policy stance." This is a hold-with-caution stance — no urgency to cut further, data-dependent, willing to stay restrictive. This is lean hawkish relative to the prior cutting trajectory. Score ~55-58. Hold with mild hawkish bias.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All participants viewed it as appropriate to maintain the target range." "Almost all members agreed that the risks... were roughly in balance" (implying 1-2 didn't). The discussion shows some diversity — some emphasize upside inflation risks, some emphasize downside labor/activity risks — but no formal dissents. "Several remarked that policy could be eased if labor market conditions deteriorated." Broad agreement on the hold, minor variation on forward emphasis. Score ~20.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: A full paragraph on financial stability — banking system issues (funding risks, unrealized losses, reciprocal deposits), nonbank vulnerabilities, equity/corporate debt valuations, CRE exposures, Treasury market vulnerabilities (dealer intermediation, leveraged positions), cyber risks, central clearing migration. "Several participants" on multiple subtopics. This is moderate-to-prominent discussion. Score ~45.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Well positioned to take time to assess." Want "further progress on inflation before making additional adjustments." Policy "not on a preset course." Many could hold if economy strong and inflation elevated. Several said policy could be eased if labor weakened or inflation fell. This is data-dependent with a mild hawkish lean — the bar for further cuts has risen. Score ~58. Mild tightening bias in forward guidance (hold/patience signal after recent cuts).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The vast majority of participants observed that the current high degree of uncertainty made it appropriate for the Committee to take a careful approach." Factors: potential trade/immigration policy changes, neutral rate uncertainty, "especially difficult to distinguish between relatively persistent changes in inflation and more temporary changes." "Increased upside risks to the outlook for inflation, and uncertainties concerning the neutral rate of interest." This is elevated uncertainty. Score ~62.

### Cross-dimension coherence check:
- OUTPUT_GAP (53) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (51): Both near 50, consistent with economy near potential and labor near balance. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (57) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (58): Both lean mildly hawkish. Coherent — hold with patience, bar for cuts raised.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says "somewhat elevated," 2.8% core. Score 58 = modestly above target. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Economy solid, at/slightly above trend. Score 53. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Yields risen notably, but equity/credit supportive. Score 55 = somewhat tight. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Holding after cuts, wanting more inflation progress before cutting again. Score 57. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 58 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "participants observed that inflation had eased significantly over the past two years. Inflation remained somewhat elevated, however, relative to the Committee's 2 percent longer-run goal, and progress toward that goal had slowed over the past year."
- "core PCE prices rose 2.8 percent" over 12 months
- "many participants, however, emphasized that additional evidence of continued disinflation would be needed to support the view that inflation was returning sustainably to 2 percent"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants see inflation as moderately above target — clearly improved from peaks but still "somewhat elevated" at 2.8% core, with progress slowing and upside risks from potential policy changes.

**Minority views**: Some participants noted month-over-month readings showed "notable progress," pulling slightly toward neutral. Others cited tariff pass-through and strong demand as upside risks.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 53 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the economy had continued to expand at a solid pace and that recent data on economic activity, and consumer spending in particular were, on balance, stronger than anticipated"
- "favorable aggregate supply developments—including increases in labor supply, business investment, and productivity—continued to support a solid expansion of business activity"

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy expanding solidly, modestly above trend, but favorable supply-side developments mean no clear overheating signal. Near potential with a slight positive tilt.

**Minority views**: Some contacts reported increased uncertainty about government policies, and the agricultural sector faced "significant strains."

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 51 +/-4 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "labor market conditions had remained solid and that those conditions were broadly consistent with the Committee's goal of maximum employment"
- "supply and demand in the labor market roughly in balance and in light of recent productivity gains, labor market conditions were unlikely to be a source of inflationary pressure in the near future"
- "unemployment rate had stabilized at a relatively low level"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market at full employment but no longer tight — balanced supply and demand, stable conditions, not a source of inflation pressure. Essentially at NAIRU.

**Minority views**: Participants "generally noted that labor market indicators merited close monitoring," suggesting some lingering concern about potential softening.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 58 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "consumer spending in particular were, on balance, stronger than anticipated... supported by a solid labor market, elevated household net worth, and rising real wages"
- "investment in equipment and intangibles was strong over the past year, though it appeared to have slowed in the fourth quarter"
- "District contacts or surveys of businesses reported substantial optimism about the economic outlook"

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending above trend and stronger than expected. Business investment solid but decelerating in Q4. Net spending picture is above trend.

**Minority views**: "Several participants cautioned that low- and moderate-income households continued to experience financial strains" and rising delinquencies on credit cards and auto loans.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 55 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "many participants noted that certain financial conditions had tightened over the past several months. For example, longer-term Treasury and corporate bond yields and mortgage rates had risen notably."
- "a couple of participants commented that high equity valuations or low credit spreads were providing some support to economic activity"

**Center-of-gravity**: Mixed financial conditions — rates and yields have tightened notably, but equity valuations remain high and credit spreads low. Net somewhat tight.

**Minority views**: The split between rate-based tightening and asset-price looseness creates genuine ambiguity, reflected in the wider uncertainty band.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 57 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "all participants viewed it as appropriate to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 4-1/4 to 4-1/2 percent"
- "the Committee was well positioned to take time to assess the evolving outlook... they would want to see further progress on inflation before making additional adjustments"
- "many participants noted that the Committee could hold the policy rate at a restrictive level if the economy remained strong and inflation remained elevated"

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous hold after three consecutive cuts. Clear signal that the bar for further cuts has risen — patience is the dominant theme. Lean hawkish: hold with no urgency to ease.

**Minority views**: "Several remarked that policy could be eased if labor market conditions deteriorated, economic activity faltered, or inflation returned to 2 percent more quickly than anticipated" — a dovish contingency.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 20 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "all participants viewed it as appropriate to maintain the target range" — unanimous on decision
- "almost all members agreed that the risks... were roughly in balance" — near-consensus on risk assessment
- Some variation in emphasis on inflation vs. labor risks, but no formal dissent or sharp disagreement

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on both the hold decision and the cautious outlook. Minor differences in risk emphasis but no meaningful division.

**Minority views**: "A couple commented that the risks to achieving the price stability mandate currently appeared to be greater than the risks to achieving the maximum employment mandate" — slight divergence on risk weighting.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 45 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Full paragraph devoted to financial stability: banking system (funding risks, unrealized losses, reciprocal deposits), nonbank vulnerabilities, CRE exposures, Treasury market (dealer capacity, leveraged positions, central clearing), cyber risks
- "Several participants" mentioned on multiple stability subtopics; "a few" on others

**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate, substantive discussion of financial stability covering multiple sectors. Not a dominant theme but clearly a meaningful part of the discussion with specific concerns identified.

**Minority views**: None — this is an attention measure rather than directional.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 58 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the Committee was well positioned to take time to assess the evolving outlook for economic activity, the labor market, and inflation, with the vast majority pointing to a still-restrictive policy stance"
- "they would want to see further progress on inflation before making additional adjustments to the target range for the federal funds rate"
- "policy decisions were not on a preset course and were conditional on the evolution of the economy, the economic outlook, and the balance of risks"

**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal is mildly hawkish — patience after recent cuts, emphasis on needing more inflation progress, data-dependent but with the bar for further cuts clearly elevated.

**Minority views**: Several noted that easing could resume under certain conditions (labor deterioration, faster disinflation), providing a dovish contingency.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 62 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the vast majority of participants observed that the current high degree of uncertainty made it appropriate for the Committee to take a careful approach"
- "in the period ahead, it might be especially difficult to distinguish between relatively persistent changes in inflation and more temporary changes that might be associated with the introduction of new government policies"
- "uncertainties concerning the neutral rate of interest, the degree of restraint from higher longer-term interest rates, or the economic effects of potential government policies"

**Center-of-gravity**: Elevated uncertainty, driven by multiple sources: new government policy effects (trade, immigration), neutral rate ambiguity, and difficulty parsing inflation signals. The "vast majority" explicitly cited high uncertainty as justification for caution.

**Minority views**: None dissenting on the uncertainty assessment — broad agreement that uncertainty is elevated.

---

### Optional Dimensions

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Score: 53 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
- "core goods prices had not declined as much on net in recent months compared with earlier in 2024" — some firming in goods prices, mild upward pressure relative to the prior disinflationary trend.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Score: 56 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
- "housing services inflation, which had remained elevated for much of the previous year, had shown a decline, as had market-based measures of core nonhousing services inflation." But "some nonmarket- and insurance-based services price categories... had shown less improvement." Mixed but still somewhat elevated.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Score: 52 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
- Housing services inflation "which had remained elevated for much of the previous year, had shown a decline." Improvement but from elevated levels — near neutral/slightly above.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Score: 52 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
- "some participants noted that some market- or survey-based measures of expected inflation had increased recently, although many participants emphasized that longer-term measures of expected inflation had remained well anchored." Anchored but with some upward drift in shorter-term measures.

**CREDIT_STRESS**: Score: 42 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
- "low credit spreads were providing some support to economic activity" — spreads compressed. But rising delinquencies on credit cards and auto loans. Net: slightly below normal stress (spreads tight but consumer credit showing some strain).

**EQUITY_STRESS**: N/A
- Equity valuations mentioned as "high" but in context of supporting activity, not as a stress/concern indicator. Insufficient participant concern discussion.

**BANKING_STRESS**: Score: 40 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
- "bank funding risks had lessened and that many banks had improved their ability to access the discount window." But "some banks remained vulnerable to a rise in longer-term yields and the associated unrealized losses on bank assets." Some increased reliance on reciprocal deposits "not been tested in a time of stress." Mixed but modestly below severe stress — some vulnerabilities but improving trend.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Score: 55 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
- "Several participants commented on vulnerabilities in the Treasury market, including concerns about dealer intermediation capacity and the degree of leveraged positions in the market." Central clearing migration noted as important. Somewhat elevated concern.

**DOLLAR_CONCERN**: N/A — not discussed by participants.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: Score: 58 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
- "recent productivity gains" noted as supporting expansion and helping keep labor conditions non-inflationary. "Favorable aggregate supply developments—including increases in labor supply, business investment, and productivity—continued to support a solid expansion." Above-trend productivity perceived.
