# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2007-01-31

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). This is a pre-2009 unsegmented format, but the text is clearly participant-attributed throughout, using language like "participants noted," "all members favored," "many participants observed," etc. There is no staff-attributed content requiring exclusion.

### Era Context
January 2007: The Fed funds rate is at 5.25%, having been on hold since the last hike in June 2006. The economy is in a late-expansion phase. Housing has been declining but the broader economy remains resilient. Core inflation has been running above the implicit 2% target but showing signs of improvement. The subprime crisis is still months away from becoming acute.

### Quantifier Distribution
- "all" / "all members": appear 4 times (concern about inflation, favoring hold, predominant concern is inflation, statement language)
- "many": 1 time (labor markets taut)
- "several": 1 time (trend growth estimate)
- "some": 4 times (occupations with wage pressures, participants citing upside risk, areas with worker scarcity, caution in business investment)
- "a few": 0 times
- "one": 1 time (hiring difficulties)

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as above target. "The prevailing level of inflation was uncomfortably high." "All meeting participants expressed some concern about the outlook for inflation." "The upside risks to inflation remained the Committee's predominant concern." However, there is improvement: "incoming data had suggested some improvement in core inflation" and "a further gradual decline was seen as the most likely outcome." The level is "uncomfortably high" but trending better. The word "uncomfortably" is strong but short of "significantly" or "considerably." Core PCE projected at 2-2.25% for 2007 (modestly above 2% target). Direction: above target, but with improving trajectory. Score should be in the 58-65 range. The "uncomfortably high" language and unanimous concern push toward 62.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy operating at or modestly above potential. "Resource utilization was elevated." Growth is "at or modestly below the economy's trend rate." The economy is "resilient." Housing is a drag but consumer spending is solid. "Apparent underlying strength in aggregate demand." This is not running hot but above potential. Score should be 52-56. "Elevated" resource utilization is a clear above-potential signal.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Many participants observed that labor markets remained relatively taut, with significant wage pressures being reported in some occupations." Shortages of skilled and unskilled workers reported. "Tightness in labor market was seen as an upside risk to inflation." Unemployment projected at 4.5-4.75%. This is a tight labor market. Score 58-63. "Relatively taut" with "significant wage pressures" in some areas and worker scarcity.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending is solid and above trend. "Strength in consumer spending," "solid growth in consumer spending," "healthy holiday sales," "continuing strength in consumption suggested upside risks." Business investment is "weaker than anticipated" but expected to firm. Nonresidential construction offsetting some housing weakness. Net position: consumer spending above trend, business investment somewhat below, housing declining. The net is modestly above trend given the heavy emphasis on consumption strength. Score 53-57.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Favorable financial conditions" mentioned as supporting investment. "Strong corporate balance sheets, high profitability." No discussion of tight credit, widened spreads, or stress. Conditions are described as supportive/accommodative for the economy. Score 25-32 (loose side). Financial conditions are clearly described as favorable/supportive.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All members favor holding at 5.25%. "Additional policy firming was possible" in the statement. The predominant concern is inflation, not growth. They are on hold but with a clear tightening bias. No one is calling for easing. This is a hold-with-hawkish-lean posture. Score 58-63. The unanimous hold with explicit retention of tightening bias language.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All members favored keeping the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent." No dissents mentioned. The discussion of whether to change balance-of-risks language concluded with agreement not to change. There is broad agreement. Score 12-18.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Housing market discussed at length but primarily as an economic growth issue, not financial stability. Financial conditions mentioned briefly as "favorable." No discussion of systemic risk, credit stress, bubbles, or financial stability per se. Some attention to housing spillover risk. Score 30-38. Housing gets attention but framed as growth risk, not financial stability.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Additional policy firming was possible" is a clear mild tightening bias. They are not signaling imminent action but keeping the door open. The predominant concern is inflation. Score 62-68. Clear retention of tightening bias in forward guidance.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Mixed signals. Housing risks "had diminished somewhat." "The risk of a much larger contraction in housing had diminished." But "continued risks to the economic outlook" remain. Inflation outlook is uncertain ("did not yet see a downtrend in core inflation as definitively established"). Overall moderate uncertainty, some standard caveats. Score 35-42.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (54) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (60): Both above 50, labor slightly higher given explicit "taut" language. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (60) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (65): Both lean hawkish, forward guidance slightly more so given explicit "additional firming possible." Coherent.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (28) is loose while HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (60) is hawkish: consistent -- loose financial conditions despite rate being at 5.25% supports the hawkish lean (conditions not yet restrictive enough to quell inflation concerns).

### Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says "uncomfortably high." Score 62 = above midpoint. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. "Resource utilization was elevated." Score 54 = above midpoint. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. "Favorable financial conditions" = loose. Score 28 = below midpoint. Matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 62 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "The prevailing level of inflation was uncomfortably high, and resource utilization was elevated. The upside risks to inflation remained the Committee's predominant concern."
- "All meeting participants expressed some concern about the outlook for inflation... participants did not yet see a downtrend in core inflation as definitively established."
- "Incoming data had suggested some improvement in core inflation, and a further gradual decline was seen as the most likely outcome."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** All participants view inflation as uncomfortably above target with upside risks predominating, though recent readings show some improvement and a gradual decline is expected.

**Minority views:** None explicitly stated; unanimous concern, but the improving trajectory introduces the uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 54 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Resource utilization was elevated."
- "Economic activity would likely advance at a pace at or modestly below the economy's trend rate of growth."
- "The apparent underlying strength in aggregate demand" and "the expansion remained resilient."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The economy is operating modestly above potential with elevated resource utilization, though near-term growth is expected to be at or slightly below trend.

**Minority views:** "Several participants viewed [trend growth] as likely to be higher than the staff's estimate," which would imply a smaller positive gap.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 60 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Many participants observed that labor markets remained relatively taut, with significant wage pressures being reported in some occupations."
- "Continuing shortages of skilled workers in technical and professional fields, recent reports suggested a scarcity of less skilled and unskilled workers in some areas."
- "The possibility that labor costs might rise more rapidly as a result of the tightness in labor market was seen as an upside risk to inflation."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Labor markets are tight with widespread shortages across skill levels, though aggregate compensation measures show only moderate increases so far.

**Minority views:** "Aggregate measures of labor compensation were showing only moderate increases" tempers the tightness signal somewhat.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 55 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Strength in consumer spending outside the housing sector supported the view that the expansion remained resilient."
- "Contacts reported healthy holiday sales in many regions... continuing strength in consumption suggested upside risks as well."
- "Business fixed investment overall continued to be weaker than anticipated, suggesting some caution on the part of businesses in expanding capacity."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Consumer spending is solid and above trend, but business fixed investment is weaker than anticipated, and housing remains a drag; the net position is modestly above trend.

**Minority views:** "Some participants" cited the personal saving rate as posing "a significant upside risk."

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 28 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Favorable financial conditions, strong corporate balance sheets, high profitability" cited as supporting investment.
- No discussion of credit tightness, widening spreads, or financial stress.

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions are described as favorable and supportive, indicating loose conditions with no signs of stress.

**Minority views:** None apparent.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 60 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "All members favored keeping the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent at this meeting."
- "All members agreed that the predominant concern remained the risk that inflation would fail to moderate as desired."
- "All members agreed that the statement should continue to stress that some inflation risks remained and note that additional policy firming was possible."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Unanimous hold with an explicit tightening bias; inflation is the predominant concern and additional firming remains on the table.

**Minority views:** No dovish dissent or even reservation is mentioned; extremely cohesive hawkish lean.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 15 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "All members favored keeping the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent."
- "All members agreed that the predominant concern remained the risk that inflation would fail to moderate."
- Discussion of balance-of-risks language resolved with consensus not to change.

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Near-unanimous agreement on both the decision and the assessment; no dissents and no notable reservations reported.

**Minority views:** None reported.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 35 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Housing market discussed at length as a growth risk: "the ongoing contraction in the housing sector and the potential for spillovers to other sectors remained notable downside risks."
- Financial conditions mentioned as "favorable" but briefly; no systemic risk, credit quality, or bubble discussion.

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Housing receives meaningful attention but is framed as an economic growth risk rather than a financial stability concern; financial conditions are mentioned only briefly and positively.

**Minority views:** None on financial stability specifically.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 65 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Additional policy firming was possible" retained in the statement.
- "The upside risks to inflation remained the Committee's predominant concern."
- "The economy seemed likely to expand at a moderate pace over coming quarters" with inflation pressures "likely to moderate over time."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Forward signal is mildly hawkish, retaining an explicit tightening bias while signaling patience; the door is open to further hikes if inflation does not moderate.

**Minority views:** None; the committee discussed whether to change the balance-of-risks language and decided unanimously against doing so.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 38 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Participants did not yet see a downtrend in core inflation as definitively established."
- "The ongoing contraction in the housing sector and the potential for spillovers to other sectors remained notable downside risks to economic activity, although those risks had diminished somewhat."
- "The effects of some of these factors on inflation could well be temporary."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Moderate uncertainty, primarily around the inflation trajectory and housing spillovers, but overall outlook is characterized with reasonable confidence and diminishing downside risks.

**Minority views:** Some participants flagged upside risks to growth (personal saving rate), adding to the range.
