# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2005-12-13

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format, so I must focus on participant-attributed language ("meeting participants noted," "members favored," "some members," etc.) and exclude staff-attributable content.

**Era context**: December 2005 -- the Fed is deep in its "measured pace" tightening cycle that began June 2004. The fed funds rate is being raised to 4.25%. Housing is showing early signs of cooling. Energy prices elevated from hurricanes. Economy resilient. This is a late-cycle tightening environment.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all members" (rate hike), "most members" (further firming interpretation), "many participants" (energy price concern), "some members" (dropping "measured"), "a number of participants" (near potential), "some firms" (cost pass-through). Generally broad agreement on the hike, with differences about how much more is needed.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly below concerning levels but with upside risks. "Concerns about near-term inflation pressures had eased somewhat." Core inflation pass-through from energy "had been muted." But "many participants remained concerned that elevated energy prices could put pressure on core inflation." And resource utilization could "tighten further and inflation pressures build." Direction: modestly above balanced -- contained currently but risks tilted upward. Score should be in the 51-58 range. Higher = more pressure. Evidence says moderate but with upside risk = slightly above midpoint.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "The economy was possibly producing in the neighborhood of its potential." "Expansion had shown considerable resilience." "Spending apparently retaining considerable momentum." The bright-line rule: "in the neighborhood of its potential" = score 48-52, but with upside risk language ("resource markets could tighten further") pushing slightly above. Score ~52.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Limited direct labor market discussion. "Moderate gains" in labor compensation. "Further advances in employment and income" expected to support consumption. No language about slack or tightness explicitly. Implied near full employment given "near potential" language. Score around 50-52.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "remained vigorous" outside autos. "Holiday sales were said to be off to a good start." Business investment "had accelerated some since midyear." "Spending apparently retaining considerable momentum." This is clearly above trend. Score 58-62.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Supportive financial conditions" mentioned. "Sustained period of favorable financial conditions on asset prices and aggregate demand." Policy "no longer be characterized as accommodative." Financial conditions are described as loose/supportive. Higher = tighter. Conditions are loose, so score should be low: 18-25.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All members voted for 25bp hike. "Members generally anticipated that policy would likely need to be firmed further." But "the number of additional firming steps required probably would not be large." This is a hike with signaling of a few more -- moderate hawkish but nearing the end. Score ~65.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All members favored raising the target." "Views differed on how much further tightening might be required." Some disagreement on forward path but unanimity on current action. "Some members thought that the word 'measured' was no longer necessary." Score ~25-30.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Housing discussion with investor/speculator concerns. "Favorable financial conditions on asset prices." Housing slowing signs discussed. But not dominant. Moderate discussion. Score ~35-40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Some further measured policy firming is likely to be needed." But "the number of additional firming steps required probably would not be large." This signals continued but limited tightening -- moderately hawkish forward lean approaching the end of the cycle. Score ~65-68.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The policy outlook was becoming considerably less certain." "Policy decisions going forward would depend to an increased extent on the implications of incoming economic data." "Effects were especially difficult to judge." This is somewhat elevated uncertainty. Score ~42-48.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (~52) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (~51): Both near balanced/slightly above -- coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (~65) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (~67): Both moderate hawkish -- coherent. The forward guidance signals a few more hikes but nearing the end.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS low (~22) is consistent with participants noting "supportive/favorable financial conditions."

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 54 ±7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "concerns about near-term inflation pressures had eased somewhat"; "indirect effects of elevated energy prices on core inflation had been muted"; but "many participants remained concerned that elevated energy prices could put pressure on core inflation"; "resource markets could tighten further and inflation pressures build"
- Center of gravity: Inflation currently contained but with recognized upside risks from energy and resource utilization, placing it modestly above balanced.
- Minority: "many participants" still concerned about energy pass-through to core, keeping the upper band relevant.

### OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 52 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "the economy was possibly producing in the neighborhood of its potential"; "the persistent strength in spending of late suggested that resource markets could tighten further"; "expansion had shown considerable resilience"
- Center of gravity: Economy near potential with possible slight upside, consistent with "neighborhood of potential" language.
- Minority: Some saw risk of moving above potential ("resource markets could tighten further").

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 51 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "measures of labor compensation showed only moderate gains"; "further advances in employment and income" expected; economy "possibly producing in the neighborhood of its potential"
- Center of gravity: Near full employment implied by at-potential output, but moderate wage gains suggest no acute tightness. Limited direct discussion lowers confidence.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 60 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "consumer spending outside the auto sector appeared to have remained vigorous"; "Holiday sales were said to be off to a good start"; "Business investment spending had accelerated some since midyear"; "spending apparently retaining considerable momentum"
- Center of gravity: Both consumer and business spending above trend, with "vigorous" and "accelerated" language placing this clearly above the midpoint.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 22 ±6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Supportive financial conditions and an apparent increase in confidence"; "sustained period of favorable financial conditions on asset prices and aggregate demand"; policy "no longer be characterized as accommodative" (but conditions still loose)
- Center of gravity: Financial conditions described as loose/supportive/favorable -- well below the neutral midpoint for tightness.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 65 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "all members favored raising the target federal funds rate 25 basis points"; "members generally anticipated that policy would likely need to be firmed further"; "the number of additional firming steps required probably would not be large"
- Center of gravity: Unanimous hike with expectation of continued but limited further tightening -- moderate hawkish, approaching the end of the cycle.
- Minority: "Views differed on how much further tightening might be required."

### DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 27 ±5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "all members favored raising the target" (unanimous); "Views differed on how much further tightening might be required"; "Some members thought that the word 'measured' was no longer necessary"
- Center of gravity: Unanimous on current action with some differences on forward guidance language and path -- typical one-dissent-equivalent range.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 38 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Housing discussion including "damping the demand for houses by investors and speculators"; "favorable financial conditions on asset prices and aggregate demand"; "tentative signs that activity was beginning to slow in the housing sector"
- Center of gravity: Moderate attention to housing valuations and financial conditions effects on asset prices, but not a dominant topic.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 67 ±6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "some further measured policy firming is likely to be needed"; "the number of additional firming steps required probably would not be large"; "policy decisions going forward would depend to an increased extent on the implications of incoming economic data"
- Center of gravity: Clear signal of continued but limited tightening ahead, with increasing data dependence -- moderately hawkish forward lean.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 45 ±7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "the policy outlook was becoming considerably less certain"; "policy decisions going forward would depend to an increased extent on the implications of incoming economic data"; "such effects were especially difficult to judge"
- Center of gravity: Elevated uncertainty about the policy path, though not about the economic outlook itself. "Considerably less certain" is meaningful language.
