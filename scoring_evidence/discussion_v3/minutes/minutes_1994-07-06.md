**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format, but participant language cues are clear ("members commented," "members observed," "many members," etc.). Staff content is minimal and distinguishable.

**Era context**: Mid-1994 — the Fed was in a tightening cycle that began in February 1994. The economy was growing above potential after recovering from the 1990-91 recession. Inflation was still relatively low but there were emerging concerns about overheating. I will score based solely on what the text says.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all" (1x — retain M2/M3 ranges), "most" (1x — risks tilted to upside), "many" (2x — slowing signs, labor shortages), "a number of" (3x — caution among contacts, commercial vacancy, limited margins), "some" (many instances — slowing, constraints, contacts, dollar effects), "one" (1x — explanation re: capacity/productivity).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as slightly above midpoint — current inflation is contained but risks tilt upward. Key: "little change, or perhaps a small rise, in inflation over the 1994-1995 forecast horizon," "most believed that those risks were tilted to the upside," rising materials costs, dollar decline adding price pressure, food/energy no longer favorable. But: "highly competitive markets rendered it very difficult or impossible to pass these higher costs through," "labor compensation increases had remained subdued." Direction: modestly above neutral — inflation not yet elevated but pressures building. Score ~55-58.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants explicitly say "business activity apparently still increasing at a pace above the economy's long-run growth potential" and "remaining margins of unemployed labor and other production resources... now appeared to be quite limited." Bright-line rule: "above potential" language requires >50. Members expect a slowing toward potential but acknowledge currently above. Score ~55-58.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "remaining margins of unemployed labor... now appeared to be quite limited," "shortages of some types of labor in many parts of the country," "sizable wage increases continued to be cited for some industries... operating at full capacity." But: "labor compensation increases had remained subdued" overall, unemployment projected at 6-6.25%. This is a mixed picture — tight in spots, but not universally. Score ~53-57.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending showing "some moderation in the growth of expenditures," higher interest costs constraining durables. But business fixed investment "thought likely to continue to provide appreciable stimulus." Exports improving. Overall: still solid growth but moderating from above-trend. "Continuing growth... ranging from relatively modest to quite robust." Score ~55-60.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Higher interest rates are cited as constraining consumer durables and housing; "higher financing costs" for business investment. But "financing for such projects was more readily available than earlier" for commercial construction. Bond market had tightened significantly in 1994. Score ~55-60 — somewhat tight, conditions have tightened from the rate hikes.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: This appears to be primarily a Humphrey-Hawkins review meeting, not a rate-decision meeting. However, the tone is clearly that "such a slowing seemed necessary to forestall a buildup of inflation pressures," "most believed that those risks were tilted to the upside." The discussion of monetary ranges and containing price pressures signals continued tightening bias. Members generally support policy aimed at restraining growth to potential. Score ~63-67.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Although views differed to some degree" but "members generally concluded" on inflation outlook. "All the members indicated that they were in favor of retaining the current ranges." There's broad agreement on the economic assessment and policy direction, with minor differences about risk balance ("some... fairly evenly balanced... but most believed... tilted to the upside"). Score ~20-25.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Very limited. One brief mention: "rapid debt growth... could have adverse implications for inflation and financial stability." No substantive discussion of financial stability risks, asset prices, or systemic concerns. This is consistent with 1993-96 era anchor of 1-10. Score ~8-12.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is hawkish-leaning. Members see growth above potential, risks tilted to upside for inflation, slowing "seemed necessary to forestall a buildup of inflation pressures." Carrying forward monetary ranges with possible downward adjustment in debt range for 1995. This signals continued tightening posture. Score ~65-70.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "considerable uncertainty" appears twice; "particularly difficult to assess the outlook for inflation"; "uncertainties about remaining margins of slack"; "the extent of that slowing remained subject to considerable uncertainty." This is somewhat elevated genuine uncertainty. Score ~45-50.

### Cross-dimension coherence:

- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (65) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (67): Both moderately hawkish — coherent. Tightening cycle, forward bias toward further restraint.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 56 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the members generally concluded that the various factors affecting the course of inflation were likely to result, on balance, in little change, or perhaps a small rise, in inflation over the 1994-1995 forecast horizon"
- "most believed that those risks were tilted to the upside"
- "highly competitive markets rendered it very difficult or impossible to pass these higher costs through to prices of finished goods"
- CPI projections of 2-3/4 to 3% for 1994 and 2-3/4 to 3-1/2% for 1995 — "a slight increase from the average rate over the past year"

**Center-of-gravity**: Members see inflation as roughly contained now but with building pressures from materials costs, the weaker dollar, food/energy reversals, and operating near capacity — placing this modestly above neutral.

**Minority views**: "Some members observed that the overall behavior of prices had been somewhat more favorable than they would have predicted" (suggesting less pressure), while "most" see risks tilted to upside.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 56 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the expansion continued to display considerable momentum, with business activity apparently still increasing at a pace above the economy's long-run growth potential"
- "remaining margins of unemployed labor and other production resources, while difficult to assess, now appeared to be quite limited"
- Members expect slowing "to a pace closer to that of the economy's long-run potential"

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is clearly running above potential with limited remaining slack, but moderation is expected. The "above potential" language is explicit, placing this above 50 per the bright-line rule, but the degree is moderate — not extreme overheating.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 55 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "remaining margins of unemployed labor... now appeared to be quite limited"
- "shortages of some types of labor in many parts of the country"
- "Exceptions involving sizable wage increases continued to be cited for some industries, such as construction and trucking, that were operating at full capacity"
- But: "labor compensation increases had remained subdued" and unemployment projected at 6-6.25%

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is modestly tight with localized shortages and wage pressures in specific sectors, but overall wage growth remains subdued and unemployment is around 6%, suggesting tightness is not extreme.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT

- Business investment: "thought likely to continue to provide appreciable stimulus to the expansion"
- Exports: "outlook for exports appeared to have improved"

**Center-of-gravity**: Spending and investment are above trend but moderating. Consumer spending is decelerating from higher rates while business investment remains strong and exports are improving. Net position is above trend but not booming.

- But: "financing for such projects was more readily available than earlier" for commercial real estate

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions have tightened somewhat from the 1994 rate hikes, with visible effects on consumer durables and housing, though credit availability for commercial projects has improved. Net: somewhat tight.

**Center-of-gravity**: Members clearly lean hawkish — they view the economy as above potential, inflation risks as tilted up, and policy restraint as necessary. This is a meeting within a tightening cycle where the consensus supports continued vigilance.
