# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2020-12-16 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document type
This is a **minutes** document (synthesized summary of participant views). It is segmented, with a clear "Participants' Views on Current Conditions and the Economic Outlook" section and a "Committee Policy Action" section. The quantifier language of minutes applies: "all," "most," "many," "several," "some," "a few," "a couple of."

### Era context
December 2020: The economy is roughly 9 months into pandemic recovery. COVID-19 cases are surging again heading into winter. Vaccines have just been announced (Pfizer/Moderna), providing medium-term optimism, but near-term conditions are deteriorating. The Fed is at the zero lower bound with ongoing QE ($80B Treasuries + $40B MBS per month). The economy has recovered substantially from the Q2 2020 trough but remains well below pre-pandemic levels.

### Quantifier distribution
- "All participants" / "All members": used for agreement on accommodative stance, asset purchase guidance, maintaining target range
- "Participants" (unqualified, implying broad consensus): used extensively for observations on activity, labor, inflation, financial conditions, uncertainty
- "Several": labor force participation below pre-pandemic, improvements in labor market lagging activity, inflation compensation pickup, downside risks
- "Some": concern about lasting labor market damage, role of 13(3) facilities, future adjustments to asset purchases
- "A number of": small businesses vulnerable, downside risks from CARES Act facilities ending, gradual tapering sequence
- "A few": small business permanent closures, upside inflation risk, costs/risks of asset purchases, policy tools well positioned
- "A couple of": low inventories supporting production, technology-driven deflation persistence, weighting toward longer maturities, banking data on delinquencies
- "Various": low-income households hard hit, communicating progress assessment

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW target. "Increases in consumer prices had been soft of late," prices in pandemic-affected categories "continued to be depressed," rents "remained low." These downward pressures expected to "continue in the near term." However, participants "generally saw these downward pressures on inflation starting to abate next year" and seven participants expected above-2% inflation by 2023. A couple noted persistent deflationary forces from technology disruption. The center of gravity is clearly below target but with a sense that the trough is near and recovery is ahead. Score should be in the 26-35 range -- below target with slack keeping prices in check, but not deep deflation concern. Direction: below midpoint. Higher = more pressure. Evidence says low pressure. Score below 50 -- consistent.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as WELL BELOW potential. "Economic activity and employment had continued to recover but remained well below their levels at the beginning of the year." The economy was "still far from having attained conditions consistent with the Committee's dual mandate." Recovery had been "stronger than anticipated" but "pace of recovery had slowed" and the "expansion was expected to slow even further in coming months." The bright-line rule: "well below" = score must be < 50. But recovery is ongoing from the Q2 2020 trough. The economy has regained more than half of lost output. Score should be in the 28-35 range -- deep below potential but recovering, not at the catastrophic Q2 2020 levels.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see labor markets as very slack. Conditions were "still a long way from those consistent with the Committee's maximum employment goal." Only "a little more than half of the 22 million jobs lost" had been regained. Labor force participation "remained below its pre-pandemic level." Several noted labor market improvements were "lagging that of economic activity." Concern about permanent layoffs increasing. Score should be in the 22-28 range -- high unemployment, significant slack, but some recovery from the worst.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Household spending on goods "especially durables, had been strong." Residential investment and home sales "remained robust." Business equipment investment "had picked up further." However, services spending was being "damped" by the virus surge. Small businesses "in especially vulnerable positions." The near-term outlook is for slowing. The net position: some sectors strong (goods, housing, equipment) but services weak and overall still below pre-pandemic. Score around 38-42 -- below trend on net, but not sharply contracting; strong goods partially offset by weak services.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Overall financial conditions remained accommodative." Equity prices rose, corporate spreads narrowed. BUT "smaller firms and some households reliant on bank lending continued to face tight lending standards." Financing for small businesses was "especially strained." Important bifurcation: large firms see loose conditions, small firms see tight conditions. The center of gravity described by participants leans accommodative overall with pockets of tightness. Score around 30-35 -- somewhat loose to neutral overall, with the bifurcation widening uncertainty bands.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Strongly dovish. "All participants judged that maintaining an accommodative stance of monetary policy was essential." "All participants supported enhancing the Committee's guidance on asset purchases" -- they are ADDING more accommodation via outcome-based QE guidance. Some discussed potential future increases in purchase pace. A number discussed eventual tapering but only "once substantial further progress had been attained." The discussion is firmly in QE territory -- how much more accommodation to provide, not whether to ease. Score should be 12-18 -- strong dovish, maintaining max accommodation and enhancing forward guidance on purchases.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Very low disagreement. "All participants" supported the enhanced guidance. "All members" agreed on maintaining rate range. "Nearly all favored maintaining the current composition." Minor differences: "a couple" open to longer maturities, "a few" emphasized costs/risks of purchases. No formal dissents mentioned. Score around 12-18 -- near-consensus with only minor tactical differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Moderate discussion. Participants discussed financial conditions in a dedicated paragraph -- accommodative overall but bifurcated. Small business closures, role of 13(3) facilities, CARES Act expiration risks. This is substantive but not dominant. Score around 35-42 -- limited to moderate discussion; several participants addressed it meaningfully but it was not the dominant topic.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Very dovish forward signal. New outcome-based guidance: purchases continue "until substantial further progress has been made." Rate guidance: maintain range "until labor market conditions had reached levels consistent with maximum employment and inflation had risen to 2 percent and was on track to moderately exceed 2 percent for some time." This is threshold-based forward guidance committing to accommodation for an extended period. A few noted all tools "well positioned to respond" by increasing accommodation if needed. Score around 5-10 -- strong easing commitment with outcome-based thresholds, very close to the Evans Rule type guidance.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Elevated. "Participants continued to see the uncertainty surrounding the economic outlook as elevated, with the path of the economy highly dependent on the course of the virus." "Significant uncertainties regarding how quickly the deployment of vaccines would proceed." Multiple downside risks enumerated (fiscal, pandemic, Brexit, small business). But vaccine news seen as "reducing downside risks over the medium term" and "a number of participants saw risks as more balanced than earlier." Score around 60-68 -- elevated uncertainty but moderating somewhat from earlier pandemic meetings.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks
- OUTPUT_GAP (30) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (25): Both well below 50, labor slightly lower because participants explicitly noted labor was lagging activity recovery. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (15) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (8): Both very dovish, with forward guidance slightly more dovish because it formalizes outcome-based thresholds. Coherent.

### Polarity checks
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says soft/depressed. Score ~30 (below 50). Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says well below. Score ~30 (below 50). Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says very slack. Score ~25 (below 50). Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence says accommodative overall. Score ~32 (below 50). Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says strongly dovish. Score ~15 (well below 50). Correct.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish signal. Evidence says strong easing commitment. Score ~8 (very low). Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 28 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Increases in consumer prices had been soft of late, as prices of products in those categories most affected by social distancing -- such as hotel accommodations and air travel -- continued to be depressed and increases in rents remained low."
- "These patterns were expected to continue in the near term as pandemic concerns intensified over the winter."
- "A couple of participants suggested that, as a result of ongoing technology-enabled disruption to business models and practices or lasting pandemic-induced restraint on firms' pricing power, downward pressure on inflation could persist."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants broadly viewed inflation as soft and below target, held down by pandemic-related slack and depressed services prices, though they expected downward pressures to start abating next year with vaccine deployment.

**Minority views**: Seven participants (up from two in September) expected inflation above 2% by 2023, suggesting some see medium-term upside. A few noted upside risk from supply constraints coupled with strong recovery. This informs the +6 uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity and employment had continued to recover but remained well below their levels at the beginning of the year."
- "The economy... was still far from having attained conditions consistent with the Committee's dual mandate."
- "The economic recovery thus far had been stronger than anticipated... but viewed the more recent indicators as signaling that the pace of recovery had slowed."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy remained well below potential despite a stronger-than-expected recovery from the Q2 2020 trough. The near-term outlook was for further slowing due to the pandemic surge, while the medium-term outlook improved with vaccine news.

**Minority views**: Limited divergence on this assessment; broad agreement the economy was far below potential.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 25 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Labor market conditions generally had continued to improve, but they were still a long way from those consistent with the Committee's maximum employment goal."
- "A little more than half of the 22 million jobs lost over March and April having been regained."
- "Several participants assessed that improvements in the labor market were lagging that of economic activity."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The labor market had significant remaining slack, with roughly half of pandemic job losses still unrecovered, participation depressed, and disproportionate harm to low-wage and minority workers. Several noted permanent layoffs were rising.

**Minority views**: Some were more concerned about lasting scarring than others, but all agreed substantial slack remained.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 40 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Household spending on goods, especially durables, had been strong... residential investment and home sales remained robust."
- "Business equipment investment had picked up further, with strong readings registered on new orders and shipments."
- "The ongoing surge of COVID-19 cases and the related mandatory and voluntary measures prompted greater social distancing and damped spending, especially on services requiring in-person contact."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: A sharply bifurcated picture: goods consumption, durables, housing, and business equipment were strong, but services spending was depressed by the pandemic surge. Small businesses were struggling. The net assessment is below trend but not deeply contracting.

**Minority views**: The divergence between sectors creates a wider uncertainty band. Various participants emphasized the vulnerability of low-income households and small businesses, while others noted strong goods and housing sectors.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 32 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Overall financial conditions were accommodative, in part reflecting policy measures to support the economy and the flow of credit to households and businesses."
- "Financing conditions eased further for large corporations... equity prices rose and corporate credit spreads continued to narrow."
- "Smaller firms and some households reliant on bank lending continued to face tight lending standards... financing conditions for small businesses were especially strained."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions were broadly accommodative, with large firms benefiting from easy capital market access, but a significant bifurcation existed with small businesses and some households facing tight lending conditions.

**Minority views**: The bifurcation between large and small firms creates notable dispersion. "A few participants" pointed out a sizable fraction of small businesses had permanently closed. Downside risk from CARES Act facility expiration noted by "a number of" participants.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 15 +/-4 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All participants judged that maintaining an accommodative stance of monetary policy was essential to foster economic recovery and to achieve an average inflation rate of 2 percent over time."
- "All participants supported enhancing the Committee's guidance on asset purchases... adopting qualitative, outcome-based guidance indicating that increases in asset holdings would continue."
- "Some participants noted that the Committee could consider future adjustments to its asset purchases -- such as increasing the pace of securities purchases."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Unanimous strong dovish consensus: all participants supported maintaining maximum accommodation and enhancing forward guidance on QE. Some even discussed the possibility of increasing purchases. The discussion was entirely about how to calibrate ongoing accommodation, not whether to ease.

**Minority views**: "A few" underlined the importance of evaluating costs and risks of purchases -- a mild hawkish note, but firmly within the accommodative framework. No meaningful hawkish dissent.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 15 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All participants supported enhancing the Committee's guidance on asset purchases."
- "All members agreed to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 0 to 1/4 percent."
- "Nearly all favored maintaining the current composition of purchases, although a couple of participants indicated that they were open to weighting purchases of Treasury securities toward longer maturities."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Near-unanimous agreement on all major decisions. Differences were limited to minor tactical questions such as maturity composition and the importance of evaluating purchase costs/benefits. No formal dissents.

**Minority views**: Minor tactical differences around purchase composition and pace do not rise to meaningful dissent.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 38 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Dedicated paragraph on financial conditions with bifurcation between large and small firms.
- "Some participants noted the important role played by the various section 13(3) facilities implemented in 2020 in serving as temporary backstops to key credit markets."
- "A number noted that they saw downside risks associated with" the expiration of CARES Act facility funding.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions and stability received a moderate level of attention. Participants discussed the bifurcation in credit access, small business distress, the role of emergency facilities, and risks from their expiration. This was meaningful but secondary to the main economic and policy discussions.

**Minority views**: Limited divergence on emphasis level.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 8 +/-4 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Increases in asset holdings would continue... until substantial further progress has been made toward reaching the Committee's maximum employment and price stability goals."
- Rate guidance: "Maintain this target range until labor market conditions had reached levels consistent with the Committee's assessments of maximum employment and inflation had risen to 2 percent and was on track to moderately exceed 2 percent for some time."
- "If progress toward the Committee's goals proved slower than anticipated, the new guidance relayed the Committee's intention to respond by increasing monetary policy accommodation."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Extremely dovish forward signal. New outcome-based thresholds for both rates and asset purchases effectively commit to maximum accommodation for an extended period, with asymmetric guidance that progress shortfalls would trigger more easing.

**Minority views**: None meaningfully diverging from this framework.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 63 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants continued to see the uncertainty surrounding the economic outlook as elevated, with the path of the economy highly dependent on the course of the virus."
- "Participants saw significant uncertainties regarding how quickly the deployment of vaccines would proceed as well as how different members of the public would respond to the availability of vaccines."
- "A number of participants saw risks to economic activity as more balanced than earlier."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Uncertainty remained elevated due to pandemic trajectory, vaccine deployment unknowns, and fiscal policy uncertainty. However, it was moderating from earlier pandemic meetings as vaccine news provided more clarity on the medium-term outlook and risks were seen as becoming more balanced.

**Minority views**: Some saw risks as more balanced; others emphasized continued significant downside risks. This is captured in the +/-7 band.
