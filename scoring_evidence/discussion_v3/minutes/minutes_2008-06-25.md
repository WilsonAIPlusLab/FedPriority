# FOMC Meeting Scoring — 2008-06-25 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). This is an unsegmented pre-2009 minutes document, so I must carefully distinguish participant language ("participants noted," "members agreed," "some commented") from staff content ("the staff reported," "the forecast of core inflation"). The opening paragraph about core inflation forecasts and PCE headline appears to be staff forecast language; I will focus scoring on participant-attributed content.

**Era context**: June 2008 — the U.S. is in the early/middle phase of the Global Financial Crisis. Bear Stearns collapsed in March 2008. The economy is slowing, housing is declining sharply, but the full crisis (Lehman) is still three months away. Stagflationary pressures: energy prices surging, growth slowing, financial stress elevated but not yet at peak panic levels. The fed funds rate had been cut aggressively from 5.25% to 2.00% between September 2007 and April 2008.

**Quantifier distribution**: "most" (4 uses: spending not presaging strengthening; consumer spending delayed; aggregate housing weakness; persistent labor slack), "many" (2 uses: financial institutions losses; some participants), "some" (7 uses: liquidity facilities; pass-through to prices; wages moderate; inflation expectations; regional banks; anecdotal construction; real funds rate), "a few" (2 uses: tentative housing bottom; foreign demand slowing), "participants agreed" (1 use: upside risks to inflation intensified). The dominant signal is toward weak growth with rising inflation concerns.

### Dimension-by-dimension analysis:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as above the midpoint. "Participants were concerned about the inflationary consequences of recent increases in the prices of energy, food, and imports." "Participants had become more concerned about upside risks to the inflation outlook." "The near-term outlook for inflation had deteriorated." Core inflation was "stable of late," but headline was rising sharply, and participants agreed upside risks had "intensified." This is clearly elevated concern — not yet extreme (core stable, slack expected to moderate), but firmly above target. Direction: above midpoint. Magnitude: "concerned," "deteriorated," "intensified" = significant concern, suggesting 63-70 range. Score ~67.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as below potential. "Economic activity would probably continue to expand slowly over the next several quarters." "Most participants anticipated persistent slack in labor markets, with the unemployment rate rising further through next year." "Continued slack in resource utilization." The economy is expanding slowly but participants describe persistent slack, below-potential growth, with downside risks. However, this is not yet deep recession — spending was "less weak than anticipated." Direction: below midpoint. "Slowly expand" with "persistent slack" suggests 33-38 range. Score ~35.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor markets in most regions continued to weaken gradually." "Most participants anticipated persistent slack in labor markets, with the unemployment rate rising further through next year, before declining slightly in 2010." "Wage growth had been quite moderate." Direction: below midpoint. Labor markets weakening but not yet in freefall — "gradually" weakening with persistent slack anticipated. Score ~33.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending showed "more resilience" in Q2 "than had been expected," but "most participants thought that much of the recent strength probably indicated only a more delayed slowing." "Business spending was expected to remain sluggish." Housing "remained bleak." Multiple headwinds: "falling wealth and real income, tightening credit conditions, rising energy prices, and sharply declining consumer sentiment." Direction: below trend. The overall picture is one of below-trend spending with significant drags. Score ~33.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Most participants viewed markets as remaining under considerable stress." "Term spreads in interbank funding markets had declined, but remained elevated by historical standards." "Financial market conditions, while better in many respects, appeared to remain fragile." Credit tightening for households — "second mortgages, automobile loans, and home equity lines of credit were becoming harder to obtain." "Credit availability was likely to remain constrained for some time." Conditions improved somewhat but remain quite tight. Direction: clearly above midpoint (tight). Score ~72.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Members generally agreed that the risks to growth had diminished somewhat since the time of the last FOMC meeting while the upside risks to inflation had increased." The decision was to hold rates steady (no cut). "Some participants" argued the real funds rate was negative and "some firming in policy would be appropriate very soon, if not at this meeting." But "other participants observed that the high level of risk spreads and the restricted availability of credit suggested that overall financial conditions were not especially accommodative." The center of gravity is a hold with divided views — hawkish voices gaining strength but not yet dominant. This is roughly balanced to slightly hawkish. Score ~52.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: There is notable disagreement. On monetary policy stance: "some" argued for firming soon vs. "others" noting conditions were already tight. On inflation expectations: "some" saw short-term survey effects vs. "others" seeing broad-based erosion. On how to read real rates. The document doesn't mention formal dissents, but there are clear camps. Score ~45.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial conditions receive extensive discussion — a full paragraph on market conditions, bank equity declines, securitization concerns, capital raising, and the anticipation of protracted deleveraging. Credit conditions discussed throughout consumption and investment sections too. This is a prominent topic. Score ~68.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is mixed. "Some" argued "some firming in policy would be appropriate very soon, if not at this meeting." But risks to growth remained "tilted to the downside." The committee held rates. The forward lean is very slightly hawkish — hold with a growing tightening contingent, but not a clear tightening signal. Score ~52.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants continued to see significant downside risks to growth." "Financial market conditions... appeared to remain fragile." Multiple risk factors discussed at length. "A difficult adjustment process involving both lower growth and higher rates of inflation" — stagflation uncertainty. "Adverse shocks to output and inflation" acknowledged. Uncertainty is elevated. Score ~62.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 67 ±8 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Participants were concerned about the inflationary consequences of recent increases in the prices of energy, food, and imports, and they expected headline inflation to rise in the very near term." "Participants agreed that the possibilities of greater pass-through of cost increases into prices, higher long-run inflation expectations feeding into labor costs and other prices, and further increases in energy prices all posed upside risks to inflation that had intensified since the time of the April FOMC meeting." "The near-term outlook for inflation had deteriorated."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Participants see inflation as clearly above target and worsening, with upside risks intensifying, though core inflation is stable and slack is expected to moderate pressures over time.
- **Minority views**: "Some participants noted that wage growth had been quite moderate, reinforcing a view that longer-term inflation expectations and labor cost pressures had remained fairly well contained." This tempers the score modestly.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 35 ±6 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Economic activity would probably continue to expand slowly over the next several quarters, restrained by a range of factors." "Most participants anticipated persistent slack in labor markets, with the unemployment rate rising further through next year." "Spending in recent months had evidently been less weak than anticipated" — some upside revision but still clearly below potential.
- **Center-of-gravity**: Economy operating below potential with persistent slack expected; not yet in deep recession but clearly sub-par growth with significant headwinds.
- **Minority views**: None significant — broad agreement on below-potential growth.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 33 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Labor markets in most regions continued to weaken gradually." "Most participants anticipated persistent slack in labor markets, with the unemployment rate rising further through next year, before declining slightly in 2010." "Wage growth had been quite moderate."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Labor markets clearly loosening, with persistent slack anticipated and moderate wage growth confirming below-full-employment conditions.
- **Minority views**: None noted — broad agreement.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 33 ±7 [High confidence]**

- **Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending expected to slow; business investment sluggish; housing bleak. Overall spending well below trend despite temporary fiscal stimulus resilience.
- **Minority views**: Some resilience noted in recent consumer data and nonresidential construction, but viewed as temporary/lagged.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 72 ±8 [High confidence]**

"Second mortgages, automobile loans, and home equity lines of credit were becoming harder to obtain." "Financial market conditions, while better in many respects, appeared to remain fragile."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions significantly tighter than normal — stress, elevated spreads, credit restrictions on households and businesses, though some modest improvement from peak stress.
- **Minority views**: "Some participants noted that the availability of the liquidity facilities... had probably bolstered the confidence of investors and lenders" — modest improvement recognized.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 52 ±10 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Members generally agreed that the risks to growth had diminished somewhat... while the upside risks to inflation had increased." "Some participants" argued "some firming in policy would be appropriate very soon, if not at this meeting." "Other participants observed that the high level of risk spreads and the restricted availability of credit suggested that overall financial conditions were not especially accommodative."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Hold decision with the committee roughly balanced — hawkish voices growing due to inflation concerns, but dovish camp pointing to tight financial conditions and growth risks. Net: very slight hawkish lean.
- **Minority views**: Clear hawk camp wanting to firm soon; clear dove camp noting effective tightness of conditions. Wide dispersion.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 45 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: Opposing camps on monetary policy stance (firm soon vs. conditions already tight); divided views on inflation expectations ("some noted" short-term effects vs. "others" saw "broad-based" erosion); divided views on real rate implications.
- **Center-of-gravity**: Notable disagreement across multiple dimensions — both on the economic assessment and policy response — but no mention of formal dissents.
- **Minority views**: The disagreement is roughly symmetric — hawks and doves have substantial representation.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 68 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: Extensive discussion of financial market stress, bank equity declines, securitized product aversion, capital raising difficulties, deleveraging needs, credit conditions throughout. "Some anticipated that this process could well be protracted, and that financial intermediation consequently would be impeded for some time, holding back growth well into 2009."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability is a prominent topic receiving substantial attention — nearly all aspects of the economic discussion reference financial conditions.
- **Minority views**: None — universal attention to financial conditions.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 52 ±10 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Some firming in policy would be appropriate very soon, if not at this meeting" (hawkish forward signal from some). But "risks to growth remained tilted to the downside" and financial conditions "fragile" (dovish counterweight). The committee held rates with no clear directional signal.
- **Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal is roughly neutral — a hold with conflicting directional pressures. Inflation concerns create mild tightening lean, but growth/financial risks prevent a clear signal.
- **Minority views**: Hawks want to firm soon; doves emphasize ongoing financial stress.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED

"A difficult adjustment process involving both lower growth and higher rates of inflation" — stagflationary uncertainty. "Financial market conditions... appeared to remain fragile."
