# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2014-07-30 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented -- participant discussion section is clearly separated from staff review.

**Era context**: July 2014. Mid-recovery from the GFC. The economy was rebounding from a weak Q1 2014 (weather effects). Taper of QE3 was underway. Unemployment had been declining but still above long-run levels. Inflation was below the 2% target but moving closer to it.

**Quantifier distribution**: "generally" (used frequently -- views, agreement), "most" (risks nearly balanced, inflation to rise, forward guidance), "many" (improvement in sentiment, inflation gradual rise, convergence sooner), "several" (wage pressures, statistical models, upside risks not increased, inflation slow), "some" (downside risks, financial stability, prompt reduction), "a few" (household caution, unemployment as summary stat, inflation persistently below, upside risks from labor tightening).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target but moving closer. "Inflation firmed in recent months" and "most participants anticipated that it would continue to move up toward the Committee's 2 percent objective." "Inflation had moved somewhat closer to the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective." "Most now judged that the downside risks to inflation had diminished." Still below target -- "a few participants continued to see inflation as likely to persist below the Committee's objective over the medium term." Direction: below 50 but approaching. The language is "somewhat closer" and "gradually" -- this puts it in the 41-49 range. Score ~44. Polarity check: higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says inflation is firming but still below target. Score below 50 is correct.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Rebound in real GDP in the second quarter" after weak Q1. "Continued moderate economic expansion." "Labor market conditions had moved noticeably closer to those viewed as normal in the longer run." But "many participants continued to see a larger gap between current labor market conditions and those consistent with their assessments of normal levels." "Resource slack diminished." The economy is still below potential but the gap is closing. The bright-line rule: "slack remains" language present -> score MUST be < 50. Anchor for 2010-14 recovery is 35-42. Given the noticeable improvement and "closer to normal," I'd put this at the higher end: ~42. Polarity check: higher = more above potential. Economy still below potential. Score below 50 is correct.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Cumulative progress over the past year had been greater than anticipated and labor market conditions had moved noticeably closer to those viewed as normal." But "many participants continued to see a larger gap" and cited "still-elevated levels of long-term unemployment and workers employed part time for economic reasons as well as low labor force participation." "Labor compensation was still rising only modestly." "Several other participants pointed to reports that wage pressures had increased in some regions and occupations." Direction: below full employment but improving notably. Anchor 35-40 for 2011-14. Given the "noticeably closer" language and debate about remaining slack, I'd score ~39. Polarity check: higher = tighter. Still slack, so below 50. Correct.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Household spending appeared to be rising moderately." "Business contacts in several Districts reported a pickup in consumer spending." "Many participants reported continued improvement in sentiment among their business contacts." "Information from businesses suggested increases in spending on capital equipment or a pickup in investment." But "recovery in the housing sector remained slow" and "several participants reported that businesses remained somewhat uncertain about the economic outlook and thus were still cautious about stepping up capital spending." Overall: moderate and improving. "Rising moderately" = around trend. Anchor 35-42 for 2011-13 "moderate," 50-55 for 2014-19 "solid pace." This meeting is transitional -- moderate with positive signals but housing dragging. Score ~48. Polarity check: higher = stronger. Moderate pace with positive signals but housing drag. Near trend is correct.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Credit was readily available" and "strengthening in demand for bank credit." "Difficulty in obtaining mortgage credit, particularly for younger, first-time homebuyers." Financial stability section notes "valuation pressures in some particular asset markets" and "other measures of vulnerability in the financial system were at low to moderate levels." Overall conditions are loose/accommodative. Score ~30. Polarity check: higher = tighter. Credit readily available = loose = low score. Correct.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The Committee continued tapering QE ($10B reduction). "Most anticipated that progress toward those goals would continue." "Many participants noted that if convergence toward the Committee's objectives occurred more quickly than expected, it might become appropriate to begin removing monetary policy accommodation sooner." "Some participants viewed the actual and expected progress... as sufficient to call for a relatively prompt move toward reducing policy accommodation." But "most participants indicated that any change in their expectations for the appropriate timing of the first increase in the federal funds rate would depend on further information." "Several participants continued to believe that inflation was likely to move back to the Committee's objective very slowly, thereby warranting a continuation of highly accommodative policy." Direction: Still in QE taper mode, leaning toward eventual tightening but data-dependent. The anchor for 2014-15 pre-liftoff is 40-50. The debate shows a lean toward hawkish from "some" but "most" want to wait for data. Score ~47. Polarity check: higher = more hawkish. Mixed with a slight hawkish lean from some but majority data-dependent and still tapering. Low-to-mid 40s to low 50s. Score 47 captures this.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Most" vs "some" vs "a few" disagreements on multiple fronts. Key disagreements: (1) degree of remaining labor slack -- "a few" vs "many" on how to measure it; (2) inflation outlook -- "most" see inflation rising vs "a few" see it persistently below; (3) timing of rate hikes -- "some" want prompt action vs "most" data-dependent vs "several" want highly accommodative policy. "These participants were increasingly uncomfortable with the Committee's forward guidance." Multiple areas of meaningful disagreement but no formal dissents mentioned in this excerpt. Score ~40. Anchor: moderate differences with notable camps forming.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: One dedicated paragraph on financial stability. "Participants noted evidence of valuation pressures in some particular asset markets." "Some participants discussed how the Committee might better incorporate financial stability risks." Not a dominant topic but more than passing mention. Score ~30. Anchor: 20-35 for 2013-19 typical.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Committee continued tapering and reiterated data-dependent approach. "Some participants viewed the actual and expected progress... as sufficient to call for a relatively prompt move toward reducing policy accommodation." These participants were "increasingly uncomfortable with the Committee's forward guidance" suggesting it was too dovish. "Most participants indicated that any change... would depend on further information." Forward signal is still accommodative but with growing hawkish voices looking toward normalization. Anchor: 8-15 for 2014 taper, 25-40 for 2015 pre-liftoff. This meeting is in the taper but looking forward to liftoff discussion. Score ~35. Polarity check: higher = more hawkish forward signal. The forward signal is still accommodative overall but shifting. Mid-30s is appropriate.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Most participants continued to view the risks to the outlook for economic activity and the labor market as nearly balanced." "Some pointed to possible sources of downside risk, including persistent weakness in the housing sector, a continued slow rise in household income, or spillovers from developments in the Middle East and Ukraine." "Some noted that it increased uncertainty about the outlook" (re: Q1 GDP drop). "Several participants reported that businesses remained somewhat uncertain about the economic outlook." Moderate uncertainty language -- not elevated, mostly "risks nearly balanced" with some specific concerns. Score ~30. Anchor: 25-35 for 2013-14 stable expansion.

### Cross-dimension coherence check:
- OUTPUT_GAP (42) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (39): Both below 50, consistent -- economy below potential with labor slack. Labor slightly lower because of emphasis on remaining slack measures. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (47) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (35): Some divergence -- current action is near-neutral/data-dependent (still tapering, approaching end), but forward signal is still more accommodative. This makes sense: they are tapering now but the first rate hike is still distant and "most" want to wait for data.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 44 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Inflation firmed in recent months, and most participants anticipated that it would continue to move up toward the Committee's 2 percent objective." "Participants noted that inflation had moved somewhat closer to the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective and generally saw the risks of inflation running persistently below their objective as having diminished somewhat." "A few participants continued to see inflation as likely to persist below the Committee's objective over the medium term."
- **Center of gravity**: Inflation is below 2% target but firming, with most expecting a gradual return to target. Downside risks diminished but not eliminated.
- **Minority views**: A few see persistent below-target inflation; a few others argue tightening labor market increases upside risks.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 42 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Meeting participants generally viewed the rebound in real GDP in the second quarter and the ongoing improvement in labor market conditions as supporting their expectations for continued moderate economic expansion." "Many participants continued to see a larger gap between current labor market conditions and those consistent with their assessments of normal levels of labor utilization." "Resource slack diminished."
- **Center of gravity**: Economy below potential but gap narrowing notably; moderate expansion with slack still present.
- **Minority views**: A few see the unemployment rate alone as adequate (suggesting less slack); many emphasize broader slack measures.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 39 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Participants generally agreed that both the recent improvement in labor market conditions and the cumulative progress over the past year had been greater than anticipated and that labor market conditions had moved noticeably closer to those viewed as normal in the longer run." "Many participants continued to see a larger gap between current labor market conditions and those consistent with their assessments of normal levels of labor utilization." "Labor compensation was still rising only modestly."
- **Center of gravity**: Labor market improving faster than expected but still below full employment, with multiple slack indicators cited.
- **Minority views**: Several note wage pressures in specific regions/occupations; a few focus only on the unemployment rate (suggesting less slack).

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 48 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Household spending appeared to be rising moderately and was expected to contribute to stronger economic growth in the second half of the year." "Many participants reported continued improvement in sentiment among their business contacts." "In some cases, the information from businesses suggested increases in spending on capital equipment or a pickup in investment." "The recovery in housing activity remained slow according to most participants."
- **Center of gravity**: Consumer spending moderate and improving; business investment firming; housing remains a drag. Net position near trend.
- **Minority views**: A few raised concerns about household caution and slow wage growth limiting spending.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 30 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Contacts in a number of areas indicated that credit was readily available, and reports from participants' business and financial contacts indicated a strengthening in demand for bank credit." "Difficulty in obtaining mortgage credit, particularly for younger, first-time homebuyers." "Vulnerabilities in the financial system were at low to moderate levels."
- **Center of gravity**: Financial conditions generally accommodative/loose with credit readily available, though mortgage credit tight for some segments.
- **Minority views**: Mortgage credit difficulty partially offsets the generally loose conditions.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 47 +/-8 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Many participants noted that if convergence toward the Committee's objectives occurred more quickly than expected, it might become appropriate to begin removing monetary policy accommodation sooner than they currently anticipated." "Some participants viewed the actual and expected progress toward the Committee's goals as sufficient to call for a relatively prompt move toward reducing policy accommodation." "Most participants indicated that any change in their expectations for the appropriate timing of the first increase in the federal funds rate would depend on further information." "Several participants continued to believe that inflation was likely to move back to the Committee's objective very slowly, thereby warranting a continuation of highly accommodative policy."
- **Center of gravity**: Data-dependent with continued tapering; majority wants to wait for more evidence before signaling rate hikes, but a vocal minority pushes for sooner normalization.
- **Minority views**: "Some" want prompt tightening (hawkish); "several" want prolonged accommodation (dovish). Wide band reflects this spread.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 40 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Participants differed, however, in their assessments of the remaining degree of labor market slack and how to measure it." "These participants were increasingly uncomfortable with the Committee's forward guidance." Split on inflation risks -- "most" see diminished downside vs "a few" see persistent below-target vs "a few others" see increased upside.
- **Center of gravity**: Notable disagreements on labor market slack measurement, appropriate pace of normalization, and inflation risk balance. Multiple identifiable camps, no formal dissents mentioned.
- **Minority views**: Disagreement spans economic assessment (labor slack) and policy prescription (timing of liftoff).

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 30 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "In their discussion of financial stability issues, participants noted evidence of valuation pressures in some particular asset markets, but those pressures did not appear to be widespread." "Some participants discussed how the Committee might better incorporate financial stability risks in its discussion of macroeconomic risks."
- **Center of gravity**: Dedicated but brief discussion; valuation pressures noted but viewed as contained. Some interest in incorporating financial stability into the framework.
- **Minority views**: None notable -- general agreement that risks were well contained.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 35 +/-8 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Most participants indicated that any change in their expectations for the appropriate timing of the first increase in the federal funds rate would depend on further information on the trajectories of economic activity, the labor market, and inflation." "Some participants... suggested that the guidance should more clearly communicate how policy-setting would respond to the evolution of economic data." "Several participants continued to believe that inflation was likely to move back to the Committee's objective very slowly, thereby warranting a continuation of highly accommodative policy as long as projected inflation remained below 2 percent."
- **Center of gravity**: Forward signal is still accommodative overall -- continued tapering with data dependence for liftoff. Pressure from some to shift guidance in a hawkish direction but majority holds.
- **Minority views**: Some push for more hawkish guidance; several want prolonged accommodation.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Most participants continued to view the risks to the outlook for economic activity and the labor market as nearly balanced." "Some pointed to possible sources of downside risk, including persistent weakness in the housing sector, a continued slow rise in household income, or spillovers from developments in the Middle East and Ukraine." "Some noted that it increased uncertainty about the outlook" regarding Q1 GDP drop.
- **Center of gravity**: Risks largely balanced; moderate uncertainty with some specific concerns noted. Not elevated.
- **Minority views**: Some see specific downside risks from housing, geopolitics.

### Optional Dimensions

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Score 48 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
- "Inflation expectations remained stable." "Several reported results from various statistical models of inflation and inflation expectations." "A few others argued that the recent tightening of the labor market had increased the upside risks to inflation and inflation expectations." Expectations anchored near target, stable. Slightly below 50 because the context is still below-target inflation.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Score 55 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
- "Continued rise in house prices." "Rising demand for rentals." Shelter costs rising but discussed mainly in housing activity context rather than inflation sub-component. Modest upward pressure.

**CREDIT_STRESS**: Score 35 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
- "Credit was readily available" and "strengthening in demand for bank credit." But "difficulty in obtaining mortgage credit, particularly for younger, first-time homebuyers." Below-normal stress overall.
