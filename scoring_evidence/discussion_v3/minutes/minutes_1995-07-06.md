# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1995-07-06

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Unsegmented pre-2009 format, but participant language cues are clear throughout ("members commented," "many emphasized," "most members"). There is one staff projection reference regarding M2/M3 growth which I will ignore for scoring.

**Era context**: Mid-1995. The economy experienced a soft patch / growth pause after the 1994-95 tightening cycle. Inventory correction underway. Long-term rates had come down, equity prices rising. The Fed was considering easing.

**Quantifier distribution**: "most members" (downside risk tilt, inflation deceleration), "many" (strengthening prospects, easing resource pressures, export downside risk, inflation moderation), "several" (inventory adjustment concerns), "some" (less inventory correction than expected, inflation risks remain, export growth risks), "a few" not prominently used. The center of gravity is cautiously optimistic about a recovery from a soft patch, with inflation pressures seen as diminishing.

**Dimension-by-dimension reasoning**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Members say "risk of continuing increases in inflation had fallen considerably," "inflation should moderate," "underlying trend of inflation was now tilted toward gradual deceleration." However, there's still acknowledgment of an earlier "uptick in inflation," raw material price increases, and dollar depreciation effects. CPI forecasts around 3-3.375% for 1995 (above what we'd now call the 2% target, but in 1995 context this was moderate). The center of gravity sees inflation as modestly elevated but clearly easing. Score should be in the 45-55 range -- pressures present but diminishing. The "inflation risks had diminished" language and "gradual deceleration" tilt it slightly below 50. Direction: slightly below midpoint, around 45.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: "apparent pause in the expansion," economy expected to return to "a pace in the neighborhood of the economy's potential." Growth forecasts 1.5-2% for 1995 (below potential), expected to pick up to 2.25-2.75% in 1996. "Risks tilted to the downside." "Pressures on resources having diminished." This is clearly below potential currently, expected to return to potential. Score should be below 50 -- around 42-45. The economy is below potential but not deeply so, with recovery expected.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment expected to edge higher to 5.75-6.125%. "Labor costs remaining subdued." "Continuing reports of labor scarcities in some areas and industries" but "increases in nominal labor costs generally had remained subdued." "Marginally higher rates of unemployed labor." This is near but slightly below full employment with some slack emerging. Score around 48-50. The mixed signals (some scarcities but subdued costs, rising unemployment) put it right near balanced.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer expenditures "fallen short of earlier expectations" but "signs of some firming." Business fixed investment "moderated since earlier in the year" but "still registering strong gains." Housing "stagnated" but expected to improve. Members "generally expected a resumption of moderate growth." This is below trend currently but not contracting -- around 42-46. The moderation is real but not severe.

5. **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Favorable financial climate," "stimulative effects of lower interest rates," "ready availability of financing from market sources and banking institutions," "rising equity and bond prices." Financial conditions are clearly loose/accommodative. Score should be low -- around 25-30. Conditions are described as supportive and stimulative.

6. **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The discussion strongly leans dovish. Risks are "tilted to the downside," inflation pressures diminishing, growth below potential. The text mentions the possibility of "some easing in policy were to be approved during this meeting." Members are focused on downside risks. This is a clear easing lean -- around 35-38. Not emergency dovish, but moderate dovish with a policy easing on the table.

7. **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: On the economic outlook, there's broad agreement (most see downside risks, many see inflation moderating). On M2 ranges, there's a split: "majority favored or could accept" current range vs. "members preferring a somewhat higher M2 range." But this is a technical disagreement, not a fundamental policy split. On M3, "all members" agreed. Overall moderate agreement with some differences. Score around 25-30.

8. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial conditions are discussed but purely as a positive backdrop (lower rates, rising equity prices, credit availability). No concern about instability, bubbles, or stress. This is early-to-mid 1990s -- financial stability was not a focus. Score around 10-15. Brief mentions of financial conditions as context, not as a stability concern.

9. **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The text hints at easing ("some easing in policy were to be approved during this meeting"). The forward outlook is data-dependent with downside risk emphasis. Members expect growth to recover but are prepared to ease. This signals a mild easing bias -- around 35-38. Not aggressive easing signaling but a lean toward accommodation.

10. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Risks tilted to the downside," "timing remained uncertain," "substantial uncertainty remained about projections of money growth," members acknowledge risks are "substantial." Several sources of uncertainty identified (inventories, exports, fiscal policy effects). Score around 40-45. Somewhat elevated uncertainty.

**Cross-dimension coherence check**:
- OUTPUT_GAP (43) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (49): These are close and consistent -- economy slightly below potential, labor market near balance but with emerging slack. The small gap is explained by labor market being a lagging indicator.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (37) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (36): Both lean dovish, consistent.

**Polarity checks**:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says pressures diminishing, moderate level. Score 46 = slightly below midpoint. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says below potential. Score 43 = below midpoint. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says loose/accommodative. Score 27 = well below midpoint. Matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 46 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the risk of continuing increases in inflation had fallen considerably; indeed, in the view of many members inflation should moderate over the projection period"
- "most of the members believed that the underlying trend of inflation was now tilted toward gradual deceleration"
- "some still saw the potential for little or no progress in unwinding the recent uptick in inflation"
- CPI forecasts: 3.125-3.375% for 1995, 2.875-3.25% for 1996

**Center-of-gravity**: Members see inflation as modestly present (above what we'd consider target) but clearly diminishing, with resource pressures easing. The "recent uptick" is acknowledged but expected to reverse.

**Minority views**: "Some still saw the potential for little or no progress in unwinding the recent uptick in inflation" -- this widens the band slightly upward.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 43 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the apparent pause in the expansion was likely to prove temporary, and their forecasts generally pointed to an upturn in overall economic activity to a pace in the neighborhood of the economy's potential"
- "much of the economic information... had suggested a greater softening in the economy than they had anticipated"
- "risks to the outlook were tilted to the downside"
- Real GDP growth central tendency: 1.5-2% for 1995 (below potential)

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is currently below potential due to the growth pause and inventory correction, but expected to return to potential by late 1995 or early 1996. Downside risks predominate.

**Minority views**: Some noted "most recent data and some of the anecdotal reports... had a better tone," slightly offsetting the downside lean.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 49 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "pressures on resources having diminished and likely to ease somewhat further"
- "despite continuing reports of labor scarcities in some areas and industries, increases in nominal labor costs generally had remained subdued"
- Unemployment expected to edge higher to 5.75-6.125%
- "marginally higher rates of unemployed labor and other resources"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is near balance but with slight slack emerging. Some localized scarcities persist but overall labor cost pressures are contained and unemployment is expected to edge higher.

**Minority views**: None notable -- broad agreement on this dimension.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 44 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "consumer expenditures had fallen short of earlier expectations, but signs of some firming were visible"
- "Business fixed investment appeared to have moderated since earlier in the year, though expenditures... were still registering strong gains"
- "Housing activity had stagnated in recent months"
- "members generally expected a resumption of moderate growth in consumer spending"

**Center-of-gravity**: Spending and investment are below recent trend -- consumer spending disappointed, business investment moderating, housing stagnant. However, levels remain positive (not contracting) and recovery is expected.

**Minority views**: "some concern was expressed about the vulnerability of capital spending to a downturn in the growth of sales"

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 27 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "favorable financial climate, including the stimulative effects of lower interest rates on interest-sensitive sectors of the economy"
- "ready availability of financing from market sources and banking institutions"
- "impact of rising equity and bond prices on balance sheets"
- "ready availability of financing on favorable terms"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly accommodative/loose. Multiple references to easy credit, lower rates, rising asset prices, and favorable financing terms. No signs of tightness anywhere.

**Minority views**: None -- unanimously positive assessment of financial conditions.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 37 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "risks to the outlook were tilted to the downside"
- "the risk of continuing increases in inflation had fallen considerably"
- Reference to "some easing in policy were to be approved during this meeting"
- Focus on downside risks (inventories, exports) rather than overheating

**Center-of-gravity**: Members lean dovish. The growth pause, diminished inflation risks, and downside risk tilt all point toward an easing bias. Policy easing is actively being considered at this meeting.

**Minority views**: "Some still saw the potential for little or no progress in unwinding the recent uptick in inflation" -- a small hawkish minority concerned about inflation persistence.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 27 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Broad agreement on economic outlook: "most" see downside risks, "many" see inflation moderating
- Technical disagreement on M2 range: "majority favored... existing 1 to 5 percent range" vs. "members preferring a somewhat higher M2 range"
- "The Committee was unanimous" on debt monitoring range
- M3 range: "all the members indicated that they preferred or could accept an increase"

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on the economic outlook and policy direction. The main disagreement is technical (M2 range), not fundamental. Minor differences in inflation risk assessment.

**Minority views**: M2 range dissenters and inflation-concerned members create mild divergence.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 12 +/-4 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Financial conditions discussed as backdrop: "favorable financial climate," "ready availability of financing," "rising equity and bond prices"
- No discussion of financial stability risks, bubbles, excessive leverage, or systemic concerns
- Financial health of depository institutions mentioned positively: "financial health of depository institutions had improved markedly"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are mentioned as a supporting factor for the economy but not as a stability concern. This is consistent with mid-1990s meetings where financial stability was not a primary focus.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 36 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "some easing in policy were to be approved during this meeting" -- easing direction signaled
- Downside risk emphasis suggests continued accommodative lean going forward
- "monetary policy makers would need to carefully monitor possible transition effects" of fiscal consolidation
- No forward tightening signals whatsoever

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is mildly dovish/easing-biased. Members are focused on downside risks and inflation moderation, with easing on the table. However, this is not aggressive easing -- more a data-dependent lean toward accommodation.

**Minority views**: Those concerned about inflation persistence may have preferred a more neutral forward stance.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 42 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "risks to the outlook were tilted to the downside"
- "the ongoing adjustments to business inventories could prove to be more pronounced and of longer duration than they anticipated"
- "the timing remained uncertain, a resumption of growth at a moderate rate was viewed as a likely prospect"
- "substantial uncertainty remained about projections of money growth"
- "they acknowledged that the risks to such an outcome remained substantial"

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is somewhat elevated. Members identify multiple risk factors (inventories, exports, fiscal transition effects) and acknowledge substantial risks, though they have a baseline expectation of recovery. Not extreme uncertainty but clearly above normal.
