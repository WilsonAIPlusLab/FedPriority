# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2015-01-28 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Post-2009 segmented format with clear participant language cues ("participants," "members," "many," "several," "a few").

**Era context**: January 2015. The economy was in a solid expansion phase following the GFC recovery. Oil prices had fallen sharply. The fed funds rate was still at the effective lower bound, and the Committee was actively debating the timing of liftoff. Inflation was well below 2% largely due to energy price declines. Labor market was improving but still had residual slack.

**Quantifier distribution**: "participants" (general/most) used broadly; "many" used for those cautious about premature tightening and those wanting to drop "patient" language; "several" and "some" used for sub-topics; "a few" used for minority views on both sides; "one participant" recommended more accommodation.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as well BELOW target. "Inflation had declined further below the Committee's longer-run objective, largely reflecting declines in energy prices." Core inflation also moved down. "Several participants saw the continuing weakness of core inflation measures as a concern." Market-based inflation compensation declined substantially. However, participants "generally anticipated that inflation would rise gradually toward 2 percent" over time. The current reading is clearly below target with some concern, but most view it as transitory. Direction: below midpoint. Score should be in the 25-35 range. Energy-driven deflation concerns plus core weakness, but expectation of return to target prevents going lower. Polarity check: higher = more pressure. Evidence says less pressure. Score below 50. Correct.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as "expanding at a solid pace" and "real GDP continued to grow faster than potential GDP late in the year." This is explicit "above potential" language. The bright-line rule requires > 50. However, language also says "the underutilization of labor resources was continuing to diminish" — so there is still some slack. The economy is growing above trend (closing the gap) but the level is near potential or slightly below. The key phrase is "grow faster than potential GDP" which describes growth rate, not level. Other language: "slack continued to diminish" implies the output gap level is still slightly below potential. But growth is "solid" and above trend. I will score this around 47-49, reflecting that the level is still slightly below potential (slack diminishing) even though the growth rate exceeds potential growth. Polarity check: higher = more above potential. Slack still diminishing = still slightly below. Score just under 50. Correct.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor market conditions improved further, with strong job gains and a lower unemployment rate." "Underutilization of labor resources was continuing to diminish." "Some participants believed that considerable labor market slack remained, especially when indicators other than the unemployment rate were taken into account, including the unusually large fraction of the labor force working part time for economic reasons." "A few others saw only a limited degree of remaining labor underutilization or anticipated that underutilization would be eliminated relatively soon." Center of gravity: improving but still below full employment. Score around 42-45. Polarity check: higher = tighter. Still some slack = below 50. Correct.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Household spending was rising moderately." Oil price declines boosted purchasing power. Low interest rates, easing credit standards, continued employment gains supporting spending. But "recovery in the housing sector remained slow" and "tepid nominal wage growth...could become a significant restraining factor." Business conditions "generally solid" with optimism. Capital investment "poised to pick up" but energy sector investment could slow. Net: moderate, around trend. Score around 50-52. Polarity check: higher = stronger. Moderate/solid = near midpoint. Correct.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Longer-term interest rates declined substantially. "A couple of participants noted that the reduction in longer-term real interest rates tended to make U.S. financial conditions more accommodative." "Easing credit standards" mentioned. Foreign central bank actions driving flows into Treasuries pushing yields down. Some concern about dollar appreciation creating restraint on net exports. Overall, conditions described as accommodative/loose. Score around 30-35. Polarity check: higher = tighter. Conditions described as accommodative = below 50. Correct.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: This is the pre-liftoff debate. "Many participants indicated that their assessment of the balance of risks...had inclined them toward keeping the federal funds rate at its effective lower bound for a longer time." "Some observed that...it might be appropriate to begin policy firming in the near term." "One participant recommended...more, not less, accommodation." The center of gravity is hold-with-dovish-lean, but the discussion is clearly about when to begin normalization (not whether). Many lean toward patience. Score around 44-47. The anchor for 2014-15 pre-liftoff is 40-50. Polarity check: higher = more hawkish. Majority leans toward patience/hold longer = slightly dovish side. Correct.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: There is notable disagreement. The document shows a range from one participant wanting more accommodation to some wanting near-term firming. Many want to hold longer, some want to start sooner. The "patient" language debate shows concern about market reactions. Different views on inflation compensation interpretation. No formal dissents mentioned, but clear camps. Score around 40-45. Polarity check: higher = more disagreement. Clear camps but no formal dissents. Moderate disagreement. Correct.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Some discussion of financial stability: "maintaining the federal funds rate at its effective lower bound for an extended period or raising it rapidly...could adversely affect financial stability." Discussion of market-based inflation compensation, longer-term interest rate declines, dollar appreciation, financial conditions. But financial stability is not a dominant theme — it is a secondary consideration in the policy timing debate. Score around 30-35. Polarity check: higher = more attention. Limited but present. Correct.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The discussion is about approaching normalization but not yet. "Many participants regarded dropping the 'patient' language...as risking a shift in market expectations." They want to signal data dependence. The forward signal leans toward eventual tightening but with patience. "Provided that the data-dependent nature of the path...could be communicated...the precise date at which firming commenced would have a less important bearing." Score around 50-55. The Committee is tilting toward eventual normalization but not yet committing. The anchor for 2015 pre-liftoff is 25-40, but this meeting is well into that period and normalization is actively being discussed as a near-term prospect, so slightly higher. Score 52. Polarity check: higher = more hawkish forward signal. Leaning toward eventual normalization but patient = slightly above neutral. Correct.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Risks to the outlook for economic activity and the labor market were seen as nearly balanced." But there is "wide agreement that it would be difficult to specify in advance an exhaustive list of economic indicators." Discussion of foreign risks (China, Greece, geopolitical). Debate about inflation compensation interpretation. "Uncertainties about the inflation outlook" explicitly mentioned. Score around 40-45. Elevated somewhat but not extreme. Polarity check: higher = more uncertainty. Moderate uncertainty language. Correct.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (48) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (43): Both slightly below 50, coherent. Labor slightly lower because explicit "considerable slack" language from some participants.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (45) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (52): Slight divergence is explained by the fact that current action is hold (dovish lean) but the forward signal is toward eventual normalization (mildly hawkish forward). This is the classic pre-liftoff pattern.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 28 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Inflation had declined further below the Committee's longer-run objective, largely reflecting declines in energy prices"
- "Several participants remarked that inflation measures that excluded energy items had also moved down in recent months"
- "Several participants saw the continuing weakness of core inflation measures as a concern"
- "Market-based measures of inflation compensation 5 to 10 years ahead had registered a further decline"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants clearly see inflation well below target, driven by energy but with core also softening. Most expect a gradual return to 2% but there is genuine concern about the decline in inflation compensation and weak core readings.

**Minority views**: "A few participants voiced concern that nominal wage growth might rise rapidly and inflation might exceed 2 percent for a time" (upside minority). "A few participants suggested that the weakness of nominal wage growth indicated that core and headline inflation could take longer to return to 2 percent" (downside minority). Band reflects these dispersed views.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 48 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity had been expanding at a solid pace"
- "Real GDP continued to grow faster than potential GDP late in the year and during January"
- "The underutilization of labor resources was continuing to diminish"
- "Risks to the outlook for economic activity and the labor market were seen as nearly balanced"

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is growing above its potential growth rate, which means the remaining (small) output gap is closing. The level is near but still slightly below potential, as underutilization is "continuing to diminish" (not yet eliminated).

**Minority views**: Narrow range of views on this dimension; broad agreement on solid growth trajectory.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 43 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Labor market conditions improved further, with strong job gains and a lower unemployment rate"
- "Some participants believed that considerable labor market slack remained, especially when indicators other than the unemployment rate were taken into account, including the unusually large fraction of the labor force working part time for economic reasons"
- "A few others saw only a limited degree of remaining labor underutilization or anticipated that underutilization would be eliminated relatively soon"

**Center-of-gravity**: Improving but still below full employment. The center sees diminishing but still meaningful slack.

**Minority views**: Range from "considerable slack" (some) to "limited underutilization" (a few), producing a wider uncertainty band.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 51 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Household spending was rising moderately"
- "Recent declines in oil prices, which had boosted household purchasing power, were among the factors likely to underpin consumer spending"
- "Industry contacts pointed to generally solid business conditions"
- "Recovery in the housing sector remained slow" and energy sector investment could damp activity

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending moderate with positive tailwinds from oil; business conditions solid; but housing slow and energy investment a drag. Net assessment is roughly at trend, perhaps slightly above.

**Minority views**: Limited disagreement on this dimension.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 32 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "A couple of participants noted that the reduction in longer-term real interest rates tended to make U.S. financial conditions more accommodative"
- "Easing credit standards" cited as supporting household spending
- "Low interest rates" noted as a positive factor
- Substantial decline in nominal longer-term interest rates noted

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are described as accommodative — low rates, easing credit standards, declining long-term yields. Dollar appreciation provides some offset but the net picture is loose conditions.

**Minority views**: Some noted dollar strength as a restraining factor, but this was more about trade effects than overall financial tightness.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 45 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Many participants indicated that their assessment of the balance of risks...had inclined them toward keeping the federal funds rate at its effective lower bound for a longer time"
- "Some observed that...it might be appropriate to begin policy firming in the near term"
- "One participant recommended that...the Committee consider ways to use its tools to provide more, not less, accommodation"
- Active discussion of when to begin normalization — liftoff is on the table

**Center-of-gravity**: The majority leans toward patience/holding longer at the lower bound, but a significant minority sees near-term firming as appropriate. The overall lean is slightly dovish — hold with eventual normalization in mind.

**Minority views**: From one participant wanting more accommodation to some wanting near-term firming, producing a wide band.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 42 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Range from one participant wanting more accommodation to some wanting near-term firming
- Disagreement on inflation compensation interpretation: "A number of them judged that the decline mostly reflected a reduction in the risk premiums" vs. "other participants put some weight on the possibility that the decline...reflected a reduction in expected inflation"
- Disagreement on labor slack: "Some...considerable labor market slack remained" vs. "a few others saw only a limited degree of remaining labor underutilization"

**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate disagreement across multiple dimensions — timing of liftoff, inflation outlook interpretation, labor market slack assessment. No formal dissents but distinct camps with different views.

**Minority views**: N/A (this dimension captures the spread itself).

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 30 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Maintaining the federal funds rate at its effective lower bound for an extended period or raising it rapidly...could adversely affect financial stability"
- Discussion of longer-term interest rate declines and market-based inflation compensation
- Dollar appreciation and foreign capital flows discussed

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability is mentioned as one consideration in the liftoff timing debate and receives some discussion regarding market dynamics, but it is not a prominent or central topic. Most discussion focuses on real economy conditions and inflation.

**Minority views**: Limited range on this dimension.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 52 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Discussion about approaching normalization: "the federal funds rate may have already been kept at its lower bound for a sufficient length of time"
- "Many participants regarded dropping the 'patient' language...as risking a shift in market expectations"
- "A number of participants suggested that they would need to see further improvement in labor market conditions...before beginning policy normalization"
- "The Committee should communicate clearly that policy decisions will be data data-dependent"

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is roughly neutral with a very mild tilt toward eventual tightening. Normalization is on the horizon but the Committee is not yet ready to signal it strongly. Data dependence is emphasized.

**Minority views**: Range from those ready to signal near-term firming to those wanting more accommodation, but center is data-dependent patience with normalization approaching.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 42 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The risks to the outlook for economic activity and the labor market were seen as nearly balanced"
- "Wide agreement that it would be difficult to specify in advance an exhaustive list of economic indicators"
- "Many participants continued to judge that a deterioration in the foreign economic situation could pose downside risks"
- "Uncertainties about the inflation outlook" noted; debate about inflation compensation interpretation

**Center-of-gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty, particularly around inflation dynamics and international developments. Risks seen as "nearly balanced" but with notable concerns about foreign developments, inflation compensation, and the uncharted nature of the liftoff decision.

**Minority views**: Limited disagreement on the level of uncertainty; broad agreement it was somewhat elevated.
