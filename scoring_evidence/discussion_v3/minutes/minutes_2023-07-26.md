# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2023-07-26

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented -- participant views are clearly separated from staff content.

**Era context**: July 2023. The Fed is deep into its aggressive tightening cycle that began in March 2022. The fed funds rate is being raised to 5.25-5.50% at this meeting. Inflation has come down from its 2022 peak but remains well above target. The economy has been surprisingly resilient despite cumulative tightening.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all/unanimous" used for banking soundness and balance sheet runoff; "almost all" for the rate hike decision; "most" for upside inflation risks; "various" for inflation expectations stability and CRE/banking risks; "several" for core services ex-housing inflation persistence and nonbank risks; "some" for downside risks to activity; "a number of" for two-sided risks and balance sheet runoff timing; "a couple" for preferring no hike.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as clearly above target. Key language: "inflation remained well above the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective," "unacceptably high," "elevated inflation was continuing to harm businesses and households." However, there are "tentative signs that inflation pressures could be abating" -- softening core goods, slower shelter price increases, lower short-term expectations. The tone is: still high but moderating. This is not peak 2022 alarm, but inflation is still a dominant concern. Direction: above midpoint, significantly. Magnitude: "well above" and "unacceptably high" = 15-25 points above midpoint, so ~65-75. But it's moderating from peak -- not accelerating. Score ~68. Polarity check: higher = more pressure. Evidence says elevated but moderating. 68 fits.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic activity had been expanding at a moderate pace." "Real GDP growth had continued to exhibit resilience" and "the economy had been showing considerable momentum." Participants view a "gradual slowdown" as "in progress" but activity is still resilient. They want "below-trend growth" which implies current growth is at or slightly above trend. "Moderate pace" + "considerable momentum" + "resilience" suggests modestly above potential. Bright-line: language leans toward above-trend resilience. Score ~53-55. Polarity check: higher = more above potential. Evidence says moderate/resilient growth, above trend. 54 fits.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor market continued to be very tight" -- explicit language. But "signs that demand and supply were coming into better balance" -- declines in job openings, lower quits, slower wage growth, rising participation. Still, "payrolls had slowed recently, it continued to exceed values consistent over time with an unchanged unemployment rate" and "nominal wages were still rising at rates above levels assessed to be consistent with the sustained achievement of the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective." Direction: tight but easing from peak. "Very tight" = clearly above midpoint. Score ~65. Anchors say 65-75 for 2022-23. The easing signs push toward the lower end. Score 65. Polarity check: higher = tighter. Evidence says very tight but easing. 65 fits.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending showed "considerable resilience" with "strong household balance sheets, robust job and income gains." But expected to slow due to "tight financial conditions," "declining stock of excess savings," "softening labor market conditions." Business side: "various improvements in firms' cost structures" but also expectation that "firms would reduce the pace of their investment spending and hiring." Manufacturing weakness noted. Net: currently solid/above trend but expected moderation. Score the current perception: resilient, above trend. ~57. Polarity check: higher = stronger. 57 fits.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Tighter credit conditions for households and businesses were likely to weigh on economic activity." "Tight financial conditions, primarily reflecting the cumulative effect of the Committee's shift to a restrictive policy stance." "Ongoing tightening of credit conditions in the banking sector." Participants see conditions as tight and tightening further via bank credit. "Putting the stance of monetary policy further into restrictive territory." Score ~63-67. Anchors: 60-70 for 2022 rate hikes era. The banking credit tightening adds to this. Score 65. Polarity check: higher = tighter. Evidence clearly says tight. 65 fits.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Almost all" judged it appropriate to raise rates to 5.25-5.50%. "A couple" favored holding. "Most participants continued to see significant upside risks to inflation, which could require further tightening." Forward-looking: data-dependent, "extent of additional policy firming that may be appropriate." "A number of participants judged that...risks to the achievement of the Committee's goals had become more two sided." This is a hike meeting with most seeing potential for more, but with growing two-sided risk awareness. The couple who preferred holding plus the two-sided risk language tempers the hawkishness slightly. Score ~70. Anchors: 70-75 for hiking cycle. The "data-dependent" and "two-sided" language pulls below 75. Score 70.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Almost all" supported the hike; "a couple" preferred holding or could have supported holding. No formal dissents mentioned in voting text (all members agreed per the Committee Policy Actions section -- "members agreed to raise"). The "a couple" language in the participant discussion refers to participants, not voting members necessarily. The two-sided risk discussion shows some spread in views. Overall broad agreement with minor differences. Score ~25-30. Score 27.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: A dedicated paragraph on financial stability discussion. Topics: banking system sound/resilient, stress test results, unrealized losses, uninsured deposits, CRE risks, nonbank institutions, liquidity facilities. "Various participants" and "several participants" engaged. This is moderate-to-meaningful discussion, not just passing. Score ~42. Anchors: 20-35 typical for 2013-19; the banking stress discussion in 2023 raises it. Score 42.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is data-dependent with a tightening bias. "Stance of monetary policy be sufficiently restrictive to return inflation to 2 percent." "Most participants continued to see significant upside risks to inflation, which could require further tightening." But also: "a number of participants judged that...risks...had become more two sided" and emphasis on data dependence. The signal is: possibly more tightening but not committed. Score ~72. This is a tightening bias but data-dependent. Anchors: 80-95 for aggressive 2022 hiking. By mid-2023 the forward signal is less aggressive. Score 72.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants generally noted a high degree of uncertainty regarding the cumulative effects on the economy of past monetary policy tightening." "Uncertainty about the economic outlook remained elevated." "The extent of these effects remained uncertain." Multiple references to uncertainty about lags. This is elevated uncertainty, explicitly stated. Score ~55-60. "High degree of uncertainty" and "elevated" push toward upper range. Score 58.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (54) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (65): Some divergence. Labor market is tighter than the output gap suggests. This is consistent with the text -- labor market described as "very tight" while GDP growth is just "moderate pace." The labor market lagged the economic slowdown. Explained.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (70) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (72): Aligned. Both moderately hawkish with data dependence tempering.

### Anchor sanity:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 68 for July 2023: Inflation moderating from 2022 peak (anchors 82-88). 68 is below peak but still elevated. Consistent.
- OUTPUT_GAP 54 for 2023: Economy resilient, above trend. Consistent with late-cycle above-potential reading.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 68 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "inflation remained well above the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective and that elevated inflation was continuing to harm businesses and households"
- "inflation remained unacceptably high and that further evidence would be required for them to be confident that inflation was clearly on a path toward the Committee's 2 percent objective"
- "Participants cited a number of tentative signs that inflation pressures could be abating"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants view inflation as still significantly above target and harmful, but acknowledge tentative signs of moderation -- placing this clearly above midpoint but well below the 2022 peak alarm levels.

**Minority views**: "Several participants commented that significant disinflationary pressures had yet to become apparent in the prices of core services excluding housing" -- more concerned minority pushes uncertainty band upward.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 54 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "economic activity had been expanding at a moderate pace"
- "real GDP growth had continued to exhibit resilience in the first half of the year and that the economy had been showing considerable momentum"
- "A gradual slowdown in economic activity nevertheless appeared to be in progress"

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is operating modestly above potential -- resilient growth with "considerable momentum" despite cumulative tightening, though a slowdown is underway.

**Minority views**: Some participants emphasized downside risks from cumulative tightening effects, widening the band slightly.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 65 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the labor market continued to be very tight"
- "signs that demand and supply were coming into better balance" -- "declines in job openings, lower quits rates, more part-time work, slower growth in hours worked"
- "nominal wages were still rising at rates above levels assessed to be consistent with the sustained achievement of the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is very tight but clearly easing from its peak, with multiple indicators showing rebalancing. Still well above full employment equilibrium.

**Minority views**: Narrow range of views -- all acknowledge both tightness and easing signs.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 57 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "consumer spending had recently exhibited considerable resilience, underpinned by...strong household balance sheets, robust job and income gains"
- "firms would reduce the pace of their investment spending and hiring in response to tight financial conditions"
- "factors that could lead to lower economic activity -- such as continuing economic uncertainty, the vulnerabilities of the CRE market, and the ongoing weakness of manufacturing output"

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending is resilient and above trend, while business investment shows mixed signals with manufacturing weakness. Net assessment is above trend but expected to moderate.

**Minority views**: Mixed signals between resilient consumption and weakening business/manufacturing spending create some spread.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 65 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "tighter credit conditions for households and businesses were likely to weigh on economic activity, hiring, and inflation"
- "tight financial conditions, primarily reflecting the cumulative effect of the Committee's shift to a restrictive policy stance"
- "ongoing tightening of credit conditions in the banking sector, as evidenced in the most recent surveys of banks"

**Center-of-gravity**: Conditions are clearly tight and tightening further through the bank credit channel, consistent with cumulative monetary policy tightening. Not crisis-level but meaningfully restrictive.

**Minority views**: Broad agreement on tight conditions; uncertainty is about the magnitude of future effects, not the current state.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 70 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "almost all participants judged it appropriate to raise the target range for the federal funds rate to 5-1/4 to 5-1/2 percent"
- "most participants continued to see significant upside risks to inflation, which could require further tightening of monetary policy"
- "A couple of participants indicated that they favored leaving the target range for the federal funds rate unchanged"

**Center-of-gravity**: Strong hawkish consensus for this hike with most seeing potential need for additional tightening, tempered by growing acknowledgment of two-sided risks and data dependence.

**Minority views**: "A couple" preferred holding; "a number" saw risks as more two-sided -- these pull the uncertainty band toward the dovish side.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 27 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "almost all participants judged it appropriate to raise the target range" (near-consensus on action)
- "A couple of participants indicated that they favored leaving the target range...unchanged or that they could have supported such a proposal"
- "a number of participants judged that...risks to the achievement of the Committee's goals had become more two sided"

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on the rate hike with only a couple of mild dissenters. Some divergence on forward outlook (two-sided risks vs. upside inflation risks) but no fundamental disagreement.

**Minority views**: The "couple" preferring to hold and the divergence on risk balance add moderate texture but not deep division.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 42 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "participants observed that the banking system was sound and resilient and that banking stress had calmed in recent months"
- "Various participants commented on risks that could affect some banks, including unrealized losses on assets...significant reliance on uninsured deposits, and increased funding costs"
- "Several participants noted the susceptibility of some nonbank financial institutions...to runs or instability"

**Center-of-gravity**: A dedicated and substantive financial stability discussion covering banking, CRE, nonbanks, and liquidity readiness. More than routine but not a dominant meeting theme -- reflecting the post-SVB environment.

**Minority views**: Views largely aligned that banking system is sound but risks warrant monitoring.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 72 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "it was critical that the stance of monetary policy be sufficiently restrictive to return inflation to the Committee's 2 percent objective over time"
- "most participants continued to see significant upside risks to inflation, which could require further tightening"
- "policy decisions at future meetings should depend on the totality of the incoming information" (data dependence)

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal leans hawkish -- most see potential need for further tightening -- but is wrapped in data-dependence language rather than a commitment to additional hikes. Not as aggressive as 2022 forward guidance.

**Minority views**: "A number" saw two-sided risks, and emphasis on data dependence introduces some ambiguity about the forward path.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 58 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants generally noted a high degree of uncertainty regarding the cumulative effects on the economy of past monetary policy tightening"
- "uncertainty about the economic outlook remained elevated"
- "discussed the extent to which the effects on the economy stemming from the tightening...had already materialized"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants express elevated uncertainty, explicitly characterized as "high degree" and "elevated," primarily about the lagged effects of cumulative tightening. This goes beyond routine hedging.

**Minority views**: Broad agreement on elevated uncertainty; no notable divergence.
