I will score ONLY portions with participant language cues ("participants noted," "participants agreed," "many participants worried," etc.) and ignore staff forecast sections.

Financial markets are in severe turmoil. The Fed held the funds rate at 2.0% at this meeting (did not cut). This is one of the most consequential FOMC meetings in modern history.

**Dimension-by-dimension reasoning**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as having been elevated but moderating. "Participants generally thought that the outlook for inflation had improved" due to falling oil/commodities, stronger dollar, weakening labor market. But "recent high inflation readings suggested that price pressures could persist" and "firms were continuing to attempt to pass through" costs. The center of gravity is: inflation had been elevated, but is now expected to moderate. Current perception is still somewhat above target but declining. Direction: modestly above midpoint but trending down. Score in 55-62 range. The "somewhat more confident about moderation" language pulls it down from where it would otherwise be. I'll score 57.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy well below potential. "Economic growth was likely to be sluggish in the second half of 2008." "Sharp deceleration in economic activity in the third quarter." "Consumer spending softened appreciably." "Contraction in the housing sector." "Increased economic slack." The bright-line rule: "increased economic slack" = below potential = must be < 50. Several had marked down their outlook. Deep weakness described. Score: 28. The economy is in serious trouble but participants still expected gradual recovery -- not yet at Depression-level language.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "The fall in employment in August highlighted concerns that an adverse dynamic was taking hold." "Deterioration in labor market conditions over the summer." "Weakening of the labor market." Clear deterioration but not yet Depression-level. Score: 28.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Recent readings on consumer spending had been weak despite the tax rebates." "Consumption may remain soft." "Many participants worried" about deterioration. Business spending "had held up well over the summer" but "investment spending was likely to be on the soft side going forward." Net: consumer spending weak, business investment holding but expected to soften. Score: 28 -- contracting/sharp pullback territory for consumption, with business investment partially offsetting.

5. **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: This is the Lehman weekend. "Financial market strains had intensified." "Increasing difficulty in obtaining funding and raising capital." "Further tightening of credit availability." "Credit conditions generally tight." "Short-term funding markets remained severely strained." This is at or near crisis-level tightness. Score: 88.

6. **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The Committee held rates at 2.0% despite the Lehman crisis. The document is truncated before the full policy discussion, but the available text shows deep concern about economic weakness and financial stress, with inflation being the restraining factor. The decision to hold (not cut) despite Lehman suggests a balanced-to-mildly-dovish stance where inflation concerns prevented easing. "Participants agreed that economic growth was likely to be sluggish" but inflation concerns remained. The hold decision with dual concerns suggests: ~42 (lean dovish to balanced -- dovish impulses restrained by inflation worry).

7. **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: The document doesn't clearly describe the vote or formal dissents in the available text. However, the discussion shows "participants agreed" on sluggish growth but some divergence: "several had marked down" vs "most continued to expect gradual recovery." The inflation discussion shows "generally somewhat more confident" but with caveats about persistent pressures. Moderate differences exist. Score: 35.

8. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: This meeting is dominated by financial stability concerns. Lehman bankruptcy, AIG distress, funding market strains, bank capital needs -- these are discussed extensively throughout. "Strains on the financial system, and their interactions with housing developments and the real economy" -- the adverse feedback loop is a major theme. Financial stability IS a dominant topic at this meeting. Score: 90.

9. **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Document is truncated before the full forward guidance discussion. From available text, the tone is one of uncertainty about the path ahead, with inflation concerns preventing a clear easing signal despite severe financial stress. "Highly uncertain about future financial developments." The hold decision with no clear signal of imminent easing suggests a data-dependent/mildly dovish lean. Score: 38 (easing lean with data dependence, restrained by inflation).

10. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants were highly uncertain about future financial developments and their implications for the broader economy." "Economic prospects relatively uncertain." Financial crisis unfolding in real time. This is textbook high uncertainty. "Highly uncertain" = 70-80 range per rubric. Score: 78.

**Cross-dimension coherence checks**:
- OUTPUT_GAP (28) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (28): Both below 50, consistent -- recession dynamics.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (42) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (38): Both mildly dovish, consistent -- dovish impulses restrained by inflation.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (88) and FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (90): Both very high, consistent for Lehman crisis meeting.

**Polarity checks**:

- OUTPUT_GAP (28): Higher = more above potential. Evidence says significant slack. Below 50. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

**Score: 57 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants acknowledge inflation has been elevated and firms are still passing through costs, but falling commodity prices, stronger dollar, and weakening labor market are expected to bring moderation. Net perception: modestly above target but improving.

**Minority views**: Some participants appear more worried about persistence of price pressures ("recent high inflation readings suggested that price pressures could persist"), widening the band.
