# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2023-02-01 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized participant views). Segmented -- participant discussion section is clearly delineated from staff content.

**Era context**: February 2023 -- the Fed had been hiking aggressively through 2022 (including multiple 75bp hikes) and was now stepping down to 25bp. Inflation had peaked mid-2022 but remained well above 2%. The labor market was historically tight. Growth was below trend but positive.

**Quantifier distribution**: Heavy use of "participants agreed" (consensus language), "several," "a number of," "some," "a few," and "a couple of." "All participants" used for forward guidance on ongoing increases. "Almost all" for the 25bp hike. "A few" for 50bp preference.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as well above target. "Inflation was unacceptably high," "still well above the Committee's longer-run goal of 2 percent" -- consensus language. However, they also note "a welcome reduction in the monthly pace of price increases" and core goods prices declining. The signal is: still very elevated but showing initial signs of easing. This is clearly above 50. "Well above" and "unacceptably high" = strong language, 15-25 points above midpoint. But it's no longer accelerating -- some moderation evident. Anchor: 82-88 for 2022 Q2-Q3 peak. By Feb 2023, inflation is moderating from peak. Score around 72-76. Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says high but easing from peak. Score matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants note "growth in economic activity in 2022 had been below its longer-run trend," "expected that real GDP growth would slow further in 2023," "below-trend growth...would be needed." Consumer spending declined in Nov/Dec. Business investment "subdued." However, the economy is not in recession -- still growing modestly. Language consistently uses "below trend" and "modest growth." Bright-line rule: "below trend" language = below 50. But participants also note "the labor market remained very tight" with demand "substantially exceeding" supply. The output gap assessment is mixed -- below-trend growth but labor market still tight suggests economy is near or modestly above potential even as growth slows. The emphasis on "below-trend growth" is about the growth rate, not the level of output relative to potential. The tight labor market suggests the level is still near or slightly above potential. Score ~48-52. The tension between below-trend growth and very tight labor market suggests approximately at potential but decelerating. Score: 50.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "The labor market remained very tight," "labor demand substantially exceeded the supply of available workers," "unemployment rate had returned to a historically low level," "job vacancies remained high," "wage growth remained elevated." This is consensus language ("participants agreed"). Some tentative signs of improvement but from very high levels. Anchors: 65-75 for 2022-23. "Very tight" with "substantially exceeded" = strong language. Score ~70.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Real consumer spending had declined in November and December," "consumption would likely grow at a subdued rate in 2023," "growth in business fixed investment spending had been subdued," housing "continued to weaken." This is below-trend language across all components. Some support from excess savings but waning. Score: 35-38. "Subdued" and "declined" = below trend.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Financial conditions remained much tighter than in early 2022." However, "several participants observed that some measures of financial conditions had eased over the past few months." Persistently tight but partially easing. Participants noted importance of conditions being consistent with policy restraint. "Much tighter" = clearly elevated. Anchors: 60-70 for 2022 rate hikes. By Feb 2023, still tight but some easing. Score ~62.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Almost all" agreed on 25bp hike (stepping down from 50bp in Dec). "A few" favored 50bp. "All participants continued to anticipate that ongoing increases in the target range...would be appropriate." Strong commitment to returning inflation to 2%. "Restrictive policy stance would need to be maintained." The center of gravity is: hiking, expecting more hikes, but slowing the pace. This is solidly hawkish but less aggressive than late 2022. Anchors: 80-85 for late 2022 75bp hikes. Stepping down to 25bp with continued hike expectations = ~70-73.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Almost all" agreed on 25bp, "a few" preferred 50bp. This is modest disagreement -- the minority wanted MORE, not less. No formal dissents mentioned. Balance sheet runoff was unanimous. Moderate agreement with a small hawkish minority. Score ~30.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: A full dedicated paragraph on financial stability: CRE valuations, nonbank run risk, unrealized bank losses, Treasury market functioning, crypto, climate risk, debt ceiling. "Several participants discussed vulnerabilities," "a few participants commented on international stresses," "a number of participants noted" Treasury market importance, "several participants discussed" climate risk. This is a moderately prominent topic with specific substantive discussion. Score ~45-50.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "All participants continued to anticipate that ongoing increases in the target range for the federal funds rate would be appropriate." "Restrictive policy stance would need to be maintained until...inflation was on a sustained downward path to 2 percent, which was likely to take some time." "A number of participants observed that financial conditions had eased...which some noted could necessitate a tighter stance." Forward signal is clearly hawkish: more hikes coming, maintain restrictive stance. Anchors: 80-95 for 2022 aggressive hiking. By Feb 2023, still signaling further hikes but pace is slowing. Score ~76.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The uncertainty associated with their outlooks for economic activity, the labor market, and inflation was high." Explicit consensus statement. Multiple risk factors cited: China, Ukraine, debt ceiling, recession possibility. "Heightened uncertainty regarding the economic outlook." This is clearly elevated. "High" = 60-75 range. Score ~62.

### Cross-dimension coherence check:
- OUTPUT_GAP (50) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70): These diverge -- growth is at/below trend but labor market very tight. The text explicitly supports this divergence: below-trend growth coexisting with very tight labor because of supply constraints. Valid.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (72) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (76): Aligned -- both solidly hawkish. Forward guidance slightly more hawkish because "all" anticipate ongoing increases, while current action was a step-down.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 74 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Inflation was unacceptably high" (participants agreed)
- "Inflation data received over the past three months showed a welcome reduction in the monthly pace of price increases but stressed that substantially more evidence of progress across a broader range of prices would be required"
- "Core goods prices had declined notably...Participants agreed that they had observed less evidence of a slowdown in the rate of increase of prices for core services excluding housing"

**Center of gravity**: Participants unanimously view inflation as well above target and unacceptable, though acknowledging initial signs of moderation from peak levels. The persistence in services ex-housing keeps concern elevated.

**Minority views**: "A few participants remarked that the risks to their inflation outlook had become more balanced" -- modestly reduces uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 50 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Growth in economic activity in 2022 had been below its longer-run trend and expected that real GDP growth would slow further in 2023"
- "The labor market remained very tight...labor demand substantially exceeded the supply of available workers"
- "Recent indicators pointed to modest growth in spending and production"

**Center of gravity**: The economy presents a mixed picture -- below-trend growth rates but a still-tight labor market suggesting the output level remains near potential. Growth is decelerating toward or through potential.

**Minority views**: "Some participants judged that recent economic data signaled a somewhat higher chance of continued subdued economic growth" -- tilts downside.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 70 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants agreed that the labor market remained very tight and assessed that labor demand substantially exceeded the supply of available workers"
- "The unemployment rate had returned to a historically low level in December, job vacancies remained high, and wage growth remained elevated"
- "Tentative signs that imbalances between demand and supply in the labor market were improving, with job vacancies and payroll gains declining somewhat from high levels"

**Center of gravity**: Unanimous view of a very tight labor market with historically low unemployment and excess demand for workers, though with early signs of gradual rebalancing.

**Minority views**: None significant -- broad consensus on tightness.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 36 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Real consumer spending had declined in November and December...anticipated that consumption would likely grow at a subdued rate in 2023"
- "Growth in business fixed investment spending had been subdued in the fourth quarter and was being restrained by past interest rate increases"
- "Activity in the housing market had continued to weaken"

**Center of gravity**: Spending and investment are below trend across all components -- consumer spending declining, business investment subdued, housing weakening. Excess savings provide some support but are waning.

**Minority views**: "Some participants commented that the easing of COVID-related lockdown restrictions in China or stronger-than-expected growth...in the euro area could provide support to final demand" -- modest upside.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 63 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Financial conditions remained much tighter than in early 2022"
- "Several participants observed that some measures of financial conditions had eased over the past few months"
- "Persistently tight financial conditions were seen as tilting the risks to the downside around the baseline projection for real economic activity"

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly tighter than normal and constraining activity, but some recent easing from peak tightness levels has been noted.

**Minority views**: The easing noted by "several" creates some divergence from the "much tighter" consensus view, widening the band.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 72 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Almost all participants agreed that it was appropriate to raise the target range for the federal funds rate 25 basis points at this meeting"
- "All participants continued to anticipate that ongoing increases in the target range for the federal funds rate would be appropriate"
- "A few participants stated that they favored raising the target range for the federal funds rate 50 basis points"

**Center of gravity**: The committee is actively hiking and expects further hikes, but is stepping down the pace to 25bp. The center clearly favors continued tightening at a measured pace, with a small minority wanting faster action.

**Minority views**: "A few" preferred 50bp -- hawkish minority pulls band slightly upward.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 28 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Almost all participants agreed that it was appropriate to raise the target range...25 basis points" -- near-consensus on action
- "A few participants stated that they favored raising the target range...50 basis points" -- small minority disagreement on pace
- "All participants agreed...to continue the process of reducing the Federal Reserve's securities holdings"

**Center of gravity**: Broad agreement on the direction and action, with minor disagreement about pace (25 vs 50bp). No fundamental disagreements about the economic assessment or policy framework.

**Minority views**: The 50bp preference is a tactical, not directional, disagreement -- limiting the dissent score.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 47 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Several participants discussed vulnerabilities in the financial system associated with higher interest rates, including the elevated valuations for some categories of assets, particularly in the CRE sector; the susceptibility of some nonbank financial institutions to runs; and the effect of large, unrealized losses on some banks' securities portfolios"
- "A number of participants noted the importance of orderly functioning of the market for U.S. Treasury securities"
- "A number of participants stressed that a drawn-out period of negotiations to raise the federal debt limit could pose significant risks to the financial system"

**Center of gravity**: Financial stability received a dedicated and substantive discussion paragraph covering multiple specific vulnerabilities (CRE, nonbanks, bank securities losses, Treasury market, crypto, climate, debt ceiling). "Several" and "a number of" participants engaged meaningfully.

**Minority views**: None -- this is attention-based, and attention was moderate to substantial.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 76 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All participants continued to anticipate that ongoing increases in the target range for the federal funds rate would be appropriate to achieve the Committee's objectives"
- "A restrictive policy stance would need to be maintained until the incoming data provided confidence that inflation was on a sustained downward path to 2 percent, which was likely to take some time"
- "A number of participants observed that financial conditions had eased in recent months, which some noted could necessitate a tighter stance of monetary policy"

**Center of gravity**: The forward signal is clearly hawkish -- unanimous expectation of further rate increases, commitment to maintaining restrictive stance for an extended period, and concern that easing financial conditions could require even tighter policy.

**Minority views**: None meaningfully dovish on the forward path.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The uncertainty associated with their outlooks for economic activity, the labor market, and inflation was high" (participants observed -- consensus)
- Multiple risk factors enumerated: China's COVID reopening, Russia-Ukraine war, debt ceiling risks, recession possibility, synchronous global tightening
- "Heightened uncertainty regarding the economic outlook"

**Center of gravity**: Participants explicitly characterize uncertainty as "high" with multiple enumerated risk factors in both directions. This is genuine uncertainty, not rhetorical hedging.

**Minority views**: "Several of those participants observed that risks to the economic outlook were becoming more balanced" -- some see reduced uncertainty.
