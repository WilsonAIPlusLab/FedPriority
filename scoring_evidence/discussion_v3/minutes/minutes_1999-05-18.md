# FOMC Scoring: 1999-05-18 (Minutes, Independent)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Unsegmented pre-2009 format — staff and participant content are mixed, but participant language cues are clear ("members commented," "members expressed concern," "all the members supported," etc.). I will score only participant-attributed content.

**Era context**: May 1999, late-1990s expansion. The economy was in the middle of a productivity-driven boom, with the dot-com equity rally, tight labor markets, and benign inflation despite above-trend growth. The Fed had cut rates in fall 1998 (LTCM crisis) and was now contemplating whether/when to reverse those cuts.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all the members" (support hold + asymmetric), "most members" (odds tilted toward further robust growth), "several members" (commodity weight caution on CPI), "a number of members" (asymmetric not necessarily near-term action). The center of gravity is clear: unanimous hold with tightening bias, growing inflation concern but no action yet.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as currently benign but with rising risk. "Current indicators continued on the whole to point to quiescent wage and price behavior" but "members expressed concern about what they now saw as a greater risk of rising inflation." The trend toward lower inflation is "coming to an end" and there are "harbingers of a less favorable inflation performance." This is a transitional moment: current inflation is low, but concern is mounting. Direction: modestly below midpoint for current conditions, but worry is pulling upward. Net: slightly below 50 but close. Score around 45-48.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "rapid pace that had prevailed in recent quarters—a pace greater than the growth in the economy's potential." This is bright-line above-potential language. "Further robust growth that would add to pressures on already tight labor markets." Economy clearly running above potential. Score must be >50. The language "greater than the growth in the economy's potential" is clear. Score around 58-62.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Already tight labor markets," "persistently tight labor markets," "drawing down the pool of available workers," shortages of skilled labor in construction. This is clearly tight. Anchors suggest 65-75 for 1999-2000. Score around 68.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Impressive strength in private domestic spending," "notable gains in consumer and business expenditures," "appreciable growth in outlays for residential construction." Equipment spending expected to post "further sizable gains." Construction at "high levels." This is clearly above trend — "robust." Score around 65-68.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Marked improvement in overall financial market conditions since the fall of last year," "ample availability of financing on relatively favorable terms," "sharp rise in stock market prices," "recovery in credit markets, the rise in equity prices." Financial conditions are loose/accommodative. Higher = tighter, so this should be well below 50. Score around 20-25.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All members supported hold + asymmetric directive tilted toward tightening. "Concerns about the outlook for inflation had increased significantly." They chose not to act now but adopted a tightening bias. This is a hold with clear hawkish lean. The announcement of asymmetry is described as a "significant change." But they also explicitly note it "should not be viewed as necessarily implying a near-term policy change." Score: lean hawkish, around 57-62.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All the members supported" the proposal. No dissent mentioned. Minor differences in emphasis (how much to weight CPI data, etc.) but fundamentally unanimous. Score around 12-18.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Stock market prices discussed prominently as a factor in consumer spending and business confidence. Financial market conditions discussed as context. But this is not framed as financial stability concern per se — it's about the wealth effect on demand. There's no discussion of systemic risk, bubbles, or financial fragility in participant language. Limited but present. Score around 20-28.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Asymmetric directive tilted toward tightening, announced publicly. "Committee's resolve to keep inflation at a low level." But also "should not be viewed as necessarily implying a near-term policy change." This is a mild-to-moderate tightening bias signal. Score around 62-67.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Timing and extent of the moderation remained subject to substantial uncertainty," "subject to a wide range of uncertainty," "considerable uncertainty" about productivity and labor pressures, stock market behavior "could not readily be predicted." Multiple references to genuine uncertainty. Score around 50-55.

- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (59) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (64): Both lean hawkish, with forward guidance slightly more hawkish due to the announced asymmetric directive. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 47 +/-8 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "current indicators continued on the whole to point to quiescent wage and price behavior"; "the firming of oil and other commodity prices... could be read as suggesting at least that the trend toward lower inflation was coming to an end and perhaps also as harbingers of a less favorable inflation performance going forward"; "members were concerned that if recent developments continued... inflation was more likely to rise over time"
- Center of gravity: Inflation is currently well-contained, but members see a clear shift in risk toward rising inflation. The balance tips to just below neutral — current conditions are benign but the trajectory concern pulls the score close to 50.
- Minority views: "Several members commented in particular that substantial weight should not be attached to the one-month jump in the just-released CPI data" — some caution against over-reading inflation signals.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 60 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "a pace greater than the growth in the economy's potential"; "most members believed that for the year ahead the odds around their forecasts were tilted toward further robust growth that would add to pressures on already tight labor markets"
- Center of gravity: Economy clearly operating above potential with demand outpacing supply capacity, though productivity acceleration is partially closing the gap.
- Minority views: Members expect some slowing but timing is uncertain; no one argues economy is below potential.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 68 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "pressures on already tight labor markets"; "persistently tight labor markets"; "drawing down the pool of available workers"; "shortages of skilled labor and some construction materials"
- Center of gravity: Broad agreement that labor markets are tight with worker shortages in multiple sectors. Consistent with late-1990s boom.
- Minority views: None noted on the other side; the tightness is treated as consensus fact.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 66 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "impressive strength in private domestic spending during the first several months of the year featured notable gains in consumer and business expenditures and appreciable growth in outlays for residential construction"; "Expenditures by business firms for durable equipment were expected to post further sizable gains"
- Center of gravity: Spending across consumers, business, and housing is well above trend, supported by loose financial conditions and strong confidence.
- Minority views: Some mention of expected moderation in equipment spending and possible loss of momentum in housing, but current levels are clearly strong.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 22 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "marked improvement in overall financial market conditions since the fall of last year, including the ample availability of financing on relatively favorable terms for many borrowers and the sharp rise in stock market prices"; "recovery in credit markets, the rise in equity prices"
- Center of gravity: Financial conditions are clearly loose/accommodative, with ample credit availability and strong equity markets. This represents a reversal from the LTCM-related tightening of fall 1998.
- Minority views: "Rise in long-term interest rates" noted as a mild offset but overall picture is strongly accommodative.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 59 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "all the members supported a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance and to adopt and announce an asymmetric directive that was tilted toward tightening"; "their concerns about the outlook for inflation had increased significantly since the previous meeting"; but "it was preferable to defer any policy action and to monitor the economy closely"
- Center of gravity: Unanimous hold with a publicly announced tightening bias. Concerns have grown but not enough to warrant action. This is a lean-hawkish hold.
- Minority views: "A number of members emphasized, however, that the adoption and announcement of an asymmetrical directive should not be viewed as necessarily implying a near-term policy change."

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 12 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "all the members supported a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance and to adopt and announce an asymmetric directive"
- Center of gravity: Near-consensus. All members agree on both the hold and the asymmetric directive. Minor shading in emphasis but no meaningful disagreement.
- Minority views: None; differences are only in degree of inflation concern, not in prescription.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 25 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Stock market discussed as driver of consumer confidence and business investment; "ample availability of financing"; "concerns about overbuilding were reported in a few parts of the country"
- Center of gravity: Financial conditions discussed as economic context (wealth effects, credit availability) rather than as a stability concern. No discussion of systemic risk, bubbles, or financial fragility per se.
- Minority views: None specifically on financial stability.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 64 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "adoption and announcement of an asymmetric directive that was tilted toward tightening"; "important that the public... understood the Committee's resolve to keep inflation at a low level"; but "should not be viewed as necessarily implying a near-term policy change or indeed any change over time unless circumstances warranted"
- Center of gravity: A mild tightening signal. The announced asymmetry is a deliberate public communication of tightening bias, but hedged with conditionality.
- Minority views: None dissented from the forward signal.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 52 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "timing and extent of the moderation remained subject to substantial uncertainty"; "subject to a wide range of uncertainty"; "the extent of prospective pressures in labor markets and the outlook for productivity subject to considerable uncertainty"
- Center of gravity: Elevated uncertainty, particularly about whether/when growth will moderate and about the productivity outlook. Multiple uses of "substantial" and "considerable" uncertainty language.
- Minority views: None; uncertainty is broadly shared.
