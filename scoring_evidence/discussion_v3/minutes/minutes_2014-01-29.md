# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2014-01-29 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Segmented -- participant views section is clearly separated from staff review.

**Era context**: January 2014. The economy is in mid-recovery from the GFC. The Fed began tapering asset purchases in December 2013. Unemployment is declining but still elevated. Inflation is running below the 2% target. The federal funds rate is at the effective lower bound.

**Quantifier distribution**: "generally" (2x for economic outlook), "most" (risks more nearly balanced; further step down appropriate), "many" (payrolls discussion; monitor inflation), "several" (temporary factors; broader unemployment concepts; forward guidance items), "some" (consumers more optimistic; cyclical participation decline hard to gauge), "a number of" (confidence in projection; housing activity; factors supporting capex; wage growth low = slack), "a few" (participation decline reflects slow recovery; worker shortages; raise rates relatively soon), "a couple of" (low inflation and slack question taper pace; excessive risk-taking concerns; standard policy rules suggest rate increase).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target. "Inflation remained below the Committee's longer-run objective." They anticipate it returning to 2% over the medium run, but "several factors that cast doubt on this outcome were also mentioned, including slow growth in labor costs, the lack of pricing power." "Inflation persistently below the Committee's objective would pose risks to economic performance." This is clearly below-target inflation with concern about persistence. Direction: below midpoint. The language is not alarming/deflationary but persistently below target with downside worries. Score should be in the 30-38 range. Anchor: 38-42 for 2013-19 below target. Given the explicit concern about persistence and multiple downside factors, I'll score toward the lower end: 33.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says below target with downside factors. Score of 33 (below midpoint) matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic activity had strengthened more in the second half of 2013 than they had expected." But "several participants observed that temporary factors had helped boost real GDP" and "participants generally did not expect the recent pace of economic growth to be sustained." They expected "moderate pace" going forward. The unemployment rate "remained elevated." Economy still below potential but improving. Bright-line: no "above potential" language; "moderate pace" expansion from below potential. Score should be in the 37-42 range. The economy is recovering, with upside surprise, but still below potential. Score: 40.

Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says still below but recovering. Score 40 (below midpoint) matches.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Unemployment rate had declined but remained elevated." "Considerable labor market slack remained despite the reduction in the unemployment rate." "Still-elevated levels of underemployment and long-term unemployment, and the apparent absence of wage pressures." Broader measures "remained well above the official unemployment rate." "Low rates of increase in most measures of wages as consistent with continued labor market slack." Only "a few participants noted worker shortages in specific regions." Center of gravity: significant remaining slack. Score: 34.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter labor market. Evidence says substantial slack remains. Score 34 matches.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumer spending had advanced strongly in late 2013." "Business investment appeared to be on a more solid uptrend." "Business contacts in many parts of the country reported that they were guardedly optimistic." Households "remained cautious" but factors supporting spending were cited. "Business sector was willing to increase spending on capital projects." Housing recovery "slowed somewhat." Net: moderately positive, somewhat above trend for consumption, guardedly optimistic for investment. Score: 50. Consumer spending strengthened but from a below-trend base, and the strength was partly temporary. Investment outlook improving but still "guardedly optimistic." Score: 48.

Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending. Evidence shows strengthening but cautious/guarded. Score 48 matches.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "December decision to make a modest reduction in the monthly pace of asset purchases had not resulted in an adverse market reaction." "Current market expectations... were reasonably well aligned with participants' own expectations." "Continued improvement in credit conditions." "Easing of lending standards." Holdings still "maintaining downward pressure on longer-term interest rates... making broader financial conditions more accommodative." Conditions are described as accommodative/loose. Score: 25.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence says accommodative/loose. Score 25 matches.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Most participants judged... a further modest step down in the pace of purchases was appropriate." The Committee agreed to reduce purchases by $10B. "A couple of participants observed that continued low readings on inflation and considerable slack raised questions about the desirability of reducing the pace of purchases" but even they didn't oppose. "A few participants raised the possibility that it might be appropriate to increase the federal funds rate relatively soon." But the dominant action is continued tapering with commitment to keep rates low "well past" the 6.5% unemployment threshold. This is dovish-to-balanced: still in QE (though tapering), rates at zero, forward guidance emphasizing "low for long." But the tapering IS a mild hawkish movement from the pure QE stance. Anchor: 40-50 for 2014-15 pre-liftoff. Score: 43.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence shows continued accommodation with gradual taper -- lean dovish but moving toward neutral. Score 43 matches.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All members agreed" on the further reduction in purchases. There are diverse views on forward guidance form (quantitative vs. qualitative), and "a few" wanted to raise rates soon while others disagreed. But the policy action itself had unanimous support. The range of views on forward guidance is notable but doesn't constitute formal disagreement on the action. Moderate differences about the path forward without formal dissents. Score: 30.

Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Unanimous on action but diversity on forward guidance. Score 30 matches.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: "A couple of others argued that the current highly accommodative stance of monetary policy could lead investors to take on excessive risk and so undermine longer-term financial stability." "Several participants suggested that risks to financial stability should appear more explicitly in the list of factors." Discussion of emerging market volatility. Some attention to financial conditions but not a dominant theme. Score: 32.

Polarity check: Higher = more attention. Financial stability mentioned by several participants but not a major focus. Score 32 matches.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The Committee retained threshold-based forward guidance and stated it would be "appropriate to maintain the current target range for the federal funds rate well past the time that the unemployment rate declines below 6-1/2 percent, especially if projected inflation continues to run below" target. "Several participants argued that the forward guidance should give greater emphasis to the Committee's willingness to keep rates low if inflation were to remain persistently below." But "a few participants raised the possibility that it might be appropriate to increase the federal funds rate relatively soon." Center of gravity: dovish forward guidance emphasizing low for longer. This is in the taper period but with strong "low for long" rate guidance. Anchor: 8-15 for 2014 taper. The forward signal on rates is clearly dovish, though tapering is underway. Score: 18.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence says "well past" threshold, low for long. Score 18 (dovish) matches.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Most participants still viewed the risks to the outlook... as having become more nearly balanced." This suggests declining uncertainty. But there are caveats: emerging market developments "could pose downside risks," debate about reliability of unemployment rate as indicator, "several factors that cast doubt" on inflation returning to target. Moderate but not especially elevated uncertainty. Score: 30.

Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. Evidence shows "more nearly balanced" risks with some specific concerns. Score 30 matches.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (40) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (34): Both below 50, labor slightly lower reflecting explicit "considerable slack" language. Coherent -- labor is lagging the output recovery.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (43) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (18): Divergence is explained: current action is moderate (tapering = mild hawkish shift from pure QE), but the forward signal on rates is strongly dovish ("well past" threshold). This is the classic taper-era split.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 33 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Inflation remained below the Committee's longer-run objective over the intermeeting period." "Several factors that cast doubt on this outcome were also mentioned, including slow growth in labor costs, the lack of pricing power reported by business contacts in various parts of the country." "Inflation persistently below the Committee's objective would pose risks to economic performance."
- Center of gravity: Inflation is running below 2% target with multiple factors suggesting it could stay low; participants expect gradual return to target but acknowledge downside risks.
- Minority views: No participants argued inflation was at or above target; concern was about the persistence of low inflation.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 40 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Economic activity had strengthened more in the second half of 2013 than they had expected." "Participants generally did not expect the recent pace of economic growth to be sustained, but they nonetheless anticipated that the economy would expand at a moderate pace in coming quarters." Unemployment "remained elevated."
- Center of gravity: Economy recovering and closing the gap but still operating below potential; moderate growth expected going forward.
- Minority views: None significantly divergent on this assessment.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 34 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Broader concepts of the unemployment rate... remained well above the official unemployment rate, suggesting that considerable labor market slack remained." "A number of participants saw the low rates of increase in most measures of wages as consistent with continued labor market slack." "Still-elevated levels of underemployment and long-term unemployment, and the apparent absence of wage pressures."
- Center of gravity: Labor market improving but substantial slack persists, confirmed by broad measures of underemployment and weak wage growth.
- Minority views: "A few participants noted worker shortages in specific regions and occupations" -- these were clearly localized, not representative of the center.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 48 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Consumer spending had advanced strongly in late 2013, contributing importantly to the pickup in growth." "Business contacts in many parts of the country reported that they were guardedly optimistic about prospects for 2014." "More reports that the business sector was willing to increase spending on capital projects."
- Center of gravity: Spending strengthened noticeably in late 2013 but from a below-trend base; investment outlook improving but cautious. Near trend but not convincingly above it.
- Minority views: Some noted temporary factors in the GDP data; housing recovery slowed.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 25 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "The Committee's December decision to make a modest reduction in the monthly pace of asset purchases had not resulted in an adverse market reaction." "Continued improvement in credit conditions." "Easing of lending standards." Holdings "maintaining downward pressure on longer-term interest rates... making broader financial conditions more accommodative."
- Center of gravity: Financial conditions remain accommodative/loose, with credit conditions continuing to improve and lending standards easing.
- Minority views: One participant worried about sharp rises in longer-term rates; a couple worried about excessive risk-taking from accommodative policy.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 43 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Most participants judged that the incoming information about the economy was broadly in line with their expectations and that a further modest step down in the pace of purchases was appropriate." "All members agreed that the cumulative improvement in labor market conditions... indicated that it would be appropriate to make a further measured reduction." "Maintain the current target range for the federal funds rate well past the time that the unemployment rate declines below 6-1/2 percent."
- Center of gravity: Unanimous support for continued gradual taper, but maintaining highly accommodative stance via near-zero rates and continued (though reduced) purchases. Lean dovish overall.
- Minority views: "A few participants raised the possibility that it might be appropriate to increase the federal funds rate relatively soon" (hawkish outliers). "A couple" questioned taper pace given low inflation (dovish outliers).

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "All members agreed" on further measured reduction in purchases. But "a range of views was expressed about the form that such forward guidance might take." "A few participants raised the possibility that it might be appropriate to increase the federal funds rate relatively soon" while "other participants... suggested that prescriptions from standard policy rules were not appropriate."
- Center of gravity: Unanimous on the policy action; meaningful but manageable diversity on forward guidance and rate path. No formal dissents.
- Minority views: The range on forward guidance form (quantitative vs. qualitative) and rate timing creates moderate spread.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 32 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "A couple of others argued that the current highly accommodative stance of monetary policy could lead investors to take on excessive risk and so undermine longer-term financial stability." "Several participants suggested that risks to financial stability should appear more explicitly in the list of factors that would guide decisions about the federal funds rate." Discussion of emerging markets and Puerto Rico financing.
- Center of gravity: Financial stability received limited but substantive discussion; a few participants pushed to make it more prominent in the policy framework.
- Minority views: None -- this was not a point of heated disagreement, just varying emphasis.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 18 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Appropriate to maintain the current target range for the federal funds rate well past the time that the unemployment rate declines below 6-1/2 percent, especially if projected inflation continues to run below the Committee's longer-run objective." "Several participants argued that the forward guidance should give greater emphasis to the Committee's willingness to keep rates low if inflation were to remain persistently below."
- Center of gravity: Forward guidance is clearly dovish -- low for long, well past the unemployment threshold, especially with low inflation.
- Minority views: "A few participants raised the possibility that it might be appropriate to increase the federal funds rate relatively soon" and cited standard policy rules, but they were a clear minority.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 30 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Most participants still viewed the risks to the outlook for the economy and the labor market as having become more nearly balanced in recent months." "Developments in several emerging market economies, if they continued, could pose downside risks." Debate about reliability of unemployment rate as an indicator.
- Center of gravity: Risks seen as more nearly balanced, suggesting normal-to-somewhat-elevated uncertainty. Not especially heightened.
- Minority views: Some concern about emerging market contagion and interpretation of labor market signals added modest uncertainty.

- Near target but with concern about shorter-horizon anchoring. Slightly below 50.
