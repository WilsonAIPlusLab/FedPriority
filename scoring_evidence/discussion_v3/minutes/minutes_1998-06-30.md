# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1998-06-30 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). This is an unsegmented pre-2009 format, so I must carefully distinguish participant language ("members noted," "members observed") from staff language ("staff estimates," "staff projection," "staff analysis"). There are staff references in the monetary aggregates section that I will exclude.

**Era context**: Late 1990s expansion. The economy is running strong, labor markets are very tight, but inflation remains subdued thanks to productivity gains, the strong dollar, and low commodity/energy prices. The Asian financial crisis is an ongoing downside risk. The Fed funds rate was at 5.50% and had been held there since March 1997.

**Quantifier distribution**: "members generally agreed," "many of the members," "several," "some," "a number of members," "a few members," "all the members." The language is predominantly consensus-based with limited sharp disagreement.

**For each core dimension:**

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below the midpoint but with upward risks. Key phrases: "current unusually low level," "price inflation had displayed a modest uptilt recently," "overall price inflation had remained subdued when viewed over a longer horizon," "signs of a continuing acceleration, should they become evident, would be a matter of growing concern." There's acknowledgment of tight labor markets feeding through, but productivity gains and competitive markets keep it in check. Members note they "had tended in recent years to anticipate greater inflation than had materialized." Direction: modestly below target / slightly rising from low levels. Score should be 41-48 range -- not quite at 50, because inflation is "unusually low" and "subdued" but with a "modest uptilt."

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: Economy is operating at "high levels of business activity," with "very tight labor markets," strong consumer spending, strong business investment, strong housing. Multiple sectors described as running hot. The expansion is expected to moderate but from above-trend levels. Members project only "a very slight rise" in unemployment to "still quite low rates" of 4.5-4.75%. This is clearly above potential. Score should be 57-63 per late-90s boom anchor.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Explicit repeated references: "very tight labor markets" (appears twice), "scarcities of labor," "greater, albeit still occasional, indications of heightened worker demands in labor negotiations," "ample job opportunities," unemployment at very low rates. This is firmly tight. Score should be 65-70.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending has "solid growth," business fixed investment on a "strong uptrend," residential construction "considerable strength," nonresidential "very high levels of construction activity." Some moderation expected but from very strong levels. Score should be 62-67 (above trend, robust).

5. **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "ready availability of debt and equity financing on relatively attractive terms," "very attractive terms on home mortgages," "sharp increases...in household wealth" from stock market. Financial conditions are clearly loose/accommodative. Score should be 20-28 (loose).

6. **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: This is notably a meeting focused on economic assessment and monetary aggregate ranges, NOT a policy action meeting per se (it's the Humphrey-Hawkins meeting). Members "generally agreed that the expansion...was likely to be relatively moderate" and see "substantial risks...on both sides." There is no explicit discussion of rate changes in the participant section. The tone is watchful -- concerned about upside inflation risks but also significant downside risks from Asia. The retention of current ranges and emphasis on "price stability over time" suggests a hold-steady posture. Score should be around 50-55 (balanced to mildly hawkish lean given inflation concerns).

7. **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All the members favored or could support the retention of the current ranges." The discussion shows broad agreement on the economic outlook with risks on both sides. "A few members" had different views on monetary ranges but "could support" the consensus. Low disagreement. Score: 15-20.

8. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Asia crisis discussed at length as a risk factor. Stock market wealth effects mentioned. Some speculative construction noted. But financial stability is not the dominant focus -- it's one risk among others. Score: 30-38.

9. **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: No explicit forward guidance language about policy direction. The discussion is data-dependent and balanced between upside inflation risks and downside Asia risks. Score: 50-55 (neutral, slightly hawkish lean due to inflation concerns).

10. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Many of the members commented...that the already substantial risks surrounding the economic outlook had increased on both sides," "adding significantly to the uncertainties," "too soon to judge," inventory sector "always subject to a high degree of uncertainty." Multiple explicit uncertainty references. Score: 48-55 (elevated).

**Polarity checks**:
- OUTPUT_GAP ~60: Higher = more above potential. Economy described as running at high levels with tight labor. Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS ~67: Higher = tighter. "Very tight labor markets." Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS ~24: Higher = tighter. Conditions described as loose/accommodative. Low score correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN ~52: Higher = more hawkish. Balanced with mild inflation concern. Correct.

**Cross-dimension coherence**:
- OUTPUT_GAP (~60) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (~67): Both above 50, consistent with late-90s boom. Labor tightness somewhat higher, reflecting explicit "very tight" language.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (~52) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (~52): Aligned -- both near-neutral with mild hawkish lean. Makes sense given balanced risks.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 44 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "such growth would be consistent with some limited increase in inflation from the current unusually low level"
- "some key measures of price inflation had displayed a modest uptilt recently"
- "overall price inflation had remained subdued when viewed over a longer horizon"
- "members had tended in recent years to anticipate greater inflation than had materialized"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants perceive inflation as still subdued/unusually low but with early signs of a modest uptilt. Tight labor markets create future risk, but productivity and competitive markets provide offset. Slightly below the neutral midpoint.

**Minority views**: Some members see rising labor costs as a growing threat; others note competitive forces will keep inflation in check for some time. This modest spread widens the band slightly.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "high levels of business activity across the nation"
- GDP growth projected at 3-3.25% for 1998, moderating to 2-2.5% in 1999
- Unemployment at "still quite low rates centering on 4-1/2 to 4-3/4 percent"
- "very tight labor markets" (repeated)

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is clearly operating above potential with high activity levels and very tight labor markets. Moderation is expected but from above-trend levels. Consistent with late-90s boom anchor of 57-63.

**Minority views**: Several noted "signs of some slowing in the expansion of regional economic activity," but from elevated levels.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 67 +/-5 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "very tight labor markets" (used twice in the document)
- "scarcities of labor or construction materials"
- "greater, albeit still occasional, indications of heightened worker demands in labor negotiations"
- "ample job opportunities"
- Unemployment projected at 4.5-4.75%, well below most NAIRU estimates of the era

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor markets are very tight by any measure. Multiple references to shortages, rising worker demands, and very low unemployment. Firmly in the 66-80 "very tight" range but not at the extreme.

**Minority views**: None expressed disagreement on labor tightness; this is a consensus view.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 63 +/-5 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "rising levels of employment and incomes were continuing to foster solid growth in consumer spending"
- "Business fixed investment remained on a strong uptrend"
- "very high levels of construction activity in several areas"
- "residential construction activity also displayed considerable strength across much of the country"

**Center-of-gravity**: Spending and investment are broadly above trend across consumer, business, and residential/nonresidential construction sectors. Some moderation expected but from strong levels.

**Minority views**: "some anecdotal and other evidence suggested that retail sales had moderated in recent weeks" -- this is a minor offset that keeps the score from going higher.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 23 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "ready availability of debt and equity financing on relatively attractive terms"
- "very attractive terms on home mortgages"
- "sharp increases that had occurred in household wealth as a consequence of the extended uptrend in stock market prices"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly loose and supportive. Easy credit, attractive financing terms, and wealth effects from rising equity markets all point to accommodative conditions. Score in the "loose" range.

**Minority views**: None expressed concern about tight financial conditions. Some noted speculative activity, which reinforces the loose-conditions reading.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 52 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "All the members favored or could support the retention of the current ranges" (hold steady)
- Risks seen as "on both sides of their forecasts"
- Upside: "persistent strength in domestic final demand might well add to inflationary pressures"
- Downside: Asian crisis spillover risks "had increased"

**Center-of-gravity**: This is a Humphrey-Hawkins meeting focused on monetary aggregate ranges, not a rate-decision meeting. The tone is balanced -- inflation risks argue for hawkishness, but Asia risks argue for caution. Net position is near-neutral with the barest hawkish lean from inflation concerns.

**Minority views**: The two-sided risk assessment creates the wider uncertainty band.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 18 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "members generally agreed that the expansion in economic activity was likely to be relatively moderate"
- "All the members favored or could support the retention of the current ranges"
- "a few members" had alternative views on monetary range methodology but "could support" consensus

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on both the economic assessment and the monetary range decisions. Minor tactical differences but no substantive dissent.

**Minority views**: "A few members" wanted ranges centered on growth expectations -- a methodological, not policy, disagreement.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 33 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]

**Evidence**:
- Extended discussion of Asian financial crisis and spillover risks: "greater financial turmoil and economic weakness"
- Stock market wealth effects mentioned as supporting demand
- "some of the construction was being undertaken on a speculative basis" with potential "overbuilding"
- "a very small but growing possibility of marked and spreading weakness that might have a more major effect on U.S. financial markets"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability concerns (primarily Asian contagion risk) receive meaningful but not dominant attention. It is discussed as one risk factor among several rather than the central focus of the meeting. The speculative building/overbuilding mention adds to this. Limited but present discussion.

**Minority views**: Some members saw greater Asia risks than others.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 52 +/-7 [Low confidence]

**Evidence**:
- No explicit forward guidance about rate direction in this document
- "the Committee's commitment to a policy of achieving price stability over time" (hawkish lean)
- But also: "many of the members commented...that the already substantial risks surrounding the economic outlook had increased on both sides"

**Center-of-gravity**: No directional forward signal. The meeting is focused on monetary aggregate ranges. The commitment to price stability provides a mild hawkish anchor, but the two-sided risk assessment keeps it near neutral.

**Minority views**: N/A -- no explicit forward guidance debate.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 +/-6 [High confidence]

**Evidence**:
- "the already substantial risks surrounding the economic outlook had increased on both sides of their forecasts"
- "Members commented that it was too soon to judge the eventual extent and duration of the turmoil in Asia"
- "there seemed to be a very small but growing possibility of marked and spreading weakness"
- "nonfarm inventory investment...was always subject to a high degree of uncertainty"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants express elevated uncertainty, especially around Asia risks and two-sided risks to the outlook. The language goes beyond standard caveats -- "substantial risks...had increased" is genuine uncertainty, not rhetorical hedging.

**Minority views**: Broadly shared sense of elevated uncertainty.
