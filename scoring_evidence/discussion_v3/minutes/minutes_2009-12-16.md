# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2009-12-16

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). This is a post-2009 segmented document with clear "Participants' Views" section separated from staff content.

**Era context**: December 2009 -- roughly one year after the GFC trough. The economy was in the early stages of recovery from the deepest recession since the Great Depression. The fed funds rate was at the zero lower bound, and the Fed was executing large-scale asset purchases (QE1). Unemployment was near 10%.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all" (3 uses -- agreement on growth strengthening, subdued inflation, path of rates), "most" (3 uses -- incoming data in line, slow pickup, slack keeping inflation subdued), "many" (3 uses -- cautious capital spending, balanced inflation risks, business contact reports), "several" (1 use -- labor market recovery needs more evidence), "some" (6 uses -- downside inflation risk, upside inflation risk, housing outlook tentative, concerned about self-sustaining recovery, commodity price pressures, tight credit), "a few" (2 uses -- expand QE, excess reserves risk), "one" (1 use -- scale back purchases).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW target. "Participants agreed that underlying inflation currently was subdued and was likely to remain so for some time." "Most participants anticipated that substantial slack... would keep inflation subdued." Wages and unit labor costs decelerating. "Most firms had little ability to raise their prices." Some saw downside risk (could edge further below mandate-consistent levels), others upside risk from commodities and monetary stimulus. The center of gravity is clearly below target but not at deflationary alarm levels. Score should be in the 20-28 range. "Subdued" language = meaningfully below target. Direction check: Higher = more inflation pressure; evidence says subdued/below target = low score. Matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as FAR BELOW potential. "Substantial slack in labor and product markets." "Resource slack was expected to diminish only slowly." The recovery is underway but very gradual. "Moderate pace of expansion would imply slow improvement." Bright-line rule: "substantial slack" = must be well below 50. Given "substantial" and multiple indicators of slack, this is in the 20-28 range. The economy is recovering (not in freefall), so not in the 1-15 catastrophic range.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see labor markets as very LOOSE. "Unemployment to remain elevated for quite some time." "Employment-to-population ratio had fallen to a 25-year low." "Aggregate hours... had dropped more than during the 1981-82 recession." "Few firms were hiring." "Unusually large fraction... working part time for economic reasons." This is deeply slack labor market. Score in the 16-22 range. Direction check: Higher = tighter; evidence says very loose. Low score matches.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "on a moderately rising trend" but constrained by uncertain job prospects, tight credit, low wealth. Business investment "appeared to have stabilized" but "cautious business sentiment... likely to keep new capital spending subdued." CRE "continued to fall markedly." Housing showed improvement from very low levels but outlook tentative. Net picture: below trend but improving from lows. Score in the 30-36 range. Not collapsing (that was earlier), but still subdued/below trend.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Mixed picture. "Financial market conditions were generally regarded as having become more supportive." Equity prices rose, credit spreads narrowed. BUT "securitization markets were still substantially impaired." "Bank loans continued to contract sharply in all categories." "Bank lending terms and standards were seen as likely to remain tight." Small firms facing "substantial constraints in gaining access to credit." CRE had "very tight credit conditions." The center of gravity: conditions are improving but still tight. Score in the 60-68 range. Not crisis-level frozen, but still meaningfully tight.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All members agreed that no changes... were warranted." Maintained 0-0.25% rate and LSAPs. "Extended period" language reiterated. "A few members noted... it might become desirable... to provide more policy stimulus by expanding the planned scale." Only "one member" thought purchases could be scaled back. The lean is clearly dovish -- maintaining maximum accommodation with some wanting more. Score in the 15-22 range. Direction check: Higher = more hawkish; this is dovish. Low score matches.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All members agreed" on no changes. The range of views spans from "a few" wanting more QE to "one" wanting less, but the vote was unanimous. There are differences in inflation risk assessment (some downside, some upside) and policy direction preferences, but these are within a framework of broad agreement on the current stance. Score in the 25-35 range (broad agreement with notable minority differences).

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Substantial discussion of financial conditions -- securitization markets, bank lending, credit spreads, small vs. large firm access, CRE credit, asset values, MBS purchases winding down, special liquidity facilities expiring. This is a prominent topic but not the dominant one (the economy and labor market get more attention). Score in the 50-58 range.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Decided to reiterate its anticipation that economic conditions... were likely to warrant exceptionally low rates for an extended period." This is explicitly dovish forward guidance. "A few" want potentially more QE; only "one" wants scaling back. The forward signal is strongly accommodative. Score in the 8-15 range. Direction check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal; "extended period" at zero = very dovish. Low score matches.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Near-term outlook remains uncertain." "Some participants remained concerned about the economy's ability to generate a self-sustaining recovery." Risks noted around housing (tax credits expiring, MBS wind-down, foreclosures). Business contacts emphasizing uncertainty about policy (healthcare, tax, environmental). Differing views on inflation risks. But "most saw incoming information as broadly in line with projections" and "downside risks had diminished a bit further." Elevated but not extreme uncertainty. Score in the 45-55 range.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (24) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (20): Both well below 50, consistent with deep slack. Labor slightly lower reflecting the extremely weak labor data. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (18) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (12): Both dovish, with forward guidance slightly more dovish due to explicit "extended period" language. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 23 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants agreed that underlying inflation currently was subdued and was likely to remain so for some time."
- "Most participants anticipated that substantial slack in labor and product markets, along with well-anchored inflation expectations, would keep inflation subdued in the near term."
- "Some noted the risk that, over the next couple of years, inflation could edge further below the rates they judged most consistent with the Federal Reserve's dual mandate."
- "Some saw inflation risks as tilted to the downside... But others felt that inflation risks were tilted to the upside, particularly in the medium term."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The clear consensus was that inflation was subdued and expected to remain so, driven by substantial economic slack and anchored expectations, though views on medium-term risks were divided between downside and upside.

**Minority views**: "Some" saw upside risks from commodities, import prices, and potential future excess reserve drawdowns, while "some" saw downside risks from slack. These opposing minorities widen the uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 24 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Substantial slack in labor and product markets."
- "Resource slack was expected to diminish only slowly."
- "The projected pace of real output growth... was expected to exceed that of potential output by only enough to produce a very gradual reduction in economic slack."
- "Most anticipated that the pickup in output and employment growth would be rather slow relative to past recoveries from deep recessions."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants saw the economy as far below potential with substantial slack that would diminish only gradually, consistent with a deep recession that was beginning to recover.

**Minority views**: Narrow disagreement on this point -- "participants agreed" growth was strengthening, but all recognized substantial slack.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 20 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The weakness in labor markets continued to be an important concern... who generally expected unemployment to remain elevated for quite some time."
- "The unemployment rate was not the only indicator pointing to substantial slack in labor markets: The employment-to-population ratio had fallen to a 25-year low, and aggregate hours of production workers had dropped more than during the 1981-82 recession."
- "Few firms were hiring." "Many business contacts again reported that they would be cautious in their hiring."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Labor markets were deeply slack with unemployment elevated, hiring minimal, a record-low employment-to-population ratio, and widespread expectations of only gradual improvement.

**Minority views**: Minor positive note that temporary employment was rising, but this did not meaningfully offset the consensus view of deep weakness.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 33 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending appeared to be on a moderately rising trend, reflecting gains in after-tax income and wealth this year."
- "Uncertain job prospects, modest growth in real incomes, tight credit, and wealth levels that remained relatively low... were seen as likely to weigh on consumer confidence and the growth of consumer spending."
- "Many participants expressed the view that cautious business sentiment, together with low industrial utilization rates, was likely to keep new capital spending subdued."
- "CRE activity continued to fall markedly."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumer spending was rising modestly but constrained; business investment had stabilized but remained subdued; CRE was still deteriorating. The net picture is below trend but no longer contracting sharply.

**Minority views**: Some participants were more optimistic about housing and consumer spending trajectories; others more worried about sustainability without government support.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 63 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Financial market conditions were generally regarded as having become more supportive of continued economic recovery."
- "Securitization markets were still substantially impaired."
- "Bank loans, however, continued to contract sharply in all categories."
- "Smaller firms... reportedly faced substantial constraints in gaining access to credit."
- "Bank lending terms and standards were seen as likely to remain tight."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions were improving (spreads narrowing, equities rising) but remained meaningfully tight, particularly for bank-dependent small businesses and in securitization markets. The improvement from crisis levels pulls the score down from crisis range but persistent tight credit keeps it elevated.

**Minority views**: Broad agreement on this mixed picture; no strong disagreement noted.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 18 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All members agreed that no changes to the Committee's large-scale asset purchase programs, or to its target range for the federal funds rate, were warranted."
- "A few members noted that... it might become desirable at some point in the future to provide more policy stimulus by expanding the planned scale of the Committee's large-scale asset purchases."
- "One member thought that the improvement... suggested that the quantity of planned asset purchases could be scaled back."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Unanimously dovish stance -- maintaining zero rates and completing QE1 purchases, with the lean of the Committee slightly toward more accommodation if needed, reflecting deep economic slack.

**Minority views**: "A few" wanted potentially more QE (even more dovish, ~10-12); "one" wanted to scale back (mildly less dovish, ~30-35). This range informs the uncertainty band.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 30 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All members agreed that no changes... were warranted" (unanimous vote).
- Range from "a few" wanting more QE to "one" wanting less.
- Differing inflation risk views: "Some saw inflation risks as tilted to the downside... But others felt that inflation risks were tilted to the upside."
- "Many participants viewed the risks to their inflation outlooks as being roughly balanced."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Unanimous vote with broad agreement on current policy, but notable differences in views on inflation risk direction and desired future policy trajectory (more vs. less QE), placing this in the broad-agreement-with-minor-differences range.

**Minority views**: The divergence between those wanting more QE and the one wanting less represents meaningful philosophical differences about the policy path, even though all agreed on the current action.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 55 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Extended discussion of bank lending contraction, securitization impairment, small vs. large firm credit access.
- Discussion of special liquidity facility expirations and their implications.
- "Members emphasized that they were prepared to modify these plans if necessary to support financial stability."
- Participants assessed asset values ("did not seem out of line with improving fundamentals") and risk appetite.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial stability was a moderately prominent topic with several paragraphs devoted to credit conditions, bank stress, securitization markets, and the winding down of emergency facilities, though the primary focus remained on the real economy.

**Minority views**: Broad agreement on the importance of monitoring financial conditions.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 12 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Decided to reiterate its anticipation that economic conditions, including low levels of resource utilization, subdued inflation trends, and stable inflation expectations, were likely to warrant exceptionally low rates for an extended period."
- "A few members noted that... it might become desirable at some point in the future to provide more policy stimulus by expanding the planned scale of the Committee's large-scale asset purchases."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal was strongly dovish -- explicit "extended period" language for near-zero rates, with a lean toward potentially more (not less) accommodation, and QE1 purchases still being completed.

**Minority views**: "One member" saw potential for scaling back if recovery strengthens, but this was a clear outlier.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 50 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The near-term outlook remains uncertain."
- "Some participants remained concerned about the economy's ability to generate a self-sustaining recovery without government support."
- "Many also noted widespread reports from business contacts that uncertainties about health-care, tax, and environmental policies were adding to businesses' reluctance."
- "Most participants saw the incoming information as broadly in line with the projections... their views on the economic outlook had not changed appreciably."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Uncertainty was elevated with multiple sources of concern (sustainability of recovery, housing risks, policy uncertainty for businesses), but "downside risks to the outlook for economic growth had diminished a bit further" and projections were largely unchanged, suggesting not extreme levels of uncertainty.

**Minority views**: Some participants more worried about self-sustaining recovery; the broad group was somewhat more confident about the trajectory.
