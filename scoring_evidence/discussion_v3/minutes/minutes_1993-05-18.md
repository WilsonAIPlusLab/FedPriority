# FOMC Discussion Scoring — 1993-05-18 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented (pre-2009), so I must distinguish participant language ("members noted," "members expressed") from staff content.

**Era context**: Mid-1993. The economy is in a sluggish recovery following the 1990-91 recession. Unemployment remains elevated at 7.0%. Inflation has recently ticked up, surprising participants. The federal funds rate is low. Clinton-era fiscal legislation (deficit reduction, tax proposals) creates uncertainty.

**Quantifier distribution**: "many" (2 uses for members), "several" (1), "some" (3-4), "a number of" (1), "a majority" (1), "one" (2, for outlier views), "all but two" (final vote). The language shows moderate-to-broad agreement on the economic picture but some divergence on inflation outlook and policy bias.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as having risen and as concerning. "Members expressed particular concern about the rise in various measures of inflation over the past several months." However, "many members" thought it "premature to conclude that the inflationary trend had tilted upward." Some stressed risk of sustained higher inflation, others pointed to "still considerable slack." The center of gravity: inflation has risen above expected, inflation expectations have worsened, but it may be temporary. This is above the midpoint — participants perceive inflation pressure as somewhat elevated but not clearly persistent. Direction: above 50 but not dramatically. Score range: 55-62.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: The economy has "sustainable momentum" but the expansion "has slowed." Unemployment at 7.0% — well above NAIRU. "Still considerable slack in labor and product markets" is explicitly stated. Business conditions are "uneven," retail sales "lackluster," nonresidential construction "subdued." The bright-line rule says "slack remains" must be < 50. Direction: below potential. Score range: 35-40.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 7.0%. Firms are using overtime and temporary workers rather than hiring permanently. "Continuing efforts to constrain costs and to hold down or reduce employment levels." Job growth occurring but limited. "The downtrend in labor compensation inflation also seemed to have stalled" — but that's stalling at low levels. Direction: clearly below full employment, meaningful slack. Score range: 30-36.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Retail sales "generally lackluster." Consumer spending had "some retrenchment" after a late-1992 surge. Business equipment spending continued but many firms "holding off on making major new investment commitments." Nonresidential construction "relatively subdued." Members anticipate "moderate support" from consumers. Direction: below trend but not contracting. Score range: 36-42.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Relatively low interest rates, receptive financial markets, and the more aggressive lending policies of some depository institutions" — this is loose to accommodative. Bond yields rose somewhat. Mr. Angell notes "low real interest rates, a very steep yield curve." Direction: conditions are loose/accommodative. Score range: 25-32.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The Committee voted to hold rates unchanged but adopted an asymmetric directive biased toward tightening. "A majority of the members" supported the tightening bias. One member (Angell) wanted immediate tightening. One member (Boehne) dissented against the tightening bias, preferring a symmetric directive. "Some members preferred to retain a directive that did not incorporate a presumption about the likely direction." The center of gravity: hold steady with a tightening bias — lean hawkish. Score range: 53-58.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Two formal dissents — Angell (wanted immediate tightening) and Boehne (wanted symmetric directive, opposed the tightening bias). These dissents pull in opposite directions. "Some members" initially preferred symmetric but most accepted asymmetric. The disagreement extends to the inflation outlook (some vs. others differ on whether inflation will persist). Score range: 40-48.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Minimal. Brief mentions of bond, foreign exchange, and commodity markets in the context of inflation expectations monitoring, not financial stability per se. No discussion of systemic risk, asset bubbles, or financial stability concerns. Consistent with the 1993 era anchor of 1-10. Score range: 8-12.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The asymmetric directive with a tightening bias is the forward signal. "A majority of the members" supported "positioning policy for a move toward restraint should signs of intensifying inflation continue to multiply." But "policy would still be stimulative after a modest tightening move." The forward signal is a mild tightening lean, contingent on data. Score range: 58-65.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Several emphasized that the outlook was subject to substantial uncertainty." "A considerable degree of uncertainty surrounded the outlook for inflation." Uncertainty about fiscal legislation is repeatedly mentioned. The word "uncertain/uncertainties" appears many times. Direction: elevated uncertainty. Score range: 55-62.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (~37) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (~33): Both below 50, consistent with slack economy. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (~55) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (~62): Both lean hawkish, with forward guidance slightly more so due to asymmetric directive. The divergence is small and makes sense — current action is hold, forward signal leans toward tightening. Coherent.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (~58) is above 50 while OUTPUT_GAP (~37) is below 50: This reflects the specific 1993 puzzle discussed by members — inflation rising despite slack. Explained by the text.

### Polarity verification:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says inflation has risen, expectations worsening. Score above 50. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Economy has slack. Score below 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Conditions are loose. Score below 50. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Tightening bias adopted. Score above 50. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 58 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Members expressed particular concern about the rise in various measures of inflation over the past several months."
- "Higher inflation expectations, if sustained, would be detrimental to economic performance, and the risks of an uptrend in inflation clearly had increased."
- "It was premature in the view of many members to conclude that the inflationary trend had tilted upward."

**Center-of-gravity**: Members see inflation as somewhat elevated above expectations, with worsening inflation expectations. However, many attribute the rise to temporary factors and note continued slack as a restraining force. The concern is real but not yet indicating entrenched higher inflation.

**Minority views**: A number of members "stressed the risk that a faster rate of inflation might well tend to be sustained" (more hawkish), while others gave "more emphasis to the still considerable slack" (less concerned). This widens the band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 37 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The economic expansion has slowed in recent months." Unemployment at 7.0%.
- "Still considerable slack in labor and product markets."
- "Members continued to report uneven conditions ranging from apparently moderate gains in some parts of the country to mixed or marginally declining activity in others."

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is clearly operating below potential with considerable slack, but expansion continues with "sustainable momentum." Not a deep recession, but a sluggish recovery with persistent slack.

**Minority views**: Limited divergence on this dimension; the disagreement is more about inflation than output.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 33 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Continuing efforts to constrain costs and to hold down or reduce employment levels, notably of permanent workers."
- "Business firms generally appeared disposed to continue to meet increases in demand through overtime work and temporary workers."
- Unemployment at 7.0%; "still considerable slack in labor and product markets."

**Center-of-gravity**: The labor market is clearly slack. Firms are reluctant to hire, preferring temporary workers. Unemployment is well above full employment. Compensation inflation has "stalled" its downtrend, suggesting very limited labor market pressure.

**Minority views**: None significantly diverge on labor market assessment.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 39 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Anecdotal reports from around the country suggested generally lackluster retail sales over the first four months of the year."
- "Spending for business equipment had continued to help maintain the expansion" but "numerous firms reported that they were holding off on making major new investment commitments."
- "Nonresidential construction remained uneven and on the whole relatively subdued."

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending is below trend (lackluster), business equipment spending is a bright spot but being curtailed by uncertainty, and construction is subdued. Overall somewhat below trend but not contracting.

**Minority views**: Limited; the picture is relatively consistent across members.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 28 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Relatively low interest rates, receptive financial markets, and the more aggressive lending policies of some depository institutions."
- Mr. Angell: "low real interest rates, a very steep yield curve."
- "Monetary policy would still be stimulative after a modest tightening move."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are accommodative/loose. Low real interest rates, receptive markets, and improving lending conditions. This is clearly below the midpoint on the tightness scale.

**Minority views**: No disagreement on conditions being loose.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 55 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All but two of the members indicated that they preferred or could accept a directive that called for maintaining the existing degree of pressure on reserve positions and that included a bias toward possible firming."
- "A majority of the members" supported "positioning policy for a move toward restraint should signs of intensifying inflation continue to multiply."
- Mr. Angell dissented wanting immediate tightening; Mr. Boehne dissented preferring no tightening bias.

**Center-of-gravity**: The Committee holds steady but adopts a tightening bias. This is a lean-hawkish posture — not yet acting but positioning to act. "Some members" initially preferred symmetric but accepted asymmetric. The current action is unchanged, so the score stays near 50 but tilted hawkish.

**Minority views**: Angell (more hawkish, immediate action) and Boehne (more dovish, symmetric directive) create spread.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 44 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Two formal dissents: Angell (wanted tightening) and Boehne (opposed tightening bias).
- "The members differed to some extent with regard to the most likely outcome" for inflation.
- "Some members preferred to retain a directive that did not incorporate a presumption about the likely direction" — minority initially opposed.

**Center-of-gravity**: Two dissents pulling in opposite directions is notable. Additionally, the inflation outlook generated genuine disagreement among members, with "a number" stressing sustained risk and "others" emphasizing slack. The policy debate also featured a significant minority preferring symmetric. This is moderate-to-notable disagreement.

**Minority views**: The bidirectional nature of dissents (one hawkish, one dovish) elevates the intensity score.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 10 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Markets mentioned only instrumentally: "developments in markets affected by inflation psychology, such as those for bonds, foreign exchange, and sensitive commodities" — as indicators to monitor for inflation, not as financial stability concerns.
- No discussion of systemic risk, asset valuations, bank health, or financial stability per se.

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability is essentially not a topic. Market references serve inflation monitoring, not financial stability analysis. Consistent with typical 1993-era low emphasis.

**Minority views**: None.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 62 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "In the view of a majority of the members, wage and price developments over recent months were sufficiently worrisome to warrant positioning policy for a move toward restraint."
- "Slightly greater reserve restraint would be acceptable or slightly lesser reserve restraint might be acceptable" — asymmetric language leaning toward tightening.
- "If a policy tightening action were not needed, an asymmetric directive would nonetheless underscore the Committee's concern about recent inflation readings."

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is a mild tightening lean, contingent on incoming inflation data. The asymmetric directive signals the next move is more likely to be a tightening. However, it is conditional ("should signs continue to multiply") and the Committee agreed on intermeeting consultation before acting, indicating caution.

**Minority views**: Boehne and "some members" preferred no forward tightening bias.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 58 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Several emphasized that the outlook was subject to substantial uncertainty stemming to an important extent from the unsettled course of legislation aimed at reducing the federal deficit."
- "A considerable degree of uncertainty surrounded the outlook for inflation."
- "Prevailing economic uncertainties, notably those generated by the current legislative debate about federal taxes and spending."

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is elevated. Multiple references to "substantial uncertainty," "considerable uncertainty," and "uncertainties" driven by both inflation developments and fiscal legislation. Members explicitly acknowledge not understanding recent inflation dynamics ("it remained difficult to find a satisfactory explanation"). This is genuine uncertainty, not rhetorical hedging.

**Minority views**: Limited divergence — most members express elevated uncertainty.
