# FOMC Meeting Scoring - 2007-06-28

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). This is a pre-2009 unsegmented minutes document, so I must focus on participant-attributed language ("participants noted," "members agreed," "several cited," etc.) and ignore staff-reported content.

**Era context**: June 2007 -- late in the mid-2000s expansion. The fed funds rate is at 5.25%. Housing correction is underway, subprime strains are emerging but have not yet spread broadly. Growth is moderate, labor market tight, and inflation is the "predominant concern."

**Quantifier distribution**: Key quantifiers used:
- "participants" (general/all): noted moderate growth, inflation pressures, labor tightness
- "generally": agreed housing drag, anticipated moderate consumption, growth risks more balanced
- "most": judged labor market tight
- "a number of": noted elevated inventories, subprime issues, forces sustaining inflation
- "some": noted risks to investment, saving rate risks, productivity decline, lower NAIRU
- "several": noted higher interest rates/credit tightening, cited TIPS inflation compensation uptick, emphasized holding inflation expectations

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly above target but with risk of persistence. Core PCE projections are 2-2.25% for 2007 and 1.75-2% for 2008. Recent core data were "encouraging" but "not convincing." Multiple forces sustaining pressure: resource utilization, energy/commodity prices, dollar decline, slower productivity. "Predominant concern" is inflation failing to moderate. Direction: above midpoint. The language is "somewhat elevated" with persistent risk but recent improvement. Score range: 58-65.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Economy expanding at "moderate pace," growth "close to that of potential." Below-trend growth recently but labor market tight. Real GDP projections of 2-2.5% for 2007 are below potential, but resource utilization is described as "generally high." The text says growth is near potential and resource utilization is high, suggesting slightly above or at potential. Score range: 50-54.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Most participants judged labor market conditions to remain rather tight, particularly for the most skilled workers." Unemployment projected at 4.5-4.75%. "Continued tautness" noted as a puzzle. This is clearly tight. Score range: 57-63.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumption rising "more slowly" but expected to be moderate, supported by strong labor market. Business spending data "more encouraging" with orders stepping up. But housing is a significant drag. Net: moderate/at trend with mixed signals. Score range: 46-52.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Credit conditions described as "generally quite accommodative" but with tightening emerging. "Availability of credit to some highly leveraged and other lower-rated borrowers appeared to be tightening a bit." Longer-term rates moved higher. This is a transitional moment -- mostly still loose but early tightening signs. Score range: 35-42.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Members agreed to maintain 5.25%. Inflation is "predominant concern." No discussion of cuts. Forward stance is data-dependent. The tone is hold-steady with hawkish bias -- inflation risk predominates, no easing discussed. Score range: 58-65.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Members agreed" to maintain rate. No dissents mentioned. Views appear broadly aligned -- growth risks "more balanced," inflation remains predominant concern. Some differences on specifics (productivity, NAIRU) but broad agreement on action and outlook. Score range: 16-25.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Subprime mentioned, rising delinquency rates, credit availability for leveraged borrowers tightening, "complex and illiquid financial instruments" -- these are meaningful but not dominant topics. Housing drag is discussed prominently with financial stability implications. Several paragraphs touch on financial conditions. Score range: 42-52.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Future policy adjustments would depend on the outlook for economic growth and inflation." Inflation is predominant concern. No signal of imminent change in either direction. Data-dependent with hawkish lean. Score range: 58-64.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Housing is "most significant downside risk." Multiple risks cited on both sides. Several "could" and "risk" statements. But no language of unusual or heightened uncertainty -- it's standard risk-balancing. Score range: 35-45.

### Cross-dimension coherence check:
- OUTPUT_GAP (52) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (60): Labor is tighter than output gap suggests, which the document explicitly discusses as a "puzzle" related to productivity slowdown. Divergence is explained.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (62) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (60): Aligned -- hold with hawkish lean, data-dependent.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says elevated but moderating. Score ~62 = modestly above. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Conditions still "generally quite accommodative" but tightening emerging. Score ~38 = somewhat loose. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 62 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "participants agreed that the risk that inflation would fail to moderate as expected remained their predominant concern"; "a number of forces could sustain inflation pressures, including the generally high level of resource utilization, elevated energy and commodity prices, the decline in the exchange value of the dollar"; "core consumer price inflation had moderated of late" but "total consumer price inflation had moved substantially higher"
- Center of gravity: Participants see inflation as modestly above target with clear upside risks, though recent core readings are encouraging. The "predominant concern" framing elevates this above a simple "somewhat elevated."
- Minority views: Recent core data improvement provides some downside, but no participant is cited as sanguine about inflation.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 52 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "economic activity appeared to have expanded at a moderate pace"; "growth rising gradually to a pace close to that of potential output"; "generally high level of resource utilization"
- Center of gravity: Economy near potential with high resource utilization but growth recently below trend. Net assessment is approximately at or very slightly above potential.
- Minority views: Housing downside risk could pull below potential, but not the center of gravity view.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Most participants judged labor market conditions to remain rather tight, particularly for the most skilled workers"; "continued tautness of labor markets was something of a puzzle"; unemployment projected at 4.5-4.75%
- Center of gravity: Clearly tight labor market, acknowledged by most participants. Not at extreme tightness levels but firmly in the "tight" range.
- Minority views: "Some participants further noted that the level of the unemployment rate consistent with stable inflation could be lower than previously thought" -- this would imply less tightness than headline suggests.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 50 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "personal consumption expenditures appeared to be rising more slowly in recent months" but "participants generally anticipated moderate gains in consumption spending"; "recent data on business spending were more encouraging" with "orders and shipments for nondefense capital goods had stepped up"
- Center of gravity: Consumption slowing, business investment improving, housing contracting. Net picture is moderate/at trend.
- Minority views: Housing drag and potential saving rate increase create downside; strong equity markets and business spending create upside.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 38 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "credit market conditions seemed to remain generally quite accommodative"; "availability of credit to some highly leveraged and other lower-rated borrowers appeared to be tightening a bit"; "investors seemed to reevaluate the risks associated with investments in complex and illiquid financial instruments"
- Center of gravity: Conditions still accommodative overall but with early, nascent signs of tightening at the margin for riskier borrowers. The "generally quite accommodative" language dominates.
- Minority views: The emerging tightness in leveraged credit is noted but described as marginal and recent.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "members agreed that maintaining the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent for this meeting was appropriate"; "the risk that inflation would fail to moderate as expected remained their predominant concern"; "future policy adjustments would depend on the outlook"
- Center of gravity: Firm hold at 5.25% with inflation as predominant concern. No discussion of easing. The bias is clearly toward the hawkish side -- holding at a high rate with upside inflation risk dominant.
- Minority views: Growth risks "more balanced" suggests some easing of prior hawkish urgency, but no dovish voices emerge.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 20 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "members agreed" on rate decision; "participants generally agreed" on housing drag; "members generally anticipated" core inflation subdued. Broad consensus language throughout.
- Center of gravity: Strong agreement on holding rates, on the economic outlook, and on inflation as predominant concern. Minor differences on productivity and NAIRU questions but no dissents or sharp disagreements.
- Minority views: Some differences in emphasis (e.g., saving rate direction, productivity trends) but nothing approaching formal disagreement.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 48 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "rising mortgage delinquency rates and related difficulties in the subprime mortgage market"; "availability of credit to some highly leveraged and other lower-rated borrowers appeared to be tightening a bit"; "investors seemed to reevaluate the risks associated with investments in complex and illiquid financial instruments"; discussion of interest rate effects on housing/credit
- Center of gravity: Financial stability issues receive meaningful attention across multiple paragraphs -- subprime, leveraged lending, complex instruments, credit availability. Not the dominant topic but clearly a significant secondary focus. This is the pre-crisis moment where financial concerns are rising.
- Minority views: N/A

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE

- Center of gravity: Data-dependent with a hawkish lean. The "predominant concern" framing on inflation signals that the next move, if any, would more likely be tightening than easing. But no explicit signal of imminent action.
- Minority views: Growth risks acknowledged as more balanced, providing some hedge against a purely hawkish forward signal.
