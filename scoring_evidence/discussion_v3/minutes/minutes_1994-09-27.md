# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1994-09-27

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Unsegmented pre-2009 format -- staff and participant content mixed. I will score only portions with participant language cues ("members commented," "members noted," "members cited," etc.).

**Era context**: September 1994. The Fed had been tightening policy throughout 1994 (series of rate hikes starting in February). The economy was in a robust expansion, capacity utilization was high, and the concern was whether inflation would pick up. The August 1994 meeting had approved a discount rate increase from 3.5% to 4%. This meeting is a "hold with asymmetric tilt toward tightening" meeting.

**Quantifier distribution in this document**:
- "most of the members" -- agreed on maintaining steady policy
- "many members" -- used repeatedly (reports of labor scarcities, ready availability of financing, additional restraint could be needed, information should provide firmer basis)
- "some members" -- particular concern about above-trend growth, misgivings about asymmetric directive, moderation in consumer spending expected
- "a number of members" -- external sector contributing, monetary aggregates relevant, sluggish money growth
- "one member" -- favored immediate tightening (Broaddus), concern about dollar, credibility of anti-inflationary stance

### Dimension-by-dimension directional assessment:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as above the midpoint. "Some measures of inflation had picked up recently," "many private forecasters anticipated higher inflation in 1995 than in 1994," "the potential for additional inflation remained substantial." However, the pass-through to final goods prices has been "muted," wage inflation has not broadly risen. The pressure is building in pipeline/materials but not yet fully in final goods. This is a "risks of some rise in inflation rates probably had increased" situation -- somewhat elevated, with concern about further increases. Direction: above 50, but not dramatically so given that broad price measures remain contained. Score range: 58-65.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants describe "output near maximum sustainable levels," "robust expansion," "high levels of business activity," "growth in overall demand at a pace above the economy's long-run potential," "increasing capacity constraints." This is clearly above potential. The bright-line rule requires >50, and the language of "above potential" and "near maximum sustainable levels" pushes this firmly into the 55-65 range. Score: 60.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Increasing reports of scarcities of specific types of labor resources," "increasingly tight labor markets in many parts of the country." But "had not resulted in higher overall wage inflation" -- only "some upward pressure on wages of certain categories of workers." Unemployment was 6.1%, which in 1994 context was considered near or slightly above NAIRU. Tight but not extremely so. Score range: 55-60.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumer spending had been more buoyant than expected," "business fixed investment was viewed as likely to rise substantially further," "fairly brisk retail sales," motor vehicle sales improving. Some moderation expected ahead but current conditions are clearly above trend. Score range: 60-65.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: This is interesting. Despite the rate hikes, "many members stressed the ready availability of financing from increasingly aggressive bank lenders," "capital markets were providing continued support to a wide variety of borrowers," conditions that "might even be providing an extra impetus to spending." Market rates are up but actual financial conditions remain loose/accommodative. Score range: 28-35 (loose conditions despite tightening policy).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Most of the members agreed on the desirability of maintaining a steady policy course, at least for the near term." But "for many members this suggested that additional monetary restraint could well be needed at some time." They shifted to asymmetric bias toward tightening. One dissent for immediate tightening. This is a hold with a clear hawkish lean -- not hiking now but signaling readiness. Score range: 62-68.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal dissent (Broaddus). "Some members" had misgivings about asymmetric directive. "Views differed with regard to the likely need for some policy tightening over the weeks immediately ahead." This is a typical one-dissent meeting with some expressed reservations. Score range: 30-38.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Discussion of financial conditions is present but limited to credit availability and bank lending aggressiveness. No discussion of systemic risk, asset bubbles, or financial stability per se. Brief mentions as context for economic conditions. For 1994, this is typical low-emphasis. Score: 15-20.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Asymmetric directive toward tightening. "Additional monetary restraint could well be needed at some time." "Should incoming information point to a greater likelihood that price pressures would intensify, the Committee would need to act promptly and forcefully." This is a clear mild tightening signal. Score range: 65-72.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "How large this rise might be or when it might be reversed was very difficult to predict." "Inventory investment remained a key uncertainty." "It was extremely difficult to evaluate whether the earlier tightening moves were exerting a lesser effect than usual or it simply was more delayed." Multiple sources of genuine uncertainty about lags, inventory dynamics, and inflation path. Somewhat elevated. Score range: 42-50.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says pressures building. Score above 50. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says near/above maximum sustainable. Score above 50. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence says conditions remain loose/accommodative. Score below 50. Matches.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Hold with asymmetric tilt toward tightening. Score above 50. Matches.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Asymmetric toward firming, "act promptly and forcefully" if needed. Score above 50. Matches.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (60) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (57): Both above 50, labor slightly lower because wage pressures haven't fully materialized. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (65) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (68): Both hawkish-leaning, forward guidance slightly more hawkish because of the "promptly and forcefully" language. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 62 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "some measures of inflation had picked up recently and that many private forecasters anticipated higher inflation in 1995 than in 1994"
- "the risks of some rise in inflation rates probably had increased"
- "the potential for additional inflation remained substantial"
- Mitigating: "the pass-through of the higher costs of materials to the prices of final goods had been muted"

**Center of gravity**: Members perceive inflation as modestly above target with significant upside risk. Pipeline pressures are building (materials costs, capacity constraints) but final goods prices remain contained for now. The language of "picked up" and "substantial potential" places this clearly above neutral but short of alarming.

**Minority views**: One member (Broaddus) saw "signs of increasing price pressures and rising inflationary expectations" as already demanding action, suggesting he perceived higher pressure than the majority.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "output near maximum sustainable levels"
- "growth in overall demand at a pace above the economy's long-run potential"
- "increasing capacity constraints" and "capacity utilization moved up further from already high levels"

**Center of gravity**: Members clearly see the economy as operating above potential, with demand growth exceeding sustainable rates. The language "near maximum sustainable levels" and "above the economy's long-run potential" is unambiguous.

**Minority views**: None noted -- this was a consensus assessment.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 57 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "many of them referred to increasing reports of scarcities of specific types of labor resources"
- "increasingly tight labor markets in many parts of the country had not resulted in higher overall wage inflation, but members reported some upward pressure on the wages of certain categories of workers in strong demand"

**Center of gravity**: Labor markets are tight and tightening further, with specific skill shortages emerging. However, broad wage inflation has not materialized, keeping this below the "very tight" threshold.

**Minority views**: No explicit divergence noted on labor assessment.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "consumer spending had been more buoyant than expected over recent months"
- "business fixed investment was viewed as likely to rise substantially further over the next several quarters"
- "fairly brisk retail sales in many areas recently"
- Moderating factor: "the rate of growth had been moderating this year" for business equipment

**Center of gravity**: Both consumption and investment are above trend. Consumer spending surprised to the upside. Business investment growth has moderated from the extremely rapid 1993 pace but remains strong. Net assessment is clearly above trend.

**Minority views**: Some expected moderation ahead as pent-up demand is satisfied, but current conditions are strong.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 30 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "many members stressed the ready availability of financing from increasingly aggressive bank lenders"
- "despite higher interest rates, capital markets were providing continued support to a wide variety of borrowers"
- "a financial climate that might even be providing an extra impetus to spending"
- "the ready availability of bank credit and the receptivity of financial markets more generally argued that many borrowers...currently had access to ample financing"

**Center of gravity**: Despite the policy tightening actions of 1994, actual financial conditions remain loose. Members repeatedly emphasize that credit is freely available and bank lenders are aggressive. This is clearly on the accommodative side.

**Minority views**: "A number of members commented that the sluggish to moderate growth of a wide variety of money and credit measures provided some assurance" -- monetary aggregates suggest some restraint, even if credit conditions don't.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 65 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "most of the members agreed on the desirability of maintaining a steady policy course, at least for the near term"
- "for many members this suggested that additional monetary restraint could well be needed at some time"
- "the members generally supported a shift from the symmetry in the August directive to asymmetry toward restraint"
- "should incoming information point to a greater likelihood that price pressures would intensify, the Committee would need to act promptly and forcefully"

**Center of gravity**: The Committee is on hold but with a clear hawkish lean. They shifted to an asymmetric directive toward tightening and multiple members expect further tightening will be needed. This is a moderate hawkish stance -- holding but ready to tighten.

**Minority views**: One member (Broaddus) dissented in favor of immediate tightening. "Some members" had misgivings about the asymmetric directive language (not the direction, but the communication).

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 33 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- One formal dissent: "Mr. Broaddus dissented because he believed that a prompt move to somewhat greater monetary restraint was needed"
- "views differed with regard to the likely need for some policy tightening over the weeks immediately ahead"
- "some members indicated that they could accept an asymmetric directive, but they expressed misgivings"
- Vote: 11-1

**Center of gravity**: One formal dissent with several expressed reservations. This is a typical one-dissent meeting with moderate but not deep disagreement. The disagreement is about timing (now vs. later), not direction.

**Minority views**: Broaddus wanted immediate action; some had concerns about asymmetric directive communication.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 18 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "many members stressed the ready availability of financing from increasingly aggressive bank lenders" -- financial conditions discussed but as an economic input, not a stability concern
- No discussion of systemic risk, asset bubbles, or financial fragility

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are discussed as they relate to the economic outlook (credit availability supporting spending), but there is no explicit financial stability discussion. Consistent with mid-1990s low emphasis on financial stability as a separate concern.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 68 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "additional monetary restraint could well be needed at some time"
- "the Committee would need to act promptly and forcefully to avert an upward ratcheting of inflationary expectations"
- Asymmetric directive: "somewhat greater reserve restraint would be acceptable"

**Center of gravity**: The forward signal is clearly hawkish. The Committee has adopted an asymmetric tilt toward tightening and explicitly signals willingness to act "promptly and forcefully" if inflation pressures intensify. This is a clear tightening bias.

**Minority views**: Some members were uncertain about the timing of further action, but the directional signal was broadly shared.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 47 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "how large this rise might be or when it might be reversed was very difficult to predict at this point"
- "the prospects for inventory investment remained a key uncertainty in the outlook"
- "it was extremely difficult to evaluate whether the earlier tightening moves were exerting a lesser effect than usual or it simply was more delayed, or whether the members might have misjudged the underlying strength of the expansion"

**Center of gravity**: Elevated uncertainty on several fronts: the transmission lag of prior tightening, inventory dynamics, and the inflation path. Multiple uses of "very difficult to predict" and "extremely difficult to evaluate" indicate genuinely elevated uncertainty, above normal but not extreme.
