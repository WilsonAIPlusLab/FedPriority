# FOMC Meeting Scoring — 2000-11-15 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Unsegmented pre-2009 format — staff and participant content mixed. I will score only portions with participant language cues ("members," "the members," "several," "a few," etc.) and ignore staff-attributed content.

**Era context**: November 2000. The fed funds rate is at 6.5%, the highest in the hiking cycle. The economy is slowing from the late-1990s boom. Equity markets (especially tech) have been declining. Energy prices are elevated. The labor market remains very tight. This is the inflection point where the economy is transitioning from above-potential to a slowdown.

**Quantifier distribution**: The document uses "all the members" (unanimity on hold), "several" (near-term shift possibility), "a few" (close call on risk statement; core inflation near acceptable limits), "some" (dollar depreciation, financial weakness abroad, energy prices). The dominant signal is unanimity on hold with inflation-weighted risk assessment.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly above target and trending higher. Evidence: "members continued to be concerned about the outlook for inflation," "core consumer price measures also displayed a gradual uptrend," "measures of labor compensation appeared to be accelerating," "risks were in the direction of a heightening in inflation pressures." A few noted core inflation "already were near or slightly above levels that they viewed as acceptable for the longer run." Direction: above midpoint. Magnitude: "concerned" + "gradual uptrend" + "risks weighted toward" = moderately above. Score ~60-65. Polarity check: higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says more pressure perceived. Matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Aggregate demand currently appeared to be growing at a pace a little below the rate of increase in the economy's output potential." "Growth in overall demand now seemed to have declined to a more sustainable pace and probably would continue to expand for a time at a rate below that of the economy's output potential." This says demand growth is BELOW potential growth, which implies the output gap is narrowing/closing. But the economy was previously above potential and is now approaching it from above. The language says "a pace a little below" potential growth — meaning the gap is closing but they still describe tight labor markets and resource pressures. They don't say the economy IS below potential, just that growth is below potential growth rate. The economy was still operating near or slightly above potential. Score ~50-53. Polarity check: higher = more above potential. Evidence says economy near potential, growth decelerating. Score around 50-52.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "labor markets were likely to remain unusually tight for some time," "very tight conditions in labor markets," "wages to catch up to earlier gains in labor productivity." Direction: clearly tight. "Unusually tight" = substantially above midpoint. Score ~68-72. Anchors suggest 65-75 for 1999-2000. Polarity check: higher = tighter. Evidence says very tight. Matches.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Investment growth has moderated substantially ("substantial moderation in the growth of business fixed investment"), especially in high-tech. Consumer spending shows "some softening." But investment is still growing, just at a slower pace. Consumer spending "held up well in the third quarter." Net: slowing from above-trend toward trend. Score ~45-50. The language of "substantial moderation" and "weakening" pulls below 50, but "still growing at a robust pace" (high-tech) and "appreciable further growth" in investment keeps it from going too low. Score ~46.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Very clearly discussed. "Less accommodative financial conditions," "some further tightening since the meeting in early October," "tightening terms and standards at banks," "spreads in securities markets had widened, most sharply on obligations of borrowers rated below investment grade," "considerable weakening recently in overall growth of credit." This is clearly tight. Score ~62-68. Polarity check: higher = tighter. Evidence says tightening. Matches.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All members supported holding rates steady. They unanimously retained the inflation-weighted risk statement. But they discussed whether to shift to a balanced risk statement — "a stronger case could be made for a shift" — and chose not to, partly because it might "induce an undesirable softening in overall financial market conditions." They wanted to acknowledge "increased downside risks to the economic expansion." This is a hold with a hawkish tilt but notably softening from previous meetings. Score ~55-58. They are holding, biased toward inflation concerns, but clearly recognizing the shift toward balanced/easing. Not actively considering tightening.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All the members" supported holding. "They were unanimous" on retaining the inflation risk statement. "A few indicated that the decision was a close call" on the risk statement. This is broad agreement with minor nuances. Score ~18-22.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Substantial discussion of financial conditions — entire paragraphs on tightening credit, equity declines, spreads widening, effects on borrowing and investment. Also discussion of foreign financial risks. This is a prominent topic. Score ~50-58.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: They retained the inflation-weighted risk statement but added recognition of downside risks to growth. The forward signal is mildly hawkish — still weighted toward inflation — but softening. Score ~58-62. They're signaling that the next move could be in either direction, but inflation bias remains.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "A great deal of uncertainty, including the potential for more difficulties in the Middle East, surrounded the timing and extent" of energy price declines. "The degree to which growth in demand might remain sufficiently damped to contain and offset those inflation pressures was quite uncertain." "Whether offsetting increases in the growth of labor productivity would materialize was open to question." Multiple sources of uncertainty. Score ~48-55.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (~51) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (~70): Divergent. Explanation: The economy is decelerating (output gap closing) but labor markets remain very tight due to lag — tightness built up during the boom hasn't unwound yet. This is consistent with late-cycle dynamics.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (~56) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (~60): Aligned — both mildly hawkish/hold with inflation bias, but softening.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 62 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "members continued to be concerned about the outlook for inflation"
- "core consumer price measures also displayed a gradual uptrend"
- "the members generally agreed that the risks were in the direction of a heightening in inflation pressures"
- "a few members commented that measures of core inflation already were near or slightly above levels that they viewed as acceptable for the longer run"

**Center of gravity**: Members unanimously perceived inflation as modestly above comfort levels with upside risks, driven by tight labor markets, energy costs, and a gradual uptrend in core measures.

**Minority views**: None really — the concern was near-universal. Some favorable factors (productivity, competitive pressures) provided partial offset, widening the band slightly.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 52 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Aggregate demand currently appeared to be growing at a pace a little below the rate of increase in the economy's output potential"
- "growth in overall demand now seemed to have declined to a more sustainable pace and probably would continue to expand for a time at a rate below that of the economy's output potential"
- Context of tight labor markets and resource pressures suggests economy still at or slightly above potential even as growth decelerates.

**Center of gravity**: The economy was near potential — previously above, now with demand growth slightly below potential growth rate, implying the gap is closing from above. Still near/slightly above potential.

**Minority views**: "several noted the possibility that a less restrictive fiscal policy stance would be bolstering demand in the years ahead" — suggesting some saw potential re-acceleration.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 70 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "labor markets were likely to remain unusually tight for some time"
- "the anticipated persistence of very tight conditions in labor markets that would enable wages to catch up to earlier gains in labor productivity"
- "measures of labor compensation appeared to be accelerating"

**Center of gravity**: Unanimous view that labor markets were very tight and would remain so, with wage pressures building.

**Minority views**: None — this was a consensus view.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 46 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "substantial moderation in the growth of business fixed investment in recent months"
- "recent deceleration was especially pronounced in expenditures for high-tech equipment and software, though such spending was still growing at a robust pace"
- Consumer spending: "limited information available on more recent developments suggested some softening"
- "appreciable further growth in investment spending seemed to be in prospect for coming quarters, though undoubtedly at a slower pace"

**Center of gravity**: Both investment and consumption were decelerating from above-trend toward or slightly below trend. Still growing, but moderation is the dominant theme.

**Minority views**: Some saw resilience in long-term business optimism; others focused on downside risks from profit squeezes and wealth effects.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 64 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "emergence in recent months of less accommodative financial conditions for many businesses, including some further tightening since the meeting in early October"
- "spreads in securities markets had widened, most sharply on obligations of borrowers rated below investment grade"
- "tightening terms and standards at banks for business borrowers"
- "considerable weakening recently in overall growth of credit to nonfinancial businesses"

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions had tightened meaningfully, with widening spreads, tighter bank lending standards, and reduced credit availability — clearly above neutral.

**Minority views**: None — this was stated as fact/consensus observation.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 56 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all the members supported a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance" at 6.5%
- "persisting risks of heightened inflation pressures remained a policy concern"
- "they were unanimous in concluding that such a change [to balanced risk] would be premature at this time"
- "a stronger case could be made for a shift to a balanced risk statement than at the previous meeting" and "several commented that developments might be moving in a direction that would make a shift advisable in the relatively near future"

**Center of gravity**: Hold with inflation bias retained, but clearly softening. The committee is transitioning from hawkish to balanced. No one advocated tightening; the debate was about how much to soften the forward posture.

**Minority views**: "A few indicated that the decision [on risk statement] was a close call" — leaning more balanced/dovish.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 18 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all the members supported a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance"
- "they were unanimous in concluding that such a change would be premature at this time"
- "A few indicated that the decision was a close call for them" — minor internal variation

**Center of gravity**: Near-unanimous agreement on both the action and the risk assessment, with only minor nuance on the closeness of the risk statement call.

**Minority views**: The "close call" members represent mild variation, not true dissent.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 55 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Full paragraph devoted to financial conditions (tightening credit, widening spreads, equity declines)
- "Rising interest and energy costs in conjunction with restraint on the prices of final output had depressed the earnings and stock market valuations of many firms, notably in the high-tech area"
- Discussion of foreign financial risks: "financial and economic weakness in a number of foreign economies... risk of economic and financial distress, with possible spillover effects"
- "a shift in the Committee's published views might induce an undesirable softening in overall financial market conditions"

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions were a moderately prominent topic — several paragraphs discussed credit tightening, equity declines, and their real economy effects. Not the dominant meeting theme (inflation was), but substantially discussed.

**Minority views**: N/A — broadly discussed.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 60 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Retained inflation-weighted risk statement — forward signal leans hawkish
- "it was desirable to provide some recognition of the emergence of increased downside risks to the economic expansion in the statement"
- "several commented that developments might be moving in a direction that would make a shift advisable in the relatively near future"

**Center of gravity**: Forward signal is mildly hawkish — inflation risk retained — but clearly softening, with the committee signaling it is approaching a shift to balanced.

**Minority views**: Those for whom it was a "close call" pull the guidance slightly more balanced.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 50 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "a great deal of uncertainty, including the potential for more difficulties in the Middle East, surrounded the timing and extent of such an outcome" (energy prices)
- "the degree to which growth in demand might remain sufficiently damped to contain and offset those inflation pressures was quite uncertain"
- "Whether offsetting increases in the growth of labor productivity would materialize was open to question"
- "economic growth had rebounded sharply from temporary slowdowns previously in the current expansion" — uncertainty about whether this slowdown is different

**Center of gravity**: Multiple dimensions of genuine uncertainty acknowledged — energy, productivity, demand trajectory. Language like "a great deal of uncertainty" and "quite uncertain" indicates elevated but not extreme uncertainty.

**Minority views**: N/A.
