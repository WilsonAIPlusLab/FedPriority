# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2017-12-13 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented format -- participant views are clearly separated from staff content.

**Era context**: December 2017. The economy is in mid-expansion, near full employment, with the Fed in a gradual hiking cycle (this meeting raises the target to 1.25-1.50%). Tax reform legislation (TCJA) is under active consideration. Inflation has been running persistently below 2% despite tightening labor markets -- the "missing inflation" puzzle of 2017.

**Quantifier distribution**: "almost all" (supported rate hike), "most" (gradual approach), "many" (tax boost to spending; cyclical pressures to raise inflation), "several" (low inflation expectations concern; yield curve monitoring), "some" (accommodative financial conditions; inflation could stay below target longer), "a few" (tax effects on labor supply; transitory factors; alternative frameworks; faster pace preferred; slower pace preferred), "a couple" (preferred no hike; concern about financial stability risks; balance sheet normalization).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target. PCE at 1.6%, core at 1.4%. "Many" expect cyclical pressures to push inflation up over the medium term, but "some" worry it could stay below target longer. "Several" concerned about declining inflation expectations. The center of gravity is clearly below 2% with a forward expectation of gradual return. Direction: below midpoint. The anchor for 2013-19 below-target is 38-42. Given the explicit below-2% readings and persistent shortfall concern, this fits around 38-40.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see "above-trend growth" and "further strengthening in labor market conditions." The text mentions the economy "projected to move further above its potential" (staff language, but participants echo via "output expanded well beyond its maximum sustainable level" as a risk). Participants say "continued above-trend growth." This is consistent with the economy at or modestly above potential. The bright-line rule: "above-trend" language means >50. Score ~52-54.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment declining further, payroll gains "well above" pace consistent with stable unemployment, "further tightening in labor market conditions," "in several Districts... difficulty in finding qualified workers," "labor shortages were making it hard to fill customer demand." Wage growth "generally modest" however. This is a tight labor market but without severe wage pressure yet. Anchor for 2016-17 is ~50. Given the explicit tightening language and shortages, this is somewhat above the 2016-17 anchor -- perhaps 55-58.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "expanding at a moderate rate," "moderate growth in consumer spending" expected, "strong pre-holiday sales" in a few Districts, business investment "picked up in recent quarters," business contacts "optimistic." Tax reform expected to provide "modest boost." This is solidly at or slightly above trend. Score ~53-56.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Some participants observed that financial conditions remained accommodative, citing low interest rates, narrow credit spreads, high equity values, a lower dollar, and some evidence of easier terms for lending to risky borrowers." "Highly accommodative financial conditions" mentioned as posing risks. Conditions are clearly on the loose/accommodative side. Higher = tighter, so this should be well below 50. Anchor for 2017-19 normal is 45-55, but "accommodative" is explicitly loose. Score ~30-35.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Almost all" supported a 25bp hike. "Most" reiterated gradual approach. "A few" wanted faster pace, "a few" wanted slower. Two members dissented (preferred no hike). The center of gravity is a 25bp hike with gradual continuation -- solidly hawkish but moderate. Anchor for 2017-18 hiking is 70-75. Given two dissents on the dovish side and "a few" wanting faster pace, the center is somewhat moderated. Score ~67-70.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Two formal dissents (dovish -- preferred no hike). "A few" wanted faster pace than SEP median, "a few" wanted slower. There are distinct camps but the vast majority agree. Two dissents with some range of views on pace. Anchor for typical one-dissent meeting is 25-35; two dissents with different camps pushes this to ~35-42. Score ~38.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Substantial discussion of financial conditions (accommodative conditions, elevated asset valuations, low volatility). Significant discussion of yield curve flattening and its implications. "A couple" expressed concern about risks to financial stability from accommodative conditions. Several paragraphs devoted to this topic. This is above the 2013-19 typical anchor of 20-35. Score ~40-45.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Most" reiterated "gradual approach to raising the target range." Risks discussed in both directions -- "steeper path" if inflation builds vs "flatter trajectory" if inflation fails to return to 2%. The forward signal is for continued gradual tightening but data-dependent. "A few" wanted faster, "a few" wanted slower. This is a mild tightening bias forward. Anchor for 2015-18 hiking cycle is 60-75. Given the emphasis on "gradual" and the two-directional risk discussion, this is on the lower end. Score ~65.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Near-term risks to the economic outlook appeared to be roughly balanced." "Considerable uncertainty remained about government policies." "Magnitude of effects was uncertain" (tax reform, multiple mentions). Uncertainty about inflation persistence. This is somewhat elevated -- more than standard caveats but not extreme. Score ~35-38.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (~53) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (~57): Both modestly above 50, consistent -- economy near/slightly above potential with a tight labor market. The labor market is a bit tighter than the output gap suggests, which is consistent with the late-cycle pattern where labor tightness leads.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (~68) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (~65): Aligned -- current action is a hike and forward signal is continued gradual tightening. The current action is slightly more hawkish than the forward signal, which makes sense given the emphasis on "gradual" and data dependence going forward.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (39): Higher = more pressure. Evidence says below target, muted. Low score = less pressure. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP (53): Higher = more above potential. Evidence says above-trend growth. Score >50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (32): Higher = tighter. Evidence says accommodative/loose. Low score. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (68): Higher = more hawkish. Almost all supported hike with gradual continuation. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 39 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "PCE price inflation over the 12 months ending in October, at 1.6 percent, continued to run below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent; core PCE price inflation... was only 1.4 percent"
- "Many indicated that they expected cyclical pressures associated with a tightening labor market to show through to higher inflation over the medium term"
- "some participants observed that there was a possibility that inflation might stay below the objective for longer than they currently expected"

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation is running well below 2%, with "many" expecting a gradual return but "some" worried it could stay low longer. The balance tips modestly below-target with forward hopes of normalization.

**Minority views**: "Several" concerned about declining inflation expectations (pushes lower); "a few" noted transitory factors explain the softness (pushes higher in forward view). Band of +/-6 reflects this range.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 53 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "incoming information on spending and the labor market as consistent with continued above-trend growth and a further strengthening in labor market conditions"
- "economic activity had been rising at a solid rate"
- Participants discussed the risk that "output expanded well beyond its maximum sustainable level"

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is growing above trend with a slight positive output gap. Participants see the economy as modestly above potential, consistent with late 2017 conditions.

**Minority views**: Narrow -- broad agreement on the characterization. Uncertainty is more about the trajectory (tax reform effects) than the current level.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 57 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "unemployment rate declining further and payroll gains well above a pace consistent with maintaining a stable unemployment rate over time"
- "in several Districts, reports from business contacts or evidence from surveys pointed to some difficulty in finding qualified workers; in some cases, labor shortages were making it hard to fill customer demand"
- "pace of wage increases had generally been modest and in line with inflation and productivity growth"

**Center-of-gravity**: The labor market is notably tight -- low and falling unemployment, worker shortages in several Districts -- but wage pressures remain limited, preventing a higher score.

**Minority views**: "A few" judged tightness would translate to wage acceleration; "another observed that the absence of broad-based upward wage pressures suggested scope for further improvement." Band of +/-5.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 55 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Household spending had been expanding at a moderate rate, and growth in business fixed investment had picked up in recent quarters"
- "Participants expected moderate growth in consumer spending in the near term, underpinned by ongoing strength in the labor market"
- "Many participants judged that the proposed changes in business taxes... would likely provide a modest boost to capital spending"

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending moderate and steady, business investment strengthening. Overall slightly above trend with anticipated tax reform boost. "Moderate" language maps to at-trend/slightly above.

**Minority views**: "Some business contacts... cautious about expanding capital spending" and may use tax savings for buybacks instead. Moderate confidence due to uncertainty about tax effects.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "some participants observed that financial conditions remained accommodative, citing a range of indicators including low interest rates, narrow credit spreads, high equity values, a lower dollar, and some evidence of easier terms for lending to risky borrowers"
- "a couple of participants expressed concern that the persistence of highly accommodative financial conditions could, over time, pose risks to financial stability"
- "term premiums on longer-term nominal Treasury securities remained low"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly accommodative/loose despite ongoing rate hikes. Multiple indicators cited pointing to easy conditions. Score well below neutral.

**Minority views**: No participants described conditions as tight. Narrow band reflects agreement on the characterization.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 68 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "almost all participants expressed the view that it would be appropriate for the Committee to raise the target range for the federal funds rate 25 basis points at this meeting"
- "Most participants reiterated their support for continuing a gradual approach to raising the target range"
- "Two members preferred to leave the target range at 1 to 1-1/4 percent"
- "A few other participants mentioned that they saw as appropriate a pace of additional policy tightening... somewhat faster than that implied by the December SEP median forecast"

**Center-of-gravity**: Strong consensus for a 25bp hike with continued gradual tightening. The center is clearly hawkish (active tightening) but moderate in pace.

**Minority views**: Two dovish dissents (no hike) and a few wanting faster tightening create a wider band of +/-8 around the hawkish-but-gradual center.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Two members preferred to leave the target range at 1 to 1-1/4 percent" (formal dissents)
- "A few participants indicated that they were not comfortable with the degree of additional policy tightening through the end of 2018 implied by the median projections"
- "A few other participants mentioned that they saw as appropriate a pace... somewhat faster"

**Center-of-gravity**: Two formal dissents plus visible disagreement about the pace of future tightening. More than minor differences but not a deep split -- the vast majority agreed on the current action.

**Minority views**: The disagreement extends beyond the two dissents to forward pace, but core action had near-consensus.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 42 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Extended discussion of accommodative financial conditions and elevated asset valuations
- Multiple paragraphs on yield curve flattening: "several participants thought that it would be important to continue to monitor the slope of the yield curve"
- "Some expressed concern that a possible future inversion of the yield curve... could portend an economic slowdown"
- "a couple of participants expressed concern that the persistence of highly accommodative financial conditions could, over time, pose risks to financial stability"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability received meaningful attention from "several" and "some" participants across multiple topics (asset valuations, yield curve, bank conditions). Above the typical 2013-19 range of 20-35.

**Minority views**: Most of the discussion is monitoring-oriented rather than alarm-driven. A couple specifically flagged stability risks.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 65 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Most participants reiterated their support for continuing a gradual approach to raising the target range"
- Participants discussed risks in both directions: "steeper path" if inflation builds vs "flatter trajectory" if inflation fails to rise
- "A few participants indicated that they were not comfortable with the degree of additional policy tightening through the end of 2018 implied by the median projections"
- "A few other participants mentioned that they saw as appropriate a pace... somewhat faster"

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is continued gradual tightening, data-dependent, with risks discussed in both directions. This is a mild-to-moderate tightening bias going forward.

**Minority views**: "A few" wanted slower and "a few" wanted faster, creating a wider uncertainty band around the gradual center.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 36 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Near-term risks to the economic outlook appeared to be roughly balanced"
- "A number of participants expressed uncertainty about the magnitude of the effects of tax reform on consumer spending"
- "magnitude of the effects was uncertain" (regarding business tax changes)
- "some participants observed that there was a possibility that inflation might stay below the objective for longer than they currently expected"

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is somewhat elevated relative to a baseline, driven primarily by tax reform effects and the inflation outlook puzzle. But risks are characterized as "roughly balanced," indicating no extreme concern.

**Minority views**: Uncertainty clusters around tax effects and inflation persistence, not the broad outlook.
