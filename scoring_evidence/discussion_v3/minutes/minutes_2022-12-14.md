# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2022-12-14 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented -- participant discussion section is clearly separated from staff material. I will focus on the "Participants' Views on Current Conditions and the Economic Outlook" and "Committee Policy Action" sections.

**Era context**: December 2022. The Fed had been aggressively hiking rates throughout 2022, including multiple 75bp moves. This meeting marks a step-down to a 50bp hike (4.25-4.50% target range). Inflation was still well above 2% but showing early signs of deceleration. The labor market remained very tight. Growth had slowed.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all participants" (agree on 50bp hike, raised rate path assessments); "most" (emphasized flexibility/optionality); "many" (highlighted balancing two risks; observed housing services rent deceleration); "several" (tentative signs of labor improvement; cautioned against premature loosening; noted upside inflation risks from expectations unanchoring); "some" (markups elevated; labor supply constrained; payroll gains robust but slowing); "a couple" (excess savings support; contract cancellations; yield curve recession signal; inflation risks becoming more balanced); "a few" (yield curve bore watching; labor demand retained workers).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants "agreed that inflation was unacceptably high" and "well above the Committee's longer-run goal of 2 percent." October/November data showed "welcome reductions in the monthly pace of price increases" but they stressed "substantially more evidence of progress" was needed. Core services ex-shelter was "high" and "closely linked to nominal wage growth." This is clearly elevated inflation that has just begun to show early signs of deceleration. The language is strongly above target but acknowledges the first signs of progress. Anchor: 82-88 for 2022 Q2-Q3 peak. By December 2022, some easing was noted in goods prices, but services inflation remained high and overall was still "unacceptably high." I would place this in the 76-82 range -- still substantially above target but with the first hints of moderation from peak.

Direction: Participants see inflation as substantially above the midpoint because it is "unacceptably high" and "well above" target.
Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says very high. Score should be high. Matches.

Investment spending was "modest" and being restrained by high borrowing costs. However, the labor market remained "very tight" with demand outpacing supply. The labor tightness dominates -- the economy is still running somewhat above potential even as growth slows toward below-trend. I will score around 52-55 -- modestly above potential given the labor market tightness, but growth is clearly decelerating.

Direction: Participants see the output gap as modestly above potential given labor market tightness, but growth is decelerating to below trend.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 78 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Participants agreed that inflation was unacceptably high" and "inflation remained well above the Committee's longer-run goal of 2 percent"
- "The inflation data received for October and November showed welcome reductions in the monthly pace of price increases, but they stressed that it would take substantially more evidence of progress to be confident that inflation was on a sustained downward path"
- "The pace of increase for prices of core services excluding shelter -- which represents the largest component of core PCE price inflation -- was high"

Center of gravity: Participants unanimously viewed inflation as unacceptably high, but acknowledged early signs of deceleration in goods prices and leading indicators for housing services. Core services ex-shelter remained stubbornly elevated.

Minority views: "A couple of participants noted that risks to the inflation outlook were becoming more balanced," but this was clearly a minority position against the dominant view of upside inflation risks.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 53 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Growth of economic activity had slowed significantly in 2022" and "economic activity appeared likely to expand in 2023 at a pace well below its trend growth rate"
- "Recent indicators pointed to modest growth of spending and production"
- Labor market "very tight" with "large imbalance between labor supply and labor demand"

Center of gravity: Despite clearly decelerating GDP growth, the very tight labor market and persistent demand-supply imbalances indicate the economy is still operating modestly above potential.

Minority views: The wide band reflects tension between slowing growth indicators and persistently tight labor conditions. Some participants appeared to view the economy as closer to or at potential given the growth deceleration.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 70 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Labor market had remained very tight, with the unemployment rate near a historically low level, robust payroll gains, a high level of job vacancies, and elevated nominal wage growth"
- "There remained a large imbalance between labor supply and labor demand"
- "Several participants commented that there were tentative signs of labor market imbalances improving, including declines in job openings and quits"

Center of gravity: The labor market was very tight by all measures, though several participants noted early signs of improvement at the margin.

Minority views: "Some participants pointed out" that alternative employment measures "suggested that job growth in 2022 may have been weaker than indicated by payroll employment," introducing some uncertainty about the true degree of tightness.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 42 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Growth in consumer spending in September and October had been stronger than they had previously expected" but "budgets were stretched for low-to-moderate-income households" and consumers "shifting their spending to less expensive alternatives"
- "Higher mortgage interest rates had notably restrained housing activity"
- "Growth in investment spending appeared modest and was being restrained by high borrowing costs"

Center of gravity: Consumer spending was holding up better than expected but showing signs of strain, housing was notably weak, and business investment was modest. The net picture is slightly below trend.

Minority views: Views on excess savings diverged -- "a couple" saw continued support while "a couple of other participants" saw savings declining faster. "Considerable uncertainty around the consumer spending outlook."

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 62 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Since the November meeting, financial conditions had eased, with the market-implied path for the federal funds rate beyond 2023 and longer-term yields coming down noticeably"
- "Higher mortgage interest rates had notably restrained housing activity" and "high borrowing costs" restrained investment
- Participants worried that "an unwarranted easing in financial conditions...would complicate the Committee's effort to restore price stability"

Center of gravity: Financial conditions were tight and exerting meaningful restraint on economic activity, but had eased somewhat since the November meeting, which participants viewed as unwelcome.

Minority views: "A few participants" noted yield curve inversion as a recession signal; "a couple of them" attributed it partly to expected inflation decline rather than recession.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 76 +/-4 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "All participants agreed that it was appropriate to raise the target range for the federal funds rate 50 basis points"
- "No participants anticipated that it would be appropriate to begin reducing the federal funds rate target in 2023"
- "Several participants commented that historical experience cautioned against prematurely loosening monetary policy"
- "Most participants emphasized the need to retain flexibility and optionality"

Center of gravity: Unanimously hawkish, with a 50bp hike (step-down from 75bp) and clear expectation of further increases. Emphasis on maintaining restrictive stance for a sustained period, tempered by data-dependence language.

Minority views: Minimal divergence on the hawkish direction; disagreement centered on degree of future tightening and risk balance rather than direction.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 22 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "All participants agreed" on the 50bp hike; all agreed inflation was unacceptably high
- "No participants anticipated" rate cuts in 2023
- "A couple of participants noted that risks to the inflation outlook were becoming more balanced" vs. the dominant view of upside risks

Center of gravity: Broad consensus on the action and assessment. Differences were in emphasis (degree of flexibility, risk balance) rather than direction.

Minority views: Very limited -- "a couple" on balanced inflation risks, some divergence on excess savings outlook.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 38 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- Discussion of eased financial conditions since November and concern that "unwarranted easing in financial conditions...would complicate the Committee's effort to restore price stability"
- "A few participants remarked that the current configuration of nominal yields...had historically preceded recessions"
- Discussion of credit use by consumers and mortgage qualification issues

Center of gravity: Financial conditions were discussed meaningfully as part of the monetary policy transmission channel, but this was not a financial stability-focused meeting. No systemic stress concerns; the discussion was about policy effectiveness rather than stability risks.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 82 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Participants continued to anticipate that ongoing increases in the target range for the federal funds rate would be appropriate"
- "No participants anticipated that it would be appropriate to begin reducing the federal funds rate target in 2023"
- "A restrictive policy stance would need to be maintained until the incoming data provided confidence that inflation was on a sustained downward path to 2 percent, which was likely to take some time"
- "All participants had raised their assessment of the appropriate path of the federal funds rate relative to their assessment at the time of the September meeting"

Center of gravity: The forward signal was clearly hawkish -- more hikes ahead, restrictive for a sustained period, no rate cuts in 2023. Tempered by emphasis on data dependence and flexibility.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Participants generally noted that the uncertainty associated with their economic outlooks was high"
- "Heightened uncertainty regarding the outlooks for both inflation and real economic activity"
- "Most participants emphasized the need to retain flexibility and optionality"
- Multiple global uncertainty sources: China zero-COVID relaxation, Russia-Ukraine, synchronous global tightening

Center of gravity: Participants explicitly and broadly characterized uncertainty as high, citing multiple domestic and international sources. This goes beyond standard hedging -- "high" uncertainty expressed by "most" participants.
