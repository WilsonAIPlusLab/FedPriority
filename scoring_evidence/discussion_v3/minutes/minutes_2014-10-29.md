# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2014-10-29 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized participant views). Segmented -- participant discussion is clearly separated from staff content.

**Era context**: October 2014 -- mid-recovery period. Economy expanding moderately, labor market improving but still with slack, inflation running below 2% target. QE tapering concluded at this meeting. Fed funds rate at 0-0.25%, with normalization discussion underway but no liftoff yet. Dollar strengthening; foreign growth concerns (Europe, China, Japan). Energy prices declining.

**Quantifier distribution**: "most" used frequently (economic views, inflation expectations, forward guidance), "many" used for several topics (energy price boost, limited foreign spillovers, inflation compensation monitoring), "several" and "a number of" used for secondary views, "a few" and "a couple" for minority positions.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW target. "Inflation was continuing to run below the Committee's longer-run objective." "Most participants anticipated that inflation was likely to edge lower in the near term, reflecting the decline in oil and other commodity prices." Market-based inflation compensation declined. "A few expressed concern that inflation might persist below the Committee's objective for quite some time." The center of gravity is clearly below target, with near-term downward pressure from energy prices, though most expected a medium-term return to 2%. Direction: below midpoint. Magnitude: "below objective" + "edge lower near term" = somewhat to considerably below. Score ~32. Higher = more pressure. Evidence says low/declining pressure. Score matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see economy as BELOW potential but improving. "Economic activity continued to expand at a moderate pace." "Underutilization of labor resources was gradually diminishing." "A number of participants noted that underutilization of labor market resources remained." "Some underutilization in the labor market remained." The language consistently says below potential with gap gradually closing. Bright-line rule: "underutilization" = below potential = must be < 50. Score ~42. The gap is narrowing, so closer to 50 than deep recession.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see labor market as BELOW full employment but improving. "Labor market conditions improved somewhat further, with solid job gains and a lower unemployment rate." But "underutilization of labor resources was gradually diminishing" (still present). "A couple of participants judged that the large number of individuals working part time for economic reasons and the continued drift down in the labor force participation rate suggested that the unemployment rate was understating the degree of labor market underutilization." "Relatively few pointing to emerging wage pressures." Direction: below full employment. Score ~38. Anchors: 35-40 for 2011-14 range.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Participants see spending as MODERATE, roughly at trend. "Household spending advanced at a moderate pace." "Recent retail or auto sales had been robust." "Many participants judged that the recent significant decline in energy prices would provide a boost to consumer spending." Business conditions improving. Housing sector "remained slow." Business fixed investment "advancing." Net: moderate with some positive momentum. Score ~50-52. The combination of moderate household spending with robust pockets and slow housing nets to roughly at-trend.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see financial conditions as LOOSE/accommodative. "Financial conditions remained highly accommodative." This is stated explicitly. Mid-October turbulence was noted but judged as not fundamentally altering the accommodative stance. Score ~25. Higher = tighter. Evidence says highly accommodative = loose = low score. Matches.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: This is the meeting where QE was concluded, which itself is a step toward normalization. Rates held at zero. Debate about "considerable time" language. "Some participants preferred to eliminate" the considerable time language (more hawkish), while "other participants thought that the 'considerable time' phrase was useful." "A couple of others thought that the current forward guidance might be read as suggesting an earlier date of liftoff than was likely to prove appropriate" (dovish). The center of gravity: ending QE is a hawkish step, but still at zero rates with "considerable time" retained. Pre-liftoff stance leaning toward eventual normalization. Anchor: 40-50 for 2014-15 pre-liftoff. Score ~48. Balanced to slight lean toward eventual tightening.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All members but one supported concluding the Committee's asset purchase program." So there was one dissent on ending QE. Debate about "considerable time" language showed different camps but not deep division. "A number of participants" vs "some participants" vs "other participants" on forward guidance language -- shows moderate differences in views. Score ~30. One dissent, moderate language disagreements.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Dedicated paragraph on financial market developments and financial stability. "Many participants commented on the turbulence in financial markets that occurred in mid-October." Discussion of market dynamics, wholesale funding markets. Also discussion about whether to reference financial market developments in the statement. Moderate attention. Score ~40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Retaining "considerable time" language and zero rates. "Most participants supported retaining the language... indicating that the Committee anticipates that economic conditions may warrant keeping the target range for the federal funds rate below longer-run normal levels even after employment and inflation are near mandate-consistent levels." But also ending QE and debating removing "considerable time." Forward signal: leaning toward eventual normalization but still in accommodative stance. Anchor: 25-40 for 2015 pre-liftoff. Score ~35. Mild easing lean with data dependence, moving toward normalization.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Most viewed the risks to the outlook for economic activity and the labor market as nearly balanced." But "a number of participants noted that economic growth over the medium term might be slower than they currently expected if the foreign economic or financial situation deteriorated significantly." Discussion of market-based inflation measures declining with various explanations. Mid-October turbulence noted. "Inherent uncertainty of the economic and financial outlook." Somewhat elevated but not extreme. Score ~35.

### Cross-dimension coherence check:
- OUTPUT_GAP (42) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (38): Both below 50, consistent -- economy below potential with labor slack remaining. Labor slightly lower reflecting explicit underutilization language. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (48) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (35): Some divergence. Current action (ending QE) is a step toward normalization (~48), but forward signal still emphasizes accommodation and "considerable time" (~35). This divergence is explained by the fact that ending QE was a current action while forward guidance remained dovish. This is sensible.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 32 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Inflation was continuing to run below the Committee's longer-run objective." "Most participants anticipated that inflation was likely to edge lower in the near term, reflecting the decline in oil and other commodity prices and lower import prices." "A few expressed concern that inflation might persist below the Committee's objective for quite some time." Market-based inflation compensation declined.
- **Center-of-gravity**: Inflation clearly below the 2% target and expected to decline further near-term before returning to target over the medium term. Consistent with the 2013-19 "below target" anchor range.
- **Minority views**: "A few" concerned about persistent below-target inflation (more dovish). No participants raised upside inflation concerns. Band slightly wider on downside.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 42 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Economic activity continued to expand at a moderate pace." "Underutilization of labor resources was gradually diminishing." "A number of members judged that, while some underutilization in the labor market remained, it appeared to be gradually diminishing."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Economy operating below potential but gap is narrowing. Moderate growth pace consistent with gradual convergence toward potential. Bright-line rule satisfied: underutilization language = below 50.
- **Minority views**: Some participants emphasized more remaining slack than others, but broad agreement on the direction of improvement.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 38 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Labor market conditions improved somewhat further, with solid job gains and a lower unemployment rate." "Underutilization of labor had continued to diminish, although a number of participants noted that underutilization of labor market resources remained." "A couple of participants judged that the large number of individuals working part time for economic reasons and the continued drift down in the labor force participation rate suggested that the unemployment rate was understating the degree of labor market underutilization." "Relatively few pointing to emerging wage pressures."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Labor market improving but still below full employment with meaningful remaining slack. Wage pressures largely absent.
- **Minority views**: A couple saw more slack than headline unemployment suggested; one noted some wage gains for job-switchers. Band reflects this range.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 51 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Household spending advanced at a moderate pace" with "robust" auto/retail sales. "Many participants judged that the recent significant decline in energy prices would provide a boost to consumer spending." "Business fixed investment was advancing." But "the recovery in the housing sector remained slow." Business contacts reported improvement, credit availability, and loan growth.
- **Center-of-gravity**: Spending roughly at trend -- moderate consumer spending with positive momentum from energy prices, advancing business investment, offset by slow housing recovery.
- **Minority views**: One participant pointed to "restrained discretionary spending on the part of low- and middle-income households."

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 25 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Financial conditions remained highly accommodative." "Contacts in several regions reported ready availability of credit, strong loan growth." Mid-October market turbulence occurred but "despite the market volatility, financial conditions remained highly accommodative."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions clearly loose/accommodative despite temporary market turbulence.
- **Minority views**: Some noted mid-October turbulence and "further pockets of turbulence were likely," but this did not shift the overall assessment of accommodative conditions.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 48 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: QE concluded: "all members but one supported concluding the Committee's asset purchase program." Rates held at zero. "Considerable time" language retained. "Some participants preferred to eliminate language... indicating that the current target range... would likely be maintained for a 'considerable time'" (hawkish faction). "A couple of others thought that the current forward guidance might be read as suggesting an earlier date of liftoff than was likely to prove appropriate" (dovish faction).
- **Center-of-gravity**: Ending QE is a step toward normalization, but maintaining zero rates and "considerable time" language keeps the stance accommodative. Balanced to very slight dovish lean.
- **Minority views**: Hawks wanting to drop "considerable time" vs. doves worried about premature tightening signals create a range.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "All members but one supported concluding the Committee's asset purchase program" (one formal dissent). Debate on "considerable time" language: "Some participants preferred to eliminate" it vs. "other participants thought that the... phrase was useful." "A couple of members suggested including language... indicating that recent foreign economic developments had increased uncertainty" but "participants generally judged that such wording would suggest greater pessimism... than they thought appropriate."
- **Center-of-gravity**: One dissent on QE conclusion, moderate tactical disagreements on forward guidance language, but no fundamental division on economic assessment or policy direction.
- **Minority views**: The debates were about communication rather than fundamental policy disagreement.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 40 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: Dedicated discussion: "In their discussion of financial market developments and financial stability issues..." "Many participants commented on the turbulence in financial markets that occurred in mid-October." Discussion of whether to reference market developments in the statement. "A couple of participants noted the potential usefulness of collecting additional data on wholesale funding markets." Debate about including market references in statement.
- **Center-of-gravity**: Moderate attention to financial stability -- several participants engaged meaningfully, but it was not the dominant topic of the meeting.
- **Minority views**: Minimal disagreement on the importance of monitoring; differences were about whether to reference it in the statement.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 35 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Most participants supported retaining the language... indicating that the Committee anticipates that economic conditions may warrant keeping the target range for the federal funds rate below longer-run normal levels even after employment and inflation are near mandate-consistent levels." "Considerable time" retained. "A couple of others thought that the current forward guidance might be read as suggesting an earlier date of liftoff than was likely to prove appropriate."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Forward guidance retains accommodative lean ("considerable time," below-normal rates even after reaching mandate levels), while data-dependent language added. Pre-liftoff period with easing lean.
- **Minority views**: "Some participants preferred to eliminate" the "considerable time" language (more hawkish). "A couple" thought current guidance suggested too-early liftoff (more dovish).

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 35 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Most viewed the risks to the outlook for economic activity and the labor market as nearly balanced." But "a number of participants noted that economic growth over the medium term might be slower than they currently expected if the foreign economic or financial situation deteriorated significantly." "Participants expressed different views about how to interpret" declining market-based inflation measures. "Inherent uncertainty of the economic and financial outlook."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty, mostly around foreign risks and interpretation of declining inflation compensation, but risks viewed as "nearly balanced" by most.
- **Minority views**: Some focused on downside foreign risks; different interpretations of market-based inflation measures.
