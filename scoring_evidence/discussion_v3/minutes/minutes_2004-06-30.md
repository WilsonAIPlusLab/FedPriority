# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2004-06-30

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format -- staff and participant content are mixed. I will score only portions with participant language cues ("members commented," "members saw," "several noted," etc.) and ignore staff-attributable content.

**Era context**: June 2004 -- the economy is expanding solidly after the 2001 recession and the prolonged low-rate period. This is the meeting where the FOMC made its first rate hike of the cycle, raising the fed funds rate from 1% to 1.25%. Inflation had been picking up modestly but remained relatively contained. Unemployment was still above historical lows but improving.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all" (support 25bp hike), "many" (strength in capital spending; state/local spending), "several" (less confidence in inflation forecasts), "a number" (inflation risks tilted upside without tightening), "a few" (remove forward guidance characterization; signs of housing slowdown), "some" (limited inflationary impetus from dollar; continuation of robust consumer spending).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly above the neutral midpoint. Evidence: "somewhat increased price pressures," "some recent acceleration of consumer prices," "some increase in near-term inflation expectations." Energy and intermediate materials passing through to core prices. However, considerable debate about whether this is transitory. Core PCE forecasts of 1.5-2% for 2004 and 1.5-2.5% for 2005 -- roughly near target. The language is "somewhat elevated" with offsetting factors (transitory, productivity gains, remaining slack). Direction: modestly above midpoint. Score: ~57. Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says "somewhat increased" -- matches a score modestly above 50.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants describe the economy as "expanding briskly" with "solid gains in output and employment." However, language about "continuing to move toward full utilization of its resources" and "higher levels of resource utilization" indicates the economy is still BELOW potential but closing the gap. Unemployment forecasts of 5.25-5.5% for Q4 2004 are still above estimated NAIRU of that era. The phrase "persisting, albeit diminishing, margins of unemployed labor and other productive resources" is key -- still below potential. Direction: below potential but closing. Score: ~45. Bright-line rule: "margins of unemployed labor" = below potential = must be < 50. Score 45 checks out.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Notable improvement in labor market conditions," "improved conditions" referenced in the statement, "sizable further growth in employment." But still "persisting, albeit diminishing, margins of unemployed labor." Unemployment forecast 5.25-5.5%. The labor market is improving but still has slack. Direction: below full employment but tightening. Score: ~40.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Very strong business investment: "many of the members emphasized the strength in business capital spending." Consumer spending "rising more moderately" in Q2 but expected to renew strength. "Strong profit margins and cash flow, low capital costs." Housing at "generally high levels." Overall picture is above-trend spending led by business investment. Direction: above trend. Score: ~60.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Accommodative financial conditions" cited as supporting expansion. "Financial market conditions had tightened in recent months, but short-term interest rates were quite low, especially when judged against the recent level of inflation." Low capital costs. Direction: conditions are loose/accommodative despite some recent tightening. Score: ~28 (loose side). Higher = tighter. Evidence says accommodative/loose = lower score.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All of the members indicated that they could support an upward adjustment." This is the first rate hike. Language about "measured" pace. But also: "a more aggressive pace toward reaching a neutral policy stance might be called for." "A number of members emphasized that they would view the risks as tilted to the upside in the absence of further policy tightening." This is clearly hawkish -- unanimous support for hiking, forward lean toward continued tightening. Score: ~63. This is the beginning of a tightening cycle with unanimous support but "measured" language tempering it.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All of the members indicated that they could support" the 25bp hike. Very broad agreement. Only minor differences: "a few indicated...preference would be to remove any characterization of possible future policy actions." Some disagreement on inflation outlook (transitory vs. persistent camps). But no dissents, broad consensus on action. Score: ~22.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Brief mentions of financial conditions as context ("accommodative financial conditions," market conditions tightened). No substantive discussion of financial stability risks, asset bubbles, or systemic concerns. Housing mentioned but in economic activity context, not stability context. Score: ~18.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Members could envision a series of gradual or 'measured' policy moves." "A more aggressive pace toward reaching a neutral policy stance might be called for." They retained "roughly in balance" risk assessment but many emphasized willingness to respond. The forward signal is clearly toward further tightening, but at a "measured" pace. Score: ~65.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Considerable uncertainty still surrounded the overall extent to which competitive pressures would allow producers to pass through rising costs." "Several noted that they now had less confidence in those forecasts than earlier." "Just how much slowing of price increases was likely after some relatively elevated readings was difficult to forecast." "A marked degree of uncertainty surrounded this outlook" (fiscal). Somewhat elevated uncertainty, focused on inflation pass-through. Score: ~42.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (45) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (40): Both below 50, consistent with economy still below potential with remaining slack. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (63) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (65): Both in moderate hawkish territory, signaling continued tightening at measured pace. Coherent.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (57) with HAWKISH lean (63): Rising inflation motivating the start of tightening. Coherent.

### Polarity verification:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. "Somewhat increased" = above 50. Check.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. "Still below potential" = below 50. Check.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. "Accommodative" = well below 50. Check.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. "Measured" tightening ahead = above 50. Check.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 57 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "evidence accumulated over the intermeeting period...pointed to some recent acceleration of consumer prices and to some increase in near-term inflation expectations"
- "large increases in prices of energy and intermediate materials, both of which appeared to be passing through at least in part to core consumer prices"
- "some of the rise in core inflation in recent months appeared to have resulted from what might well prove to be transitory factors"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants see inflation as modestly elevated with recent acceleration, but debate whether it is transitory. Core PCE forecasts of 1.5-2% (2004) and 1.5-2.5% (2005) straddle the target.

**Minority views**: Some emphasized "considerable momentum" unlikely to be reversed quickly (more concerned), while others emphasized transitory factors and remaining slack (less concerned). This spread widens the uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 45 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "economy that was expanding briskly and was likely to continue to do so for some time"
- "persisting, albeit diminishing, margins of unemployed labor and other productive resources"
- "the economy continued to move toward full utilization of its resources"

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is expanding strongly but still below potential, with slack diminishing. Unemployment forecast of 5.25-5.5% confirms remaining gap.

**Minority views**: Little disagreement on this dimension; broad consensus.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 40 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "notable improvement in labor market conditions"
- "persisting, albeit diminishing, margins of unemployed labor"
- Unemployment forecast: 5.25-5.5% Q4 2004, 5-5.5% Q4 2005

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market improving notably but still slack, with unemployment above full employment. Conditions are tightening but haven't reached NAIRU.

**Minority views**: No significant disagreement noted.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 60 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "many of the members emphasized the strength in business capital spending"
- "Business and consumer expenditures were on a strong uptrend"
- "Consumer outlays were rising more moderately in the second quarter and somewhat below expectations"
- "Currently low inventory-to-sales ratios, indeed reports of emerging bottlenecks in some markets"

**Center-of-gravity**: Business investment very strong; consumer spending modestly softer in Q2 but expected to rebound. Net position is clearly above trend, led by robust capex.

**Minority views**: Consumer spending slightly disappointing relative to expectations provides some offset to very strong investment.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 28 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "continuing stimulus from fiscal and monetary policies, accommodative financial conditions"
- "financial market conditions had tightened in recent months, but short-term interest rates were quite low, especially when judged against the recent level of inflation"
- "low capital costs"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions remain accommodative/loose despite some recent tightening. Real rates are very low.

**Minority views**: Some tightening in market conditions acknowledged, preventing an even lower score.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 63 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all of the members indicated that they could support an upward adjustment in the target for the federal funds rate from a level of 1 percent to 1-1/4 percent"
- "members commented that they could envision a series of gradual or 'measured' policy moves"
- "a more aggressive pace toward reaching a neutral policy stance might be called for"
- "a number of members emphasized that they would view the risks as tilted to the upside in the absence of further policy tightening"

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous support for the first rate hike with clear expectation of further measured tightening. Some members lean toward potentially faster tightening if needed.

**Minority views**: "A few indicated...their preference would be to remove any characterization of possible future policy actions" -- a minor tactical disagreement, not directional.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 22 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all of the members indicated that they could support an upward adjustment"
- Minor differences on inflation outlook (transitory vs. persistent camps)
- "a few indicated...their preference would be to remove any characterization of possible future policy actions"

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad consensus on both the action and the general direction. Differences are about inflation interpretation and communication tactics, not policy direction.

**Minority views**: Inflation debate (transitory vs. persistent) represents meaningful intellectual disagreement but does not translate to policy disagreement at this meeting.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 18 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "accommodative financial conditions" mentioned as context for economic outlook
- Brief mention of housing: "only a few signs that rising mortgage rates were beginning to hold down sales"
- No discussion of asset valuations, systemic risk, or financial imbalances

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions mentioned only as backdrop to economic assessment. No substantive financial stability discussion.

**Minority views**: None noted.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 65 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "members commented that they could envision a series of gradual or 'measured' policy moves as likely to be consistent with the attainment of the Committee's objectives"
- "a more aggressive pace toward reaching a neutral policy stance might be called for"
- "many members also underscored their view that the statement should make clear that the Committee would be prepared to respond to significant changes in economic prospects"

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear forward signal of continued tightening at a measured pace, with the possibility of acceleration if inflation warrants. The "measured" language moderates the hawkishness somewhat.

**Minority views**: Some members lean toward potentially faster tightening, pushing the upper bound of the uncertainty range.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 42 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Considerable uncertainty still surrounded the overall extent to which competitive pressures would allow producers to pass through rising costs"
- "several noted that they now had less confidence in those forecasts than earlier"
- "Just how much slowing of price increases was likely after some relatively elevated readings was difficult to forecast"
- "a marked degree of uncertainty surrounded this outlook" (re fiscal policy)

**Center-of-gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty, primarily concentrated on inflation dynamics and pass-through. Members explicitly note reduced confidence in their forecasts. Not crisis-level uncertainty but above normal.

**Minority views**: The uncertainty is broadly shared rather than disputed.
