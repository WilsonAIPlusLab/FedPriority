# FOMC Discussion Scoring — 1995-11-15 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented (pre-2009), so staff and participant content are mixed. I will score only participant-attributed language ("members commented," "members observed," "members noted," etc.) and ignore staff review sections. The final paragraphs (starting "The information reviewed at this meeting suggests...") are staff/directive language and will be excluded from scoring.

**Era context**: November 1995 — mid-cycle in the 1990s expansion. The Fed had been tightening through 1994-early 1995 and was now on hold. The economy was slowing from a strong Q3. Budget negotiations (government shutdown) were ongoing. Inflation was subdued despite tight labor markets.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all but one" (hold policy), "a majority" (symmetric directive), "a number of members" (growth near potential; basis for optimism on inflation), "many" (subdued labor costs; little progress on inflation likely), "several" (current policy somewhat restrictive; questioned foreign growth), "some" (easing eventually needed; concern about benefit costs), "a few" (biased directive concern), "one member" (immediate easing — Lindsey dissent).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Members noted "generally high levels of resource use, including tight labor markets" yet inflation "had been more subdued than many had expected." A number saw basis for "mild optimism," but many viewed "little or no progress toward price stability" as more likely, with some concerned about "upward drift." Labor costs described as "subdued." This is a picture of inflation roughly contained but not declining — modestly below the midpoint concern level. Participants see inflation as slightly below worrisome levels but acknowledge risks are not fully to the downside. Score around 42-46: subdued current readings but persistent risk from tight resource use.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Appreciable slowing in the economic expansion" from unexpected Q3 strength. "A number of members believed that growth around potential was a probable outcome." Others worried about "further ebbing in the strength of final demands." Manufacturing stagnating. The center of gravity is near potential, perhaps very slightly below given the slowing. Score ~48-50.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Tight labor markets in many parts of the country," "relatively low unemployment," "high levels of resource use." Despite this tightness, wage pressures remained "subdued." The tightness is clearly acknowledged. Unemployment at 5.5% (from directive section, but members discuss tight markets). Score ~55-58 — tight but not severely so.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "on a firm growth trend" but with "cautionary note" from motor vehicle weakness. Business fixed investment expected to provide "sizable" but slowing growth. Consumer debt concerns. Overall: moderate, slightly positive. Score ~50-53.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Several commented that current monetary policy might be viewed as somewhat restrictive" but this "did not appear as yet to be inhibiting declines in intermediate- and long-term interest rates, increases in stock prices, or the availability of financing." Financing described as "relatively attractive terms," "ample availability of financing on relatively liberal terms." Financial conditions are actually loose despite somewhat restrictive policy. Score ~30-35 (somewhat loose).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All but one member favored or could accept an unchanged policy stance." "Some" members thought easing would eventually be needed. "Other members" thought unchanged was desirable and direction of next move was "open to question." One dissent for easing (Lindsey). The center is hold-with-mild-easing-bias from a subset. Score ~45-48: lean slightly dovish to balanced.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal dissent (Lindsey). "Some mix of views" on growth outlook. "Somewhat differing views regarding the stance of monetary policy." Some wanted easing eventually, others thought direction uncertain. Moderate but not deep disagreement. Score ~30-35: broad agreement with one dissent and some differences on outlook.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Minimal — brief mentions of stock market gains and bond market as supportive factors, mention of "potential unsettlement in financial markets" from government shutdown. No sustained financial stability discussion. Score ~10-15.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "A majority of the members expressed a preference for retaining a symmetric directive." Some preferred tilt toward easing. The forward signal is roughly neutral with a mild easing lean from a minority. Score ~43-47: slightly dovish-leaning but mostly neutral.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Some mix of views," "absence of any clear signs regarding the future strength of the expansion," "the current uncertainties surrounding the economic outlook," fiscal policy "could not be anticipated with any degree of precision," "an especially broad range of outcomes seemed possible." Moderately elevated uncertainty. Score ~40-45.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (~49) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (~56): Divergence is explained — labor markets are tight from the prior expansion, but growth is slowing toward/slightly below potential. This lag is consistent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (~46) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (~45): Aligned — both slightly dovish-leaning to balanced.

### Polarity verification:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says subdued but with risk. ~44 is slightly below midpoint. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence says conditions are loose/accommodative. ~32 is below midpoint. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 44 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "despite generally high levels of resource use, including tight labor markets in many parts of the country, inflation had been more subdued than many had expected over the past several months"
- "many viewed as more likely the prospect of little or no progress toward price stability over coming quarters and some expressed concern about the potential for an upward drift in the rate of inflation"
- "Many emphasized the persistence of subdued increases in labor costs"

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation is currently subdued and better than expected, but the majority sees no further improvement and some see upside risk. This places it slightly below the midpoint — contained but not comfortably low.

**Minority views**: A number saw "basis for mild optimism" (lower score); some worried about "upward drift" (higher score). The uncertainty band reflects this range.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 49 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "recent statistical and anecdotal information pointed on balance to an appreciable slowing in the economic expansion"
- "A number of members believed that growth around potential was a probable outcome"
- "Other members expressed concern about some signs of further ebbing in the strength of final demands"

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is approximately at potential, with the balance of risks tilting marginally below. The Q3 strength has faded and the outlook is for moderate growth near potential.

**Minority views**: Some see growth slipping below potential (Lindsey and others concerned about final demand weakness).

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 56 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "tight labor markets in many parts of the country"
- "relatively high levels of resource use"
- "relatively low unemployment" and "subdued increases in labor costs" despite tightness

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor markets are clearly tight — above the balanced midpoint — but not severely so, as wage pressures remain contained. This is a moderate tightness level.

**Minority views**: Broadly consistent across members; some note that worker willingness to accept limited compensation may begin to erode.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 51 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "consumer spending appeared to be on a firm growth trend, though weakness in overall sales of motor vehicles in recent months and a decline in total retail sales in October had introduced a cautionary note"
- "Further sizable growth in business fixed investment, but at a pace well below that experienced in recent years"
- "some members emphasized that the growth in consumer debt was likely to exert an increasingly inhibiting effect on consumer spending"

**Center-of-gravity**: Spending and investment are at a moderate pace — roughly at trend with some deceleration. Consumer side mixed (firm trend but cautionary signals); business investment positive but slowing.

**Minority views**: Some more pessimistic on consumer durables and debt constraints; others see holiday season as promising.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 32 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Several commented that current monetary policy might be viewed as somewhat restrictive, though the degree of restraint...did not appear as yet to be inhibiting declines in intermediate- and long-term interest rates, increases in stock prices, or the availability of financing from lending institutions"
- "ample availability of financing on relatively liberal terms" (business investment context)
- "favorable developments this year in the bond and stock markets"

**Center-of-gravity**: Despite somewhat restrictive policy, financial conditions are loose — long-term rates declining, stocks rising, credit freely available. Conditions are clearly on the accommodative side.

**Minority views**: Limited — broad agreement that financial conditions are supportive.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 46 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "all but one member favored or could accept an unchanged policy stance"
- "In the view of some, private spending was not likely to have sufficient momentum to overcome the effects of increased fiscal restraint if the current stance of monetary policy were maintained. In the circumstances, an easing at some point would be needed"
- "Other members believed that an unchanged policy was desirable under current conditions and that the direction and timing of the next policy move was more open to question"

**Center-of-gravity**: The committee is on hold with a mild dovish tilt. Some members lean toward eventual easing; others see the direction as uncertain. One dissent for immediate easing. The center is just slightly dovish of balanced.

**Minority views**: Lindsey wanted immediate easing (strong dovish). Some other members see next move as uncertain in direction (balanced to mildly hawkish).

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 33 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "all but one member" supported the action — one formal dissent (Lindsey)
- "Members expressed somewhat differing views regarding the stance of monetary policy that was likely to prove consistent with the Committee's objectives over time"
- "some mix of views among the members concerning how far the slowing might proceed"

**Center-of-gravity**: One formal dissent with moderate disagreement about the future direction of policy. Not a deep split — the committee broadly agrees on holding but differs on the next move.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 12 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "favorable developments this year in the bond and stock markets" (passing reference)
- "the potential unsettlement in financial markets that might develop at some point" (regarding government shutdown)
- Brief mentions of financing availability

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability is not a meaningful topic. References to financial markets are incidental to the economic outlook discussion, not focused on stability concerns.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 45 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "a majority of the members expressed a preference for retaining a symmetric directive"
- "The remaining members said that they preferred a directive that was tilted toward an easing policy action. Such an instruction...would be consistent with what they viewed as the most likely policy course over coming months"
- "the potential need to adjust policy during the relatively short intermeeting period was remote"

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is roughly neutral (symmetric directive) with a minority leaning toward easing. No tightening signal. The net forward tone is slightly dovish of neutral.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 42 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the absence of any clear signs regarding the future strength of the expansion in relation to the economy's potential or the future course of inflation"
- "an especially broad range of outcomes seemed possible" (regarding fiscal policy)
- "the current uncertainties surrounding the economic outlook were not likely to be resolved during the weeks immediately ahead"

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is somewhat elevated — the fiscal situation is highly uncertain, the economic outlook is mixed, and the relationship between resource use and inflation is being questioned. Not extreme, but above normal.
