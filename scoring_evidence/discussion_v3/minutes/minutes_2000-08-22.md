# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2000-08-22

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is **minutes** (synthesized summary of participant views). It is an unsegmented pre-2009 format, so I must focus on participant-attributable language ("members agreed," "members noted," "many members emphasized") and ignore staff-reported content.

### Era Context
August 2000 -- late in the tech/productivity boom. The Fed had been tightening through 1999 and into 2000 (fed funds at 6.5%). The economy was running hot with very tight labor markets, strong investment spending, and rising productivity. This was the pause point after a series of rate hikes.

### Quantifier Distribution
- "all the members" -- agreed on hold, agreed on risks weighted toward inflation (x2)
- "members agreed" -- demand/supply coming into balance; risks weighted toward inflation
- "most" -- pickup in prices reflected energy effects
- "many members" -- need to be prepared to act promptly
- "a number of members" -- concerned about taut labor markets showing through to costs
- "members noted" -- various observations (multiple uses)
- "members observed" -- consumer spending moderated

No "several," "some," or "a few" used prominently for disagreement -- this suggests broad consensus.

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as modestly above neutral. Overall inflation "picked up to fairly high levels by the standards of recent years" due to energy. But core PCE "remained relatively damped and had risen only a little." Members see "a mild upward trend in key measures of inflation as a distinct possibility." Labor market tightness is a concern for future cost pressure. This is elevated concern but core still contained -- above 50 but not strongly so. Direction: modestly above midpoint. Score ~58.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Economy described as having "unusually tight" labor markets, demand growth slowing but still above potential. "Growth of aggregate demand and that of aggregate supply were coming into closer balance" -- implying they had been above. "Exceptionally tight" labor markets. Business investment at "exceptional rates." This is above potential but closing. Direction: above midpoint. Score ~58.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Unusually taut labor markets," "exceptionally tight," "unusually low unemployment rates." This is very clear -- highly tight labor market. The anchor for 1999-2000 is 65-75. Score ~70.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending "moderated substantially," housing "softened." But business investment "continued to rise at exceptional rates." Net: consumer weakening from very high levels, investment still booming. Overall still above trend but moderating. Score ~57.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Real interest rates for private borrowers were still at relatively elevated levels," "banking institutions were continuing to report further tightening of their standards and terms for business loans." But "recent rally in domestic financial markets could be viewed as having partially eroded the degree of monetary restraint." Dollar had firmed. Overall somewhat tight conditions. Score ~58.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All members voted to hold at 6.5%. All agreed risks weighted toward inflation. "Many members emphasized that the Committee needed to be prepared to act promptly should inflationary pressures appear to be intensifying." This is a hold with clear hawkish bias -- lean hawkish. Score ~58.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All the members endorsed a proposal to retain the current stance." All agreed on the risk assessment. No dissent mentioned, no minority camps described. Near-consensus. Score ~12.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Some discussion of equity prices leveling out, banking tightening standards, current account deficit concerns, dollar risks. Moderate but not dominant. Score ~30.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Risks remained weighted toward higher inflation." "Committee needed to be prepared to act promptly." But also "incurred little risk in leaving the stance of policy unchanged... waiting to see." This signals tightening bias but data-dependent. Score ~62.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Considerable caution needed to be exercised in assessing the outlook for productivity." "Historical episodes involving major changes in productivity trends had been rare." "Judgments about labor market pressures, productivity, and inflation had to be viewed with care." "Subject to considerable uncertainty." Multiple references to uncertainty about productivity trends and their implications. Somewhat elevated. Score ~45.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (58) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70): Labor is tighter than the output gap score suggests, but this is because demand growth is moderating (closing the gap) while labor markets remain exceptionally tight as a lagging indicator. This divergence is explained in the text.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (58) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (62): Aligned -- hold with hawkish bias and forward tightening lean. Consistent.

### Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says pickup in overall, core contained but risks to upside. Score 58 = modestly above. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says coming into balance from above. Score 58 = modestly above. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says elevated real rates, bank tightening, but some easing from market rally. Score 58 = somewhat tight. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 58 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Overall measures of price inflation had picked up to fairly high levels by the standards of recent years, largely as a result of higher energy costs."
- "Core consumer price indices remained relatively damped and had risen only a little over the last year, especially when measured by the PCE chain-price index."
- "The members saw a mild upward trend in key measures of inflation as a distinct possibility, albeit one that was subject to considerable uncertainty."

**Center-of-gravity**: Members see headline inflation elevated due to energy, core still contained but with upside risks from tight labor markets and potential productivity leveling. Modestly above the neutral midpoint.

**Minority views**: No significant minority -- broad agreement on this assessment.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 58 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Growth of aggregate demand and that of aggregate supply were coming into closer balance."
- "Labor markets would remain exceptionally tight."
- "Business outlays for capital equipment and software had continued to rise at exceptional rates."

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy still operating above potential but the gap is narrowing as demand moderates and productivity-driven supply accelerates. The "coming into closer balance" language implies still above but converging.

**Minority views**: None expressed -- consensus view.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 70 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Members anticipated that labor markets would remain exceptionally tight."
- "Unusually taut labor markets could begin at some point to show through to increases in labor compensation in excess of productivity gains."
- "A period of unusually low unemployment rates."

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous view that labor markets are exceptionally tight. "Exceptionally" and "unusually taut" are strong descriptors placing this firmly in the 66-80 range. Consistent with the 1999-2000 anchor of 65-75.

**Minority views**: None -- universal agreement on tightness.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 57 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Growth in consumer spending had moderated substantially after a period of exceptional gains."
- "Business outlays for capital equipment and software had continued to rise at exceptional rates, even after several years of rapid growth."
- "The housing sector provided the clearest indication of a response of aggregate demand to firming interest rates."

**Center-of-gravity**: Net position is above trend but moderating. Consumer spending slowing, housing declining, but business investment still booming at "exceptional rates." The mix is still above trend on balance given the extraordinary investment spending.

**Minority views**: None significant.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 58 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Real interest rates for private borrowers were still at relatively elevated levels."
- "Banking institutions were continuing to report further tightening of their standards and terms for business loans."
- "The recent rally in domestic financial markets could be viewed as having partially eroded the degree of monetary restraint."

**Center-of-gravity**: Conditions are somewhat tight -- elevated real rates and bank lending tightening, though partially offset by a market rally. Net assessment is moderately restrictive.

**Minority views**: Some tension between those emphasizing the rally's easing effect and those noting bank tightening.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 58 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All the members endorsed a proposal to retain the current stance of policy."
- "Many members emphasized that the Committee needed to be prepared to act promptly should inflationary pressures appear to be intensifying."
- "All the members agreed that the sentence should continue to indicate that the risks to the economy remained weighted toward higher inflation."

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous hold with unanimous hawkish risk assessment. Not actively tightening but maintaining clear readiness to tighten. This is a lean-hawkish hold, above neutral but not at the "conditions warrant increase" level.

**Minority views**: None -- remarkable consensus.

**Minority views**: None identified.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 62 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All the members agreed that the sentence should continue to indicate that the risks to the economy remained weighted toward higher inflation in the foreseeable future."
- "Many members emphasized that the Committee needed to be prepared to act promptly should inflationary pressures appear to be intensifying."
- "The Committee incurred little risk in leaving the stance of policy unchanged at this meeting and waiting to see how the various factors... unfolded."

**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal is mildly hawkish -- risks weighted to inflation, prepared to act, but waiting for data. This is a tightening bias tempered by data dependence.

**Minority views**: None on the forward lean.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 45 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:

- "Considerable caution needed to be exercised in assessing the outlook for productivity."
- "The members saw a mild upward trend in key measures of inflation as a distinct possibility, albeit one that was subject to considerable uncertainty."

**Center-of-gravity**: Members devoted considerable attention to uncertainty around productivity trends and their implications for inflation and growth. This goes beyond standard caveats but is not crisis-level uncertainty. "Considerable" uncertainty language places this in the somewhat elevated range.

**Minority views**: N/A.
