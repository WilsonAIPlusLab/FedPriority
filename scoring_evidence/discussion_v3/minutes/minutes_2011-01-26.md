## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 32 +-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "measures of underlying inflation had trended downward" (members' assessment)
- "Many participants expected that, with significant slack in resource markets and longer-term inflation expectations stable, measures of core inflation would remain close to current levels in coming quarters"
- "a number of participants cited concerns that inflation was below its mandate-consistent level and was expected to remain so for some time"
**Center of gravity**: Core inflation subdued and below mandate-consistent levels; commodity prices pose upside risks but have not passed through to core.
**Minority views**: "Some participants expressed concern that...firms might increase them substantially once they found themselves with sufficient pricing power." Some also worried that large Fed balance sheet could push up inflation expectations. These inform the +8 uncertainty band.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 35 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the pace of the recovery was insufficient to bring about a significant improvement in labor market conditions"
- "Most participants saw the large degree of resource slack in the economy as likely to remain a force restraining inflation"
- "unemployment was expected to remain above...levels consistent with the Committee's objectives for some time"
**Center of gravity**: Economy firming but substantial slack remains; recovery gradual with output well below potential.
**Minority views**: Upside scenario noted — "a considerably stronger recovery could take hold" — but this is presented as a risk, not the baseline. Narrow band reflects broad agreement.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 30 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Conditions in labor markets continued to improve gradually" with unemployment at 9.4%
- "workers at all skill levels were readily obtainable" (some contacts)
- "participants continued to express disappointment in both the pace and the unevenness of the improvements in labor markets"
**Center of gravity**: Labor market far below full employment; gradual improvement from deep slack but still very loose.
**Minority views**: Some contacts reported upgraded skill requirements creating mismatches, but this is a structural nuance, not tightness. Narrow band.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 42 +-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Spending by households picked up noticeably in the fourth quarter"; "households stepped up sharply their purchases of durable goods"
- "business outlays continued to grow at a moderate pace"
- "some participants noted that it was not clear whether the recent pace of consumer spending would be sustained"; "recovery in economic activity was likely to be restrained by a variety of economic factors"
**Center of gravity**: Consumer spending showed notable pickup but sustainability questioned; business investment moderate; structures weak. Net: approaching but still below trend.
**Minority views**: Optimists saw pent-up demand potentially sustaining spending; pessimists noted volatile categories drove the pickup. Moderate uncertainty band.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 47 +-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Conditions in financial markets improved somewhat further"; "Credit spreads on the debt of nonfinancial corporations continued to narrow"
- "credit conditions remained tight for smaller, bank-dependent firms"
- "tight credit conditions in a number of sectors" including housing and commercial real estate
**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions roughly neutral with improving corporate market access but persistent tightness for small firms, housing, and CRE.
**Minority views**: Some saw financial developments as consistent with more accommodative monetary policy; others highlighted remaining tight pockets. Moderate band.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 22 +-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "members agreed that no changes to the Committee's asset purchase program or to its target range for the federal funds rate were warranted"
- Committee continuing $600 billion QE2 purchases; "reaffirmed its intention to purchase $600 billion of longer-term Treasury securities"
- "A few members remained unsure of the likely effects of the asset purchase program on the economy, but felt that making changes to the program at this time was not appropriate"
**Center of gravity**: Firmly dovish — maintaining large-scale asset purchases and zero rates. No discussion of early tapering or rate increases.
**Minority views**: A few skeptical of QE effectiveness but not pushing to change it. Some concern about eventual exit, but no hawkish push. Moderate band.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 +-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Unanimous vote on ratifying transactions; no formal dissents described
- "the importance of resource slack as a factor influencing inflation was debated"
- "A few members remained unsure of the likely effects of the asset purchase program"
**Center of gravity**: Broad agreement on policy action with minor differences about the economic outlook and inflation dynamics. No formal dissents but some reservations.
**Minority views**: Debate about slack's role in inflation and QE effectiveness shows some intellectual disagreement, but it did not translate to policy dissent.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 35 +-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Dedicated paragraph on financial market conditions including equity prices, credit spreads, and bank lending
- "participants remained worried about the possible effects of spillovers from the banking and fiscal strains in peripheral Europe"
- Municipal bond market stress and CRE credit conditions discussed
**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions discussed as part of the economic landscape but not a central preoccupation. Euro-area strains mentioned as risk factor. Limited but meaningful discussion.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 12 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Continuing QE2 through end of Q2 2011; "reaffirmed its intention to purchase $600 billion"
- "the Committee should continue its planning for the eventual exit from the current exceptionally accommodative stance of policy" — but no timeline
- Federal funds rate maintained at 0 to 0.25%
**Center of gravity**: Strong forward easing bias. Ongoing large-scale asset purchases signal extended accommodation. Exit planning mentioned abstractly but no tightening signal.
**Minority views**: Exit planning language is the only hint toward normalization, but it is framed as risk mitigation, not a policy signal.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 38 +-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "participants generally expressed greater confidence that the economic recovery would be sustained"
- "downside risks to their forecasts...had diminished"
- "they continued to see significant risks to both sides of the outlook" including Europe, housing, state/local fiscal
- "incoming data...would need to be solid for a while longer to justify a significant upward revision"
**Center of gravity**: Uncertainty somewhat elevated but diminishing. Participants more confident than recent meetings but still see significant two-sided risks.
**Minority views**: Range from those seeing pent-up demand upside to those worried about European spillovers and housing. Moderate band.
