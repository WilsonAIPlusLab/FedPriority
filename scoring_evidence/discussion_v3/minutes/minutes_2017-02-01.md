# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2017-02-01 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented -- participant discussion section is clearly separated from staff review.

**Era context**: Early 2017. The Fed raised rates in December 2016 (second hike of the cycle). Economy expanding at a moderate pace, unemployment near 4.7%, inflation still below 2% but rising. Significant policy uncertainty surrounding the new administration's potential fiscal, tax, and regulatory changes.

**Quantifier distribution**: "participants generally" / "participants agreed" (broad consensus) used frequently. "Most" appears several times (inflation expectations, rate path, unemployment estimates). "Many" (raise rates fairly soon). "Several" (various risk views). "Some" (upside/downside risks). "A few" (capital spending, timing concerns). "A couple" (specific observations). Mix indicates moderate agreement with notable minority currents.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target but rising toward it. PCE at 1.6%, core at 1.7%. "Inflation had increased in recent quarters but was still below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective." "Most participants continued to expect that inflation would rise to 2 percent over the medium term." Some see upside risks from tighter resource utilization, several others see downside from dollar appreciation. Direction: below target but closing in. Score should be in the 41-49 range -- slightly below target but approaching, not deeply concerning. Score: 44.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: The staff projects the economy "operating above its longer-run potential" (this is staff language, but participants echo it). Participants say "economic activity had continued to expand at a moderate pace." The unemployment rate at 4.7% is "near levels that most participants judged to be consistent with the Committee's maximum-employment objective." Several projected "a more substantial undershooting." The economy is near or modestly above potential. Bright-line rule: "at potential" or slightly above given the language about unemployment near the longer-run normal level and some expecting undershooting. Score: 51.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 4.7%, "close to levels that most participants judged to be consistent with the Committee's maximum-employment objective." "Several participants' business contacts reported shortages of workers in some occupations." But "several other participants thought that some margins of labor underutilization remained" (prime-age LFPR, part-time for economic reasons). Center of gravity: near full employment with mixed signals on remaining slack. Score: 50.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "posted a moderate increase" and expected to continue. Business investment "increased at a moderate rate in the fourth quarter" after earlier contraction. Business sentiment very positive, but "business fixed investment had remained soft." Housing generally positive. Mixed picture: moderate consumer spending, improving but still soft business investment, positive sentiment. At or slightly below trend. Score: 50.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Financial conditions appeared to have changed little, on net" -- equity prices rose and spreads narrowed (loose) but rates increased and dollar appreciated (tight). Offsetting forces producing roughly neutral conditions. Score: 47.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Committee held rates at this meeting. "Many participants expressed the view that it might be appropriate to raise the federal funds rate again fairly soon." "Most participants continued to judge that...a gradual pace of rate increases over time was likely to be appropriate." "A few participants noted that continuing to remove policy accommodation in a timely manner, potentially at an upcoming meeting, would allow greater flexibility." One member wanted to move "relatively soon." Leaning hawkish -- hold now but clear bias toward hiking soon. Score: 63.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No dissents mentioned. Broad agreement on holding but some variation on timing of next hike. "Many" want to hike soon, "a few" see downside risks to inflation and want caution. Variation is about pace/timing, not fundamental direction. Some tension between those wanting to move soon and those wanting to wait for clarity on fiscal policy. Score: 28.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Dedicated paragraph on financial stability: valuation pressures, equity prices, low implied volatility, leverage incentives from low rates, safety and soundness reforms, macroprudential tools. "A few participants commented" on equity overvaluation risk. "Several participants emphasized" resilience from reforms. Moderate discussion by several participants. Score: 40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Many participants expressed the view that it might be appropriate to raise the federal funds rate again fairly soon." "Most continued to judge that a gradual pace of rate increases over time was likely to be appropriate." Discussion of balance sheet normalization beginning at upcoming meetings. Clear tightening lean in forward guidance but tempered by "gradual." Score: 67.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants again emphasized their considerable uncertainty about the prospects for changes in fiscal and other government policies." "Most participants continued to see heightened uncertainty regarding the size, composition, and timing of possible changes to fiscal and other government policies." "Considerable uncertainty" is prominent and repeated. Score: 52.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (51) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (50): Coherent -- both near potential/full employment.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (63) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (67): Coherent -- both lean hawkish, forward guidance slightly more so because of explicit "fairly soon" language.

### Polarity verification:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says below target but rising. 44 < 50. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says near/slightly above potential. 51 > 50. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says roughly neutral with slight looseness from equities/spreads offset by rates/dollar. 47 slightly below neutral. Matches.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Many want to hike soon. 63. Matches.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. "Fairly soon" + "gradual pace." 67. Matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 44 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Inflation had increased in recent quarters but was still below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective."
- "The 12-month change in headline PCE prices increased further, to 1.6 percent in December... The 12-month change in core PCE prices stayed near 1.7 percent."
- "Most participants continued to expect that inflation would rise to the Committee's 2 percent objective over the medium term."

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation is below the 2% target but clearly moving closer; participants see it approaching target gradually without alarm on either side.

**Minority views**: "Some saw a risk that inflationary pressures might develop more rapidly than currently anticipated" (upside). "Several others thought that progress...might lag" due to dollar appreciation or slow Phillips curve response (downside). Balanced minority views widen the band modestly.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 51 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity had continued to expand at a moderate pace."
- "The unemployment rate remained close to levels that most participants judged to be consistent with the Committee's maximum-employment objective."
- "Several participants projected a more substantial undershooting" of the longer-run normal unemployment rate.

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is essentially at potential, with moderate growth expected to push it slightly above. The staff projection references "operating above its longer-run potential," and participants broadly concur.

**Minority views**: Several see risk of significant undershooting (above potential), while others note remaining slack in labor force participation.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 50 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "At 4.7 percent in December, the unemployment rate remained close to levels that most participants judged to be consistent with the Committee's maximum-employment objective."
- "Several participants' business contacts reported shortages of workers in some occupations."
- "Several other participants thought that some margins of labor underutilization remained, citing the still-high rate of prime-age workers outside the labor force."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is approximately at full employment -- the unemployment rate is near NAIRU, with anecdotal tightness in some sectors balanced by remaining slack measures.

**Minority views**: Worker shortage reports push up; remaining underutilization (prime-age LFPR, involuntary part-time) pushes down. Relatively wide band.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 50 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending posted a moderate increase in the fourth quarter."
- "Business investment in E&I, which had been contracting earlier in 2016, increased at a moderate rate in the fourth quarter... business fixed investment had remained soft."
- "Available reports from District surveys of activity and revenues in the manufacturing and services industries were very positive."

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending at a moderate, sustainable pace; business investment improving from weakness but still soft; sentiment strongly positive but not yet translating broadly into spending. Net: at trend.

**Minority views**: Positive sentiment and fiscal optimism could boost spending; some contacts waiting for policy clarity before acting.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 47 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Financial conditions appeared to have changed little, on net, in recent months: Equity prices had risen and credit spreads had narrowed, but longer-term interest rates had increased and the dollar had appreciated further."
- No participant language suggesting stress or unusual tightness/looseness.

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are roughly neutral -- loosening forces (equities, spreads) offset by tightening forces (rates, dollar). Slightly on the accommodative side given equity gains and spread narrowing.

**Minority views**: Limited variation expressed on financial conditions per se.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 63 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Many participants expressed the view that it might be appropriate to raise the federal funds rate again fairly soon if incoming information on the labor market and inflation was in line with or stronger than their current expectations."
- "Most participants continued to judge that...a gradual pace of rate increases over time was likely to be appropriate."
- "A few participants noted that continuing to remove policy accommodation in a timely manner, potentially at an upcoming meeting, would allow the Committee greater flexibility."

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear consensus for continued gradual tightening; many leaning toward hiking fairly soon. Hold at this meeting but strong bias toward near-term action.

**Minority views**: "A few others continued to see downside risks to inflation or anticipated only a gradual return of inflation to the 2 percent objective" -- a modest dovish minority. A couple wanted faster action ("misunderstood as only one or two rate hikes per year").

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 28 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Unanimous decision to hold rates at this meeting (no dissents mentioned).
- "Participants generally characterized their economic forecasts and their judgments about monetary policy as little changed since the December meeting."
- Some variation on pace: "many" want to hike soon, "a few" see downside inflation risks, "a couple" want faster action.

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on holding and on gradual tightening path, with differences about timing of next move and risk balance. Low dissent.

**Minority views**: Differences are primarily about pace/timing rather than direction.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 40 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants considered how recent developments had affected their assessment of the stability of the U.S. financial system."
- "A few participants commented that the recent increase in equity prices might in part reflect investors' anticipation of a boost to earnings... which might not materialize."
- "Several participants emphasized that the increased resilience of the financial system since the financial crisis had importantly been the result of the key safety and soundness reforms."
- Discussion of macroprudential tools, leverage incentives, low implied volatility.

**Center-of-gravity**: A meaningful dedicated discussion of financial stability involving several participants, covering valuations, leverage incentives, and regulatory resilience. Moderate emphasis.

**Minority views**: Limited divergence -- mainly about whether current conditions warrant concern.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 67 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Many participants expressed the view that it might be appropriate to raise the federal funds rate again fairly soon."
- "A few participants noted that continuing to remove policy accommodation in a timely manner, potentially at an upcoming meeting, would allow the Committee greater flexibility."
- "Participants also generally agreed that the Committee should begin discussions at upcoming meetings about the economic conditions that could warrant changes in the existing policy of reinvesting proceeds."

**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal is clearly tilted toward further tightening -- both rate hikes and balance sheet normalization on the horizon. "Fairly soon" is a strong forward signal, modulated by "gradual."

**Minority views**: "A few others continued to see downside risks to inflation" -- a cautionary voice on the pace.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants again emphasized their considerable uncertainty about the prospects for changes in fiscal and other government policies as well as about the timing and magnitude of the net effects of such changes on economic activity."
- "Most participants continued to see heightened uncertainty regarding the size, composition, and timing of possible changes to fiscal and other government policies."
- "Near-term risks to the economic outlook appeared roughly balanced."

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is elevated, specifically around fiscal policy changes, but not paralyzing. Participants repeatedly use "considerable uncertainty" and "heightened uncertainty" while simultaneously saying near-term risks are "roughly balanced." Genuine uncertainty but focused on a specific source (policy changes).

**Minority views**: "A couple of participants argued that such uncertainty should not deter the Committee from taking further steps."
