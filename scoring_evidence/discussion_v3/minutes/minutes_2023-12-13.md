# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2023-12-13 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented — participant discussion section is clearly delineated from staff review.

**Era context**: December 2023. The Fed had held rates at 5.25-5.50% since July 2023. Inflation had been declining meaningfully from 2022 peaks but remained above 2%. The economy had been surprisingly resilient, with strong Q3 GDP growth now showing signs of slowing. The labor market was rebalancing gradually. This was the meeting where the SEP "dot plot" pivoted toward rate cuts in 2024.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all" (2x — inflation progress, hold decision), "most" (1x — restrictive stance softening spending), "many" (3x — credit usage, wage slowdown, easing financial conditions concern), "several" (8x — services inflation, housing inflation, supply healing, labor weakening risk, past policy effects, CRE, balance sheet, longer hold), "some" (2x — contacts, labor), "a few" (5x — holiday sales, supply improvement, payroll slowing, dual-mandate tradeoff, GDI weakness), "a number of" (1x — restrictive stance duration uncertainty).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants say "clear progress had been made" and "inflation had eased over the past year but remained elevated." "All participants observed clear progress." They revised down inflation projections. But inflation "remained above the Committee's objective" and they "would need to see more evidence." Core services still "increasing at an elevated pace." Energy and core goods "falling or changing little." Direction: above target but clearly improving. The language is "remained elevated" but with "clear progress" — this is moderately above target but trending down. Score around 56-60. The "elevated" language pushes above 50, but "clear progress" and "eased" pull it down from the 65-75 range. I'll score 57.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says elevated but easing. 57 = modestly above target. Matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Growth of economic activity had slowed from its strong pace in the third quarter." GDP growth expected to "cool" in 2024. Economy was strong in Q3 but "recent indicators suggested that growth... had slowed." The labor market "remained tight" but "coming into better balance." No language of "above potential" or "below potential" explicitly, but the economy was still strong — just decelerating. Consumer spending "had been strong." Business reports "mixed." The picture is of an economy near potential, perhaps slightly above, but slowing toward it. Score around 51-53.

Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Economy was strong but slowing. 52 fits.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor market remained tight" but "continued to come into better balance." Vacancy-to-unemployed ratio "declined to a value only modestly above its level just before the pandemic." Wage growth "continued to slow broadly." "Job gains had moderated since earlier this year." Several noted risk of "abrupt downshift." The market is still tight but meaningfully less so than peak. Score around 58-60. Still tight but rebalancing.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. "Remained tight" but rebalancing. 59 fits — still above neutral but not at peak tightness.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "had been strong" supported by balance sheets, labor market, income growth. But retail sales "stepped down noticeably in October." Several factors may contribute to "softer consumer spending" — slower income, diminishing excess savings, increased credit usage/delinquencies. Business contacts "mixed." Higher interest rates leading to "softer business investment." Small businesses facing tighter credit. Manufacturing "slowing growth." Net: spending was solid but showing signs of moderation. Score around 53-55. Above trend recently but softening.

Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending. "Strong" but moderating. 54 fits.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Financial conditions had eased over the intermeeting period" after "a sharp tightening since the summer." Many worried "easing in financial conditions beyond what is appropriate could make it more difficult" for inflation goal. "Tighter financial and credit conditions facing households and businesses would likely weigh on economic activity." Small businesses "experiencing tighter credit conditions." CRE refinancing against "higher interest rates." The picture: conditions had been tight, eased recently, but remained restrictive overall. Credit conditions still tight for many borrowers. Score around 60-63. Conditions are restrictive but easing at the margin.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter conditions. Conditions described as having eased but still restrictive. 61 fits.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All participants held rates. "Participants viewed the policy rate as likely at or near its peak." "Almost all participants indicated that... a lower target range for the federal funds rate would be appropriate by the end of 2024." But "remained highly attentive to inflation risks" and "policy to remain at a restrictive stance for some time." Several noted rates might need to stay high "longer than they currently anticipated." A few possible "further increases." This is a hold with a clear easing bias for 2024, but no urgency to cut soon. The center of gravity is lean dovish — holding now but signaling cuts ahead. Score around 42-45.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. The meeting signals peak rates and future cuts. 43 = lean dovish. Matches.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All participants judged it appropriate to maintain the target range." No formal dissents. However, there is some spread: "several" noted circumstances might warrant keeping rates higher for longer, while "almost all" saw lower rates in 2024. "A few" suggested dual-mandate tradeoff concerns. Broad agreement on the hold with some differences about the path forward. Score around 22-25. Broad agreement with minor variations.

Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Broad agreement, minor emphasis differences. 23 fits.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Discussion of CRE risks ("several participants noted significant share of properties would need to be refinanced"), banking system described as "sound and resilient," ON RRP facility decline discussed, easing financial conditions discussed by "many." Balance sheet runoff discussed by "several." Financial conditions are discussed moderately but not as a dominant theme. Score around 35-40.

Polarity check: Higher = more attention. Moderate discussion across several financial topics but not dominant. 37 fits.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Policy rate as likely at or near its peak." "Almost all participants indicated... a lower target range... would be appropriate by the end of 2024." But also "data-dependent approach," "restrictive stance for some time," and possible further increases still on the table. The forward signal is a mild easing bias — rates have peaked and cuts are coming, but timing is uncertain and data-dependent. Statement language: "extent of any additional policy firming that may be appropriate." Score around 35-40. Easing lean with data dependence.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. The signal is toward easing. 37 = easing lean with data dependence. Matches.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants generally perceived a high degree of uncertainty surrounding the economic outlook." "Unusually elevated degree of uncertainty." Multiple upside and downside risks enumerated. "Extent of these effects remained uncertain." This is explicit elevated uncertainty language. Score around 60-65.

Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. "High degree" and "unusually elevated" = 62. Matches.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (52) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (59): Both above 50, labor somewhat higher. This makes sense — the labor market was still tight while output growth was slowing toward potential. The lag between output slowing and labor adjusting is plausible.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (43) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (37): Both lean dovish, with forward guidance slightly more dovish. This makes sense — current action is hold (near neutral) but the forward signal points toward cuts. The forward guidance is more dovish than the current stance, which is coherent with a pivoting meeting.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 57 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All participants observed that clear progress had been made in 2023 toward the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective."
- "Inflation remained above the Committee's objective and that they would need to see more evidence that inflation pressures were abating."
- "Core services prices still increasing at an elevated pace."

**Center of gravity**: Inflation moderately above target with clear progress toward 2%, but participants not yet confident in sustained return — modestly elevated.
**Minority views**: None significantly diverge; all agree on progress + remaining elevation.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 52 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Recent indicators suggested that growth in economic activity had slowed from its strong pace in the third quarter."
- "Real GDP growth would cool" in 2024 with "rebalancing of the labor market."
- Consumer spending "had been strong" but showing signs of moderation.

**Center of gravity**: Economy near potential after strong Q3, now slowing — slightly above potential but converging.
**Minority views**: A few noted GDI weakness suggesting momentum "not as strong as indicated by GDP readings."

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 59 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "While the labor market remained tight, it continued to come into better balance."
- "Ratio of vacancies to unemployed workers had declined to a value only modestly above its level just before the pandemic."
- "Nominal wage growth had continued to slow broadly" (many).

**Center of gravity**: Labor market still tight but actively rebalancing — above full employment but much less so than earlier in 2023.
**Minority views**: Several noted risk of "abrupt downshift" if demand weakens further.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 54 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending had been strong, supported by the healthy balance sheets of many households, a strong labor market, and robust income growth."
- "Retail sales growth had stepped down noticeably in October."
- "Higher interest rates were leading firms to reassess future projects and were contributing to softer business investment" (several).

**Center of gravity**: Consumer spending solid but moderating; business investment softening under rate pressure. Net: slightly above trend but fading.
**Minority views**: Some contacts "remaining relatively optimistic" while others expect "slower growth for 2024."

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 61 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "After a sharp tightening since the summer, financial conditions had eased over the intermeeting period."
- "Tighter financial and credit conditions facing households and businesses would likely weigh on economic activity."
- Small businesses "experiencing tighter credit conditions and increasing delinquencies" (couple).

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions still restrictive overall despite recent easing; credit tight for many borrowers, especially smaller firms and CRE.
**Minority views**: Many worried easing could go too far; but conditions still described as restrictive.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 43 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants viewed the policy rate as likely at or near its peak for this tightening cycle."
- "Almost all participants indicated that... a lower target range for the federal funds rate would be appropriate by the end of 2024."
- "Participants generally stressed the importance of maintaining a careful and data-dependent approach" and "policy to remain at a restrictive stance for some time."

**Center of gravity**: Hold at peak with clear lean toward easing in 2024, but no urgency — data-dependent dovish tilt.
**Minority views**: Several noted rates might stay high "longer than they currently anticipated"; possible further increases still on table for a few.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 23 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All participants judged it appropriate to maintain the target range" — unanimous hold.
- "Almost all participants indicated... a lower target range... would be appropriate by the end of 2024" — near-consensus on rate path direction.
- Minor spread: "several" noted longer hold possible, "a few" noted dual-mandate tradeoff.

**Center of gravity**: Broad agreement on both the hold decision and the directional outlook, with minor variations in timing preferences.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 37 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Several participants noted that a significant share of [CRE] properties would need to be refinanced in 2024 against a backdrop of higher interest rates."
- "Many participants remarked that an easing in financial conditions beyond what is appropriate could make it more difficult for the Committee to reach its inflation goal."
- Balance sheet runoff and ON RRP facility discussed by several participants.

**Center of gravity**: Moderate attention to financial conditions and stability across CRE, credit conditions, and balance sheet — not a dominant theme but meaningfully present.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 37 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants viewed the policy rate as likely at or near its peak."
- "Almost all participants indicated that... a lower target range for the federal funds rate would be appropriate by the end of 2024."
- "It was possible that the economy could evolve in a manner that would make further increases in the target range appropriate" — hedging language.

**Center of gravity**: Forward signal clearly leans toward easing, but with heavy data-dependence caveats and residual tightening bias in the statement language.
**Minority views**: Several noted possible longer hold; statement still references "additional policy firming."

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 62 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants generally perceived a high degree of uncertainty surrounding the economic outlook."
- "Their outlooks were associated with an unusually elevated degree of uncertainty."
- Multiple upside and downside risks enumerated (economic momentum, financial conditions, geopolitics, policy lags).

**Center of gravity**: Participants explicitly and broadly expressed elevated uncertainty, using strong language ("high degree," "unusually elevated").
