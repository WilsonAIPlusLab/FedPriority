# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2013-12-18 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is **minutes** (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented post-2009 format with clear participant attribution language. Quantifier language used throughout: "most," "many," "several," "some," "a few," "one."

### Era Context
December 2013: The economy is in its post-GFC recovery phase. This is the historic "taper" meeting where the Committee first reduced the pace of QE3 asset purchases. Unemployment had fallen to 7%, still elevated. Inflation was running below the 2% target. The federal funds rate remained at the zero lower bound with threshold-based forward guidance (6.5% unemployment, 2.5% inflation).

### Quantifier Distribution
- "all" / "almost all" / "unanimous": all anticipated unemployment decline; almost all agreed to add forward guidance language
- "most": expressed greater confidence in outlook; saw reduction in purchases as appropriate; wanted no change to threshold
- "many": expressed concern about inflation deceleration; judged cautious approach; commented meaningful progress
- "several": cited other labor indicators; saw cyclical weakness in participation; stressed unemployment still elevated; saw need to convey inflation importance
- "some": cited research on structural participation decline; questioned appropriateness of tapering with low inflation; expressed concern about unintended tightening
- "a few": noted risk of structural damage; suggested lowering unemployment threshold; proposed more deterministic wind-down path
- "one": proposed more explicit financial stability analysis; objected to reduction in purchases

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW target. "Inflation continued to run noticeably below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent." "Many participants expressed concern about the deceleration in consumer prices over the past year." Some said it was "unlikely to slow further" with steady underlying measures. Several noted transitory factors. This is clearly below-target inflation with concern, but not deflation alarm. Rubric anchor: 2013 = 38-42. The language here is somewhat more concerned than typical below-target ("noticeably below," "many expressed concern," costs of very low inflation cited), pushing toward the lower end. Score: 32 (below target with meaningful concern about low inflation).

Polarity check: Higher = more pressure. Low inflation = low score. Correct.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as BELOW potential but improving. "Economy was expanding at a moderate pace." Members weighed "prospects for sustained above-potential economic growth" -- this is forward looking, not current state. Unemployment at 7% is "elevated." "Considerable slack remaining in the labor market." Fiscal drag still present but diminishing. The discussion conveys moderate pace, below potential, with improving outlook. Score: 39 (below potential, recovering but gap persists).

Bright-line check: Slack remains language --> must be < 50. Score 39 satisfies this.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see the labor market as below full employment with improvement. Payrolls 200K+/month, unemployment fell to 7% -- "encouraging signs." But "several others continued to see important elements of cyclical weakness" -- high long-duration unemployment, involuntary part-time, depressed prime-age EPOP. "Most measures of wages had not accelerated." "Considerable slack in the labor market." This is improving but still loose. Score: 36 (below full employment, slack remains, improving).

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says slack. Low score correct.

Coherence: OUTPUT_GAP (39) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (36) both below 50, close together. Consistent.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumer spending appeared to be strengthening, with solid gains in retail sales." "Rebound in motor vehicle sales." Multiple positive factors cited. "Business investment appeared to be advancing at a moderate rate." Housing slowed ("increase in mortgage interest rates... having a greater effect"). Manufacturing "increasing at a solid rate." Positive reports from tech, rail, freight, airline, energy. Net: consumer spending strengthening, business investment moderate, housing soft. Overall moderately positive. Score: 46 (approximately at trend; moderate pace with some strengthening).

Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Evidence says moderate with pickup. Score near 46 is appropriate -- not yet above trend but improving.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Markets "reasonably well aligned" with policy communications. The discussion mentions "a further easing of credit conditions." Some worried about "potential for an unintended tightening of financial conditions" from tapering. The broader stance is still accommodative with easy credit. Several concerns about financial vulnerabilities (small-cap P/E ratios, leveraged loans, margin credit) suggest loose conditions, not tight ones. Score: 28 (loose; accommodative conditions, credit available, though concerns about misinterpretation of taper).

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says loose/accommodative. Low score correct.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: This is the taper meeting. "Most members agreed... the Committee could appropriately begin to slow the pace of its asset purchases." This is a modest reduction ($10B/month) in ongoing QE -- still purchasing $75B/month. Still at zero rates. Forward guidance strengthened to be more dovish ("well past the time that the unemployment rate declines below 6.5 percent"). One dissent against tapering. Some questioned appropriateness given low inflation. "Many judged the Committee should proceed cautiously." The center of gravity is: begin tapering modestly, but emphasize continued accommodation and low-for-longer rates. This is a marginal hawkish step from deep accommodation. Score: 42 (lean dovish to balanced; still deeply accommodative but beginning to reduce purchases).

Anchor check: 2014 taper era = 40-50. Score 42 fits -- they are still in QE, just reducing pace.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal dissent (premature to reduce purchases). Notable spread of views: "some" wanted to continue current pace; "some other participants preferred a larger reduction"; "a few proposed a more deterministic path." Forward guidance also debated (lower threshold vs. qualitative guidance). This is moderate disagreement -- one dissent, multiple camps on pace, but majority broadly agrees. Score: 38 (moderate differences; 1 dissent with 2-3 distinct camps on tactics).

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: A dedicated paragraph reviews "indicators of financial vulnerabilities." Several participants commented on small-cap valuations, equity repurchases, margin credit, leveraged loans. "A couple of participants offered views on the role of financial stability in monetary policy decisionmaking more broadly." This is a meaningful but not dominant discussion -- a dedicated passage with several participants engaging. Score: 35 (limited-to-moderate discussion; several mention meaningfully).

Anchor check: 2013-19 typical = 20-35. Score 35 is at the upper end, appropriate given the dedicated vulnerability review.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The committee strengthened dovish forward guidance. "Almost all members agreed to add language indicating... it would be appropriate to maintain the current target range for the federal funds rate well past the time that the unemployment rate declines below 6.5 percent, especially if projected inflation continues to run below the Committee's longer-run objective." Debate about lowering the threshold (a few favored, most opposed). The forward signal is: rates stay low for a long time even as purchases taper. Score: 15 (strong easing bias in forward guidance; low-for-long commitment strengthened).

Coherence check: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (42) vs FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (15) -- divergence explained by: current action is modestly hawkish (beginning taper), but forward rate guidance is strongly dovish (rates low well past threshold). This is the classic taper structure: reduce purchases while reinforcing low-rate commitment.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Most expressed greater confidence in the outlook" and "saw the risks... as more nearly balanced than earlier in the year." But: concerns about whether projected pickup was assured, low inflation persistence, potential structural damage. Budget accord "reducing uncertainty." Some questioned whether recovery was as strong as thought. Net: declining uncertainty, more balanced risks, but notable residual concerns. Score: 30 (somewhat below typical elevated; declining uncertainty with residual concerns).

Anchor check: 2013-14 = 25-35. Score 30 fits.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Summary
- OUTPUT_GAP (39) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (36): Both below 50, consistent with recovery still below potential.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (42) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (15): Divergence explained by taper initiation (modest hawkish step) combined with strengthened dovish rate guidance.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (32) and below-target language: Consistent with concern about low inflation informing dovish forward guidance.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 32 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Inflation continued to run noticeably below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent"
- "many participants expressed concern about the deceleration in consumer prices over the past year"
- "Among the costs of very low or declining inflation that were cited were its effects in raising real interest rates and debt burdens"

Center of gravity: Inflation clearly below target with active concern about the low level and its economic costs; not deflation alarm but meaningful worry about persistent undershooting.

Minority views: "Some participants suggested that inflation, while low, was unlikely to slow further, pointing to core, trimmed mean, or sticky-price inflation measures" -- this moderates the concern somewhat.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 39 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "economy was expanding at a moderate pace"
- "considerable slack remaining in the labor market" and unemployment "remained elevated"
- "almost all participants continued to project that the rate of growth of economic activity would strengthen in coming years"

Center of gravity: Economy below potential with persistent slack, but recovery solidifying and outlook improving. Moderate expansion, not yet closing the gap.

Minority views: "A few participants raised the possibility that recent declines in inflation might suggest that the economic recovery was not as strong as some thought."

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 36 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "increases in nonfarm payroll employment of more than 200,000 per month in October and November and the decline in the unemployment rate to 7 percent as encouraging signs"
- "several others continued to see important elements of cyclical weakness in the low labor force participation rate and cited other indicators of considerable slack in the labor market, including the still-high levels of long-duration unemployment and of workers employed part time for economic reasons"
- "most measures of wages had not accelerated"

Center of gravity: Labor market improving meaningfully but still well below full employment with considerable slack across multiple dimensions.

Minority views: "Some participants cited research that found that demographic and other structural factors... accounted for much of the recent decline in participation" (implying less slack than headline suggests).

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 46 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Consumer spending appeared to be strengthening, with solid gains in retail sales in recent months and a rebound in motor vehicle sales"
- "Business investment appeared to be advancing at a moderate rate"
- "Activity in the housing sector slowed in recent months"

Center of gravity: Consumer spending strengthening and business investment moderate, offset partially by housing slowdown. Net position approximately at trend with mild upward momentum.

Minority views: "A couple of participants reported that their contacts continued to focus on investments intended to reduce costs and were still cautious regarding investment to expand capacity."

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 28 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- "the projected improvement in economic activity was expected to be supported by... a further easing of credit conditions"
- "current market expectations were reasonably well aligned with the Committee's recent policy communications"
- Vulnerability concerns about "rise in forward price-to-earnings ratios," "increased level of equity repurchases," "rise in margin credit," "increase in issuance of leveraged loans" -- all indicating loose conditions

Center of gravity: Financial conditions remain accommodative/loose; credit easing continues. Concerns about misinterpretation of taper are about maintaining this looseness, not about existing tightness.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 42 +/-8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "most participants saw a reduction in the pace of purchases as appropriate at this meeting" (modest $10B taper)
- "many judged that the Committee should proceed cautiously in taking its first action to reduce the pace of asset purchases"
- "In the view of one member, a reduction in the pace of purchases was premature"
- "almost all members agreed to add language indicating... it would be appropriate to maintain the current target range for the federal funds rate well past the time that the unemployment rate declines below 6.5 percent"

Center of gravity: Modest hawkish step (beginning taper) from a deeply accommodative baseline, counterbalanced by strengthened dovish rate guidance and cautious tone. Still firmly in accommodative territory.

Minority views: "Some" wanted to continue purchases unchanged; "some other participants preferred a larger reduction" -- spread across both directions.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- One member viewed "a reduction in the pace of purchases was premature"
- "Some also expressed concern about the potential for an unintended tightening"
- Multiple camps: some wanted no taper, most favored modest taper, "some other participants preferred a larger reduction," "a few proposed... a more deterministic path"
- Forward guidance also debated: "a few members suggested lowering the unemployment threshold to 6 percent" vs. "most members wanted to make no change"

Center of gravity: One dissent with notable tactical disagreement across multiple camps on pace and forward guidance, but broad agreement on direction.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 35 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Participants also reviewed indicators of financial vulnerabilities that could pose risks to financial stability and the broader economy"
- "several participants commented on the rise in forward price-to-earnings ratios for some small-cap stocks, the increased level of equity repurchases, or the rise in margin credit"
- "A couple of participants offered views on the role of financial stability in monetary policy decisionmaking more broadly"

Center of gravity: Dedicated paragraph with several participants engaging on specific financial vulnerability indicators. More than passing mention but not a dominant theme.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 15 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "almost all members agreed to add language indicating... it would be appropriate to maintain the current target range for the federal funds rate well past the time that the unemployment rate declines below 6.5 percent, especially if projected inflation continues to run below the Committee's longer-run objective"
- "several participants saw the need to clearly convey that inflation remains an important consideration in adjusting the target funds rate"
- Threshold-based forward guidance still in place (6.5% unemployment)

Center of gravity: Forward guidance is strongly dovish -- rates to remain low well past the already-dovish threshold, reinforced with inflation conditionality. This is a strengthening of the low-for-long commitment.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "most expressed greater confidence in the outlook and saw the risks associated with their forecasts... as more nearly balanced than earlier in the year"
- "the prospect that the Congress would shortly reach an accord on the budget seemed to be reducing uncertainty"
- "the projected pickup in economic growth was not assured"; concerns about persistence of low inflation

Center of gravity: Declining uncertainty with more balanced risks, though residual concerns about inflation persistence and recovery durability. Standard-to-slightly-elevated uncertainty.
