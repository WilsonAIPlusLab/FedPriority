# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2010-09-21 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Segmented -- participant views section is clearly delineated from staff review. The file is slightly truncated at the end of the policy action section but contains the vast majority of participant discussion content.

**Era context**: September 2010 -- roughly 15 months into the recovery from the Great Financial Crisis. The economy is growing but slowly, unemployment remains very high, and the FOMC is at the zero lower bound debating whether to launch QE2 (additional asset purchases). Inflation is running below mandate-consistent levels.

**Quantifier distribution**: "many" (concern about slow growth, limited pricing power, additional accommodation appropriate), "most" (underlying inflation below mandate), "several" (retail sales stronger, members noting pace of recovery), "some" (credit improving for smaller firms, merit in waiting), "a few" (recoveries slow after financial crises), "nearly all" (maintain target range). The language heavily tilts toward concern about slack and weak growth.

**Dimension-by-dimension reasoning**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW target. "Most participants indicated that underlying inflation was at levels somewhat below those that they judged to be consistent with the Committee's dual mandate." "Many business contacts reported that they currently had little pricing power." TIPS-based inflation compensation had declined. Participants saw "only small odds of deflation" -- so not in deflation territory, but clearly below target with disinflation concerns. Direction: below midpoint. "Somewhat below" = 5-10 points below 50, but the additional evidence of declining TIPS compensation, no pricing power, and disinflation language pushes this further. Score ~25-30. Polarity check: higher = more inflation pressure. Low inflation pressure = low score. Correct.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as clearly BELOW potential. "Output and employment were increasing only slowly and at rates well below those recorded earlier in the year." "Continued high levels of slack left the economy exposed." "Wide margins of economic slack." This is a post-crisis recovery with substantial slack remaining. Bright-line rule: "below potential" language mandates score < 50. The language is strong -- "considerable slack," "well below." Score ~30-33. Anchors suggest 35-42 for 2010-14 recovery, but this is early in that period with very emphatic slack language. Score ~32.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: The labor market is clearly LOOSE. "Current sluggish pace of employment growth was insufficient to reduce unemployment at a satisfactory pace." Unemployment rate was "considerably above levels that could be explained by structural factors alone." Businesses delaying hiring. This is clearly well below full employment. Anchors: 35-40 for 2011-14, but Sept 2010 is earlier/worse. Score ~28. Polarity check: higher = tighter. Very loose = low score. Correct.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Below trend. Spending indicators "were mixed." Retail sales "a bit stronger than expected" but "shoppers remained very price sensitive." Households repairing balance sheets. Many businesses had cash reserves but "were refraining from adding workers or expanding plants and equipment." Housing "continued to be very weak." Net picture: sluggish with mixed signals, below trend but not collapsing. Score ~33-35. Anchors: 35-42 for 2011-13 "moderate." This is slightly weaker than that. Score ~34.

5. **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Mixed conditions. Banks "remained generally cautious." Credit "remained readily available for larger corporations" but improving for smaller firms. Consumer loan performance improving. Credit problems "appeared to have mostly peaked." Asset prices "little changed on net." European stresses "remained broadly contained." This is somewhat tight but improving -- not crisis levels, not normal. Score ~55. Polarity check: higher = tighter. Conditions are somewhat restrictive but improving. Score ~53.

6. **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Decidedly dovish. "Many participants noted that if economic growth remained too slow... it would be appropriate to provide additional monetary policy accommodation." They discussed further purchases of Treasuries and steps to affect inflation expectations. Discussion of price-level targeting and nominal GDP targeting -- all easing tools. "However, others thought that additional accommodation would be warranted only if the outlook worsened" -- some pushback. "Nearly all" agreed to maintain current stance (hold, not ease yet), and "some members saw merit in accumulating further information." This is a hold meeting with strong easing bias for the near future. The fact that QE2 is being actively debated and "many" favor additional accommodation places this in the 20-25 range. Anchors: 15-25 for QE era 2010-12. Score ~22.

7. **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Notable disagreement. "Many" favor additional accommodation vs. "others" who would only ease if deflation odds rose materially. Participants "differed in their assessments" of structural vs. cyclical unemployment. But no formal dissents mentioned (nearly all agreed on the hold decision). The disagreement is about the forward path, not the current action. Score ~40. The debate about whether and when to do more QE reflects meaningful dispersion but within a framework of agreement on the current action.

8. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Moderate but not dominant. Discussion of bank conditions, European stresses, credit conditions, asset price sensitivity to data. About one paragraph of substantive financial conditions discussion. Score ~35.

9. **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Very dovish forward lean. "Many participants noted that... it would be appropriate to provide additional monetary policy accommodation." Discussion of further asset purchases, inflation expectations management, price-level targeting. The forward signal is clearly toward more easing. Score ~12-15. Anchors: 8-15 for 2014 taper -- this is pre-QE2, actively discussing launching it, so similarly dovish. Score ~14.

10. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Elevated. "Considerable uncertainty about the current trajectory for the economy." "Elevated uncertainty about employment prospects." Businesses holding back due to "uncertainty about future fiscal and regulatory policies." Data described as "mixed." Some members wanted more information before deciding. Score ~58.

**Cross-dimension coherence checks**:
- OUTPUT_GAP (32) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (28): Both well below 50, consistent with deep slack. Labor slightly lower because of explicit "considerably above" language on unemployment. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (22) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (14): Both dovish, with forward guidance slightly more dovish because the discussion of QE2 tools and inflation expectation strategies signals strong easing intent ahead. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 26 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Most participants indicated that underlying inflation was at levels somewhat below those that they judged to be consistent with the Committee's dual mandate for maximum employment and price stability."
- "Many business contacts reported that they currently had little pricing power and that they anticipated limited, if any, increases in labor costs."
- "TIPS-based inflation compensation had declined, on balance, in recent quarters."
- Center of gravity: Inflation clearly running below mandate-consistent levels with little pricing power and declining market-based expectations, but participants saw "only small odds of deflation," placing this in the disinflation zone rather than deflation alarm.
- Minority views: Some commodity and import price increases noted, but these were not shifting the center of gravity.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Output and employment were increasing only slowly and at rates well below those recorded earlier in the year."
- "The sluggish pace of growth and continued high levels of slack left the economy exposed to potential negative shocks."
- "Resource slack was anticipated to still remain elevated at the end of 2012."
- Center of gravity: Economy well below potential with substantial and persistent slack. Recovery continuing but at a disappointing pace.
- Minority views: "A few" noted recoveries are typically slow after financial crises, providing some context but not disagreement on the slack assessment.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 28 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "The current sluggish pace of employment growth was insufficient to reduce unemployment at a satisfactory pace."
- "Many participants saw evidence that the current unemployment rate was considerably above levels that could be explained by structural factors alone."
- "Several participants reported feedback from business contacts who were delaying hiring until the economic and regulatory outlook became more certain."
- Center of gravity: Very loose labor market with high unemployment, slow job creation, and businesses reluctant to hire.
- Minority views: Some participants attributed more of unemployment to structural factors, but even they did not argue the labor market was tight.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 34 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Indicators of spending by businesses and households were mixed."
- "Many businesses had built up large reserves of cash... but were refraining from adding workers or expanding plants and equipment."
- "Households were continuing efforts to repair their balance sheets by saving more and paying down debt." Housing "continued to be very weak."
- Center of gravity: Below trend. Consumer spending mixed with some positive retail data but household deleveraging. Business investment held back despite cash hoards. Housing deeply depressed.
- Minority views: Retail sales "a bit stronger than expected" and "continued strong foreign demand" provide modest upside offset.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 53 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Banks remained generally cautious and uncertain about the regulatory outlook."
- "Credit remained readily available for larger corporations with access to financial markets, and there were some signs that credit conditions had begun to improve for smaller firms."
- "Credit problems more broadly appeared to have mostly peaked, although banks continued to report elevated losses on commercial real estate loans."
- Center of gravity: Conditions somewhat tight but improving. Large firms have access; small firms and CRE remain stressed. Not crisis-level but not fully normalized.
- Minority views: Improving consumer loan performance and easing for small firms suggest conditions moving toward neutral.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 22 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Many participants noted that if economic growth remained too slow to make satisfactory progress toward reducing the unemployment rate or if inflation continued to come in below levels consistent with the FOMC's dual mandate, it would be appropriate to provide additional monetary policy accommodation."
- "Participants discussed several possible approaches to providing additional accommodation but focused primarily on further purchases of longer-term Treasury securities and on possible steps to affect inflation expectations."
- "However, others thought that additional accommodation would be warranted only if the outlook worsened and the odds of deflation increased materially."
- Center of gravity: Strong dovish lean with active discussion of QE2 and other easing tools. Current action is a hold, but the majority is oriented toward additional accommodation. Decision to wait for more information, not a decision against easing.
- Minority views: "Others" set a higher bar for additional easing; "some" members preferred accumulating more information first.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 40 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Many" favor additional accommodation vs. "others" who set a higher bar. Participants "differed in their assessments" of structural unemployment extent.
- "Some members saw merit in accumulating further information" vs. those ready to act. "Some noting that the economic benefits could be small in current circumstances."
- Center of gravity: Meaningful disagreement about the threshold for and likely effectiveness of additional accommodation, but broad agreement on current action (hold) and that risks are to the downside.
- Minority views: The "others" camp represents a significant minority skeptical of QE benefits.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 35 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Banks remained generally cautious and uncertain about the regulatory outlook." "Stresses in European financial markets remained broadly contained but bore watching going forward."
- Discussion of bank capital standards, credit conditions, CRE losses, asset price sensitivity to data.
- Center of gravity: Financial conditions discussed as context for the economic outlook but not a central topic of debate. More attention than a passing mention but not a dominant theme.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 14 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Many participants noted that... it would be appropriate to provide additional monetary policy accommodation."
- Discussion of "further purchases of longer-term Treasury securities," price-level targeting, nominal GDP targeting as future easing strategies.
- "Members wanted to consider further the most effective framework for calibrating and communicating any additional steps to provide such stimulus."
- Center of gravity: Strong easing signal for the near future. The committee is actively designing the framework for additional accommodation, signaling QE2 is coming.
- Minority views: "Others" would only act if deflation odds rose, but even the hold decision is framed as a pause to refine the easing approach.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 58 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "In light of the considerable uncertainty about the current trajectory for the economy, some members saw merit in accumulating further information."
- "Elevated uncertainty about employment prospects continued to weigh on consumption spending."
- Businesses holding back due to "uncertainty about future fiscal and regulatory policies." Data described as "mixed."
- Center of gravity: Elevated uncertainty is a pervasive theme -- affecting households, businesses, and policymakers. It is cited as a reason to delay action and as a headwind to the economy. Multiple sources of uncertainty (fiscal, regulatory, demand, structural vs. cyclical unemployment).

- Center of gravity: Expectations broadly anchored but with some downward drift in market-based measures. Below the 50 midpoint (anchored at target) given the decline in TIPS compensation.
