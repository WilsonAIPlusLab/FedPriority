# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2017-06-14

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Segmented -- participant discussion is clearly separated from staff content.

**Era context**: June 2017 -- mid-hiking cycle. The Fed had been gradually raising rates since December 2015. Unemployment was low (4.3%), inflation was running below 2% (core PCE at 1.5%), and the economy was growing moderately. Balance sheet normalization was being prepared. This meeting delivered a 25bp hike.

**Quantifier distribution**: "participants agreed" / "participants generally" (strong consensus language used frequently), "most" (inflation softness as idiosyncratic), "several" (on both sides of various debates), "some" (downside inflation risks, deferring balance sheet), "a few" (worker shortages, equity valuations, balance sheet timing, less comfortable with further tightening path), "one" (dissenter against rate hike), "a couple" (inflation-unemployment link concerns).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW target. Headline and core PCE both "running somewhat below 2 percent." Core PCE dropped from 1.8% to 1.5%. "Most participants viewed the recent softness... as largely reflecting idiosyncratic factors." But "several participants expressed concern that progress toward the Committee's 2 percent longer-run inflation objective might have slowed." The center of gravity is clearly below target, but most view it as transitory. The current reading is notably soft. Rubric says score the perception NOW -- inflation is somewhat below 2%, with recent unexpected declines. This maps to the 26-40 range ("Below target; slack keeping prices in check; disinflation"). Given that it's "somewhat below" and falling further (1.5% core), I'd place this around 35. The "idiosyncratic/transitory" framing doesn't reduce the score per the gotcha, but the fact that most don't see it as deeply concerning pulls it slightly above 30. Score: 35.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. My evidence says below-target, declining. Lower score = less pressure. Score of 35 matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see "economic activity had been rising moderately." Unemployment at 4.3% is "below levels that participants judged likely to be normal over the longer run." This is a bright-line trigger: unemployment BELOW the longer-run normal rate means the economy is running above potential. Several participants endorsed a policy where "the unemployment rate would undershoot their current estimates of the longer-term normal rate for a sustained period." The economy is modestly above potential. Score must be > 50. "Moderate" growth, unemployment modestly below NAIRU. This maps to 51-58 ("Modestly above potential; above trend"). Score: 53.

Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Unemployment below NAIRU = above potential. Score > 50. Matches.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 4.3%, "below levels that participants judged likely to be normal over the longer run." "Contacts in several Districts reported shortages of workers in selected occupations." "A few participants interpreted this slowing in payroll growth as an expected development that reflected a tight labor market." However, "measures of labor compensation for the overall economy continued to rise only moderately despite strengthening labor market conditions." The labor market is tight by the unemployment measure but wage pressures remain modest. This is solidly in the 53-65 range ("Tight; low unemployment; hard to find workers; moderate wage pressure"). The limited wage response and "only moderately" rising compensation temper the score. Score: 56.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says tight (low unemployment, worker shortages) but modest wages. Score of 56 in the "tight" range. Matches.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "appeared to be rebounding after slowing in the first quarter." Participants expected "moderate growth in household spending." Business fixed investment "had continued to expand," supported by energy sector rebound. Manufacturing "strong" in several Districts. But auto sales slowed, optimism "abated somewhat," some large firms "curtailed capital spending" due to fiscal uncertainty, housing "mixed." This is a moderate picture -- rebounding from weakness, expanding but not strongly. Score around 50 ("moderate pace; sustained"). Maybe 50-52 given the moderate expansion language. Score: 51.

Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending. Evidence is moderate/rebounding, not strong. Score of 51 is near neutral. Matches.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Financial conditions had eased even as the Committee reduced policy accommodation." "Equity prices rose, longer-term interest rates declined, and volatility in financial markets was generally low." This is explicitly describing loosening conditions despite rate hikes. This maps clearly to loose conditions, in the 16-30 range. "Some participants suggested that increased risk tolerance among investors might be contributing to elevated asset prices." Conditions are explicitly described as having eased. Score: 25.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says conditions eased/loose. Low score matches.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Almost all participants expressed the view that it would be appropriate... to raise the target range... 25 basis points." One dissented (wanting to hold). "Participants generally reiterated their support for continuing a gradual approach." "Several participants expressed confidence that a series of further increases in the federal funds rate in coming years" was appropriate. "A few participants who supported an increase... were less comfortable with the degree of additional policy tightening through the end of 2018." The action is a hike (hawkish), but the forward lean is cautious/gradual. The center of gravity is: hike now, continue gradual tightening, some want to slow. This is moderate hawkish. Anchors say 70-75 for 2017-18 hiking. Given the "gradual" emphasis and "a few" uncomfortable with further tightening, I'd place this at 67.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. They hiked and see further gradual hikes. Score of 67 in moderate hawkish range. Matches.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One participant dissented on the rate hike. There was a range of views on balance sheet timing ("several preferred" near-term, "some others emphasized deferring"). Some disagreement on the pace of further hikes ("a few... less comfortable"). "Several participants expressed concern" about unemployment undershooting risks vs. "several" who endorsed it. Moderate differences with one formal dissent. Score: 35 (moderate differences, 1 dissent, some reservations).

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: There is a dedicated paragraph on financial market developments. "A few participants" noted equity valuations were high. "Some participants suggested increased risk tolerance." "A few participants expressed concern that subdued market volatility... could lead to a buildup of risks to financial stability." Later, "several participants expressed concern that a substantial and sustained unemployment undershooting might make the economy more likely to experience financial instability." One participant "emphasized the importance of remaining vigilant about financial developments." This is moderate discussion -- not the dominant topic but meaningfully addressed by several participants. Score: 40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Forward signal: "continuing a gradual approach to raising the federal funds rate." "Several participants expressed confidence that a series of further increases... in coming years." Balance sheet normalization "would likely become appropriate this year." But "a few participants... were less comfortable with the degree of additional policy tightening through the end of 2018." The forward signal is mildly hawkish -- more hikes coming, plus balance sheet normalization, but gradual. Anchors: 60-75 for 2015-18 hiking cycle. Score: 67.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Gradual further tightening plus balance sheet normalization. Score of 67 matches.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Near-term risks to the economic outlook as roughly balanced." "Participants again noted the uncertainty regarding the possible enactment, timing, and nature of changes to fiscal and other government policies." "Some participants emphasized downside risks" on inflation. "A couple of participants expressed concern" about upside inflation risk from tight labor. Uncertainty from fiscal policy is noted but not acute. General uncertainty is moderate. Anchors: 10-20 for mid-2017. The discussion mentions fiscal policy uncertainty, some downside inflation risks. I'll score slightly above the era anchor. Score: 25.

Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. Evidence shows standard caveats plus some fiscal/inflation uncertainty. Score of 25 in "normal" range. Matches.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (53) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (56): Both modestly above 50, consistent. Labor market slightly tighter than output gap, which makes sense given unemployment at 4.3% below NAIRU while GDP growth is "moderate."
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (67) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (67): Aligned. Both in moderate hawkish territory reflecting the hiking cycle with gradual approach.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 35 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Inflation measured on a 12-month basis had declined recently and... had been running somewhat below 2 percent."
- "Core PCE price inflation... moved down from 1.8 percent in March to 1.5 percent in April."
- "Most participants viewed the recent softness in these price data as largely reflecting idiosyncratic factors."
- "Several participants expressed concern that progress toward the Committee's 2 percent longer-run inflation objective might have slowed."

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation is running notably below 2%, with recent further declines; most see this as idiosyncratic but several worry it may persist.
**Minority views**: Several concerned about persistent undershooting push uncertainty upward; a couple concerned about potential overshoot if unemployment runs too low push slightly higher.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 53 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity had been rising moderately, on average, so far this year."
- "The unemployment rate... was below levels that participants judged likely to be normal over the longer run."
- "Several participants endorsed a policy approach... in which the unemployment rate would undershoot their current estimates of the longer-term normal rate for a sustained period."

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is operating modestly above potential, with unemployment below the longer-run normal level, but growth is moderate rather than rapid.
**Minority views**: Broad agreement on this assessment; narrow uncertainty band.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 56 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The unemployment rate fell from 4.5 percent in March to 4.3 percent in May and was below levels that participants judged likely to be normal over the longer run."
- "Contacts in several Districts reported shortages of workers in selected occupations and in some cases indicated that firms were significantly increasing salaries and benefits."
- "Measures of labor compensation for the overall economy continued to rise only moderately despite strengthening labor market conditions."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is tight by unemployment and shortage measures but wage pressures remain moderate, tempering the tightness signal.
**Minority views**: A couple of participants saw restrained compensation as consistent with low productivity growth.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 51 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Growth in consumer spending appeared to be rebounding after slowing in the first quarter."
- "Business fixed investment had continued to expand in recent months, supported in particular by a rebound in the energy sector."
- "Automobile sales had slowed recently." "Reports regarding housing construction from District contacts were mixed."
- "Contacts at some large firms indicated that they had curtailed their capital spending."

**Center-of-gravity**: Spending and investment are at a moderate pace -- consumer spending rebounding, business investment expanding, but with notable pockets of weakness (autos, housing mixed, some capex curtailment).
**Minority views**: Limited divergence; most agree on moderate characterization.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 25 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Equity prices rose, longer-term interest rates declined, and volatility in financial markets was generally low."
- "Financial conditions had eased even as the Committee reduced policy accommodation."
- "Some participants suggested that increased risk tolerance among investors might be contributing to elevated asset prices more broadly."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly loose and have been easing despite policy tightening, which participants explicitly note as a notable development.
**Minority views**: Broad agreement that conditions have loosened; no dissent on this characterization.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 67 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Almost all participants expressed the view that it would be appropriate for the Committee to raise the target range for the federal funds rate 25 basis points at this meeting."
- "Participants generally reiterated their support for continuing a gradual approach to raising the federal funds rate."
- "One participant did not believe it was appropriate to raise the federal funds rate target range at this meeting."
- "A few participants who supported an increase... were less comfortable with the degree of additional policy tightening through the end of 2018."

**Center-of-gravity**: Strong consensus for a 25bp hike, with support for continued gradual tightening, but some caution about the pace of further hikes.
**Minority views**: One outright dissenter (dovish); a few uncomfortable with the projected pace of tightening.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 35 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- One participant dissented against the rate hike.
- "Participants expressed a range of views about the appropriate timing of a change in reinvestment policy."
- "A few participants who supported an increase... indicated that they were less comfortable with the degree of additional policy tightening."
- Several vs. several on unemployment undershooting strategy.

**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate disagreement -- one formal dissent, some spread on the forward path and balance sheet timing, but broad agreement on the current action.
**Minority views**: Disagreement is primarily about pace and forward path rather than the current decision.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 40 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "In the assessment of a few participants, equity prices were high when judged against standard valuation measures."
- "A few participants expressed concern that subdued market volatility, coupled with a low equity premium, could lead to a buildup of risks to financial stability."
- "Several participants expressed concern that a substantial and sustained unemployment undershooting might make the economy more likely to experience financial instability."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability receives moderate attention -- a dedicated paragraph on financial conditions plus several participants linking labor market tightness to financial stability risks. Not dominant but meaningfully discussed.
**Minority views**: Most of the financial stability commentary comes from "a few" or "several," not the majority.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 67 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants generally reiterated their support for continuing a gradual approach to raising the federal funds rate."
- "Several participants expressed confidence that a series of further increases in the federal funds rate in coming years... would contribute to a stabilization... of the inflation rate."
- "It would likely become appropriate this year for the Committee to announce and implement a specific timetable for its program of reducing reinvestment."
- "A few participants... were less comfortable with the degree of additional policy tightening through the end of 2018."

**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal points to continued gradual tightening plus imminent balance sheet normalization, but with some caution about the pace.
**Minority views**: A few participants uneasy about projected pace of additional tightening.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 25 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants continued to see the near-term risks to the economic outlook as roughly balanced."
- "Participants again noted the uncertainty regarding the possible enactment, timing, and nature of changes to fiscal and other government policies."
- "Some participants emphasized downside risks, particularly in light of the recent low readings on inflation."

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is at a normal level -- risks are roughly balanced, with standard caveats about fiscal policy and some asymmetric concern about inflation.
**Minority views**: Some emphasize downside inflation risks; a couple flag upside risk from tight labor markets.
