# FOMC Meeting Scoring — 2005-02-02 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Unsegmented pre-2009 format — staff and participant content mixed. I will score ONLY participant-attributed language ("participants noted," "members favored," "several suggested," etc.) and ignore staff-reported content.

**Era context**: February 2005 — mid-cycle tightening. The Fed had been raising rates in 25bp "measured pace" increments since June 2004 (from 1.00% to now 2.50%). The economy was in solid expansion, inflation was low, and the housing boom was underway. Financial conditions were loose.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all" (favored 25bp hike, agreed on statement language), "participants" (general views on outlook, labor, inflation), "several" (upward inflation skew possibility), "some" (holiday sales, net exports, job growth anecdotes), "one participant" (output near potential). The dominant tone is broad agreement with moderate growth, low inflation, and continued measured tightening.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target/low. Core inflation "eased off" in the latest readings and H2 2004 vs H1. Inflation expectations "well-anchored." Core PCE forecasts "concentrated in the lower portion of a 1-1/2 to 2 percent range." However, "several participants suggested the possibility of an upward skew to the distribution of inflation outcomes," and some reported "increased ability to pass cost increases through." The center of gravity is low inflation with some upside risk awareness. Direction: below midpoint but not dramatically so. Score ~40. Higher = more pressure. Evidence says low and stable — matches below 50.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants describe "further removal of monetary accommodation" consistent with "gradual reduction of remaining economic slack." GDP growing at "moderate pace." One participant suggested output "might already be at or close to potential," but others said slack remained. "Continued need" for slack to diminish over coming quarters. The center of gravity is slightly below potential. Direction: below 50. Score ~47 — approaching potential but slack still discussed.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Continued modest gains in employment," unemployment expected to "continue to edge lower." "Flat pattern of growth in wages and compensation suggested an absence of pressures in labor markets." Unemployment forecast 5-5.25%. Labor market improving gradually but not tight. Direction: below midpoint. Score ~44.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Domestic demand had stayed strong." Consumer spending "strong growth," sustained by income gains, low rates, confidence. Capital spending "gains had been quite strong last year" and little evidence of significant slowdown. Firms "somewhat more confident" and "increase spending on equipment and software." Direction: above trend. Score ~60.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Conditions described as "overall accommodative financial conditions," "favorable financial conditions," "narrow credit risk spreads," "abundant liquidity in financing markets," "low longer-term interest rates on corporate borrowing." This is clearly loose. Direction: low tightness. Score ~15. Anchor: 10-20 for 2005-06 pre-crisis ease.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All members favored 25bp hike. Real fed funds rate "generally seen as remaining below levels that might reasonably be associated with maintaining a stable inflation rate" — i.e., still accommodative, more hikes needed. "Measured" pace language retained. Statement: "accommodation could be removed at a pace that was likely to be measured." This is a steady, moderate tightening posture. Direction: moderately hawkish. Score ~65.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All of the members favored raising the target by 25 basis points." "Members concurred" on statement language. Near-unanimous on everything. One participant suggested output near potential (minority view) and "several" flagged upside inflation risk, but these are minor assessment differences, not policy disagreements. Score ~12.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Participants noted "narrow credit spreads and risk premiums," "abundant liquidity," low saving rate, housing price appreciation, equity extraction. These are mentioned as context for the outlook rather than as financial stability concerns per se. "Several participants" discussed external imbalances and current account. Some attention to asset prices (housing, equity) but not framed as stability risks. Score ~25.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Accommodation could be removed at a pace that was likely to be measured but that the Committee would respond to changes in economic prospects as needed." Real rate still below neutral — more hikes expected. The pace would "depend on incoming data." This is a clear signal of continued gradual tightening. Direction: hawkish forward lean. Score ~67.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Considerable uncertainty about the sustainable rate of resource utilization and about structural productivity growth." "High level of uncertainty surrounding the prospects for and path of adjustment" (re: external imbalances). "Marked slowing in home price appreciation" as downside risk. Several sources of uncertainty mentioned, but overall tone is fairly confident about the baseline. Direction: somewhat elevated. Score ~38.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (47) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (44): Both slightly below 50, consistent — economy approaching but not yet at full capacity.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (65) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (67): Aligned — moderate hawkish current stance with continued tightening signal.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (60) is above trend while OUTPUT_GAP (47) shows remaining slack — this is coherent because strong demand is closing the gap but hasn't eliminated it.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 40 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "core inflation measures had eased off, both in the latest readings and on balance over the second half of 2004 relative to the first half"
- "the rate of core inflation likely would remain low and stable, assuming further removal of policy accommodation"
- "several participants suggested the possibility of an upward skew to the distribution of inflation outcomes"

**Center of gravity**: Participants see inflation as low and contained but with acknowledged upside risks from dollar depreciation, pass-through of costs, and potential productivity slowdown.

**Minority views**: Several flagged upside skew; anecdotal reports of increased pass-through ability widen the band upward.

---

### OUTPUT_GAP: 47 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "GDP would likely grow at a moderate pace consistent with a gradual reduction of remaining economic slack"
- "One participant suggested that...output might already be at or close to potential"
- "Others commented that recent studies did not on balance support a conclusion that structural labor market shifts had caused resource slack to be lower than commonly estimated"

**Center of gravity**: The economy is slightly below potential with slack gradually diminishing. Most participants see remaining slack; one sees output near potential.

**Minority views**: One participant placing output at/near potential pulls the band slightly upward.

---

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 44 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "continued modest gains in employment"
- "flat pattern of growth in wages and compensation suggested an absence of pressures in labor markets"
- Unemployment forecast 5 to 5-1/4 percent for Q4 2005

**Center of gravity**: Labor market improving gradually but not yet tight. Wage growth flat, consistent with remaining slack.

**Minority views**: Some anecdotal reports of job growth picking up, but not enough to shift the center.

---

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 60 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Domestic demand had stayed strong through the fourth quarter"
- "Gains in capital spending had been quite strong last year" with "little evidence...of a significant slowdown"
- "consumer spending would continue growing at a strong pace"

**Center of gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment are above trend, supported by favorable financial conditions, income gains, and confidence.

**Minority views**: Downside risks noted from potential housing slowdown and higher long-term rates, but these are forward-looking risks, not current conditions.

---

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 15 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "overall accommodative financial conditions"
- "narrow credit risk spreads...along with abundant liquidity in financing markets"
- "Low longer-term interest rates on corporate borrowing"

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly loose — narrow spreads, abundant liquidity, low rates, accommodative overall. Consistent with the 2005 pre-crisis ease anchor of 10-20.

**Minority views**: None — universally described as loose/accommodative.

---

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 65 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all of the members favored raising the target for the federal funds rate by 25 basis points to 2-1/2 percent"
- "the real federal funds rate was generally seen as remaining below levels that might reasonably be associated with maintaining a stable inflation rate over the medium run"
- "accommodation could be removed at a pace that was likely to be measured"

**Center of gravity**: Unanimous support for continued tightening at a measured pace. The real rate is still seen as below neutral, implying more hikes ahead — moderately hawkish.

**Minority views**: Minimal — all agreed on pace and direction.

---

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 12 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all of the members favored raising the target...by 25 basis points"
- "members concurred that overall economic prospects were similar to those prevailing at the time of the December meeting"
- "All members agreed that the FOMC statement for this meeting should again indicate..."

**Center of gravity**: Near-unanimous agreement on action, statement, and economic assessment. Minor differences in economic views (one on output gap, several on inflation risks) but no policy disagreement.

**Minority views**: One participant on output near potential; several on upside inflation risk — but these did not produce policy disagreement.

---

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 25 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "narrow credit spreads and risk premiums, along with abundant liquidity in financing markets, suggested that markets now assigned fairly low odds to significant downside risks"
- "marked slowing in home price appreciation and possible increases in longer-term interest rates...were seen as downside risks"
- Low saving rate discussed with reference to housing and equity wealth effects

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are discussed as context for the economic outlook (supportive of spending), not primarily as stability concerns. Some attention to housing prices and external imbalances, but not framed as systemic risk.

**Minority views**: None specifically on stability.

---

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 67 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the FOMC statement...should again indicate that policy accommodation could be removed at a pace that was likely to be measured"
- "the real federal funds rate was generally seen as remaining below levels that might reasonably be associated with maintaining a stable inflation rate"
- "The pace of policy moves at upcoming meetings, however, would depend on incoming data"

**Center of gravity**: Clear forward signal of continued gradual tightening. The "measured pace" language combined with the view that real rates are still below neutral signals more hikes ahead, with data dependence as a caveat.

**Minority views**: None — broad agreement on forward path.

---

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 38 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants noted considerable uncertainty about the sustainable rate of resource utilization and about structural productivity growth"
- "a high level of uncertainty surrounding the prospects for and path of adjustment" (external imbalances)
- "risks to the outlook for both output and inflation...appeared to remain well-balanced"

**Center of gravity**: Some genuine uncertainty about structural parameters (potential output, productivity trend) and external adjustment, but overall the baseline outlook is described with reasonable confidence. "Well-balanced" risks suggest no particular alarm.

**Minority views**: The structural uncertainty flagged by participants widens the band.
