# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2020-04-29

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized participant views). Segmented -- the text is clearly labeled "Participants' Views on Current Conditions and the Economic Outlook" and uses standard quantifier language throughout. The final section beginning "In their discussion of monetary policy" shifts to "members" language for the policy action.

**Era context**: April 29, 2020. This is the first regularly scheduled FOMC meeting after the March 2020 emergency actions (150bp cut to zero, massive QE launch, 13(3) facilities). The US is in the acute phase of the COVID-19 pandemic lockdown. GDP is collapsing in Q2 2020, unemployment claims have surged to 25+ million. This is among the most severe economic contractions in modern US history.

**Quantifier distribution**: The text is dominated by "participants" (used as a collective, implying all or nearly all). "All participants" appears for the rate decision. "Some" appears for forward guidance discussion. "Several" appears for lending facility adjustments and asset purchase clarification. "A number of" appears for financial stability risks. "A few" appears for nonbank vulnerabilities and government debt concerns.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as clearly below target and facing further downward pressure. Evidence: inflation "had been running below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective before the coronavirus outbreak"; "overall effect of the outbreak on prices was seen as disinflationary"; "weaker demand and significantly lower oil prices were holding down consumer price inflation"; "a stronger dollar and lower oil prices were factors likely to put downward pressure on inflation"; "market-based measures of inflation compensation remained very low." There is mention of "some supply constraints" creating "upward pressure on the prices of some goods," but the net effect is clearly disinflationary. Direction: well below midpoint. Language is strong -- "disinflationary" net effect, multiple downward forces. This maps to the 15-25 range (meaningful deflation risk, well below target, muted). I'll score 18. Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says low/falling inflation. Score of 18 is correct (low pressure).

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants describe the economy as collapsing. "Second quarter would likely see overall economic activity decline at an unprecedented rate"; "sharp declines in economic activity"; business activity "fallen dramatically"; manufacturing "declined sharply." The word "unprecedented" is used for the rate of decline. This maps directly to the 1-15 range ("catastrophic contraction; GDP collapsing; worst since Depression"). The anchor suggests 15-22 for COVID Q2 2020. Given that participants literally say "unprecedented rate" of decline but also note fiscal support is "providing some relief," I'll score 10. Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says massive contraction far below potential. Score of 10 is correct. Bright-line rule: massive below-potential language, score is far below 50. Correct.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Extreme decline in employment was under way"; "initial claims for unemployment insurance benefits had totaled more than 25 million"; participants "expected that the unemployment rate would soon reach the highest levels of the post-World War II period"; "widespread furloughs and layoffs"; concerned that "temporary layoffs could become permanent." This is catastrophic labor market looseness. Anchor suggests 15-22 for 2020 Q2. Language like "highest levels of the post-World War II period" and "extreme decline" puts this at the very low end. Score: 8. Polarity check: Higher = tighter labor market. Evidence says extreme looseness/job losses. Score of 8 is correct.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumer spending had plummeted across all parts of the country and in most categories of spending, with especially sharp declines" in discretionary categories. "Business activity and investment spending had also fallen dramatically." "Manufacturing output declined sharply." "Widespread reports... of firms reducing their payrolls and curtailing plans for investment spending." "Consumer confidence also plunged." This is comprehensive collapse in both consumption and investment. Anchor: 15-20 for 2020 Q2. The language is extreme -- "plummeted," "fallen dramatically," "plunged." Score: 10. Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending. Evidence says collapsing. Score of 10 is correct.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: This is nuanced. The text says the pandemic "significantly affected financial conditions and had impaired the flow of credit to U.S. households and businesses." However, it also says Fed actions "had been instrumental in easing strains in some essential financial markets." Banks "had entered the crisis well capitalized and had been able to provide necessary credit." But small businesses face "tighter lending standards" and "fewer financing options." The PPP and Fed facilities are helping maintain credit flow. So conditions were extremely tight but have partially improved from the acute March stress thanks to Fed intervention. The net assessment at this meeting is still elevated stress but not at the March 2020 peak. Corporate indebtedness is flagged. Score: 72. This reflects conditions still quite tight (impaired credit flow, tighter lending standards for small businesses) but no longer at the 85-95 crisis peak of March because of Fed intervention. Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says conditions are stressed but partially ameliorated. 72 fits.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All participants judged that it would be appropriate to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 0 to 1/4 percent." They are continuing massive QE and 13(3) lending facilities. "Some participants commented that the Committee could make its forward guidance for the path for the federal funds rate more explicit" -- discussing outcome-based or date-based forward guidance to promise even more accommodation. "Several participants remarked that a program of ongoing Treasury securities purchases could be used in the future to keep longer-term yields low." "A few participants also noted... yield curve caps." The entire discussion is about how to provide MORE accommodation, not less. This is maximum dovish. Anchor: 2-5 for March 2020. This meeting is similarly dovish -- at zero, doing QE, running 13(3) facilities, discussing even more forward guidance and yield curve control. Score: 8. The lean is not quite as emergency as March (which was 150bp cut + QE launch), but they are maintaining all of it and discussing more tools. Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence is extremely dovish. Score of 8 is correct.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All participants judged" maintaining rates. The text shows broad agreement on the assessment and policy. Discussion of forward guidance tools involves "some" and "several" and "a few" offering various ideas, but these are ideas for future consideration, not disagreements about current policy. There is no mention of dissent or opposing views on the current stance. Score: 12. Near-consensus, with very minor differences only about future tools. Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Evidence shows near-unanimity. Score of 12 is correct.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: There is a substantial multi-paragraph discussion of financial stability. "A number of participants commented on potential risks to financial stability" -- concerns about bank stress, corporate indebtedness, nonbank vulnerabilities. Stress tests discussed. "A number of participants emphasized that regulators should encourage banks to prepare for possible downside scenarios by further limiting payouts to shareholders." "A few participants stressed that the activities of some nonbank financial institutions presented vulnerabilities." This is a meaningful section but not the dominant topic of the meeting. The dominant topics are the economic collapse and policy response. Score: 60. This maps to moderate-to-prominent discussion (40-60 range, toward the upper end given multiple paragraphs and multiple participant groups discussing it). Anchor: 60-75 for 2020 recovery period. Score of 60 fits.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is strongly dovish. "Appropriate to maintain the target range... until policymakers were confident that the economy had weathered recent events and was on track to achieve the Committee's maximum employment and price stability goals." "Some participants commented that the Committee could adopt outcome-based forward guidance" or "date-based forward guidance" -- both designed to signal extended accommodation. "Several participants remarked that a program of ongoing Treasury securities purchases could be used in the future to keep longer-term yields low." "A few participants also noted... yield curve caps." All forward discussion is about more accommodation, not less. Anchor: 2-10 for 2020 emergency. Score: 8. They are discussing ways to commit even more firmly to accommodation going forward. Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence is extremely dovish forward guidance. Score of 8 is correct.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The economic effects of the pandemic created an extraordinary amount of uncertainty and considerable risks to economic activity in the medium term." "Participants discussed several alternative scenarios... that all seemed about equally likely." They describe very different possible paths -- additional waves of outbreak vs. faster recovery. "Considerable risks to the economic outlook over the medium term." This is very high uncertainty -- "extraordinary amount of uncertainty" is about as strong as it gets. Anchor: 85-95 for March 2020 emergency. This meeting still has extreme uncertainty but perhaps slightly less acute panic. Score: 85. Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. Evidence says "extraordinary amount of uncertainty." Score of 85 is correct.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:

- OUTPUT_GAP (10) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (8): Both extremely low, consistent with catastrophic recession. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (8) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (8): Both extremely dovish. Coherent -- current action is maximum accommodation and forward signal is for more/extended accommodation.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (18) low is coherent with OUTPUT_GAP (10) -- massive slack suppressing prices.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (72) elevated is coherent with the crisis context, though partially ameliorated by Fed action.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 18 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "inflation had been running below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective before the coronavirus outbreak"
- "The overall effect of the outbreak on prices was seen as disinflationary"
- "a stronger dollar and lower oil prices were factors likely to put downward pressure on inflation, and market-based measures of inflation compensation remained very low"

**Center of gravity**: Participants unanimously view inflation as well below target with multiple disinflationary forces at work; the return to 2% "would likely be further delayed."
**Minority views**: Some supply constraints noted creating "upward pressure on the prices of some goods," but this was overwhelmed by demand destruction. Narrow band reflects broad agreement.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 10 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the second quarter would likely see overall economic activity decline at an unprecedented rate"
- "the virus and the measures taken to protect public health were inducing sharp declines in economic activity"
- "In all Districts, some businesses had been forced to close temporarily"

**Center of gravity**: Participants describe a catastrophic contraction -- "unprecedented" decline in output, with the economy operating massively below potential.
**Minority views**: None on the direction; all participants agree on severe contraction. Slight variation on recovery speed informs medium-term scenarios but not current assessment.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 8 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "incoming data confirmed that an extreme decline in employment was under way"
- "initial claims for unemployment insurance benefits had totaled more than 25 million"
- "participants expected that the unemployment rate would soon reach the highest levels of the post-World War II period"

**Center of gravity**: Labor market in freefall, with historically unprecedented job losses. Concern about temporary layoffs becoming permanent and discouraged workers.
**Minority views**: None -- universal assessment of catastrophic labor market conditions.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 10 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "consumer spending had plummeted across all parts of the country and in most categories of spending"
- "business activity and investment spending had also fallen dramatically"
- "Manufacturing output declined sharply in March and was expected by participants to drop even more rapidly in April"

**Center of gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment in freefall. "Plummeted" and "fallen dramatically" leave no ambiguity.
**Minority views**: Fiscal programs providing "some relief" but not enough to alter the catastrophic assessment.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 72 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "had significantly affected financial conditions and had impaired the flow of credit to U.S. households and businesses"
- "tighter lending standards" and "fewer financing options" for small businesses
- "ongoing actions by the Federal Reserve had been instrumental in easing strains in some essential financial markets"

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions remain quite tight despite substantial improvement from March crisis peak. Credit flow impaired for many borrowers, especially small businesses, though Fed facilities and bank lending are partially offsetting.
**Minority views**: The range reflects tension between still-stressed conditions and partial improvement from Fed intervention.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 8 +/-3 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all participants judged that it would be appropriate to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 0 to 1/4 percent"
- "Some participants commented that the Committee could adopt outcome-based forward guidance" to promise extended accommodation
- "Several participants remarked that a program of ongoing Treasury securities purchases could be used in the future to keep longer-term yields low"

**Center of gravity**: Maximum dovish -- rates at zero, massive QE ongoing, 13(3) facilities running, and active discussion of additional tools (yield curve control, explicit forward guidance) to provide even more accommodation.
**Minority views**: None favoring tighter policy. Variation is only about which additional dovish tools to deploy.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 12 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all participants judged that it would be appropriate to maintain the target range"
- No mention of dissenting views or opposing positions on current policy
- Minor variation only in ideas for future tools: "some" favor explicit forward guidance, "several" discuss asset purchases, "a few" mention yield curve caps

**Center of gravity**: Near-complete consensus on both the economic assessment and the policy response. Differences are only about future tool options, not current stance.
**Minority views**: "A few participants" raised government debt concerns, but this did not translate to policy disagreement.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 60 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "A number of participants commented on potential risks to financial stability"
- "Participants were concerned that banks could come under greater stress"
- "A few participants stressed that the activities of some nonbank financial institutions presented vulnerabilities to the financial system"
- Discussion of stress tests, capital preservation, corporate indebtedness, nonbank risks

**Center of gravity**: Financial stability received meaningful attention with multiple paragraphs and multiple participant groups weighing in. It was a prominent secondary topic but not the dominant focus of the meeting (which was the economic collapse and policy response).
**Minority views**: Emphasis varied -- "a number of" participants discussed bank risks while "a few" raised nonbank concerns.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 8 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "maintain the target range... until policymakers were confident that the economy had weathered recent events and was on track to achieve the Committee's maximum employment and price stability goals"
- "Some participants commented that the Committee could adopt outcome-based forward guidance" or "date-based forward guidance"
- "A few participants also noted... yield curve caps at specified levels for a period of time"

**Center of gravity**: Forward signal is strongly dovish -- low rates until recovery confirmed, with active discussion of even stronger commitment mechanisms (outcome-based guidance, date-based guidance, yield curve control).
**Minority views**: None favoring hawkish forward signal. Variation is among different dovish tools.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 85 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the economic effects of the pandemic created an extraordinary amount of uncertainty and considerable risks to economic activity in the medium term"
- "Participants discussed several alternative scenarios... that all seemed about equally likely"
- "considerable risks to the economic outlook over the medium term"

**Center of gravity**: Participants express extreme uncertainty. "Extraordinary amount of uncertainty" is near-maximal language. Multiple equally likely scenarios ranging from protracted depression to relatively quick recovery.
**Minority views**: None -- all participants share the assessment of extreme uncertainty.

**INFLATION_GOODS**: The text mentions "some supply constraints, which had generated upward pressure on the prices of some goods" but also "reduced demand, which had exerted downward pressure on prices." Net disinflationary for goods. Score: 30 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not separately discussed as a distinct category beyond general consumption collapse. N/A.

Score: 75 +/-8 [High confidence]. Significantly stressed credit markets despite partial Fed amelioration.

**EQUITY_STRESS**: Not specifically discussed by participants in terms of equity market stress or valuation. N/A.

**BANKING_STRESS**: "banks could come under greater stress, particularly if adverse scenarios... were realized"; "high levels of indebtedness among nonfinancial corporations" exacerbating risks; stress tests important for "measuring the ability of large banks to withstand future downside scenarios"; regulators should encourage "further limiting payouts to shareholders, thereby preserving loss-absorbing capital." But also "banks had entered the crisis well capitalized." Score: 58 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]. Concern is prospective rather than realized, but meaningful enough to warrant above-neutral.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: Discussed in terms of Fed purchases supporting "smooth market functioning" and possibility of yield curve caps. Some residual stress implied by the need for continued large-scale purchases. Score: 60 +/-10 [Moderate confidence].

**DOLLAR_CONCERN**: "a stronger dollar" mentioned as a factor putting downward pressure on inflation, but not discussed as a concern in itself. N/A.
