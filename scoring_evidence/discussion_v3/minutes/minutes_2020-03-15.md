# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2020-03-15

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is **minutes** from the March 15, 2020 emergency FOMC meeting. It is a segmented document with clearly labeled "Participants' Views" and "Committee Policy Action" sections. The rubric flags this as an emergency meeting where discussion may be short/narrow.

### Era Context
March 2020: The COVID-19 pandemic was erupting globally. The U.S. economy had been on solid footing through February but was facing an unprecedented shutdown. This was the emergency meeting that brought rates to the effective lower bound (ELB). Financial markets were in "dash for cash" mode with severe Treasury and MBS market dysfunction.

### Quantifier Distribution
- "All participants" / "participants all agreed" — used for sharp deterioration in outlook, profound uncertainty, weighting on economic activity
- "Most participants" — favored 100bp cut
- "Many participants" — repeatedly downgraded outlook; pointed to funding market dislocations; reported business supply chain issues
- "Several participants" — emphasized temporary nature of shock; emphasized communications; emphasized health care capacity
- "Some participants" — noted importance of asset purchase purpose communication; noted continuing ability to provide accommodation
- "A few participants" — preferred 50bp cut; commented on discount window stigma
- "One member" — preferred 50bp cut in the vote

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants noted inflation "had been running below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective before the coronavirus outbreak." They identified a stronger dollar, weaker demand, and lower oil prices as putting further downward pressure. Market-based inflation compensation had declined. The return to 2% would be "further delayed." Direction: clearly below target and expected to fall further. This maps to the 20-30 range. The pre-crisis level was already below target (~38-42 range), and now additional deflationary forces are identified. Score around 22-25.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "All participants viewed the near-term U.S. economic outlook as having deteriorated sharply." "All saw U.S. economic activity as likely to decline in the coming quarter." The economy came in on "strong footing" through February but the pandemic was about to collapse activity. Multiple sectors already "badly hit." Businesses "would likely need to cut employment immediately." This is the onset of a catastrophic contraction. The economy had been near potential but is now expected to plunge far below. The anchor for COVID Q2 2020 is 15-22. At this exact moment (mid-March), the collapse is beginning but not yet fully realized. Score around 18-22.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "The labor market remained strong through February" and "unemployment rate had remained low." But participants noted "some firms would likely need to cut employment immediately" and workers in hard-hit sectors were severely affected. Businesses would lay off on "major scale if the downturn... perceived as likely to be protracted." The labor market WAS tight but is expected to deteriorate rapidly. Scoring the current level plus very near-term expected direction: the market is at the cusp of a massive loosening. Score around 28-32 — still reflecting that as of this meeting, unemployment is low but massive layoffs are imminent and beginning.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumption spending had been a key driver of growth... through the first two months of this year" but "the pandemic was starting to impair consumer confidence." Wide-ranging social distancing measures reducing transactions. "Pullbacks in purchases at retail stores... had reportedly intensified significantly." Business confidence hurt. Investment response depends on length/severity. Housing market "likely to be disrupted." Energy sector under stress. Direction: collapsing from a solid base. Score around 15-20 — spending is in freefall as of this meeting.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Financial markets had exhibited extraordinary turbulence and stresses." "Conditions of high volatility and illiquidity characterizing the markets for U.S. Treasury securities... and for agency MBS." "Severe illiquidity continued to prevail in key securities markets." "Acute problems that many firms were facing in issuing CP and corporate bonds." Consumer ABS spreads "widened sharply." This is crisis-level tightness. The anchor is 85-95 for March 2020 "dash for cash." Score around 90.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Most participants judged that it would be appropriate to lower the target range for the federal funds rate by 100 basis points" — to the ELB. This is emergency easing. They also agreed to purchase at least $500B in Treasuries and $200B in MBS. Discount window rate cut 150bp. Reserve requirements reduced to zero. Dollar swap lines enhanced. "A few participants preferred a 50 basis point cut" — even the dissenters wanted easing, just less. The anchor for March 2020 is 2-5. Score around 4 — this is maximum emergency dovishness with near-unanimous support for ELB plus massive asset purchases plus multiple lending facilities.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Most" favored 100bp cut. "A few" preferred 50bp. In the vote, "almost all members agreed" with one preferring 50bp. There is a minor disagreement on pace/magnitude but universal agreement on direction. "Some participants" vs "a few participants" on communications regarding asset purchases. The anchor for March 2020 is 40-50 but this meeting shows relatively narrow disagreement — everyone agrees on easing, just slight differences on magnitude and communication. Score around 30-35. One formal dissent (for 50bp instead of 100bp), some discussion differences on communications.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial conditions and market stress dominate this meeting. Treasury market dysfunction, MBS illiquidity, CP market problems, consumer ABS stress, money market fund concerns, dollar swap lines, discount window, bank lending — enormous emphasis. The anchor for March 2020 is 80-95. Score around 88.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Participants judged rate would be maintained at ELB "until policymakers were confident that the economy had weathered recent events and was on track to achieve the Committee's maximum employment and price stability goals." This is outcome-based forward guidance committing to stay at zero for an extended period. "New forward guidance or balance sheet measures could be introduced." The anchor for 2020 emergency is 2-10. Score around 5 — strong easing commitment with outcome-based conditions for lift-off.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Near-term U.S. economic outlook... had become profoundly uncertain." "All agreed... the duration of this period of weakness was uncertain." "Unpredictable effects of the coronavirus outbreak were a source of major downside risks." "Extremely large degree of uncertainty regarding how long and severe such a decline in activity would be." Staff provided two very different plausible scenarios. The anchor for March 2020 emergency is 85-95. Score around 90.

### Polarity Checks
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says below target and falling further. Score 23 (low) = correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Economy collapsing below potential. Score 18 (very low) = correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Market was tight but about to collapse. Score 30 = correct (still reflects recent tightness but imminent massive loosening).
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter conditions. Crisis-level stress. Score 90 = correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Emergency maximum easing. Score 4 = correct.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Committing to zero for extended period. Score 5 = correct.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (18) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (30): Labor is somewhat higher because the labor market was genuinely tight through February and layoffs were just beginning, while the output gap score reflects the imminent collapse. Reasonable divergence given timing.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (4) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (5): Both extremely dovish, consistent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 23 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "inflation had been running below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective before the coronavirus outbreak"
- "a stronger dollar, weaker demand, and lower oil prices were factors likely to put downward pressure on inflation in the period ahead"
- "the return of inflation to the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective would likely be further delayed"
- "Market-based measures of inflation compensation had declined"

**Center of gravity**: Participants saw inflation as already below target and expected further downward pressure from multiple forces (dollar, demand, oil). Not yet deflationary alarm, but meaningful below-target concern.

**Minority views**: None — unanimous direction on inflation weakness.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 18 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All participants viewed the near-term U.S. economic outlook as having deteriorated sharply"
- "All saw U.S. economic activity as likely to decline in the coming quarter and viewed downside risks to the economic outlook as having increased significantly"
- Multiple sectors "badly hit" — air travel, cruise lines, hotels, tourism, restaurants, energy
- "Pullbacks in purchases at retail stores... had reportedly intensified significantly"

**Center of gravity**: The economy was entering a catastrophic contraction from a position of strength. All participants agreed on sharp deterioration with significant downside risks. Activity already declining across many sectors.

**Minority views**: "Several participants emphasized that the temporary nature of the shock... implied that the current situation was not directly comparable with the previous decade's financial crisis" — suggesting potential for faster recovery, but not disputing the near-term collapse.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 30 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the labor market remained strong through February and... the unemployment rate had remained low"
- "some firms would likely need to cut employment immediately"
- "businesses would be more likely to lay off workers on a major scale if the downturn in economic activity came to be perceived as likely to be protracted"
- "workers most severely affected... were those who were ill, those with low incomes, those connected to the most hard-hit sectors"

**Center of gravity**: The labor market was still formally tight through February, but participants clearly saw imminent large-scale deterioration. The score reflects the transitional moment — tight on paper but about to collapse.

**Minority views**: Some firms "looking for ways to retain employees during the period of reduced economic activity" — suggesting not all expected immediate mass layoffs.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 17 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the pandemic was starting to impair consumer confidence and to exert an adverse effect on household balance sheets"
- "wide-ranging social-distancing measures were in operation or in prospect... would have the effect of reducing in-person transactions"
- "pullbacks in purchases at retail stores, except for emergency buying, had reportedly intensified significantly"
- "the coronavirus outbreak had inevitably hurt business confidence"
- "the housing market was likely to be disrupted by social distancing, by financial uncertainty"

**Center of gravity**: Consumer spending and business investment were in freefall. Social distancing was shutting down vast swaths of economic activity. Multiple sectors already "badly hit." Energy sector under stress.

**Minority views**: "consumption spending had been a key driver of growth... through the first two months of this year" — the recent past was solid, but the current trajectory is collapsing.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 90 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "financial markets had exhibited extraordinary turbulence and stresses"
- "conditions of high volatility and illiquidity characterizing the markets for U.S. Treasury securities... and for agency MBS"
- "severe illiquidity continued to prevail in key securities markets"
- "acute problems that many firms were facing in issuing CP and corporate bonds"
- "consumer ABS spreads widened sharply, liquidity deteriorated, and new issuance became sporadic"

**Center of gravity**: Crisis-level financial tightness across virtually all markets — Treasuries, MBS, CP, corporate bonds, consumer ABS. This is the "dash for cash" period with systemic market dysfunction.

**Minority views**: None — universal recognition of extreme stress.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 4 +/-3 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "most participants judged that it would be appropriate to lower the target range for the federal funds rate by 100 basis points, to 0 to 1/4 percent"
- Asset purchases: "increase the Federal Reserve's holdings of Treasury securities by at least $500 billion and its holdings of agency MBS by at least $200 billion"
- Discount window rate cut 150bp, reserve requirements reduced to zero, enhanced dollar swap lines
- "risk-management considerations pointed toward a forceful monetary policy response"

**Center of gravity**: Maximum emergency dovishness. 100bp cut to ELB plus massive QE plus multiple lending facility actions. This is "do whatever it takes" territory.

**Minority views**: "A few participants preferred a 50 basis point cut" — even dissenters wanted substantial easing, disagreeing only on pace.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 32 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "most participants" favored 100bp; "a few participants preferred a 50 basis point cut"
- "almost all members agreed to lower the target range... One member preferred to lower the target range by 50 basis points"
- "Some participants noted that it was important to stress... that the primary purpose of these asset purchases was to support the smooth functioning... rather than to provide further monetary policy accommodation"
- Communications concerns: "some participants remarked that the Committee's policy actions... could be interpreted as conveying negative news"

**Center of gravity**: One formal dissent (for 50bp vs 100bp). Universal agreement on direction of easing. Minor differences on communications strategy and framing of asset purchases. Moderate but not deep disagreement.

**Minority views**: The 50bp camp had substantive reasoning (preserve ammunition, avoid negative signal, ensure transmission mechanism working).

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 88 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Extensive discussion of Treasury and MBS market dysfunction, CP market stress, corporate bond market, consumer ABS, money market funds, government bond funds
- Multiple lending facility actions discussed (discount window, swap lines, reserve requirements)
- "participants expressed concern about the disruptions to the functioning of these markets, especially in view of their status as cornerstones for the operation of the U.S. and global financial systems"
- Asset purchases framed primarily as market-functioning support

**Center of gravity**: Financial stability and market functioning were a dominant topic of this meeting, rivaling the economic assessment. Multiple paragraphs devoted to specific market dislocations and policy responses to address them.

**Minority views**: None.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 5 +/-3 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "it would be appropriate to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 0 to 1/4 percent until policymakers were confident that the economy had weathered recent events and was on track to achieve the Committee's maximum employment and price stability goals"
- "new forward guidance or balance sheet measures could be introduced" — signaling willingness to do even more
- "the Committee would be prepared to increase the size of the securities purchases, as needed"

**Center of gravity**: Outcome-based forward guidance committing to ELB for an extended period, with openness to further easing measures. This is near the maximum dovish end of forward guidance.

**Minority views**: "A few participants also remarked that lowering the target range to the ELB could... foster investor expectations of negative policy rates" — suggesting some caution about forward signaling.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 90 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All participants viewed the near-term U.S. economic outlook as having... become profoundly uncertain"
- "the extremely large degree of uncertainty regarding how long and severe such a decline in activity would be"
- "the unpredictable effects of the coronavirus outbreak were a source of major downside risks"
- Staff provided two very different plausible scenarios spanning a wide range
- "such an uncertain and rapidly evolving environment"

**Center of gravity**: Extreme uncertainty — "profoundly uncertain" used by all participants. The duration, severity, and path of the crisis were unknowable. Multiple scenarios with vastly different outcomes discussed.

**Minority views**: None — universal expression of extreme uncertainty.
