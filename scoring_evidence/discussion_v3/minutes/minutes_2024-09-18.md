# FOMC Scoring Output — September 17-18, 2024

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented — participant discussion is clearly separated from staff material.

**Era context**: September 2024. Inflation had been declining from the 2022 peak but remained "somewhat elevated." The labor market had been cooling from historically tight levels. This was the meeting where the Committee cut rates by 50 basis points — the first cut of the easing cycle. The macro backdrop is one of disinflation progress, a labor market moving toward balance, and a pivot to easing.

**Quantifier distribution**: Frequent use of "almost all," "many," "several," "some," "a few," and "a couple." "All participants agreed" on rate cut. "A substantial majority" supported 50bp. "Some" preferred or could have supported 25bp.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as somewhat above target but declining. "Inflation remained somewhat elevated" is used repeatedly. "Almost all participants judged that recent monthly readings had been consistent with inflation returning sustainably to 2 percent." Core goods prices declined; nonhousing services prices moved down further. Housing services prices still rising at an elevated rate. "Almost all participants indicated they had gained greater confidence that inflation was moving sustainably toward 2 percent." Direction: modestly above target, declining. The "somewhat elevated" language places this clearly in the 51-60 range, but the strong disinflation progress and confidence in return to target pulls it toward the lower end. Score ~53. Polarity check: higher = more inflation pressure. "Somewhat elevated" = some pressure remains. 53 fits.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic activity had continued to expand at a solid pace." "Many participants emphasized that they expected that real GDP would grow at roughly its trend rate over the next few years." The economy is described as solid, not overheating and not below potential. "At roughly its trend rate" = near potential. However, "solid pace" with "favorable aggregate supply developments, including increases in productivity" suggests the economy is operating near or just slightly above potential. Score ~51. Polarity check: higher = more above potential. Near trend = near 50. 51 fits.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: This is a key dimension for this meeting. "Labor market conditions had eased further in recent months and that, after being overheated in recent years, the labor market was now less tight than it had been just before the pandemic." "Participants agreed that labor market conditions were at, or close to, those consistent with the Committee's longer-run goal of maximum employment." Payroll growth slowed, unemployment rate upticked, vacancies reduced, hiring slowed. However, "layoffs had been limited and initial claims for unemployment insurance benefits had stayed low." The labor market is described as roughly in balance — no longer tight, not yet loose. Direction: at or near balance. Score ~48. The balance language and "at or close to maximum employment" puts this near 50, but the easing direction, rising unemployment, and risk of "more serious deterioration" tilts slightly below. Polarity check: higher = tighter. Market is near balance trending looser = slightly below 50. 48 fits.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Economic activity had continued to expand at a solid pace and highlighted resilient consumption spending." Rising real household incomes bolstered consumption. But "some cited signs of a slowing in expenditures or of strains on household budgets, including increased delinquencies in credit card and automobile loans." Business contacts "were optimistic about the economic outlook, though they were exercising caution in their hiring and investment decisions." Net: solid but with some mixed signals. Direction: at or slightly above trend. Score ~52. Polarity check: higher = stronger. Solid/resilient = slightly above trend. 52 fits.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: The document discusses financial conditions indirectly. Participants discuss the "restrictive monetary policy stance" and that policy was restrictive. "Some participants cited risks that progress toward the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective could be stalled by a larger-than-expected easing in financial conditions." "They expressed a range of views about the degree of restrictiveness." The overall sense is that financial conditions are somewhat tight/restrictive due to the high policy rate. Score ~60. Polarity check: higher = tighter conditions. Restrictive stance with some participants worried about easing in conditions = somewhat tight. 60 fits.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All participants agreed that it was appropriate to ease the stance of monetary policy." "A substantial majority of participants supported lowering the target range for the federal funds rate by 50 basis points." This is clearly dovish — an active easing move, and a large one (50bp). However, "some participants observed that they would have preferred a 25 basis point reduction" and "several participants noted that a 25 basis point reduction would be in line with a gradual path." Participants emphasized this was "recalibration," not emergency easing. "If the data came in about as expected... it would likely be appropriate to move toward a more neutral stance of policy over time." There is also caution: "reducing policy restraint too soon or too much could risk a stalling or a reversal of the progress on inflation." Direction: moderate dovish. This is an easing action but framed as recalibration, not emergency. Score ~33. Polarity check: higher = more hawkish. Easing 50bp = dovish. 33 fits (moderate dovish range 26-40).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: A substantial majority supported 50bp but "some participants observed that they would have preferred a 25 basis point reduction... and a few others indicated that they could have supported such a decision." There is one actual dissent (Governor Bowman for 25bp, though that's from the vote section not explicitly here). The discussion shows meaningful debate between 25 and 50, with several making the case for 25. But the range of disagreement is about magnitude, not direction — everyone agreed on easing. Score ~38. This reflects notable but bounded disagreement about pace, not direction.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Limited direct discussion of financial stability. Mentions of "easing in financial conditions" as a risk, and brief credit card/auto loan delinquency mentions. No extended discussion of asset prices, systemic risk, or financial stability as a standalone topic. Score ~22. Brief mentions only.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "If the data came in about as expected, with inflation moving down sustainably to 2 percent and the economy near maximum employment, it would likely be appropriate to move toward a more neutral stance of policy over time." This is a clear easing lean going forward, but data-dependent and gradual. "The Committee's monetary policy decisions are conditional on the evolution of the economy... and therefore not on a preset course." "The overall path of policy normalization, rather than the specific amount of initial easing at this meeting, would be more important." Direction: easing lean with data dependence. Score ~32. Polarity check: higher = more hawkish forward signal. Easing bias going forward = below 50. 32 fits (easing lean with data dependence range 26-40).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants discussed the risks and uncertainties associated with the economic outlook." "Many participants observed that the evaluation of labor market developments had been challenging, with increased immigration, revisions to reported payroll data, and possible changes in the underlying growth rate of productivity cited as complicating factors." "Some participants noted that uncertainties concerning the level of the longer-term neutral rate of interest complicated the assessment of the degree of restrictiveness of policy." Uncertainty is elevated — labor market data hard to read, neutral rate uncertain. But not extreme. Score ~48. Polarity check: higher = more uncertainty. Elevated but not extreme. 48 fits.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (51) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (48): Slight divergence is explained — the economy is still growing solidly (near/slightly above trend) but the labor market has cooled to roughly balanced. This is consistent with a supply-side improvement narrative (productivity gains allowing growth without labor market pressure).
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (33) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (32): Well aligned — moderate dovish action with moderate dovish forward guidance. Both reflect measured easing.

### Optional dimensions:
- **INFLATION_GOODS**: Discussed — "core goods prices had declined in recent months." Declining goods prices = below 50. Score ~35.
- **INFLATION_SERVICES**: Discussed — "the rate of increase in core nonhousing services prices had moved down further." Still positive but decelerating. Score ~48.
- **INFLATION_HOUSING**: Discussed — "even though the rate of increase in housing services prices had slowed, these prices were continuing to rise at an elevated rate." Score ~62.
- **INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Well-anchored inflation expectations" cited as a factor. Score ~48 (anchored near target, perhaps slightly below given disinflationary context).
- **CREDIT_STRESS**: Brief mention of "increased delinquencies in credit card and automobile loans" and strains on low/moderate-income households. Modest stress. Score ~55.
- **EQUITY_STRESS**: N/A — no meaningful discussion of equity markets.
- **BANKING_STRESS**: N/A — no discussion.
- **TREASURY_STRESS**: N/A — no discussion.
- **DOLLAR_CONCERN**: N/A — no discussion.
- **PRODUCTIVITY**: Discussed — "increases in productivity had contributed to the recent solid expansion" and "a few participants discussed possible implications of the introduction of new technology into the workplace." Above-trend productivity. Score ~60.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 53 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Inflation remained somewhat elevated" (repeated by participants)
- "Almost all participants indicated they had gained greater confidence that inflation was moving sustainably toward 2 percent"
- "Core goods prices had declined in recent months, and the rate of increase in core nonhousing services prices had moved down further"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants see inflation as modestly above target but making clear progress toward 2%, with broad confidence in the disinflation trend.

**Minority views**: "Participants emphasized that inflation remained somewhat elevated and that they were strongly committed to returning inflation to the Committee's 2 percent objective" — some emphasis on the remaining gap.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 51 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity had continued to expand at a solid pace"
- "Many participants emphasized that they expected that real GDP would grow at roughly its trend rate over the next few years"
- "Favorable aggregate supply developments, including increases in productivity, had contributed to the recent solid expansion"

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is operating near potential, with solid growth roughly at trend, supported by productivity gains.

**Minority views**: None significant — broad agreement on solid growth at trend.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 48 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "After being overheated in recent years, the labor market was now less tight than it had been just before the pandemic"
- "Participants agreed that labor market conditions were at, or close to, those consistent with the Committee's longer-run goal of maximum employment"
- "The risk had increased that continued easing could transition to a more serious deterioration"

**Center-of-gravity**: The labor market is roughly in balance — no longer tight, not yet loose — but with downside risks noted.

**Minority views**: "Some participants highlighted the fact that the unemployment rate had risen notably, on net, since April 2023" — a few see more concern about loosening.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 52 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity had continued to expand at a solid pace and highlighted resilient consumption spending"
- "Rising real household incomes had bolstered consumption, though some cited signs of a slowing in expenditures or of strains on household budgets"
- Business contacts "were optimistic about the economic outlook, though they were exercising caution in their hiring and investment decisions"

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumption is resilient and solid, near or slightly above trend, with some mixed signals from lower-income households and cautious business investment.

**Minority views**: "A couple of participants suggested that the financial strains being experienced by low- and moderate-income households would likely imply slower consumption growth in coming periods."

**Evidence**:

- "Some participants cited risks that progress toward the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective could be stalled by a larger-than-expected easing in financial conditions"
- "Uncertainties concerning the level of the longer-term neutral rate of interest complicated the assessment of the degree of restrictiveness of policy"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are viewed as somewhat tight/restrictive, though there is uncertainty about the degree.

**Minority views**: Range of views on exact degree of restrictiveness.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 33 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All participants agreed that it was appropriate to ease the stance of monetary policy"
- "A substantial majority of participants supported lowering the target range for the federal funds rate by 50 basis points"
- "Some participants observed that they would have preferred a 25 basis point reduction of the target range at this meeting"

**Center-of-gravity**: Clearly dovish — unanimous agreement to ease, with a substantial majority favoring the larger 50bp cut framed as recalibration to align with economic conditions.

**Minority views**: A meaningful minority (some + a few others) preferred or could support 25bp, arguing for a more gradual approach. This raises the score slightly from the deep-dovish zone.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 38 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "A substantial majority of participants supported lowering the target range for the federal funds rate by 50 basis points" — but not unanimous on size
- "Some participants observed that they would have preferred a 25 basis point reduction... and a few others indicated that they could have supported such a decision"
- "Several participants noted that a 25 basis point reduction would be in line with a gradual path of policy normalization"

**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate disagreement about the pace of easing (25 vs. 50bp), but full agreement on the direction. The debate is tactical, not directional.

**Minority views**: The 25bp camp is sizable enough ("some" + "a few others") to represent a notable minority.
