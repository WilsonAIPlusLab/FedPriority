# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1999-12-21

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). This is an unsegmented pre-2009 minutes document, so I must focus on participant-attributed language ("members commented," "members noted," "several members," etc.) and ignore staff-reported content.

**Era context**: December 1999, late-cycle expansion. The U.S. economy was in the midst of the dot-com boom with strong GDP growth, very tight labor markets (unemployment at 4.1%), rising equity prices, and Y2K transition concerns. The Fed had already tightened earlier in 1999 and was holding steady at this meeting partly due to Y2K uncertainty.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all the members" (endorsed hold), "most of the members" (symmetry preference), "a few members" (asymmetric preference), "several members" (labor constraints, current account), "a number of members" (inflation concern), "some" (various factors). Strong consensus language throughout.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Members describe "relatively subdued wage and price inflation" currently, but significant forward-looking concern. "A number of members expressed concern that the anticipated moderation in overall demand might not be large enough or soon enough to forestall added pressures on already-taut labor markets." Energy prices reversing, import prices rising, medical costs increasing. But "no evidence of any erosion in the widespread expectation that inflation would remain subdued." Current inflation is benign but risks are clearly tilted upward. Direction: slightly above neutral, with upside risks recognized but not yet manifesting. Score around 52-55.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Very clear language: "the economy clearly would carry substantial expansionary momentum into the new year, quite possibly in excess of growth in the economy's long-run potential." "The increase in aggregate demand had been exceeding even the now-higher sustainable rate of growth in aggregate supply." This is unambiguous above-potential language. Per bright-line rule, must be > 50. "Substantial expansionary momentum" and "in excess of" potential pushes this into the 59-65 range. Score ~62.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Declines in the pool of available but unemployed workers to a very low level." "High levels of business activity were severely taxing available labor resources and appeared to be constraining growth in a number of industries." "Already-taut labor markets." Unemployment at 4.1%. This is clearly very tight. Anchor for 1999-2000 is 65-75. Score ~70.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Notably brisk retail sales during the current holiday season." "Capital goods markets also displayed very little evidence of any weakening." "Demand for more conventional equipment...also was relatively robust." "Further rapid growth" in capital investment expected. Housing softening somewhat but still "high level." This is clearly above trend -- robust consumption and strong investment. Score ~63.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Limited direct discussion of financial conditions. Equity markets at new highs, "ready availability of financing for most homebuyers," rising mortgage rates mentioned. The concern is about Y2K liquidity, and the Committee wants to "foster steady conditions in financial markets." Equity markets rising, credit freely available for homebuyers. Conditions appear loose to neutral. Rising mortgage rates add some tightness. Net assessment: somewhat loose, around 35.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All members voted to hold at 5.5%. But "the Committee's primary near-term objective was to foster steady conditions in financial markets during the period of the century date change." Members "generally agreed that...their concerns about rising inflation could be addressed at the meeting in early February." Most preferred symmetric directive but acknowledged "a preemptive tightening move might be warranted in the not-too-distant future." This is a hold with a clear tightening bias -- they are holding only because of Y2K. Score ~60-63.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Unanimous vote (no dissents). The only disagreement was about symmetric vs. asymmetric directive -- "most" preferred symmetric, "a few" preferred asymmetric, but even the dissenters "could readily accept a symmetrical directive." Very low disagreement. Score ~15.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Y2K concerns dominate the policy discussion -- wanting to "avoid any action that might erode the markets' confidence." Stock market mentioned as a wealth effect driver. "Several members indicated their concern about the burgeoning current account deficit and the potential that it could lead to a considerable weakening of the dollar." Some attention to financial conditions but not dominant. Score ~30.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Clear forward tightening signal: "a preemptive tightening move might be warranted in the not-too-distant future to help contain inflationary pressures." "Concerns about rising inflation could be addressed at the meeting in early February." The symmetric directive was chosen for tactical Y2K reasons, not because the outlook was balanced. Forward lean is clearly hawkish. Score ~65.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The extent of the moderation remained subject to a wide range of uncertainty." "Uncertainties about the level and growth of potential output and the dynamics of the inflation process made it difficult to relate with confidence projections of demand and activity to prospects for inflation." Multiple sources of uncertainty cited -- stock market effects, productivity trends, potential output. Somewhat elevated. Score ~42.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (62) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70): Both above 50, consistent with late-boom economy. Labor somewhat higher because of specific "severely taxing" language. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (62) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (65): Both lean hawkish, with forward tone slightly more so since the hold was purely tactical (Y2K). Coherent.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says current inflation subdued but risks rising. 53 = slightly above midpoint. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says "in excess of potential." 62 = clearly above. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says conditions generally accommodative, credit available. 35 = somewhat loose. Matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 53 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "relatively subdued wage and price inflation" (current conditions benign)
- "a number of members expressed concern that the anticipated moderation in overall demand might not be large enough or soon enough to forestall added pressures on already-taut labor markets"
- "some of the forces that had been restraining inflation--declining oil, import, and commodity prices, and subdued increases in the costs of health care--had already reversed"
- "there had been no evidence of any erosion in the widespread expectation that inflation would remain subdued over the long run"

**Center-of-gravity**: Current inflation is subdued but members perceive growing upside risks from tight labor, energy reversal, and dollar depreciation; net slightly above neutral.
**Minority views**: None strongly dissenting on inflation assessment; the range is about timing/severity of future pressures.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the economy clearly would carry substantial expansionary momentum into the new year, quite possibly in excess of growth in the economy's long-run potential"
- "the increase in aggregate demand had been exceeding even the now-higher sustainable rate of growth in aggregate supply"
- "declines in the pool of available but unemployed workers to a very low level"

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy clearly running above potential with demand exceeding supply growth despite upward revisions to potential.
**Minority views**: None; this was consensus.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 70 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "high levels of business activity were severely taxing available labor resources and appeared to be constraining growth in a number of industries and parts of the country"
- "already-taut labor markets"
- "declines in the pool of available but unemployed workers to a very low level" (unemployment at 4.1%)
- "scarcity of skilled construction workers"

**Center-of-gravity**: Very tight labor market with shortages constraining output in multiple sectors; consistent with era anchor of 65-75 for 1999-2000.
**Minority views**: None; universal recognition of tightness.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 63 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Anecdotal reports pointed to notably brisk retail sales during the current holiday season in many parts of the country"
- "The capital goods markets also displayed very little evidence of any weakening...demand for more conventional equipment...also was relatively robust"
- "Assessments of the outlook for overall business capital investment pointed to further rapid growth led by outlays for equipment"
- Housing softening "somewhat" but still at a "high level"

**Center-of-gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment running well above trend; only housing showing modest softening.
**Minority views**: Some noted vulnerability of consumer spending to stock market decline, but no one characterized spending as weak.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 35 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the advance in stock market prices to new highs in recent weeks"
- "the ready availability of financing for most homebuyers"
- "Rising mortgage rates" cited as dampening housing somewhat
- Y2K concern about maintaining "steady conditions in financial markets"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions generally accommodative with equity markets buoyant and credit available; mortgage rate increases provide some modest tightening offset.
**Minority views**: Limited discussion; uncertainty band reflects sparse direct evidence on financial conditions per se.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all the members endorsed a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance" (hold)
- "the Committee's primary near-term objective was to foster steady conditions in financial markets during the period of the century date change" (tactical reason for hold)
- "they saw little risk of a significant acceleration in inflation over the near term...and thus little cost in deferring consideration of a policy tightening action"
- "a preemptive tightening move might be warranted in the not-too-distant future"
- "a few members indicated a marginal preference for an asymmetric directive"

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous hold driven by Y2K tactical considerations, not dovish conviction; clear consensus that tightening is the likely next move, just deferred.
**Minority views**: A few wanted asymmetric (more hawkish signal) but accepted symmetric.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 15 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Votes against this action: None"
- Directive debate: "most of the members expressed a preference for retaining the symmetry" vs. "a few members indicated a marginal preference for an asymmetric directive" but "they could readily accept a symmetrical directive"

**Center-of-gravity**: Near-unanimous on both the rate decision and the directive; the only minor difference was tactical preference on directive language.
**Minority views**: The "few" preferring asymmetry is a minor tactical difference, not a substantive disagreement.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 30 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Y2K liquidity concerns: "avoid any action that might erode the markets' confidence that the Federal Reserve was fully prepared to provide whatever liquidity would be needed"
- Stock market wealth effects discussed: "spending could be vulnerable to adverse developments in the stock market"
- "several members indicated their concern about the burgeoning current account deficit and the potential that it could lead to a considerable weakening of the dollar"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability/conditions discussed meaningfully in context of Y2K and stock market wealth effects, but not as a dominant standalone topic. Limited discussion of systemic risk or market functioning beyond Y2K logistics.
**Minority views**: None.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 65 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "a preemptive tightening move might be warranted in the not-too-distant future to help contain inflationary pressures in the economy"
- "concerns about rising inflation could be addressed at the meeting in early February"
- "a few members indicated a marginal preference for an asymmetric directive that focused on the possibility of an eventual rise in interest rates"

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear forward hawkish lean; the symmetric directive was chosen for Y2K tactical reasons while the press statement would convey the inflation concern. February tightening clearly on the table.
**Minority views**: None disagreed with the forward direction; only the signaling mechanism was debated.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 42 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the extent of the moderation remained subject to a wide range of uncertainty related in part to the difficulty of anticipating trends in stock market prices"
- "Uncertainties about the level and growth of potential output and the dynamics of the inflation process made it difficult to relate with confidence projections of demand and activity to prospects for inflation"
- Y2K transition itself an uncertainty factor

**Center-of-gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty, focused on potential output measurement, stock market trajectory, and the inflation process dynamics. Not crisis-level but clearly above routine.
**Minority views**: Broadly shared sense of uncertainty about specific factors; no one expressed high confidence in forecasts.
