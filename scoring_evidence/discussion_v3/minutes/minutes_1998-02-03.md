# FOMC Meeting Scoring — 1998-02-03 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Unsegmented (pre-2009), so I must score ONLY portions with participant language cues ("members noted," "members commented," etc.) and ignore staff-attributable content.

**Era context**: Early 1998. The U.S. economy is in the late stages of the 1990s expansion. Asian financial crisis is unfolding. Unemployment is near cycle lows (~4.7%). Inflation remains subdued despite tight labor markets — the "new economy" productivity story. Fed funds rate at 5.5%.

**Quantifier inventory from the text**:
- "members" (collective, used frequently — implies broad agreement)
- "all the members" — unanimous on hold and symmetric directive
- "many emphasized" — Asian drag as key factor
- "some members" — various specific views (discount ECI, consumer sector, profit trends)
- "a number of members" — next move likely tightening
- "one member" — saw greater probability of easing

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Members observed "price inflation had remained subdued, and by some measures had declined." Despite "increasingly tight labor markets" and "faster increases in labor compensation," productivity gains kept unit labor costs "at a very modest level" and overall unit cost increases "not far above zero." Declining oil prices, dollar appreciation, restrained health costs all helped. Longer-run outlook "more clouded and under some scenarios less promising." Direction: Below midpoint — inflation is subdued now, with some upside risk longer-term. The current perception is clearly below target. Score ~38. The longer-run worry nudges it up slightly from pure "subdued" territory.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economy had entered the new year with considerable momentum" at "what appeared to be an unsustainable rate." Economy performing "very well." Growth expected to slow to "vicinity of current estimates of the economy's long-run growth potential." This is clearly above potential currently — "unsustainable rate" is strong language. Expected to move toward potential. The bright-line rule: "unsustainable rate" = above potential, score MUST be > 50. Score ~58. The economy is running hot now but expected to decelerate.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Very tight labor markets," "increasingly tight labor markets," "tightness in labor markets," unemployment at 4.7% "near its low for the current economic expansion." "Faster increases in labor compensation." "Rising labor compensation associated with tight labor markets." Direction: clearly tight. Multiple references to tightness. Score ~65. Consistent with late-1990s anchor of 65-75.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending strong — "consumer sentiment at or close to historically high levels," "upbeat anecdotal reports of retail sales," "did not rule out a more ebullient consumer sector." Business fixed investment "expected to provide substantial support," though "some moderation" likely. Residential construction "remained relatively robust." Overall picture is well above trend currently with expected moderation. Score ~62 — strong but with expected slowing.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Generally accommodative financial conditions," "debt and equity financing continued to be readily available on attractive terms," "favorable financing conditions," "lower nominal and perhaps real intermediate and long-term interest rates, rising equity prices, ready credit availability, and rapid growth of the broad measures of money and credit." On the other hand, "a real federal funds rate that was on the high side of historical experience and a substantially stronger dollar suggested some restraint." Net: conditions are loose/accommodative. Score ~25 — clearly accommodative, with minor tightening from dollar/real rates.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All the members endorsed a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance." Symmetric directive — no presumption about direction. "A number of members expressed the view that the next policy move was likely to be a tightening action and one member saw a greater probability of an easing action." Key reasoning: "steady policy would not incur an unacceptable risk" and "the greater risk would be to make a preemptive policy move on the basis of inadequate evidence." This is a hold with a very mild tilt toward future tightening (a number lean that way vs. one toward easing). Score ~52 — essentially balanced with a slight hawkish lean.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All the members" endorsed hold. Votes: unanimous (0 dissents) on both monetary ranges and policy directive. "While the members differed to some extent in their forecasts" — some differences acknowledged but not sharp. One member saw easing as more likely vs. a number seeing tightening. Score ~22 — broad agreement with minor forecast differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Asian financial turmoil discussed at length as major uncertainty for the outlook, but framed primarily as a trade/growth channel rather than financial stability per se. Stock market wealth effects mentioned. "Emerging signs of speculative overbuilding in some areas." Financial conditions discussed as part of policy transmission. Moderate attention. Score ~35 — some meaningful discussion but not a dominant theme.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Symmetric directive — "slightly higher or slightly lower federal funds rate might be acceptable." "A number of members" thought next move likely tightening, one thought easing. No directional signal given. Data-dependent posture. Score ~53 — essentially neutral with the slightest hawkish lean given the asymmetry in member views about likely next move.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Forecasts were subject to a great deal of uncertainty since there was little precedent to guide them." "Risks of a considerable deviation on the upside or the downside...were unusually high." "The eventual effects of the Asian financial turmoil...were unknown." "Prevailing uncertainties." "Range of possible outcomes was unusually wide." Very strong uncertainty language throughout. Score ~62 — elevated to high, with "unusually" and "great deal" and "unknown" language.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (58) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (65): Both above 50, consistent. Labor tightness higher because labor market described as "very tight" while output gap is above potential but expected to slow — reasonable.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (52) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (53): Both essentially balanced with slight hawkish lean. Consistent. Makes sense given unanimous hold with symmetric directive but mild tightening expectation.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (38): Higher = more pressure. Evidence says subdued/declining. 38 < 50. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP (58): Higher = more above potential. "Unsustainable rate." 58 > 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (25): Higher = tighter. "Accommodative," "readily available." 25 < 50. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 38 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Price inflation had remained subdued, and by some measures had declined, in recent months despite very tight labor markets"
- "Overall unit cost increases had remained not far above zero"
- "The longer-run outlook for inflation was more clouded and under some scenarios less promising" — factors holding down inflation "were not likely to continue to exert benign effects"

**Center of gravity**: Members see current inflation as clearly subdued, but acknowledge longer-run risks from tight labor markets and fading temporary disinflationary forces. Net position is below target with upside risk.

**Minority views**: Some members discounted the sharp ECI increase; longer-run upside risk acknowledged broadly but not dominant.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 58 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The economy had entered the new year with considerable momentum and very few indications that growth was moderating from what appeared to be an unsustainable rate"
- Expected to slow "possibly to a pace in the vicinity of current estimates of the economy's long-run growth potential"
- "The economy currently was performing very well"

**Center of gravity**: Economy is clearly running above potential currently, with broad expectation of deceleration toward potential driven by Asian drag and inventory adjustment.

**Minority views**: "Some members expressed reservations...about the extent of any weakening in inventory accumulation" — suggesting the economy might stay above potential longer.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 65 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Very tight labor markets and indications of somewhat faster increases in labor compensation"
- "Widespread indications of increasingly tight labor markets"
- Unemployment at 4.7%, "near its low for the current economic expansion"

**Center of gravity**: Unanimous view that labor markets are very tight. No member disputes this characterization.

**Minority views**: None — all agree markets are tight; differences are about whether tightness will translate into inflation.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 62 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Consumer sentiment was at or close to historically high levels"; "did not rule out a more ebullient consumer sector"
- "Business fixed investment also was expected to provide substantial support to continued economic expansion"
- "Residential construction activity had remained relatively robust"

**Center of gravity**: All three components — consumption, business investment, and housing — are above trend. Expected moderation but from very high levels.

**Minority views**: "Early signs of faltering profit trends in some industries" and "emerging signs of speculative overbuilding in some areas" introduce caution from some members.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 25 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Generally accommodative financial conditions"
- "Debt and equity financing continued to be readily available on attractive terms to most business borrowers"
- "Financial conditions seemed to be quite stimulative as evidenced by lower nominal and perhaps real intermediate and long-term interest rates, rising equity prices, ready credit availability"

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly accommodative/loose. Some offset from high real fed funds rate and strong dollar, but net assessment is stimulative.

**Minority views**: Mixed signals acknowledged — real fed funds rate "on the high side" vs. overall stimulative conditions.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 52 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All the members endorsed a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance"
- "A number of members expressed the view that the next policy move was likely to be a tightening action and one member saw a greater probability of an easing action"
- "A steady policy would not incur an unacceptable risk"; "the greater risk would be to make a preemptive policy move on the basis of inadequate evidence"

**Center of gravity**: Firmly on hold. Slight tilt toward future tightening bias among members, but strong commitment to patience given uncertainty.

**Minority views**: One member leaned toward easing; a number leaned toward future tightening. But all agreed to hold now.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 22 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All the members endorsed a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance"
- "All also favored a directive that did not include a presumption about the direction of a change"
- Unanimous votes on both monetary ranges and policy directive

**Center of gravity**: Near-consensus. Members differed somewhat in forecasts and directional lean, but all agreed on action and symmetric directive.

**Minority views**: Minor differences in forecast and directional lean (one for easing vs. a number for tightening) but not enough to generate dissent.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 35 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Extended discussion of "crises in a number of Asian economies" and "Asian financial turmoil" — though primarily framed as growth/trade impact
- "Emerging signs of speculative overbuilding in some areas"
- Discussion of financial conditions as stimulative — equity prices, credit availability

**Center of gravity**: Financial stability topics receive moderate attention, mainly through the Asian crisis lens and observations about loose conditions/equity valuations. Not a dominant concern but more than passing reference.

**Minority views**: None notable.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 53 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "A slightly higher or a slightly lower federal funds rate might be acceptable" — symmetric directive
- "A number of members expressed the view that the next policy move was likely to be a tightening action"
- "The uncertainties were sufficiently great to warrant remaining sensitive to the need for a policy change in either direction"

**Center of gravity**: Essentially neutral forward signal via symmetric directive, with a very slight tilt toward tightening based on the balance of member views about likely next move.

**Minority views**: One member saw easing as more likely.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 62 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Forecasts were subject to a great deal of uncertainty since there was little precedent to guide them"
- "The risks of a considerable deviation on the upside or the downside of their current forecasts were unusually high"
- "The range of possible outcomes was unusually wide, and the direction that policy would need to move...was unclear"

**Center of gravity**: Uncertainty is clearly elevated and explicitly characterized as unusual. Asian crisis creates two-sided risks that members find very difficult to assess.

**Minority views**: None — uncertainty is broadly shared.
