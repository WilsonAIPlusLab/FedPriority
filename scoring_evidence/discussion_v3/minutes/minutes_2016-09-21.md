## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 40 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Inflation had continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective, partly reflecting earlier declines in energy prices and in prices of non-energy imports." "Many members remarked that there were few signs of emerging inflationary pressures or that progress on inflation had been slow." "12-month core PCE price inflation had been running at a steady rate below 2 percent."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Inflation persistently below target with limited pressure, but expected to gradually return to 2%. Not alarming but not at target.
- **Minority views**: "A couple of other members pointed to recent readings on core CPI inflation as suggesting that PCE price inflation was close to meeting the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective." This pulls slightly upward.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 48 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Growth of economic activity had picked up from the modest pace seen in the first half of the year." "Some slack likely remaining in the labor market." "Some other participants believed that the economy was at or near full employment." "The economy was growing only modestly above trend."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Economy near potential with slight remaining slack. A substantial debate exists between those seeing full employment and those seeing remaining slack.
- **Minority views**: Some see the economy at full employment (would push above 50); others see meaningful slack remaining (would push below 48).

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 49 ±6 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Participants generally agreed that labor market conditions had improved appreciably over the course of the year, with monthly payroll gains averaging about 180,000." "Some participants... judged that the labor market had little or no remaining slack." "Some others noted that still-muted wage growth, a level of involuntary part-time employment that remained elevated... indicated that slack remained."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Labor market near full employment but with genuine debate about remaining slack. The split between "some" saying no slack and "some others" saying slack remains places the center near 49.
- **Minority views**: Both directions well-represented; wider uncertainty band reflects the active debate.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 48 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Household spending had been growing strongly but business fixed investment had remained soft." "Growth in consumer spending appeared to have moderated somewhat in the third quarter from its rapid second-quarter pace." "Manufacturing activity remained flat." "Sluggishness in the housing sector appeared to have continued."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Strong consumer spending offset by weak business investment, flat manufacturing, and sluggish housing. Net picture is roughly at trend.
- **Minority views**: Consumer side is clearly above trend; investment side clearly below. The net is near balance.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS

- **Evidence**: "U.S. financial conditions were broadly accommodative." "Financial conditions remained accommodative." "Global financial conditions had improved somewhat in recent months."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Conditions clearly accommodative/loose. Well below neutral tightness.
- **Minority views**: "A few participants expressed concern that the protracted period of very low interest rates might be encouraging excessive borrowing" — this flags conditions as too loose, consistent with the low score.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 55 ±8 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Members generally agreed that the case for an increase in the policy rate had strengthened." "A majority of members judged that the Committee should, for the time being, await further evidence." "Three members preferred to raise the target range for the federal funds rate by 25 basis points at this meeting." "Several stated that the decision at this meeting was a close call."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Lean hawkish — case for hiking acknowledged by all, but majority wants to wait a bit more. Close call for many.
- **Minority views**: Three members wanted to hike now (more hawkish, ~65-70); some others wanted more convincing evidence on inflation before hiking (more dovish, ~45-48).

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 55 ±7 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Three members preferred to raise the target range for the federal funds rate by 25 basis points at this meeting." "A reasonable argument could be made either for an increase at this meeting or for waiting." "Several stated that the decision at this meeting was a close call." Differing views on labor slack and inflation also evident.
- **Center-of-gravity**: Notable disagreement with 3 formal dissents and distinct camps on timing. However, the disagreement is about timing/pace rather than fundamental direction — all agreed the case for tightening had strengthened.
- **Minority views**: The 3 dissenters represent a meaningful minority with clear policy prescriptions.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

- **Center-of-gravity**: Limited discussion — a few participants raised financial stability concerns briefly. Not a major topic.
- **Minority views**: The few who raised it were concerned about low-rate risks, but it was not taken up broadly.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 62 ±6 [High confidence]**

"Some participants believed that it would be appropriate to raise the target range for the federal funds rate relatively soon if the labor market continued to improve." "Participants generally expected that, with gradual adjustments in the stance of monetary policy, economic activity would expand at a moderate pace."

- **Minority views**: "Some others preferred to wait for more convincing evidence that inflation was moving toward the Committee's 2 percent objective" — this pulls the forward signal slightly less hawkish.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
