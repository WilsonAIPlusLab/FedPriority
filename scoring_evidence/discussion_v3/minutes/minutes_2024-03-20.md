# FOMC Discussion Scoring — 2024-03-20 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented format — participant discussion section is clearly separated from staff sections.

**Era context**: March 2024. The Fed is holding rates at 5.25-5.50% after the aggressive 2022-23 hiking cycle. Inflation has come down significantly from 2022 peaks but remains above 2%. The economy has been surprisingly resilient. The key debate is when to start cutting rates.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all participants" (hold decision, importance of communication), "almost all" (appropriate to cut at some point this year), "most" (labor supply boosted by immigration, ON RRP discussion), "many" (soft retail sales, credit card/delinquency concerns, immigration uncertainty), "several" (disinflation from supply improvements, easing wage pressures, technology/productivity, investment in technology), "some" (broad-based inflation, business contacts on pricing power, inflation expectations anchored, immigration boosting consumption, financial conditions uncertainty, geopolitical risks), "a few" (residual seasonality, core non-housing services to decline, preferred continuing current pace of balance sheet runoff).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants observe "significant progress" toward 2% but note two most recent monthly readings were "firmer than expected." Some note increases were "relatively broad based." Inflation remains "elevated" above 2% (PCE at 2.5%, core at 2.9%). They "remained highly attentive to inflation risks." Still-elevated housing services inflation discussed. However, they expect inflation to return to 2% over medium term. Direction: above target but coming down, with recent upside surprises. This is modestly above target with persistent concerns — roughly 58-62 range. The "still elevated" language with "significant progress" suggests moderating from higher levels. Score: 60.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Economy expanding at a "solid rate" though slowing from "robust" Q4. Strong economic momentum noted. No language suggesting below potential — rather "strong economic momentum." Several participants note investment in technology enhancing productive capacity. Immigration boosting labor supply. This suggests modestly above potential. Score: 54.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Demand and supply in the labor market were continuing to come into better balance, although conditions generally remained tight." Strong payroll growth, low unemployment. Declining vacancies, lower quits rate. Some contacts report "less difficulty in hiring." But "portions of the labor market... remained very tight." Labor supply boosted by immigration and participation. Direction: still tight but easing. The "generally remained tight" language with clear easing indicators suggests 57-60. Score: 58.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumption "generally remained solid" but "recent readings on retail sales had been soft." Strong labor market and wage gains supporting consumption. Mixed homebuilding reports. Many point to credit card balances and delinquencies as downside risk for lower-income households. Business contacts in some Districts show "increased optimism." Several note increased investment in technology. Manufacturing "stable." Net position: solid but moderating, roughly at trend to slightly above. Score: 53.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Maintaining the current restrictive policy stance," "restrictive credit conditions were cited by a few participants as restraining sectors." Some note uncertainty about "whether conditions were or could become less restrictive than desired." Balance sheet runoff discussion prominent. Borrowing costs "remained elevated" per staff section (but that's staff — scoring participant views only). Participants reference "tight financial and credit conditions" in context of policy effects. The concern that conditions might be "less restrictive than desired" is notable. Direction: somewhat tight but with uncertainty about degree. Score: 60.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: ALL participants judged holding at 5.25-5.50% appropriate. "Almost all" judged it would be appropriate to move to less restrictive stance "at some point this year" — but NOT YET. They "did not expect it would be appropriate to reduce the target range... until they had gained greater confidence that inflation was moving sustainably toward 2 percent." Recent data "had not increased their confidence." They weigh risks of too long vs. too quick. This is a hold with a mild easing bias — they see cuts coming but are in no rush, especially given recent inflation data. Score: 52 (lean very slightly hawkish — holding at peak rates, acknowledging cuts ahead but emphasizing patience and data dependence, with recent inflation data reinforcing the hold).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All participants judged" the hold was appropriate. "Almost all" agreed on eventual cuts this year. "A few" preferred continuing current pace of balance sheet runoff vs. the vast majority wanting to slow. Disagreement is minimal on the rate decision, modest on balance sheet pace. Score: 20.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Participants discuss CRE deterioration as downside risk, "potential reemergence of stresses in the banking sector," some mention financial conditions uncertainty. Credit card delinquencies and lower-income household stress discussed by many. Extensive balance sheet / reserves discussion. But financial stability is not a dominant topic — it appears as one of several risk factors. Score: 35.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Policy rate was likely at its peak for this tightening cycle." "Almost all" judged it appropriate to cut at some point this year. But "did not expect it would be appropriate to reduce the target range... until they had gained greater confidence." Data-dependent posture. Recent inflation data "had not increased their confidence." Maintaining current stance for longer if disinflation slows, or reducing restraint if labor market weakens. This is a mild easing lean with strong data dependence — peak rates acknowledged, cuts signaled but conditional. Score: 42 (easing lean with data dependence, but tempered by the "not yet confident" language).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants discussed the uncertainties around the economic outlook." They note uncertainty about persistence of inflation, recent data "had not increased their confidence." Geopolitical risks, immigration effects on labor supply ("difficulty in assessing"), financial conditions uncertainty. "Various sources of uncertainty" noted. Multiple sources of uncertainty discussed but without the "particularly uncertain" or "uncharted" language. This is somewhat elevated. Score: 45.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (54) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (58): Both modestly above midpoint, with labor slightly higher. Coherent — economy near/slightly above potential with still-tight labor market.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (52) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (42): Slight divergence explained by: current action is a hold at restrictive levels (mildly hawkish lean), but forward signal is toward eventual easing (mild easing lean). This is consistent with peak-rate holding pattern.

### Polarity verification:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says above target, firmer readings. 60 > 50. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. "Solid" growth, "strong momentum." 54 > 50. Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. "Generally remained tight." 58 > 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. "Restrictive" conditions. 60 > 50. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Holding at restrictive levels, patient. 52 near neutral. Correct.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward. Signal is toward eventual easing but data-dependent. 42 < 50. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 60 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "significant progress had been made over the past year toward the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective even though the two most recent monthly readings on core and headline inflation had been firmer than expected"
- "Some participants noted that the recent increases in inflation had been relatively broad based and therefore should not be discounted as merely statistical aberrations"
- "they remained highly attentive to inflation risks"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants see inflation as modestly above target with recent upside surprises causing concern, but expect continued disinflation over the medium term.
**Minority views**: A few noted residual seasonality may have affected readings (slightly less concerned); some noted broad-based nature (slightly more concerned). Modest spread.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 54 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants expected that economic growth would slow from last year's strong pace" but the economy is expanding at a "solid rate"
- "strong economic momentum and disappointing readings on inflation in recent months"

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy running modestly above potential with solid growth and strong momentum, though expected to moderate.
**Minority views**: Some Districts reported only "steady or stable pace of economic activity." Narrow spread.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 58 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "demand and supply in the labor market were continuing to come into better balance, although conditions generally remained tight"
- "Participants noted strong recent payroll growth, while the unemployment rate remained low"
- "declining job vacancies, a lower quits rate, and a reduced ratio of job openings to unemployed workers"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market still tight but clearly easing from peak tightness, with immigration and participation gains increasing supply.
**Minority views**: "some participants observed that portions of the labor market, such as the health-care sector and in less urban areas, remained very tight" — pushes uncertainty band upward.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 53 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "consumption spending generally remained solid, although many commented that recent readings on retail sales had been soft"
- "strong labor market, ongoing wage gains, and a generally healthy household-sector balance sheet as likely to continue to support consumption"
- "many participants pointed to indicators such as higher credit card balances, greater use of buy-now-pay-later programs, or rising delinquency rates... as a downside risk"

**Center-of-gravity**: Spending solid but moderating, roughly at or slightly above trend, with downside risks from lower-income household stress.
**Minority views**: Mixed — some Districts optimistic, others only steady. Delinquency concerns from many participants create downside risk.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 60 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Restrictive credit conditions were cited by a few participants as restraining sectors such as equipment investment and residential investment"
- Some participants noted "uncertainty regarding the restrictiveness of financial conditions and the associated risk that conditions were or could become less restrictive than desired"
- Balance sheet discussion focused on managing the transition carefully to avoid "undue stress" in money markets

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions perceived as somewhat tight/restrictive, with some concern they might not be tight enough given resilient economy.
**Minority views**: The debate about whether conditions are sufficiently restrictive widens the uncertainty band.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 52 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all participants judged that... it was appropriate to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 5¼ to 5½ percent"
- "they did not expect it would be appropriate to reduce the target range... until they had gained greater confidence that inflation was moving sustainably toward 2 percent"
- "important to weigh the risks of maintaining a restrictive stance for too long... against the risks of easing policy too quickly"

**Center-of-gravity**: Balanced-to-very-slightly-hawkish lean — holding at restrictive levels with explicit patience, acknowledging eventual easing but not signaling imminence.
**Minority views**: Very narrow disagreement on the current decision (unanimous hold).

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 20 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all participants judged that... it was appropriate to maintain the target range"
- "almost all participants judged that it would be appropriate to move policy to a less restrictive stance at some point this year"
- "A few participants... indicated that they preferred to continue with the current pace of balance sheet runoff" (vs. vast majority favoring slowing)

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on both the rate decision and the general direction, with only minor differences on balance sheet pace and timing nuances.
**Minority views**: "A few" on balance sheet pace is a minor point of divergence.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 35 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "many participants pointed to... higher credit card balances, greater use of buy-now-pay-later programs, or rising delinquency rates"
- Downside risks include "a deterioration in conditions in domestic CRE markets, a potential reemergence of stresses in the banking sector"
- Extensive balance sheet/reserves discussion focused on smooth transition and avoiding "undue stress" in money markets

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability received meaningful but not dominant attention — CRE, banking stress, and consumer credit quality mentioned as risk factors, and the balance sheet discussion touched on funding market stability.
**Minority views**: None notable.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 42 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the policy rate was likely at its peak for this tightening cycle, and almost all participants judged that it would be appropriate to move policy to a less restrictive stance at some point this year"
- "they did not expect it would be appropriate to reduce the target range... until they had gained greater confidence that inflation was moving sustainably toward 2 percent"
- "maintaining the current restrictive policy stance for longer should the disinflation process slow, or reducing policy restraint in the event of an unexpected weakening in labor market conditions"

**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal leans toward eventual easing but is heavily conditioned on data, with recent inflation data reinforcing patience. The signal is mildly dovish in direction but cautious in timing.
**Minority views**: "Almost all" vs. implicit one or two who may not see cuts this year.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 45 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants discussed the uncertainties around the economic outlook... recent data had not increased their confidence that inflation was moving sustainably down to 2 percent"
- "Some participants pointed to geopolitical risks that might result in more severe supply bottlenecks or higher shipping costs"
- "Many participants pointed to the difficulty in assessing how recent immigration trends would influence the evolution of labor supply, aggregate demand, and overall economic activity"

**Center-of-gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty — multiple sources discussed (inflation persistence, geopolitics, immigration effects, financial conditions) but without extreme or unprecedented language.
**Minority views**: Broad agreement that uncertainty exists across multiple dimensions.

### Optional Dimensions:

**INFLATION_GOODS**: 42 — "Several participants noted that the disinflationary pressure for core goods that had resulted from the receding of supply chain bottlenecks was likely to moderate." Goods disinflation fading but still present.

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: 62 — "still-elevated rate of housing services inflation" and discussion of core non-housing services. Services inflation remains a concern.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: 63 — "Participants discussed the still-elevated rate of housing services inflation and commented on the uncertainty regarding when and by how much lower readings for rent growth on new leases would pass through to this category of inflation."

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: 48 — "Some participants observed that longer-term inflation expectations appeared to remain well anchored, as reflected in a broad range of surveys of households, businesses, and forecasters, as well as measures from financial markets." Anchored at/near target.

**CREDIT_STRESS**: 52 — CRE delinquencies rising, credit card delinquencies above pre-pandemic levels, small business credit quality deteriorating. But credit "continued to be available to most businesses, households, and municipalities." Modestly elevated.

**EQUITY_STRESS**: N/A — No meaningful participant discussion of equity market stress or valuation concerns.

**BANKING_STRESS**: 38 — "potential reemergence of stresses in the banking sector" mentioned as a downside risk, and CRE credit quality "sparked investor concerns about the health of a few small U.S. and foreign banks." Mentioned as a risk, not a current acute concern.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: N/A — No participant discussion of Treasury market dysfunction.

**DOLLAR_CONCERN**: N/A — No participant discussion of dollar concerns.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: 58 — "Several participants commented that increased efficiencies and technological innovations had the potential to raise productivity growth, which might allow the economy to grow faster without raising inflation." Also "several participants noted that their contacts had reported increased investment in technology or in business process improvements that were enhancing productive capacity."
