# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2006-08-08

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format — staff and participant content mixed, but participant language cues are clear ("participants noted," "members favored," "all members agreed," etc.). I will score only participant-attributed content.

**Era context**: August 2006. The Fed had just completed 17 consecutive rate hikes (from 1% to 5.25%). Housing was cooling. Core inflation had been running above 2%. This meeting is the first pause in the tightening cycle. Macro backdrop: late-cycle economy, above-target inflation, housing slowdown beginning.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all" (inflation concern, statement agreement), "most" (inflation likely to decline, current stance appropriate), "nearly all" (hold rates), "many" (close call, additional firming could be needed), "several" (growth rate lower than believed), "some" (housing orderly, nonresidential construction, global demand, price pressures not likely to continue).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as clearly above target. "All participants expressed concern about continued elevated readings on core inflation." "Core PCE inflation now appeared to have been running at or above a 2 percent annual rate for more than two years, with prices accelerating over the first half of 2006." The pickup is "broad-based" with pass-through ability. However, "most" expect gradual decline. The center of gravity is elevated current inflation with significant upside risks but expectation of gradual decline. Direction: above the midpoint substantially. The "all expressed concern" and "accelerating" language pushes this high. Score ~68. Higher = more pressure. Evidence says elevated, accelerating, broad-based. Matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Output was expected to advance at a pace at or slightly below the economy's potential rate of growth." GDP growth slowed in Q2 "generally in line with expectations." Housing cooling, energy restraining demand. Several noted potential growth rate "likely was lower than previously believed." The economy is described as near potential but moderating — not clearly above or below. The language "at or slightly below potential" places this right around 48-50. Score ~49.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Limited direct labor market discussion. "Federal receipts had been increasing rapidly, a development that reflected continued strong growth in labor and non-labor income." Unit labor costs marked up, "upward revisions to labor compensation." No mentions of unemployment, shortages, or slack in labor specifically. The compensation growth and resource utilization language ("pressures on resource utilization likely would not increase and could abate") suggests tightness near or slightly above full employment. Score ~54.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending restrained by energy prices and borrowing costs, "continued slowing of growth in sales." "Consumption spending seemed likely to expand at a moderate pace." Business investment a "little lower than expected" but timing, not weakness. Nonresidential construction strengthening. Strong business balance sheets. Net: moderate but slowing. Score ~47 — slightly below trend due to slowing signals.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Continued increases in energy prices and borrowing costs appeared to have restrained consumer spending." "The lagged effects of past increases in interest rates." After 17 rate hikes, conditions are clearly tighter than neutral. But no credit crisis language, no stress. Financial condition of households "appeared to remain solid." Score ~57 — somewhat tight from cumulative rate hikes but no stress.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Nearly all members favored keeping the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent." But "many members thought that the decision to keep policy unchanged at this meeting was a close call" and "additional firming could well be needed." The statement should convey "inflation risks remained dominant" and "keeping policy unchanged did not necessarily mark the end of the tightening cycle." This is a hawkish hold — pausing but with a clear tightening bias. Score ~62.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Nearly all" favored holding — implying one or possibly no formal dissents. But "many" saw it as a "close call." There are differences on whether to hike now or wait, but the decision converged. Moderate differences about timing/urgency. Score ~32.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Housing discussed as a downside risk with "considerable uncertainty." Some financial conditions mentioned (borrowing costs, household financial condition). But no discussion of systemic risk, credit markets stress, or financial stability per se. This is moderate contextual discussion, not a focal point. Score ~35.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Additional firming could well be needed." Statement should convey "inflation risks remained dominant" and pause "did not necessarily mark the end of the tightening cycle." But also data-dependent: "accumulate more information before judging." This is a mild tightening bias going forward. Score ~67.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants expressed considerable uncertainty regarding prospects for the housing sector." "The extent to which the increase in core inflation so far this year reflected transitory or persistent influences remained unclear." Uncertainty about housing wealth effects, about whether inflation would moderate. Multiple sources of genuine uncertainty. Score ~52.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (49) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (54): Close together, both near potential. Labor slightly tighter than output gap, which makes sense with compensation revisions upward. Reasonable.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (62) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (67): Both lean hawkish, with forward guidance slightly more hawkish (explicit "not end of tightening cycle"). Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 68 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All participants expressed concern about continued elevated readings on core inflation and inflation risks going forward."
- "Core PCE inflation now appeared to have been running at or above a 2 percent annual rate for more than two years, with prices accelerating over the first half of 2006."
- "The recent pickup in price increases appeared to be broad-based, and a number of business contacts reported greater ability to pass through higher costs."

**Center-of-gravity**: All participants see inflation as clearly elevated and accelerating, with broad-based price pressures and significant upside risks, though most expect eventual gradual decline.

**Minority views**: None dissent from the "elevated" characterization; the spread is about persistence vs. transitoriness, widening the band slightly.

---

### OUTPUT_GAP: 49 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Output was expected to advance at a pace at or slightly below the economy's potential rate of growth."
- "The slowing of GDP growth in the second quarter was generally in line with expectations, reflecting the continued cooling of the housing market."
- "Several participants noted that the annual revision to the national income and product accounts suggested this growth rate likely was lower than previously believed."

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy viewed as operating right around potential, with growth expected at or slightly below potential going forward.

**Minority views**: Some uncertainty about the level of potential itself given data revisions.

---

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 54 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Measures of unit labor costs had been marked up, reflecting upward revisions to labor compensation and downward revisions to labor productivity."
- "Federal receipts had been increasing rapidly, a development that reflected continued strong growth in labor and non-labor income."
- "Pressures on resource utilization likely would not increase and could abate to a degree going forward."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market near full employment with moderate compensation pressures, but expected not to tighten further.

**Minority views**: Limited direct discussion; confidence moderate.

---

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 47 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Continued increases in energy prices and borrowing costs appeared to have restrained consumer spending growth in recent months."
- "Contacts in the retail sector generally reported a continued slowing of growth in sales."
- "Overall, consumption spending seemed likely to expand at a moderate pace in coming quarters."
- Business investment: "Strong business balance sheets and high profitability were seen as supporting continued growth in expenditures on software and equipment."

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending slowing from above-trend to moderate; business investment dipped but underlying trend intact. Net slightly below trend.

**Minority views**: Business side more positive than consumer side, creating some spread.

---

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 57 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The lagged effects of past increases in interest rates" cited as restraining growth.
- "Continued increases in energy prices and borrowing costs appeared to have restrained consumer spending growth."
- "The financial condition of households... appeared to remain solid."

**Center-of-gravity**: Conditions somewhat tight from 17 cumulative rate hikes, with borrowing costs restraining demand, but no signs of financial stress.

**Minority views**: Limited direct discussion of financial conditions per se; mostly inferred from demand effects.

---

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 62 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Nearly all members favored keeping the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent at this meeting."
- "Many members thought that the decision to keep policy unchanged at this meeting was a close call and noted that additional firming could well be needed."
- "All members agreed that the statement... should convey that inflation risks remained dominant and that consequently keeping policy unchanged at this meeting did not necessarily mark the end of the tightening cycle."

**Center-of-gravity**: A hawkish hold — pausing to assess but with a clear tightening bias; inflation risks dominant, further hikes possible.

**Minority views**: "Many" saw it as a close call (would have been comfortable hiking), while "most" saw the pause as appropriate. Spread reflects timing disagreement more than direction.

---

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 32 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Nearly all members favored keeping the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent" — implies very few or one potential dissent.
- "Many members thought that the decision... was a close call" — suggests meaningful internal tension despite convergent decision.
- "Members generally saw limited risk in deferring further policy tightening."

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on the hold decision but notable tension about whether to hike; the "close call" language and "many" vs. "most" split indicates moderate differences about timing/urgency.

**Minority views**: At least one member may have preferred hiking; "many" close-call language implies nontrivial minority.

---

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 35 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Housing discussed at length as downside risk with "considerable uncertainty."
- Household financial condition noted as "solid" (brief mention).
- No discussion of systemic risk, credit market stress, or financial stability frameworks.

**Center-of-gravity**: Housing market conditions discussed as a growth risk, but financial stability per se is not a focal point of discussion.

**Minority views**: None.

---

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 67 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Additional firming could well be needed."
- "Keeping policy unchanged at this meeting did not necessarily mark the end of the tightening cycle."
- "Members generally saw limited risk in deferring further policy tightening that might prove necessary, as long as inflation expectations remained contained."

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear forward signal that further tightening remains on the table; data-dependent but with a tightening bias.

**Minority views**: Some members more confident the current stance was sufficient; the conditionality creates a mild spread.

---

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants expressed considerable uncertainty regarding prospects for the housing sector."
- "The extent to which the increase in core inflation so far this year reflected transitory or persistent influences remained unclear."
- "The slower pace of increase in housing wealth would restrain consumption growth, though by how much was uncertain."

**Center-of-gravity**: Elevated uncertainty about housing trajectory and inflation persistence, with multiple participants flagging genuine unknowns.

**Minority views**: Uncertainty is broadly shared; spread reflects different sources of uncertainty (housing vs. inflation).
