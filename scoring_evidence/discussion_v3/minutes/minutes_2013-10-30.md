# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2013-10-30 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Segmented — participant views are clearly separated from staff content. The text uses standard minutes quantifier language throughout.

**Era context**: October 2013 — mid-recovery from the GFC. The economy is growing moderately but below potential. QE3 (open-ended asset purchases) is ongoing. The "taper tantrum" occurred in mid-2013 after the June meeting signaled potential tapering. The government shutdown had just ended. Inflation is running below the 2% target. Unemployment is still elevated but declining.

**Quantifier distribution**: "generally" appears frequently (broad consensus language). "a number of" and "several" appear for moderate-sized groups. "some" and "a few" for smaller factions. "one member" for the lone dissenter favoring tapering. "most" appears regarding IOER consideration. "many" used for stressing data-dependent purchases.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target. "Inflation remained subdued and below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent." "Inflation was running below the Committee's longer-run objective." Members recognized that "inflation persistently below the Committee's 2 percent objective could pose risks to economic performance." Direction: clearly below midpoint. The language "subdued" and persistently below target, with concern about it, points to the 26-40 range. Anchor guidance says 38-42 for 2013-19 below target. Given that inflation is described as "subdued" (not just "slightly below"), and there is worry about persistent below-target readings, I score toward the lower end of the anchor range. Score: 35.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic activity was continuing to expand at a moderate pace." Growth was "somewhat weaker than many of them had previously anticipated." Fiscal policy was "exerting significant restraint on economic growth." Headwinds of "household-sector deleveraging, tight credit conditions for some households and businesses, and fiscal restraint" are referenced. The economy is clearly still below potential — there is no language of being at or above potential. The anchor for 2010-14 recovery is 35-42. "Moderate pace" of expansion with "headwinds restraining growth" suggests economy still meaningfully below potential but recovering. Score: 38.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Unemployment rate remained elevated." "Substantial remaining slack in the labor market." "Limited pace of gains in wages and payrolls." Part-time for economic reasons mentioned as evidence of slack. The September employment report was "somewhat disappointing." Labor market "continued to improve, albeit slowly." The drop in unemployment "could overstate the degree of improvement." This is clearly below full employment with substantial slack. Anchor for 2011-14 is 35-40. The emphasis on "substantial remaining slack" and elevated unemployment pushes toward the lower end. Score: 34.

**Polarity check on OUTPUT_GAP and LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Both below 50, consistent with an economy below potential with labor slack. They are close (38 and 34), which is coherent. Labor is slightly lower because the text emphasizes labor slack more explicitly ("substantial remaining slack").

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumer spending appeared to have slowed somewhat in the third quarter." Business contacts "reported continued moderate growth in sales, but remained cautious about expanding payrolls and capital expenditures." "Household spending and business fixed investment advanced" but housing "slowed somewhat." Consumer sentiment "remained unusually low." Auto sales "remained strong." Overall: below trend to moderate, with some positive factors offset by caution and slowing. Anchor for 2011-13 is 35-42. "Slowed somewhat" and cautious businesses suggest lower end. Score: 38.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Financial conditions eased notably over the intermeeting period, with declines in longer-term interest rates and increases in equity values." Interest rate volatility "substantially lower." "Reaching-for-yield behavior might be increasing again." However, "longer-term interest rates remained well above their levels in the spring." "Tight credit conditions for some households and businesses" mentioned as a headwind. Mortgage rate increases reduced housing affordability. One participant noted challenges for banking from near-zero rates (net interest margin pressure). The net picture: conditions eased notably over the intermeeting period (from the taper tantrum tightening), but credit remains tight for some and rates are above spring levels. Overall somewhat loose to neutral given the easing direction. Score: 40. This reflects conditions that are generally supportive but with some residual tightness from the spring/summer rate rise.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All members but one again judged that it would be appropriate for the Committee to await more evidence... before adjusting the pace of asset purchases." One dissenter thought easing "at the current pace was no longer necessary." "Some pointed out that, if economic conditions warranted, the Committee could decide to slow the pace of purchases at one of its next few meetings." Participants "generally expected that the data would prove consistent... and would thus warrant trimming the pace of purchases in coming months." There is extensive discussion of forward guidance tools to maintain accommodation. Reducing IOER was discussed as a possible signal. The dominant stance is: continue QE at current pace, wait for more evidence, but tapering is on the horizon. This is firmly in the QE-era dovish range. The presence of QE discussion IS the dovish signal per the rubric. The anchor for QE era 2010-12 is 15-25, but this is 2013 where tapering is actively being anticipated — making it slightly less dovish than peak QE expansion. Anchor for 2014-15 pre-liftoff is 40-50. This meeting sits between: still buying but expecting to taper soon. Score: 30. The majority wants to continue purchases now but expects tapering soon — moderate dovish with a forward lean toward less accommodation.

**Polarity check**: Higher = more hawkish. The committee is maintaining QE but discussing tapering. 30 is dovish, which matches.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Participants "generally expected that the data would prove consistent... and would thus warrant trimming the pace of purchases in coming months." Forward guidance for the federal funds rate is being strengthened with dovish commitments — discussion of lowering the unemployment threshold, adding an inflation floor, and signaling low rates even after thresholds are reached. "Even after the first increase in the federal funds rate target, the Committee anticipated keeping the rate below its longer-run equilibrium value for some time." Markets "moved out the date at which they expected to see the Committee first increase the federal funds rate target." The forward signal is: taper purchases soon (mild hawkish) but keep rates very low for a long time (dovish). The net forward signal leans dovish — the dominant discussion is about how to communicate continued accommodation and low rates well into the future. Anchor for 2014 taper is 8-15. This is pre-taper (tapering hasn't started yet, just anticipated), so the forward guidance is still strongly dovish with threshold-based commitments intact. Score: 15.

**Coherence check HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN vs FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: 30 vs 15. The forward guidance is more dovish than the current lean because participants are actively discussing ways to strengthen dovish forward guidance (lower thresholds, inflation floors, qualitative assurances about low rates) even while expecting to taper asset purchases. This divergence is logical and intentional — they want to offset taper with stronger rate guidance.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All members but one again judged" to maintain purchases — one formal dissenter. The policy planning discussion shows "a range of" views on specifics (how to taper, which assets, forward guidance details) but broad agreement on the overall stance. "A couple of participants" thought it premature to contemplate winding down before improvement. Some favored different taper splits across Treasury/MBS. This is a classic one-dissent meeting with tactical differences but strategic agreement. Anchor for typical one-dissent meeting is 25-35. Score: 30.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: "A couple of participants pointed to signs suggesting that reaching-for-yield behavior might be increasing again." One participant noted challenges for banking from near-zero rates. Financial conditions discussed in terms of their economic impact (mortgage rates, housing affordability). Financial market responses to June/September decisions discussed extensively but in a communication/policy context, not stability per se. This is limited attention to financial stability as such — mostly financial conditions as economic context. Anchor for 2013-19 typical is 20-35. Score: 25.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Several significant risks remained, including the uncertain effects of ongoing fiscal drag and of the continuing fiscal debate." "Consumer sentiment remained unusually low, posing a downside risk." "Uncertainty about future fiscal policy and the regulatory environment." Downside risks "generally viewed as having diminished... since last fall." The government shutdown effects seen as "temporary and limited" but concern about repeated fiscal impasses. Overall: moderate uncertainty, somewhat elevated by fiscal concerns but diminishing. Anchor for 2013-14 stable expansion is 25-35. The fiscal uncertainty adds some, but diminishing downside risks temper it. Score: 35.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "inflation remained subdued and below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent"; "longer-run inflation expectations remained stable and, by some measures, below 2 percent"; "inflation persistently below the Committee's 2 percent objective could pose risks to economic performance"
- Center-of-gravity: Participants uniformly view inflation as subdued and below the 2% target, with concern about persistent undershooting. No upward inflation pressures noted by any participant.
- Minority views: None — no participant voiced upside inflation concern. Narrow band.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "economic activity was continuing to expand at a moderate pace"; "growth in the second half of 2013 might prove somewhat weaker than many of them had previously anticipated"; "fiscal policy, which has been exerting significant restraint on economic growth"
- Center-of-gravity: Economy growing moderately but clearly below potential, with headwinds (deleveraging, tight credit, fiscal drag) slowing the recovery. Participants broadly projected improvement ahead but acknowledged the gap persists.
- Minority views: None significant — broad agreement on moderate below-potential growth.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 34 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "substantial remaining slack in the labor market"; "the unemployment rate remained elevated"; "limited pace of gains in wages and payrolls, as well as the number of employees working part time for economic reasons"
- Center-of-gravity: Labor market has substantial slack with elevated unemployment and underemployment. Improvement is occurring but slowly.
- Minority views: "A few participants offered reasons why recent readings on the unemployment rate might provide an accurate assessment... of improvement" — suggesting slightly less slack than the majority perceives, but this is a small minority.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Consumer spending appeared to have slowed somewhat in the third quarter"; "Business contacts generally reported continued moderate growth in sales, but remained cautious about expanding payrolls and capital expenditures"; "consumer sentiment remained unusually low"
- Center-of-gravity: Spending and investment below trend — consumer spending slowing, businesses cautious, consumer sentiment unusually low. Some bright spots (autos, manufacturing pickup) but the overall picture is sluggish.
- Minority views: None — broad agreement.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 40 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Financial conditions eased notably over the intermeeting period, with declines in longer-term interest rates and increases in equity values"; "longer-term interest rates remained well above their levels in the spring"; "tight credit conditions for some households and businesses" (listed as headwind)
- Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions eased notably from the taper-tantrum tightening but remain above spring levels. Credit conditions still tight for some borrowers. Net: somewhat loose to neutral.
- Minority views: "A couple of participants pointed to signs suggesting that reaching-for-yield behavior might be increasing again" (suggesting looser conditions); "A few participants expressed concerns about the eventual economic impact of the change in financial conditions since the spring" (suggesting residual tightness matters). Wider band reflects these differing emphases.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 30 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "all members but one again judged that it would be appropriate for the Committee to await more evidence... before adjusting the pace of asset purchases"; "generally expected that the data would prove consistent... and would thus warrant trimming the pace of purchases in coming months"; "one member... the continued easing of monetary policy at the current pace was no longer necessary"
- Center-of-gravity: Firmly dovish — continuing large-scale QE asset purchases. But the forward lean is toward tapering, and extensive discussion of tools to maintain accommodation while beginning to taper. Not maximally dovish because tapering is actively anticipated.
- Minority views: One member dissenting hawkish (favoring tapering now). "A couple of participants thought it premature to focus on" winding down scenarios (more dovish wing).

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "all members but one again judged" (one dissent); extensive tactical disagreements about taper composition, forward guidance specifics, but broad strategic agreement on maintaining accommodation for now
- Center-of-gravity: One formal dissent with modest tactical divergences — typical moderate-disagreement meeting.
- Minority views: The one dissenter plus various tactical differences (taper split across assets, forward guidance options).

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 25 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "a couple of participants pointed to signs suggesting that reaching-for-yield behavior might be increasing again"; one participant noted near-zero rates "continued to create challenges for the banking industry"
- Center-of-gravity: Limited financial stability discussion. A couple of mentions of reach-for-yield and banking challenges, but financial stability was not a major focus — most financial discussion was about conditions/communication.
- Minority views: None.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 15 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: Extensive discussion of strengthening dovish forward guidance — lowering unemployment threshold, adding inflation floor, qualitative assurances that rates stay low well after thresholds are reached; "even after the first increase in the federal funds rate target, the Committee anticipated keeping the rate below its longer-run equilibrium value for some time"
- Center-of-gravity: Forward signal is strongly dovish — participants are actively discussing how to commit to low rates for longer and how to separate the tapering of purchases from any future rate increase. The message is: rates stay very low for a very long time.
- Minority views: One member wants to begin tapering now, but even this member's view pertains to purchases, not rate guidance.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "several significant risks remained, including the uncertain effects of ongoing fiscal drag and of the continuing fiscal debate"; "downside risks to the outlook... were generally viewed as having diminished, on balance, since last fall"; "concern about the possible economic effects of repeated fiscal impasses on business and consumer confidence"
- Center-of-gravity: Somewhat elevated uncertainty, primarily driven by fiscal policy concerns (government shutdown, debt ceiling) and the fiscal drag trajectory. But overall risks seen as diminished relative to recent past.
- Minority views: None.
