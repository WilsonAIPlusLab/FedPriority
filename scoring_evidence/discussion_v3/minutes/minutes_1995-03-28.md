# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1995-03-28

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented (pre-2009), so I must focus on participant language cues ("members agreed," "members noted," "several members," etc.) and ignore staff-attributable content.

**Era context**: March 1995. The Fed had been tightening since early 1994 (a full tightening cycle of 300bp). The economy was in late expansion with signs of moderation. The last hike was February 1995 (50bp to 6%). This meeting is a "pause to assess" moment.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all the members" (hold policy, final vote), "most members" (asymmetric directive), "a number of members" (concern about insufficient slowdown), "several members" (asymmetric preference), "a few members" (further tightening needed sooner; symmetric directive preference), "members generally" (some inflation increase likely). No formal dissents -- vote was unanimous.

**Dimension-by-dimension directional signals**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Members see inflation as modestly above the comfort zone and rising. "Prices of many materials inputs...had risen sharply, but thus far there had been only a small pickup in consumer prices." "Members were concerned...business firms were reporting greater success in passing cost increases through to prices." "Members generally concluded that some increase in inflation was likely in coming months." Dollar depreciation adding pressure. This is above midpoint -- pressures building but not yet fully realized. Direction: above 50, moderately so.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic activity and utilization of labor and other producer resources were still at very high levels." "Economy expected to be operating in the neighborhood of its potential." The expansion is moderating from above-potential levels but still at high utilization. "Elevated levels of capacity utilization." This is above potential -- must be > 50. Direction: modestly above 50.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Persisting tightness in many labor markets" though "had not to this point fostered appreciable increases in wages." "Tight labor supply" mentioned in context of business investment. Unemployment was 5.4%. Members see tightness. Direction: above 50, moderately tight.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending sluggish ("sluggishness in consumer spending," "sharp reduction in spending for motor vehicles"). Housing weakened noticeably. But business investment still strong ("still strong expansion of spending on business equipment"). Overall: "expansion was moderating" but "economy appeared to retain considerable forward momentum." Net: still positive growth but slowing from above-trend. Direction: near 50, slightly below trend given the consumer and housing weakness offsetting business strength.

5. **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Favorable recent developments in financial markets, bank lending policies remained quite accommodative." Long-term rates had declined. Bond and stock prices rose. But policy tightening since 1994 is showing through in interest-sensitive sectors. Housing weakened due to higher mortgage rates. Overall conditions: mixed -- policy has tightened but market conditions have loosened (declining long rates, accommodative bank lending). Direction: near neutral, slightly loose given market easing.

6. **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Unanimous hold. But "most members expressed a preference for an asymmetric directive tilted toward restraint." "A few members indicated that further tightening might well be needed sooner rather than later." Only "a few" preferred symmetric. The lean is clearly hawkish -- holding but with tightening bias. Direction: lean hawkish, around 55-60.

7. **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Unanimous vote. Difference is between "most" (asymmetric) and "a few" (symmetric), but even the symmetric camp "could accept" asymmetric. Low disagreement. Direction: low, around 20.

8. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Minimal. Brief mentions of dollar decline, stock/bond prices as context for consumer balance sheets, Mexico situation. No sustained discussion of financial stability risks. Direction: low, around 10-15.

9. **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Asymmetric directive tilted toward restraint. "Additional monetary tightening needed at some point to contain inflation." "A few members indicated that further tightening might well be needed sooner rather than later." But also "prudent to pause and assess developments." Forward signal is mildly hawkish. Direction: 58-65.

10. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Considerable uncertainty surrounded the outlook for the external sector." "Substantial uncertainties that were involved." "The extent of the slowdown was not yet clear." "Timing and extent could not be anticipated with any precision." Multiple references to uncertainty. Direction: somewhat elevated, around 40-50.

**Cross-dimension coherence checks**:
- OUTPUT_GAP and LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Both above 50 (economy at/above potential, tight labor). Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Both lean hawkish (hold with tightening bias, asymmetric toward restraint). Coherent.

**Polarity verifications**:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says pressures building, pass-through increasing. Score above 50. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Economy at high utilization. Score above 50. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence says conditions are accommodative (bank lending, declining long rates). Score below 50. Matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 60 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Members were concerned, however, that despite continuing competitive pressures and some recent abatement in materials prices, business firms were reporting greater success in passing cost increases through to prices."
- "The members generally concluded that some increase in inflation was likely in coming months."
- "Prices of many materials inputs to the production process had risen sharply, but thus far there had been only a small pickup in consumer prices."

**Center-of-gravity**: Members see inflation pressures as clearly present and building, with pipeline pressures from materials and dollar depreciation, and increasing pass-through to finished goods. Not yet at alarming levels but trending upward. "Some increase" language suggests moderate concern -- roughly 10-15 points above midpoint.

**Minority views**: None significantly diverge; the concern is broadly shared.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 55 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity and utilization of labor and other producer resources were still at very high levels."
- "Economy expected to be operating in the neighborhood of its potential."
- "Elevated levels of capacity utilization."

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is at or modestly above potential. Growth is moderating from above-trend but utilization remains high. "Neighborhood of its potential" with "very high levels" of resource utilization suggests slightly above. Per bright-line rule, "very high levels" of utilization pushes above 50.

**Minority views**: "A number of members expressed concern that the slowdown might not be sufficient to relieve the persisting pressures on labor and capital resources" -- suggesting some see it as more clearly above potential.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Persisting tightness in many labor markets had not to this point fostered appreciable increases in wages."
- "Tight labor supply" cited as a factor driving business investment in capacity.
- "A number of members expressed concern that the slowdown might not be sufficient to relieve the persisting pressures on labor and capital resources."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor markets are clearly tight -- "persisting tightness in many labor markets" is unambiguous. Unemployment at 5.4% in this era is consistent with tight conditions. Wage pressures are muted but tightness is acknowledged. Score in the 53-65 range for "tight" per anchor table.

**Minority views**: None -- tightness is consensus.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 47 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Members reported on widespread signs that business activity, while still quite strong in many areas, was growing less rapidly on balance."
- "Members took note of the sluggishness in consumer spending that had emerged in recent months." / "Sharp reduction in spending for motor vehicles."
- "Housing market had weakened noticeably." / "Housing starts had fallen sharply."
- "Growth of business investment in plant and equipment would moderate from the extraordinary rate of the last two years but...would continue to support growth."

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending is sluggish, housing has weakened noticeably, but business investment remains solid. The overall picture is of moderation from above-trend toward trend or slightly below. "Economy appeared to retain considerable forward momentum" prevents a lower score. Net position: slightly below trend given the consumer and housing weakness.

**Minority views**: Some anecdotal reports suggest investment "might be stronger than expected."

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 40 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Bank lending policies remained quite accommodative, although business loan growth had slowed recently."
- "Favorable recent developments in financial markets" and "recent declines in long-term interest rates."
- "Rise in bond and stock prices, which had strengthened household balance sheets."
- But: "The effects of the policy tightening implemented since early 1994 seemed to be showing through in interest-sensitive sectors."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are somewhat loose to neutral. Despite significant policy tightening, market conditions have eased (long rates down, equities up, bank lending accommodative). The pass-through to housing and autos shows some tightening effect, but overall conditions remain supportive. Score in the 35-45 "somewhat loose to neutral" range.

**Minority views**: Limited discussion of financial conditions as a distinct topic.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 58 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All the members endorsed a proposal to maintain an unchanged degree of pressure in reserve markets."
- "Most members expressed a preference for an asymmetric directive tilted toward restraint."
- "A few members indicated that further tightening might well be needed sooner rather than later."
- "A few members preferred a symmetric directive" but "could accept an asymmetric intermeeting instruction."

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous hold with a hawkish tilt. The committee is pausing to assess but leans toward further tightening if needed. "Most" favor asymmetric toward restraint. This is a lean-hawkish posture -- hold with tightening bias. Score in the 55-62 range.

**Minority views**: "A few" who wanted symmetric saw risks as balanced; "a few" who wanted tightening sooner were more hawkish. Both minorities are small.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 18 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Vote: 11-0 unanimous.
- "All the members indicated that they preferred or could support a directive."
- Difference between "most" (asymmetric) and "a few" (symmetric) is minor and the symmetric camp "could accept" asymmetric.
- "Opinions differed somewhat with respect to both the likely extent of the slowdown and the prognosis for inflation" -- acknowledged but mild.

**Center-of-gravity**: Near-consensus with only minor emphasis differences. The disagreement between asymmetric and symmetric camps is narrow and easily resolved. Score in the 15-25 range.

**Minority views**: The "a few" preferring symmetric and the "a few" wanting tightening sooner create a modest spread but no real division.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 12 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Brief mention of dollar decline: "Several members noted the relatively steep decline in the value of the dollar."
- Mexico mentioned briefly: "Economic developments in Mexico were leading to lower U.S. exports."
- Stock and bond prices mentioned only as context for household balance sheets.
- No sustained discussion of financial stability risks, systemic risk, or market dysfunction.

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability is not a focus of this meeting. Mentions are passing and contextual. Consistent with the 1993-96 era anchor of 1-10, though the dollar and Mexico merit slightly higher. Score around 10-15.

**Minority views**: None.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 62 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Somewhat greater reserve restraint would be acceptable" (asymmetric toward tightening).
- "Additional monetary tightening needed at some point to contain inflation."
- "A few members indicated that further tightening might well be needed sooner rather than later."
- But: "Prudent to pause and assess developments before taking any further policy action."

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal is mildly hawkish -- the asymmetric directive explicitly signals a tightening bias. The committee expects it may need to tighten further but wants to wait for data. This is a "lean toward normalization/tightening" posture. Score in the 60-68 range.

**Minority views**: The "a few" preferring symmetric saw balanced risks going forward, pulling slightly lower.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 45 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Considerable uncertainty surrounded the outlook for the external sector."
- "Substantial uncertainties that were involved" (in the policy discussion).
- "The extent of the slowdown was not yet clear."
- "The timing and extent could not be anticipated with any precision" (regarding inventory correction).
- "It was difficult to determine whether [consumer spending patterns] represented a temporary pause or a more prolonged pullback."

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is somewhat elevated. Multiple references to inability to assess the extent of the slowdown, the external outlook, and inventory dynamics. The language "considerable" and "substantial" uncertainties pushes this above normal caveats. But this is not "uncharted territory" -- it is a standard mid-cycle uncertainty about the degree of slowdown. Score in the 40-50 range.

**Minority views**: None -- uncertainty is broadly acknowledged.
