# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2022-07-27

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented format with clear "Participants' Views" section separated from "Staff Economic Outlook."

**Era context**: July 2022 — inflation at multi-decade highs, aggressive tightening cycle underway (75bp hike at this meeting), economy showing early signs of slowing, labor market still very tight. I will score based solely on the text.

**Quantifier distribution**: Heavy use of "participants" (near-universal), frequent "many," "several," "some," "a few," and one "all" (agreeing to 75bp hike). This is a well-documented meeting with extensive discussion.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as far above target. "Inflation remained unacceptably high and was well above the Committee's longer-run goal of 2 percent." "Inflationary pressures were broad based." "Little evidence to date that inflation pressures were subsiding." "Inflation was far above the Committee's 2 percent inflation objective." This is clearly in the 80-90 range — substantially above target, broad-based, unacceptably high. The anchor for 2022 Q2-Q3 is 82-88. Polarity check: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says extreme pressure. Score matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Mixed signals. Spending and production softened; "second quarter had seen a broad-based softening in economic activity." However, labor market remained strong and economy still operating above potential (staff notes GDP above potential, participants say below-trend growth ahead would help). "Many expected that growth in economic activity would be at a below-trend pace." The economy has decelerated substantially but the labor market suggests still above or near potential. Participants note a tension: "indicators of spending and production pointed to less underlying strength in economic activity than was suggested by indicators of labor market activity." The center of gravity is that the economy is modestly above potential but decelerating toward it. Score ~53-55. Anchor: 57-63 for 2022. The softening data pulls it toward the lower end. Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Economy still above but closing gap. Score matches.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor market remained strong, with the unemployment rate very low, job vacancies and quits close to historically high levels, and an elevated rate of nominal wage growth." But "some tentative signs of a softening outlook" including rising claims, reduced quits, slower payroll growth. Center of gravity: still very tight but with first signs of easing. Score ~68-72. Anchor: 65-75 for 2022. Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Very tight with tentative softening signals. Score matches.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Clear deceleration. "Consumer expenditures, housing activity, business investment, and manufacturing production had all decelerated from the robust rates of growth seen in 2021." "Investment spending had likely declined in the second quarter." "Many signs in the data... of slower growth in consumer spending." Housing "weakened notably." But not collapsing — "aggregate balance sheet for the household sector was strong." Score ~35-40. Below trend, sluggish, decelerating significantly. Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Evidence says weakening. Score matches.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "The Committee's credibility... had already contributed to a notable tightening of financial conditions." "Emerging response of aggregate demand to the tightening of financial conditions." "Tighter financial conditions" mentioned repeatedly. "Financial market liquidity had been low in some areas but market functioning had been orderly." Conditions clearly tightened by rate hikes. Score ~63-67. Anchor: 60-70 for 2022 rate hikes. Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says notably tighter. Score matches.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All participants agreed that it was appropriate to raise the target range for the federal funds rate 75 basis points." "Moving to a restrictive stance of policy was required." "Participants continued to anticipate that ongoing increases... would be appropriate." "Some participants indicated that, once the policy rate had reached a sufficiently restrictive level, it likely would be appropriate to maintain that level for some time." But "many participants remarked... there was also a risk that the Committee could tighten... by more than necessary" — data-dependent approach. Also "it likely would become appropriate at some point to slow the pace." This is very hawkish — unanimous 75bp hike with forward expectation of continued tightening, but with emerging awareness of over-tightening risk. Score ~82-85. Anchor: 80-85 for late 2022 75bp hikes. Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Score matches.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All participants agreed" on 75bp. The main tension is between those emphasizing over-tightening risk ("many participants remarked") and those emphasizing inflation entrenchment risk. But there are no dissents and broad agreement on the action. Some variation on forward path — "some" want to slow pace eventually vs. continued aggressive tightening. Score ~25-30. Broad agreement with modest emphasis differences. Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Score matches.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: "Several of the participants who commented on issues related to financial stability" — a dedicated paragraph covering asset valuations, banking capital, digital assets, liquidity, nonbank institutions, countercyclical capital buffer. This is a meaningful discussion by several participants but not a dominant topic. Score ~40-45. Polarity check: Higher = more attention. Moderate discussion by several participants. Score matches.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Ongoing increases in the target range... would be appropriate." "Moving to a restrictive stance of policy was required." "At some point to slow the pace of policy rate increases." "Once... sufficiently restrictive level, it likely would be appropriate to maintain that level for some time." Strong tightening signal forward — continued hikes expected but with data dependence and eventual slowing. Score ~83-87. Anchor: 80-95 for 2022 aggressive hiking. Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Score matches.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Uncertainty about the medium-term course of inflation remained high." "Many participants remarked... the constantly changing nature of the economic environment and the existence of long and variable lags." Data-dependent approach emphasized. "Heightened uncertainty." Risk of over-tightening acknowledged. But participants seem fairly confident in their policy direction. Score ~55-60. Elevated uncertainty about outlook but resolute on direction. Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. Score matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 85 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants observed that inflation remained unacceptably high and was well above the Committee's longer-run goal of 2 percent."
- "Participants further observed that inflationary pressures were broad based, a pattern reflected in large one-month increases in the trimmed mean CPI and core CPI measures."
- "Participants agreed that there was little evidence to date that inflation pressures were subsiding."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Near-universal view that inflation is unacceptably high, broad-based, and showing no signs of subsiding, with upside risks to inflation.

**Minority views**: None expressing reduced inflation concern; narrow uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 54 ±8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The second quarter of this year had seen a broad-based softening in economic activity."
- "Many expected that growth in economic activity would be at a below-trend pace."
- "Indicators of spending and production pointed to less underlying strength in economic activity than was suggested by indicators of labor market activity."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Economy modestly above potential but decelerating rapidly; labor market strength suggests activity may be stronger than spending data imply, but the gap is closing.

**Minority views**: "Several participants raising the possibility that the discrepancy might ultimately be resolved by GDP being revised upward" vs. "several participants also observed that the labor market might not be as tight as some indicators suggested." Wide band reflects genuine ambiguity.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 70 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The labor market remained strong, with the unemployment rate very low, job vacancies and quits close to historically high levels, and an elevated rate of nominal wage growth."
- "Many participants also noted, however, that there were some tentative signs of a softening outlook for the labor market: These signs included increases in weekly initial unemployment insurance claims, reductions in quit rates and vacancies, slower growth in payrolls."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Labor market still very tight by historical standards but with nascent signs of softening at the edges; level remains well above full employment.

**Minority views**: Some see labor market as not as tight as headline indicators suggest (ADP, household survey softer). This widens the band.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 37 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Consumer expenditures, housing activity, business investment, and manufacturing production had all decelerated from the robust rates of growth seen in 2021."
- "Investment spending had likely declined in the second quarter."
- "Housing activity had weakened notably, reflecting the impact of higher mortgage interest rates and house prices on home affordability."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Broad-based deceleration in spending and investment, with housing notably weak and business investment likely contracting; below-trend spending characterizes this meeting.

**Minority views**: "A few participants stated that some contacts had reported a degree of short-term momentum in business activity arising from existing orders."

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 65 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The Committee's credibility... had already contributed to a notable tightening of financial conditions that would likely help reduce inflation pressures by restraining aggregate demand."
- "Many participants remarked that some of the slowing, particularly in the housing sector, reflected the emerging response of aggregate demand to the tightening of financial conditions."
- "Financial market liquidity had been low in some areas but that market functioning had, nonetheless, been orderly."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions have tightened notably from policy actions and are beginning to restrain demand, particularly in housing, but market functioning remains orderly — tight but not crisis-level.

**Minority views**: Narrow; broad agreement on the characterization.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 83 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All participants agreed that it was appropriate to raise the target range for the federal funds rate 75 basis points at this meeting."
- "Participants judged that moving to a restrictive stance of policy was required to meet the Committee's legislative mandate."
- "Many participants remarked that... there was also a risk that the Committee could tighten the stance of policy by more than necessary to restore price stability."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Unanimous support for a very large 75bp hike with commitment to reach restrictive levels, tempered by "many" acknowledging over-tightening risk and data-dependent approach — very hawkish but not the most extreme possible.

**Minority views**: The over-tightening risk acknowledgment by "many" participants prevents a score above 85.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 27 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All participants agreed that it was appropriate to raise the target range for the federal funds rate 75 basis points."
- Tension between over-tightening concerns ("many participants remarked") and inflation entrenchment risks, but these are presented as complementary considerations rather than opposing camps.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Broad agreement on current action and general forward direction; differences are in nuance and emphasis rather than fundamental disagreement.

**Minority views**: Minor differences about future pace and labor market interpretation do not rise to significant disagreement.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 42 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Several of the participants who commented on issues related to financial stability noted that, on balance, asset valuations had eased from elevated levels."
- "Some participants commented on the financial stability challenges posed by digital assets."
- "Several participants noted that capital at some of the largest banks had declined in recent quarters."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: A dedicated discussion of financial stability by several participants covering asset valuations, bank capital, digital assets, and market liquidity — moderate attention but not a dominant theme.

**Minority views**: N/A — this is an emphasis measure, not directional.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 85 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants continued to anticipate that ongoing increases in the target range for the federal funds rate would be appropriate."
- "Participants judged that moving to a restrictive stance of policy was required."
- "It likely would become appropriate at some point to slow the pace of policy rate increases while assessing the effects of cumulative policy adjustments."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Clear forward signal of continued tightening to restrictive levels, with acknowledgment that pace may slow eventually — strong hawkish forward guidance with data-dependent modulation.

**Minority views**: "Some participants indicated that, once the policy rate had reached a sufficiently restrictive level, it likely would be appropriate to maintain that level for some time" — this is forward but still hawkish (hold at restrictive).

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 57 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Uncertainty about the medium-term course of inflation remained high."
- "Many participants remarked that, in view of the constantly changing nature of the economic environment and the existence of long and variable lags in monetary policy's effect on the economy, there was also a risk that the Committee could tighten... by more than necessary."
- "Heightened uncertainty, concerns about inflation, tighter financial conditions, and a cutback in consumer spending had led firms to downgrade economic prospects."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Elevated uncertainty about inflation path and policy transmission lags; tension between GDP and labor data adds to analytical uncertainty, though participants are confident in their policy direction.

**Minority views**: Range of views on how tight the labor market truly is and how quickly policy will transmit.
