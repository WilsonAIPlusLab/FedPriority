# FOMC Meeting Scoring: January 25-26, 2022

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented format -- staff sections clearly separated from participant views section starting at "Participants' Views on Current Conditions and the Economic Outlook."

**Era context**: January 2022. Omicron wave is surging. Inflation is running well above 2% (CPI at 7.0%, core PCE at 4.7%). Labor market has recovered strongly from pandemic. Committee is at the zero lower bound but about to begin tightening -- this is the meeting just before the March 2022 liftoff. Balance sheet reduction principles adopted. Financial conditions still broadly accommodative.

**Quantifier distribution**: Frequent use of "participants" (collective), "many," "several," "some," "a few," "a couple," "a number of." "Most" appears for pace of asset purchase taper and rate hike pace. Unanimous vote on balance sheet principles.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as significantly above target. "Recent inflation readings had continued to significantly exceed the Committee's longer-run goal and elevated inflation was persisting longer than they had anticipated." "Elevated inflation had broadened beyond sectors most directly affected." Risks to inflation "weighted to the upside." Multiple upside risk factors cited. However, participants "generally expected inflation to moderate over the course of the year." This is January 2022 -- inflation is very high but participants still expect moderation. The current perception is clearly high pressure (well above target, broadening, persisting longer than expected). Direction: well above midpoint. Magnitude: "significantly exceed" + "broadened" + "persisting longer" = 15-25 points above midpoint. Score ~75. Anchor check: 2022 Q1 should be between the 65-72 (2021 Q3-Q4) and 82-88 (2022 Q2-Q3 peak) anchors. 75 fits well.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants describe economy as strong, with Omicron causing temporary disruption. "Indicators of economic activity and employment had continued to strengthen." "If the Omicron wave dissipated quickly, then economic activity would likely strengthen rapidly and economic growth for 2022 as a whole would be robust." Supply-demand imbalances, demand outstripping supply. The staff projection notes "level of real GDP was expected to remain well above potential" (staff view, but participants clearly echo this with language about strong demand exceeding supply capacity). Participants note supply chain bottlenecks limiting ability to meet "strong demand." Direction: above potential. Bright-line rule: above potential language -> must be >50. Magnitude: strong demand, supply constraints binding, but Omicron temporarily suppressing activity. Score ~58. Anchor: 57-63 for 2022 fits.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Very extensive discussion. "Labor market was very tight, including near-record rates of quits and job vacancies as well as nominal wage growth that was the highest recorded in decades." "Many participants commented that they viewed labor market conditions as already at or very close to those consistent with maximum employment." "Broad shortage of workers across many parts of the economy." Unemployment at 3.9%. "A couple of participants" noted economy may not have reached max employment yet (minority view). Direction: very tight. Magnitude: "very tight," "near-record," "highest in decades" = 20+ points above midpoint. Score ~72. Anchor: 65-75 for 2022 fits well.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed signals. Omicron "damped consumer demand, particularly for services." But "almost all of those participants anticipated that household demand would recover briskly." Strong household balance sheets, rising wages supporting spending. Business contacts reporting "generally robust demand." Supply constraints limiting business investment. This is a temporarily suppressed but fundamentally strong demand picture. Net: above trend but with temporary Omicron drag. Score ~58. Anchor: 62-68 for 2021 Q3-2022 Q1 surge; the Omicron drag pulls this slightly below that range.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Overall financial conditions had remained accommodative, in part reflecting policy measures to support the economy." Staff review notes financing conditions "remained broadly accommodative" across multiple categories -- corporate, municipal, residential mortgage, consumer credit. However, equity indexes decreased markedly, VIX rose considerably. Some tightening in market conditions. Direction: still accommodative (loose) but beginning to tighten. Score ~35. Conditions are still loose but moving toward neutral. Anchor: should be between 10-20 (very loose) and 45-55 (normal). 35 fits.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Very clear hawkish lean. Participants agreed "it would soon be appropriate to raise the target range." "Most participants suggested that a faster pace of increases in the target range for the federal funds rate than in the post-2015 period would likely be warranted." Balance sheet reduction to begin "sometime later this year." "Most participants noted that, if inflation does not move down as they expect, it would be appropriate for the Committee to remove policy accommodation at a faster pace." A couple wanted to end asset purchases even sooner. Rate still at zero this meeting, but strong forward hawkish lean. Direction: clearly hawkish. Magnitude: not yet hiking (holding at 0), but consensus to begin soon and go faster than 2015. Score ~68. Anchor: between 60-65 (liftoff) and 70-75 (hiking). This is pre-liftoff but with strong consensus for imminent action plus faster pace.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Unanimous vote on balance sheet principles. Broad agreement on need to tighten soon. "A couple of participants" wanted faster taper, "a couple" thought max employment not yet reached. These are minor variations. The main direction is highly consensual. Score ~22. Low disagreement with minor emphasis differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Moderate discussion. A dedicated paragraph in participants' views. "A few participants noted that asset valuations were elevated." "A couple" cited prolonged accommodation contributing to imbalances. "A couple of other participants cited reasons why elevated asset valuations might prove to be less of a threat." "Some participants saw emerging risks to financial stability associated with the rapid growth in crypto-assets." Not a dominant topic but more than passing mention. Score ~35. Anchor: 20-35 for 2013-19 typical; this is slightly more prominent given crypto and asset valuation discussion.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Strong hawkish forward signal. Rate hikes imminent ("soon be appropriate to raise"). Faster pace than 2015 expected. Balance sheet reduction later this year. Conditional acceleration if inflation doesn't moderate. This is a clear tightening signal. Score ~78. Anchor: 80-95 for 2022 aggressive hiking; this is the beginning so slightly below peak forward hawkishness.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants agreed that uncertainty regarding the path of inflation was elevated and that risks to inflation were weighted to the upside." "Uncertainty about real activity was also seen as elevated." Multiple risk factors cited (China zero-COVID, geopolitical turmoil, pandemic worsening, wage-price dynamics). "Current highly uncertain environment." Direction: clearly elevated. Magnitude: "elevated" uncertainty, multiple sources. Score ~55. Anchor: 40-50 range is where I'd expect moderate elevation; the explicit language about "elevated" uncertainty pushes to ~55.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (58) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (72): Both above 50, labor tighter than output gap. Makes sense -- labor market was tighter than goods/services output gap due to supply constraints and participation shortfall. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (68) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (78): Forward guidance is more hawkish than current action (still at zero). This divergence is expected -- they held rates but signaled aggressive future tightening. Coherent.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 75: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says significantly above target, broadening. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP 58: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says strong demand exceeding supply. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS 35: Higher = tighter. Evidence says "accommodative." Low score = loose. Matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 75 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Recent inflation readings had continued to significantly exceed the Committee's longer-run goal and elevated inflation was persisting longer than they had anticipated"
- "Some participants commented that elevated inflation had broadened beyond sectors most directly affected by those factors, bolstered in part by strong consumer demand"
- "Participants agreed that uncertainty regarding the path of inflation was elevated and that risks to inflation were weighted to the upside"

**Center of gravity**: Participants perceive inflation as substantially above target, broadening, and persisting longer than expected, with upside risks. However, they generally expect moderation over the year, tempering the score slightly below 2022 peak levels.

**Minority views**: "A few participants pointed to the possibility that structural factors that had contributed to low inflation in the previous decade... may reemerge" -- a mild downside counterpoint, reflected in the uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 58 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Indicators of economic activity and employment had continued to strengthen"
- "Supply chain bottlenecks and labor shortages had continued to limit businesses' ability to meet strong demand"
- "Economic growth for 2022 as a whole would be robust" (if Omicron dissipates quickly)

**Center of gravity**: Economy running above potential with strong demand outstripping constrained supply, though Omicron temporarily suppressing activity in Q1. Level of GDP above potential.

**Minority views**: Omicron downside risk and "a number of participants noted downside risks to the outlook" widen uncertainty slightly.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 72 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Labor market was very tight, including near-record rates of quits and job vacancies as well as nominal wage growth that was the highest recorded in decades"
- "Many participants commented that they viewed labor market conditions as already at or very close to those consistent with maximum employment"
- "Broad shortage of workers across many parts of the economy"

**Center of gravity**: Very tight labor market by multiple measures -- low unemployment, record vacancies/quits, rapid wage growth, and broad worker shortages. Most see conditions at or near maximum employment.

**Minority views**: "A couple of participants commented that, in their view, the economy likely had not yet reached maximum employment" citing participation rates still below pre-pandemic levels.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 58 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Many participants noted that the onset of the Omicron wave had damped consumer demand, particularly for services"
- "Almost all of those participants anticipated that household demand would recover briskly if the Omicron wave subsided quickly, with spending supported by strong household balance sheets"
- "Participants' contacts also reported continued widespread input cost pressures, which, amid generally robust demand, they reported having largely been able to pass on to their customers"

**Center of gravity**: Fundamentally strong demand, temporarily suppressed by Omicron. Robust household balance sheets, rising wages, and strong business demand underpin above-trend spending, but Q1 drag from Omicron and supply constraints limit realized investment and consumption.

**Minority views**: None significant; broad agreement on the temporary-drag-on-strong-fundamentals narrative.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 35 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Overall financial conditions had remained accommodative, in part reflecting policy measures to support the economy and the flow of credit to U.S. households and businesses"
- Staff review: "Market-based financing conditions remained accommodative, and bank lending standards eased for most loan categories"
- "Some participants commented on the risk that financial conditions might tighten unduly in response to a rapid removal of policy accommodation"

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions remain broadly accommodative across credit markets, though equity markets have pulled back and rates are beginning to move higher in anticipation of tightening. Still on the loose side of neutral.

**Minority views**: Concern about potential rapid tightening ahead reflects awareness that conditions could shift quickly.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 68 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants agreed that it would be appropriate for the Committee to keep the target range for the federal funds rate at 0 to 1/4 percent... They also anticipated that it would soon be appropriate to raise the target range"
- "Most participants suggested that a faster pace of increases in the target range for the federal funds rate than in the post-2015 period would likely be warranted"
- "A couple of participants stated that they favored ending the Committee's net asset purchases sooner to send an even stronger signal"

**Center of gravity**: Strong consensus for imminent rate liftoff and a faster pace of tightening than the post-2015 cycle. Balance sheet reduction also planned for later in the year. Held at zero this meeting but with very clear hawkish forward lean.

**Minority views**: A couple wanted even faster action (pushing hawkish); "some participants commented on the risk that financial conditions might tighten unduly" (mild caution). Net: narrow disagreement band.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 22 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All participants supported the proposed principles for reducing the size of the balance sheet. The Committee voted unanimously"
- "Most participants preferred to continue to reduce the Committee's net asset purchases according to the schedule announced in December" -- broad agreement
- Minor variation: "a couple of participants" wanted faster taper; "a couple" questioned whether max employment achieved

**Center of gravity**: Near-consensus on policy direction with only minor differences on pace and labor market assessment. No formal dissents. Broad agreement on the hawkish pivot.

**Minority views**: Small differences on timing/pace, not on direction.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 35 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "A few participants noted that asset valuations were elevated across a range of markets and raised the concern that a major realignment of asset prices could contribute to a future downturn"
- "Some participants saw emerging risks to financial stability associated with the rapid growth in crypto-assets and decentralized finance platforms"
- "A couple of other participants cited reasons why elevated asset valuations might prove to be less of a threat to financial stability"

**Center of gravity**: Moderate discussion of financial stability -- a dedicated paragraph with several participants weighing in on asset valuations, crypto risks, and leveraged nonbank institutions. Not a dominant concern but more than passing mention.

**Minority views**: Mixed views on severity of asset valuation concerns.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 78 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "It would soon be appropriate to raise the target range" -- imminent liftoff signal
- "Most participants suggested that a faster pace of increases in the target range for the federal funds rate than in the post-2015 period would likely be warranted"
- "Most participants noted that, if inflation does not move down as they expect, it would be appropriate for the Committee to remove policy accommodation at a faster pace than they currently anticipate"

**Center of gravity**: Clear and strong tightening forward signal. Imminent rate hikes, faster pace than prior cycle, balance sheet reduction on the horizon, and conditional acceleration clause. This is a strong hawkish forward commitment just short of the peak 2022 levels (when they were actually executing 75bp hikes).

**Minority views**: "Some participants commented on the risk that financial conditions might tighten unduly" provides a slight dovish counterweight.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants agreed that uncertainty regarding the path of inflation was elevated and that risks to inflation were weighted to the upside"
- "Uncertainty about real activity was also seen as elevated"
- "Maintaining flexibility to implement appropriate policy adjustments on the basis of risk-management considerations should be a guiding principle in conducting policy in the current highly uncertain environment"

**Center of gravity**: Elevated uncertainty explicitly acknowledged across both inflation and real activity. Multiple specific risk factors enumerated (China zero-COVID, geopolitics, pandemic variants, wage-price dynamics). Not extreme or unprecedented but clearly above normal.

**Minority views**: Broad agreement on elevated uncertainty; no significant divergence here.
