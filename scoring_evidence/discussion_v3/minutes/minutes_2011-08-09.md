# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2011-08-09 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented — clear "Participants' Views" section beginning at line 28.

**Era context**: August 2011. The U.S. economy was in a sluggish recovery from the Great Recession. GDP growth had slowed sharply in early 2011. The debt ceiling crisis was underway. European sovereign debt issues loomed. QE2 had just ended in June 2011. The Committee adopted explicit calendar-based forward guidance ("at least through mid-2013") at this meeting — a significant dovish move. Three dissents (Fisher, Kocherlakota, Plosser) against the new guidance.

**Quantifier distribution**: "most" (members agreed outlook deteriorated), "many" (downside risks, downward revision), "several" (adverse shocks, financial constraints), "some" (additional accommodation warranted, financial market strains, none of the tools would help much), "a few" (more substantial move, calendar-based guidance concerns, IOER reduction), "a couple" (long-term unemployment, cyclical impetus weaker).

**Dimension-by-dimension reasoning**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants note inflation "had moderated in recent months after having been somewhat elevated earlier this year." Transitory factors (energy, supply chains) pushed up prices but were waning. "Apparently significant slack," "slow wage growth," "little evidence of pricing power" — inflation likely to decline. But "a number of participants noted that core inflation had moved up, on balance, since last fall." Center of gravity: modestly above target recently but expected to fall. The current perception is slightly elevated but moderating. Score should be in the 51-58 range — inflation was "somewhat elevated" earlier, now moderating, with some noting core had risen. Direction: slightly above midpoint. Score ~53.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic growth so far this year was considerably slower than they had expected." "Deterioration in labor market conditions." "Weakness in consumer spending." The economy is clearly below potential with significant slack. "Apparently significant slack in labor and product markets." This is firmly below 50. Given "considerably slower" and "significant slack," this is in the 30-38 range. Score ~33.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Overall labor market conditions had deteriorated in recent months." "Exceptionally high level of long-term unemployment." "Relatively weak conditions." "Deterioration in overall labor market conditions" from state/local job shedding. Clearly slack labor market. Anchors suggest 35-40 for 2011-14. Given the deterioration language, score ~33.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Most meeting participants indicated that the weakness in consumer spending in recent months was unexpected." "Flattening out of consumer spending." Business investment "continued to advance, although at a slower pace." Housing "quite subdued." This is below trend — sluggish, unexpected weakness. Score ~33.

5. **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Financial markets were volatile." "Broad declines in stock prices." "Reduced willingness of investors to bear risk." "Conditions in funding markets had tightened." But also: "condition of U.S. banks had strengthened" and "credit quality...had continued to improve." "Still-tight credit conditions" restraining consumers. Somewhat tight conditions. Score ~58.

6. **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Most members agreed that the economic outlook had deteriorated by enough to warrant a Committee response." They adopted forward guidance ("at least through mid-2013"). "Some members expressed the view that additional accommodation was warranted." "A few members felt that recent economic developments justified a more substantial move." But "three members dissented because they preferred to retain the forward guidance language" — i.e., they opposed the dovish shift. The center of gravity is clearly dovish — providing additional forward guidance accommodation. This is QE-era dovish territory, anchors suggest 15-25. But this meeting didn't do QE — it did forward guidance. "Some" thought none of the tools would help. Score ~25.

7. **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Three formal dissents (Fisher, Kocherlakota, Plosser) — very unusual. "A few members felt that recent economic developments justified a more substantial move" (wanting more). "Some participants judged that none of the tools available...would likely do much." Clear camps: dovish majority vs. hawkish dissenters, plus some wanting even more accommodation. Three dissents is historically rare. Score ~70.

8. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial markets discussed — volatility, stock declines, European situation, funding market tightening, bank conditions. It's a moderate discussion but not the dominant topic. "Several participants also pointed to financial constraints." The European situation mentioned as a potential shock. Score ~42.

9. **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The Committee adopted "at least through mid-2013" language — explicit date-based forward guidance for near-zero rates. This is a strong easing commitment. Per the anchor table, this is in the 1-5 range for "threshold-based or date-based guidance promising near-zero rates." Score ~5.

10. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The degree of uncertainty surrounding the outlook for economic growth as having risen appreciably." "Many participants also saw an increase in the downside risks." "The underlying strength of the economic recovery remained uncertain." "Uncertainty about the economic outlook, regulatory policy, and fiscal policy continued to restrain hiring." "Depressed business confidence as well as uncertainty." Very prominent uncertainty theme. Score ~65.

**Polarity checks**:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says moderating but still slightly elevated. 53 = slightly above midpoint. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says significantly below potential. 33 = below midpoint. Matches.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says deteriorating, slack. 33 = loose. Matches.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. "At least through mid-2013" is extremely dovish. 5 = very dovish. Matches.

**Cross-dimension coherence**:
- OUTPUT_GAP (33) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (33): Both well below 50, consistent with significant slack. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (25) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (5): Both dovish, but forward guidance is more extreme. This makes sense: the current action was moderate (forward guidance, not QE), but the forward signal itself ("at least through mid-2013") is a very strong easing commitment. The divergence is explained by the forward guidance being explicitly date-based and extending nearly two years out.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 53 ±8 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Inflation had moderated in recent months after having been somewhat elevated earlier this year." "With apparently significant slack in labor and product markets, slow wage growth, and little evidence of pricing power among firms, inflation was likely to decline somewhat over time." "A number of participants noted that core inflation had moved up, on balance, since last fall."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Participants see inflation as recently somewhat elevated but moderating, with transitory factors waning. Net assessment is slightly above the balanced midpoint given the recent rise in core inflation.
- **Minority views**: "A couple of others...suggested that the juxtaposition of higher core inflation and somewhat lower unemployment could imply that the level of potential output was lower than had been thought" — implying more persistent inflation pressure.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 33 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Economic growth so far this year was considerably slower than they had expected." "Apparently significant slack in labor and product markets." "Many participants pointed to the recent downward revision to estimates of economic activity over the past three years."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Economy is clearly operating well below potential, with significant slack and a deteriorating trajectory. "Considerably slower" growth with unexpected weakness.
- **Minority views**: None pushing above potential. Some noted potential output itself may be lower, which would narrow the gap somewhat.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 33 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Overall labor market conditions had deteriorated in recent months." "Exceptionally high level of long-term unemployment." "The employment report for July...was still seen as indicating relatively weak conditions."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Significant labor market slack with deterioration. Well below full employment.
- **Minority views**: Narrow range of views; all see weakness, disagreements are about causes (skills mismatch vs. cyclical).

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 33 ±5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Most meeting participants indicated that the weakness in consumer spending in recent months was unexpected." "Business outlays on equipment and software continued to advance, although at a slower pace." "Housing construction was quite subdued."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending unexpectedly weak, business investment slowing, housing subdued. Below trend across the board.
- **Minority views**: Some expected auto sales to pick up as supply chain disruptions eased, providing slight upside.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 58 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Financial markets were volatile over the intermeeting period." "Conditions in funding markets had tightened." "Several participants also pointed to financial constraints, particularly depressed home prices and still-tight credit conditions."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions somewhat tight — funding markets tightened, credit still constrained, volatility elevated. But banks strengthening and credit quality improving provide partial offset.
- **Minority views**: "The condition of U.S. banks had strengthened in recent quarters and...credit quality of both businesses and households had continued to improve" — a partially offsetting signal.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 25 ±12 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Most members agreed that the economic outlook had deteriorated by enough to warrant a Committee response." "Some members expressed the view that additional accommodation was warranted." "A few members felt that recent economic developments justified a more substantial move." "Three members dissented because they preferred to retain the forward guidance language."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Clearly dovish — majority favored additional accommodation via strengthened forward guidance. QE-era meeting focused on tools for further easing.
- **Minority views**: Three dissenters opposed the dovish shift; "some participants judged that none of the tools available...would likely do much to promote a faster economic recovery." Wide band reflects this substantial dissent.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 70 ±7 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Three members dissented because they preferred to retain the forward guidance language employed in the June statement." "A few members felt that recent economic developments justified a more substantial move." "Some participants judged that none of the tools available to the Committee would likely do much."
- **Center-of-gravity**: High disagreement — three formal dissents (rare), plus a faction wanting even stronger action. Clear camps with different policy prescriptions.
- **Minority views**: The three dissenters represent a significant hawkish minority. The "more substantial move" faction represents a dovish minority.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 42 ±7 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Financial markets were volatile over the intermeeting period, as investors responded to news on the European fiscal situation and the negotiations regarding the debt ceiling." "Broad declines in stock prices and interest rates." "Potential financial and economic spillovers if the situation in Europe were to deteriorate."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Moderate discussion of financial conditions — market volatility, European risks, and funding market tightening discussed by several participants, but not the dominant theme.
- **Minority views**: N/A.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 5 ±3 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The Committee agreed...to state that economic conditions are likely to warrant exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate at least through mid-2013." "Reinforcing the Committee's forward guidance about the likely path of monetary policy was seen as a possible way to reduce interest rates."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Explicit date-based forward guidance committing to near-zero rates for nearly two more years — the strongest form of easing guidance short of threshold-based rules.
- **Minority views**: Three dissenters opposed this language. "A few participants emphasized that guidance focusing solely on the state of the economy would be preferable to guidance that named specific spans of time."

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 65 ±7 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The degree of uncertainty surrounding the outlook for economic growth as having risen appreciably." "Many participants also saw an increase in the downside risks to economic growth." "The underlying strength of the economic recovery remained uncertain."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Elevated uncertainty — participants explicitly noted it had risen "appreciably," with many seeing increased downside risks. Multiple sources of uncertainty cited (fiscal, European, household conditions).
- **Minority views**: Narrow — broad agreement on elevated uncertainty.
