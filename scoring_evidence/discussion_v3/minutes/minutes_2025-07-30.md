# FOMC Discussion Scoring — July 29-30, 2025 Minutes

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented format — participant discussion section is clearly delineated from staff review sections.

**Era context**: Mid-2025. The U.S. economy is dealing with the aftermath of significant tariff increases earlier in the year. GDP growth slowed in H1 2025, with a decline in Q1 followed by tepid growth in Q2. The labor market remains solid with low unemployment (4.1%), but there are signs of softening demand. Inflation is somewhat above 2% (PCE estimated at 2.5%, core at 2.7%), with tariffs putting upward pressure on goods prices. The fed funds rate is at 4.25-4.50%, held steady at this meeting.

**Quantifier distribution**: Key quantifiers observed:
- "Almost all" (hold rate, well-positioned)
- "Many" (inflation above goal, tariff lag effects, discussed stablecoins)
- "Several" (unemployment rate low hiring/low layoffs, growth to remain low, business investment slowed, uncertainty reduction, risks balanced, balance sheet)
- "Some" (contacts reluctant to hire/fire, softening labor demand indicators, economic activity supported by financial conditions, wait for clarity, tariff persistence depends on monetary policy)
- "A few" (tariff effects masking underlying trend, foreign exporters paying modest part, one-time price level increase, tariff-related stubborn inflation, immigration affecting labor supply, housing demand weakening, agriculture headwinds, Treasury market vulnerabilities)
- "A couple of" (tariff effects masking trend, activity supported by policy resolution, banks vulnerable to rate risk)
- "A majority of" (upside risk to inflation as greater risk)

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as above target. "Many participants observed that overall inflation remained somewhat above the Committee's 2 percent longer-run goal." Tariff effects becoming more apparent in goods prices, though services price inflation continued to slow. A couple noted that setting aside tariff effects, inflation was close to target. Participants "generally expected inflation to increase in the near term." This is clearly above target with upward pressure from tariffs — "somewhat elevated" language maps to 55-62 range. The near-term expectation of increase and "considerable uncertainty" about persistence pushes toward the higher end. Score: 58. Higher = more pressure. Evidence says above target with tariffs adding pressure. Matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Growth of economic activity slowed in the first half of the year." GDP was "tepid" in H1. Several participants stated they expected growth to remain low in H2. However, no language about "below potential" explicitly from participants. The unemployment rate is low and labor market is "at or near maximum employment." This is a mixed picture — growth slowing but still near potential. The economy is not clearly above or below potential; it is near potential but with momentum clearly decelerating. Score: 46. The slowing growth and "tepid pace" suggest slightly below potential, but the labor market at maximum employment prevents a lower score.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Unemployment rate remained low and that employment was at or near estimates of maximum employment." Several participants noted low hiring and low layoffs combination. Some noted indicators suggesting softening in labor demand (slower job growth, rising Black/youth unemployment, lower job-switcher wages). But the overall assessment is that the labor market is solid and near full employment. Score: 50. At full employment but with early signs of softening. The balance of evidence points to right at the NAIRU line, perhaps fractionally below given the softening indicators.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Growth of economic activity slowed in the first half of the year, driven in large part by slower consumption growth and a decline in residential investment." Several participants expected growth to remain low. Business investment was slowed by policy uncertainty. Some noted supportive financial conditions (elevated household net worth, low delinquencies). Several noted slower real income growth weighing on consumer spending. A few noted weakening housing demand. This is below trend — "slowed," "tepid," "remain low." Score: 40. Below trend but not contracting.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Financial conditions are described with some ambiguity. Credit spreads narrowed to "exceptionally low levels." Equity prices increased. Borrowing costs "eased moderately but remained elevated relative to post-GFC average levels." Credit "remained generally available." Several participants noted the current target range "may not be far above its neutral level" and that "broader financial conditions were either neutral or supportive of stronger economic activity." This suggests conditions are not particularly tight despite the elevated rate. Score: 48. Near neutral — rates still somewhat elevated but credit spreads tight and conditions generally supportive.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Almost all participants viewed it as appropriate to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 4¼ to 4½ percent." The Committee is in a clear hold posture. "Almost all participants agreed that, with the labor market still solid and current monetary policy moderately or modestly restrictive, the Committee was well positioned to respond in a timely way." A majority judged upside risk to inflation as the greater risk. The scenario analysis is symmetric — they'd tighten if inflation worsens or ease if labor weakens. Some noted the rate may not be far above neutral. The overall lean is a cautious hold with a slight hawkish tilt given the majority's emphasis on inflation risk as the greater concern. Score: 52. Lean slightly hawkish — holding steady with inflation risk as primary concern, but no appetite for hiking; it is a wait-and-see with mild hawkish lean.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Almost all" favored holding the rate — near consensus on the action. The risk assessment shows "a majority" saw inflation risk as greater, "several" saw risks as balanced, and "a couple" saw employment as more salient. There are differences in emphasis but broad agreement on the hold decision. Score: 25. Broad agreement on action with some variation in risk assessment.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Participants had a dedicated discussion of financial stability. Several noted elevated asset valuation pressures. A couple discussed bank vulnerabilities. A few raised Treasury market concerns. Many discussed stablecoins and GENIUS Act implications. This is a meaningful, multi-topic financial stability discussion. Score: 45. Moderate discussion — several participants addressed multiple financial stability topics meaningfully.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is data-dependent with no directional lean. "Well positioned to respond in a timely way to potential economic developments." Policy "would be informed by a wide range of incoming data, the economic outlook, and the balance of risks." Symmetric scenario analysis (tighten if inflation rises, ease if labor weakens). Some noted it would "not be feasible or appropriate to wait for complete clarity on the tariffs' effects on inflation before adjusting." Several noted the rate "may not be far above its neutral level." The forward tone is essentially neutral/data-dependent with perhaps a very slight easing bias from those noting nearness to neutral and the "don't wait for complete clarity" language. Score: 48. Near neutral, very slight dovish lean in forward signal.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants judged that uncertainty about the economic outlook remained elevated." "Considerable uncertainty remained about the timing, magnitude, and persistence of the effects of this year's increase in tariffs." Several noted some reduction in uncertainty regarding fiscal/immigration/tariff policy. A majority saw upside risk to inflation and downside risk to employment — two-sided risks. This is elevated uncertainty. Score: 62. Elevated, with "considerable uncertainty" and "remained elevated" language, though somewhat reduced from prior meetings.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (46) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (50): Consistent — both near potential/neutral, with output gap slightly below reflecting the growth slowdown while labor market is still at full employment. The divergence is explained by the lag between activity slowing and labor market deterioration.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (52) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (48): Very close, essentially neutral. The slight divergence (current lean fractionally hawkish, forward signal fractionally dovish) reflects the majority's inflation concern at present but the recognition that rates may be near neutral and the asymmetric risk to employment in the forward-looking assessment.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 58 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "many participants observed that overall inflation remained somewhat above the Committee's 2 percent longer-run goal"
- "tariff effects were becoming more apparent in the data, as indicated by recent increases in goods price inflation, while services price inflation had continued to slow"
- "participants generally expected inflation to increase in the near term"

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation is somewhat above target at 2.5-2.7% PCE, with tariffs adding upward pressure on goods prices and near-term inflation expected to rise further, though services inflation is slowing.

**Minority views**: A couple of participants suggested that setting aside tariff effects, inflation was close to target. A few emphasized tariff effects could lead to stubbornly elevated inflation. Uncertainty band reflects these divergent views on persistence. ±7.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 46 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "growth of economic activity slowed in the first half of the year"
- "Several participants stated that they expected growth in economic activity to remain low in the second half of this year"
- "the unemployment rate remained at a low level and that the labor market was at or near maximum employment"

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is operating slightly below potential — growth has been tepid and is expected to remain low, though the labor market has not yet deteriorated significantly.

**Minority views**: Some participants noted economic activity would be supported by financial conditions (elevated household net worth) and resolution of policy uncertainty over time. ±6.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 50 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the unemployment rate remained low and that employment was at or near estimates of maximum employment"
- "Several participants noted that the low and stable unemployment rate reflected a combination of low hiring and low layoffs"
- "some participants mentioned indicators that could suggest a softening in labor demand" including "slower and more concentrated job growth, an increase in cyclically sensitive Black and youth unemployment rates, and lower wage increases of job switchers"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is at full employment but balanced between low unemployment and emerging signs of demand softening — near NAIRU.

**Minority views**: A number of participants noted softness in aggregate demand could translate into weaker labor market conditions. A few noted immigration policies were affecting labor supply. ±6.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 40 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "growth of economic activity slowed in the first half of the year, driven in large part by slower consumption growth and a decline in residential investment"
- "several participants remarked that ongoing policy uncertainty had continued to slow business investment"
- "several participants observed that slower real income growth may be weighing on growth in consumer spending"

**Center-of-gravity**: Spending and investment are below trend — consumption growth has slowed, residential investment declined, and business investment is being held back by policy uncertainty.

**Minority views**: Some noted supportive factors (elevated household net worth, low delinquencies); several observed business sentiment had improved in recent months. ±6.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 48 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "borrowing costs facing businesses, households, and municipalities eased moderately but remained elevated relative to post-GFC average levels"
- Credit spreads narrowed to "exceptionally low levels relative to their historical distribution"
- Several participants noted "broader financial conditions were either neutral or supportive of stronger economic activity"
- The current rate "may not be far above its neutral level"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are near neutral — borrowing costs remain somewhat elevated, but credit is available, spreads are tight, and equity prices have risen. Overall conditions described as neutral to supportive.

**Minority views**: Potential downside risk from "a possible tightening of financial conditions due to a rise in risk premiums" was mentioned. ±7.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 52 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Almost all participants viewed it as appropriate to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 4¼ to 4½ percent"
- "Almost all participants agreed that, with the labor market still solid and current monetary policy moderately or modestly restrictive, the Committee was well positioned to respond in a timely way"
- "A majority of participants judged the upside risk to inflation as the greater of these two risks"

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear hold posture with a very slight hawkish lean — the majority views inflation risk as the more salient concern, but there is no appetite for rate increases. The Committee is in wait-and-see mode.

**Minority views**: Several participants viewed risks as roughly balanced; a couple considered downside risk to employment as more salient. ±5.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 25 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Almost all participants viewed it as appropriate to maintain the target range" — near consensus on action
- Risk assessment shows variation: "a majority" vs. "several" vs. "a couple" on which risk is greater
- "Some participants stressed that the issue of the persistence of tariff effects on inflation would depend importantly on the stance of monetary policy"

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on holding rates, with moderate differences in risk emphasis. No formal dissents implied.

**Minority views**: Minor variation in risk assessment across participants, but no sharp divisions. ±5.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 45 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "participants who commented noted vulnerabilities to the financial system that they assessed warranted monitoring"
- "Several participants noted concerns about elevated asset valuation pressures"
- "Many participants discussed recent and prospective developments related to payment stablecoins and possible implications for the financial system"
- A few commented on Treasury market vulnerabilities, bank interest rate risk

**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate financial stability discussion — multiple topics addressed (asset valuations, bank risks, Treasury market, stablecoins) by several to many participants.

**Minority views**: N/A — this is an attention measure. ±6.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 48 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "well positioned to respond in a timely way to potential economic developments"
- "monetary policy would be informed by a wide range of incoming data, the economic outlook, and the balance of risks"
- "Several participants commented that the current target range for the federal funds rate may not be far above its neutral level"
- Symmetric scenario: tighten if inflation worsens, ease if labor weakens

**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal is data-dependent and essentially neutral, with a very slight dovish lean from the recognition that rates may be near neutral and some emphasis on not needing to wait for "complete clarity" before adjusting.

**Minority views**: Some emphasized waiting for more data; the majority's inflation focus provides mild offsetting hawkish element. ±6.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 62 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "participants judged that uncertainty about the economic outlook remained elevated"
- "considerable uncertainty remained about the timing, magnitude, and persistence of the effects of this year's increase in tariffs"
- "several participants remarked that there had been some reduction in uncertainty regarding fiscal policy, immigration policy, or tariff policy"

**Center-of-gravity**: Elevated uncertainty — "remained elevated" and "considerable uncertainty" are the operative phrases, though with some improvement from prior meetings.

**Minority views**: Several noted some reduction in uncertainty, which moderates the score slightly. ±5.

## OPTIONAL DIMENSIONS

### INFLATION_GOODS: 62 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "tariff effects were becoming more apparent in the data, as indicated by recent increases in goods price inflation"
- "evidence so far suggested that foreign exporters were paying at most a modest part of the increased tariffs, implying that domestic businesses and consumers were predominantly bearing the tariff costs"

**Center-of-gravity**: Goods price inflation is clearly rising due to tariffs, with further increases expected.

---

### INFLATION_SERVICES: 46 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "services price inflation had continued to slow"
- But participants noted "it could take some time for the full effects of higher tariffs to be felt in consumer goods and services prices"

**Center-of-gravity**: Services inflation is slowing (below midpoint) though pass-through from tariffs could push it up later.

---

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 48 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "longer-term inflation expectations continued to be well anchored and that it was important that they remain so"
- "Several participants emphasized that inflation had exceeded 2 percent for an extended period and that this experience increased the risk of longer-term inflation expectations becoming unanchored"

**Center-of-gravity**: Expectations anchored at target but with some risk of unanchoring. Near 50 but with downside tail risk.
