The unemployment rate was still elevated but declining. Inflation was running below the 2% target. Chairman Bernanke's post-meeting press conference triggered a sharp rise in market rates. The discussion reflects participants debating how to communicate potential tapering of asset purchases.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 30 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's longer-run objective, but longer-term inflation expectations remained stable."
- "The trends in all broad measures remaining below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective... participants cited various alternative measures of consumer price inflation... that suggested that the slowing was broad based."
- "Many anticipated that it would remain below the Committee's 2 percent objective for some time."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants broadly recognized that inflation was running below the 2% target on a broad-based basis, with most expecting it to remain below target for some time, placing this firmly in the "below target; disinflation" range.

**Minority views**: One participant worried about "the risk of a more rapid rise in inflation over the medium term," widening the uncertainty band upward slightly.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 38 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The economy was expanding at a moderate pace."
- "Most participants still saw slack remaining in the labor market, although they differed on the extent to which the progress to date had reduced that slack."
- "Growth of economic activity was projected to strengthen further during 2014 and 2015."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy was growing moderately but still below potential, with recognized slack remaining; participants expected the gap to close gradually over the next two years.

**Minority views**: Some saw more progress in closing the gap ("cumulative decline in the unemployment rate... as considerable") while others saw less ("uncertain about the projected pickup in growth").

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 35 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Most participants still saw slack remaining in the labor market."
- "The unemployment rate remained elevated."
- "The cumulative decline in the unemployment rate and gains in nonfarm payrolls over the past nine months as considerable."
- "The rate of hiring still fell short of the pace... consistent with more-noticeable progress in labor market conditions."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The labor market was improving but still had significant slack, with elevated unemployment, weak participation, and insufficient hiring pace -- placing it below full employment but with acknowledged improvement.

**Minority views**: Participants "differed on the extent to which the progress to date had reduced that slack"; some saw more encouraging signs (breadth of job gains, decline in layoffs) while others emphasized long-duration joblessness and participation weakness.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 40 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending rose at a moderate rate, and the housing sector continued to strengthen."
- "Business investment advanced, although only modestly."
- "Reports on business spending were mixed. A number of participants continued to hear that businesses were limiting their capital spending... and that they remained reluctant to invest to expand capacity."
- "The manufacturing sector slowed considerably during the spring."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumer spending was moderate and housing was strong, but business investment was only modest and manufacturing slowed considerably, producing a net picture slightly below trend.

**Minority views**: "A few participants reported that their business contacts expressed somewhat greater confidence in the economic outlook or reported plans to expand capacity" -- a modestly more optimistic minority.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 45 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:

- "Several participants worried that higher mortgage rates and bond yields could slow the recovery."
- "Corporate credit spreads had not widened substantially and... the stock market had posted further gains."
- "Any adverse effects of the increase in rates on financial conditions more broadly appeared to be limited."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions were tightening from very accommodative levels due to the rate rise, but credit spreads remained narrow and equities gained, placing overall conditions near neutral -- slightly loose side.

**Minority views**: Several worried about the impact of higher rates while others saw limited adverse effects, producing a moderate uncertainty band.

**Evidence**:

- "The discussion centered on the possibility of providing a rough description of the path for asset purchases that the Committee would anticipate implementing."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The committee was at the inflection point of discussing reducing the pace of asset purchases, signaling a move away from maximum accommodation. But with inflation below target and slack remaining, the lean was still modestly dovish overall.

**Minority views**: Views ranged from those ready to begin tapering imminently to those worried that low inflation and growth uncertainty warranted continued accommodation, producing a wide band.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal remained accommodative -- QE was ongoing, threshold-based rate guidance was still in place -- but the discussion about communicating a tapering path represented an initial move toward less accommodation, placing this in the taper-era range.

**Minority views**: Some wanted more explicit numerical-based forward guidance (more dovish signal) while others favored the qualitative approach that could allow more flexibility.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty, with specific concerns about growth forecasts proving too optimistic (given past experience) and fiscal policy effects, partially offset by growing confidence among many participants.
