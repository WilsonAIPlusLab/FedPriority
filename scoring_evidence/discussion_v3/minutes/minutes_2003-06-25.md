# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2003-06-25

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Unsegmented pre-2009 format — staff and participant content potentially mixed, but this excerpt is clearly participant discussion (uses "members" language throughout).

**Era context**: June 2003. The economy was in a sluggish recovery from the 2001 recession. The fed funds rate was being cut to 1% at this meeting (from 1.25%). Deflation risk was a live concern. Iraq war had recently concluded major operations. Fiscal stimulus (tax cuts) just enacted.

**Quantifier distribution**: "members" (general consensus) used frequently; "most" (preferred 25bp); "some" (various reservations); "several" (communicating clearly); "all but one" (support for cut); "one member" (preferred no change). Broad agreement on the economic picture with modest differences on policy calibration.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as clearly below target and possibly falling further. "Currently elevated levels of unused labor and other resources" keeping inflation down. PCE forecasts centered on 1.25-1.5% for 2003 and 1-1.5% for 2004 — well below 2%. "Further disinflation seemed likely to be a more significant concern than rising inflation for a considerable period." "Inflation could be approaching a level that would begin to complicate the implementation of monetary policy." This is active deflation concern territory. Direction: well below midpoint. Score should be in 11-25 range. I'll score 18 — meaningful deflation risk, "subdued" with concern about further decline, but not deflationary spiral. Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says low and falling inflation. Score of 18 = low inflation pressure. Matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economy thought likely to continue to operate below its potential for an extended period." "Currently large margins of unemployed labor and other resources." GDP growth forecast of 2.5-2.75% for 2003 suggests slow recovery. "Sluggish economic expansion." "No conclusive evidence of an appreciable overall strengthening." Bright-line rule: "below potential" language means score MUST be < 50. Direction: clearly below potential with substantial slack. Score ~30 — below potential, recovering but gap persists, "slack remains." The economy is not in deep recession (that was 2001) but the gap is large and persistent. Score 32.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment forecast 6-6.25% for Q4 2003, 5.5-6% for Q4 2004. "Persisting business reluctance to hire new workers." "Lack of significant job growth." "Currently large margins of unemployed labor." Labor market is clearly slack. Direction: well below full employment. Score ~28 — significant slack, unemployment elevated, "far from full employment" territory. The 6%+ unemployment rate and persistent hiring reluctance supports low-30s/high-20s.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending "though elevated, had grown at a reduced pace." Housing "notably robust." But business fixed investment is the critical drag — "significant recovery had thus far failed to materialize." "Investment outlays tended to be limited to the replacement and upgrading of existing facilities rather than expansion." Net: consumer spending sluggish but not contracting, investment weak. "Faint signs of more positive investment prospects, though not of currently increasing investment expenditures." Direction: below trend overall. Score ~35 — "sluggish" with housing as a bright spot but business investment a significant drag.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Much improved financial conditions." "Accommodative monetary and fiscal policies along with much improved financial conditions." "Significant declines in interest rates across maturities and risk classes." "Rising prices in equity markets." "Narrowing of risk spreads." "Low cost and ample availability of financing for most business firms." "Generally improving financial conditions." Direction: conditions are clearly loose/supportive. Score ~22 — loose, "accommodative conditions," "credit freely available."

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: They cut 25bp to 1%. "All but one" supported the cut. "Some commented that a good case could be made for a 50bp easing." "One member...saw merit in keeping policy unchanged but did not oppose." The balance of risks was "tilted toward the downside." "Further disinflation seemed likely to be a more significant concern than rising inflation." This is clearly dovish — cutting rates, with some wanting more. Direction: dovish. Score ~25 — strong dovish, majority favors easing, actually implementing easing. The rate is going to 1%, near the zero bound. Score 22.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All but one" supported the cut. One member preferred no change but didn't oppose. "Most" preferred 25bp, "some" could make case for 50bp. This is a range of views but broad agreement on easing. No formal dissent mentioned (the one member "did not oppose"). Score ~25 — broad agreement with minor emphasis differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial conditions discussed as supportive/accommodative. "Rising equity markets," "narrowing risk spreads," balance sheet strengthening. But this is context for the economic outlook, not financial stability concern per se. No mention of bubbles, systemic risk, or financial fragility. Discussion of financial conditions is present but as a positive backdrop, not a stability concern. Score ~20 — brief mentions, discussed as context.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Balance of risks to the Committee's dual goals was tilted toward the downside for the foreseeable future." "A signal that the Committee had come to the end of its policy easing moves--a judgment they were not prepared to make at this time." They explicitly did NOT want to signal they were done easing. "Probability of appreciable further disinflation...exceeded the probability of a rise in inflation." Direction: easing bias forward. Score ~18 — strong easing bias, signal of continued accommodation, unwillingness to signal end of easing.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Timing and dimensions of a significantly improved economic performance remained uncertain." "Such an outcome was still a forecast whose eventual realization, including both its timing and extent, remained uncertain." "Despite some differences with regard to the timing and strength of the anticipated upturn." Members generally expect improvement but timing is uncertain. This is moderate uncertainty — they have a base case (recovery) but timing is unsure. Score ~42 — somewhat elevated uncertainty.

- INFLATION_PRESSURE (18) low pairs with dovish policy lean (22). Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 18 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The output gap would undoubtedly preclude any significant acceleration in inflation and could well cause inflation to edge down from its already low level."
- "Forecasts of inflation...pointed to the persistence of quite low inflation rates centered on ranges of 1-1/4 to 1-1/2 percent for this year and 1 to 1-1/2 percent in 2004."
- "Further disinflation seemed likely to be a more significant concern than rising inflation for a considerable period of time."

**Center of gravity**: Members see inflation as well below target with active concern about further disinflation. PCE forecasts of ~1.25% are significantly below the 2% target, and the risk is tilted toward even lower inflation.

**Minority views**: "At least some members" noted recent developments had "reduced the unwelcome prospect of substantial additional disinflation" — including an uptick in core prices, dollar decline, and anchored expectations. This provides modest upside to the band.

---

### OUTPUT_GAP: 32 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Members referred to signs of improvement in some sectors of the economy, but they saw no conclusive evidence of an appreciable overall strengthening in the sluggish economic expansion."
- "The economy thought likely to continue to operate below its potential for an extended period."
- "Currently large margins of unemployed labor and other resources."

**Center of gravity**: The economy is operating well below potential with a large, persistent output gap. Recovery is expected but has not yet materialized convincingly.

**Minority views**: Members agreed on the direction; differences were about timing of recovery, not the current state.

---

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 28 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Currently large margins of unemployed labor."
- "Lack of significant job growth resulting from persisting business reluctance to hire new workers."
- Unemployment forecasts of 6-6.25% for Q4 2003.

**Center of gravity**: Labor market is clearly slack with elevated unemployment and weak hiring. Business caution on hiring is a persistent theme.

**Minority views**: "Indications that labor and product markets might be stabilizing" — slight positive signal but no one argues tightness.

---

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 35 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Weakness persisted in business capital expenditures, which members continued to view as the critical factor inhibiting the economic expansion."
- "Consumer spending, though elevated, had grown at a reduced pace in recent quarters."
- "Investment outlays tended to be limited to the replacement and upgrading of existing facilities rather than expansion."
- Housing described as "notably robust" — a bright spot.

**Center of gravity**: Below trend overall. Business investment is the key drag, consumer spending is tepid, and housing is the one strong sector. Net assessment is sluggish.

**Minority views**: Some members cited "faint signs of more positive investment prospects" and expected fiscal stimulus to boost spending. The uncertainty band reflects possible near-term improvement.

---

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 22 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Accommodative monetary and fiscal policies along with much improved financial conditions were likely to foster a better economic performance."
- "Significant declines in interest rates across maturities and risk classes, and to rising prices in equity markets."
- "Low cost and ample availability of financing for most business firms."
- "A narrowing of risk spreads."

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly loose and accommodative. Interest rates are low, spreads are narrowing, equity markets are rising, and credit is available.

**Minority views**: None dissenting from this characterization.

---

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 22 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All but one of the members indicated that they could support a proposal to reduce the target federal funds rate 1/4 percentage point to a level of 1 percent."
- "Some commented that a good case could be made for a 1/2 percentage point easing."
- "The balance of risks to the Committee's dual goals was tilted toward the downside for the foreseeable future."

**Center of gravity**: Strong dovish lean. Cutting rates to 1% with some wanting even more. No one is arguing for tightening. The risk balance is tilted to the downside.

**Minority views**: One member "saw merit in keeping policy unchanged but did not oppose" the cut. Some preferred 50bp. The range runs from hold to 50bp cut, centering on 25bp cut with continued easing bias.

---

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 25 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All but one of the members indicated that they could support" the 25bp cut.
- "Most of the members expressed a preference for limiting the reduction to 1/4 percentage point."
- One member preferred no change but "did not oppose." Some could make a case for 50bp but accepted 25bp.

**Center of gravity**: Broad agreement on the direction (easing) with differences about magnitude. No formal dissent. The one dissenter did not even formally oppose. This is a low-dissent meeting.

**Minority views**: The 25bp vs. 50bp debate and the one hold-preferring member create a modest spread.

---

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 20 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Financial conditions discussed extensively but as a positive backdrop: "much improved financial conditions," "rising equity markets," "narrowing risk spreads."
- Balance sheet strengthening noted: "business firms and households had continued to take advantage of generally improving financial conditions to strengthen their balance sheets."
- No discussion of financial fragility, bubbles, or systemic risk.

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are discussed as context for the economic outlook, not as a stability concern. Attention level is limited — it is backdrop, not focus.

**Minority views**: None.

---

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 18 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The balance of risks to the Committee's dual goals was tilted toward the downside for the foreseeable future."
- A 50bp cut "associated with the communication of a Committee view that the risks...were balanced might be mistakenly interpreted...as a signal that the Committee had come to the end of its policy easing moves--a judgment they were not prepared to make at this time."
- "Growing market perceptions that monetary policy would remain stimulative for a longer period than previously anticipated."

**Center of gravity**: Strong easing bias forward. The Committee explicitly avoided signaling an end to easing. The forward signal is clearly in the accommodation direction.

**Minority views**: The one member who preferred no change represents a small upside risk to the signal.

---

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 42 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The timing and dimensions of a significantly improved economic performance remained uncertain."
- "Such an outcome was still a forecast whose eventual realization, including both its timing and extent, remained uncertain."
- "A likely but not inevitable prospect" (re: consumer spending acceleration).

**Center of gravity**: Members have a clear base case (recovery accelerating) but express genuine uncertainty about the timing and strength. This is somewhat elevated uncertainty — more than standard caveats but not deep uncertainty about the direction.

**Minority views**: Some members more confident about the upturn than others; the range of GDP forecasts (3.75-4.75% for 2004) shows meaningful dispersion.
