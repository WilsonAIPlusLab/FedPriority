# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2000-12-19

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format -- staff and participant content may be mixed, but this text is clearly participant-focused with quantifier language ("members commented," "all the members agreed," "some members," "a few").

**Era context**: December 2000 -- the end of the late-1990s/2000 boom. The economy is decelerating sharply from unsustainably high growth in H1 2000. The Fed had been hiking rates through May 2000 (to 6.5%). The tech bubble is deflating, equity markets declining, and the economy is tipping toward what would become the 2001 recession. The Committee is on the cusp of shifting from a tightening to an easing stance.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all the members" (2 uses), "most" / "almost all" (3 uses), "many" (1 use), "some" (5 uses), "a few" / "those few" (2 uses). Strong consensus language on the economic slowdown; minority language on desire for immediate easing.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as diminishing. "Core inflation had remained quiescent." "The risks of higher inflation had diminished materially." But labor markets "still very tight" and there are upside risks from labor costs, energy, dollar depreciation. On balance, the center of gravity is that inflation pressure is moderating toward acceptable levels but has not disappeared. "Subdued inflation was a reasonable prospect." This is slightly below the midpoint -- pressures were recently elevated but are coming down. Score around 45-48. Higher = more pressure. Evidence says pressure is easing from a modestly elevated level but not gone. ~46.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Significant slowing in the expansion of business activity." "The deceleration in the economy had occurred from an unsustainably high growth rate in the first half of the year." The economy had been running above potential and is now decelerating. Members still see "high level of resource utilization." The economy was above potential but is coming back toward it. Not yet below potential -- "moderate economic expansion" continues. Score should be slightly above 50 -- economy still above potential but gap closing fast. ~52-54.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor markets in general were still very tight and likely would remain taut relative to historical experience." Even with manufacturing softening, "many of the factory workers losing their jobs were readily finding employment elsewhere in what generally continued to be characterized as very tight labor markets across the country." Only "modest reduction in labor market strains in some areas." This is clearly tight -- "very tight" language. Score 65-70. Anchor for 1999-2000 is 65-75.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending slowing -- "slowing in the growth of consumer spending," "consumer sentiment seemed to have deteriorated appreciably," "retail sales were widely indicated to have softened." Business investment: "Growth in business expenditures for equipment and software had moderated substantially in recent months from very high rates." Inventory cutbacks significant. Housing softening in some regions. This is clearly below trend now -- spending and investment are decelerating notably. But still expanding, not contracting. Score around 35-40. "Well below that of earlier in the year" but still positive growth.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Tightening financial conditions in the form of declining equity prices and stricter credit terms for many business borrowers." "Sharp deterioration in the equity prices of high-tech companies." "Negative wealth effects of further declines in stock market prices." Conditions have tightened meaningfully. Score ~60-63. Somewhat tight to tight.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All members support unchanged policy (hold at 6.5%). But the balance of risks statement shifted to "economic weakness." "Those few members who expressed a preference for easing at this meeting" accepted delay. "The Committee should be prepared to respond promptly to indications of further weakness." The lean is clearly dovish -- holding steady but with strong easing bias going forward. This is a pivotal meeting where the tightening cycle is definitively over and easing is imminent. Score ~38-42. Lean dovish -- hold with clear easing bias.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissents. "All the members indicated that they could support an unchanged policy stance." But "those few members who expressed a preference for easing" and "one view" preferred balanced risks statement. Broad agreement with minor differences -- 0 dissents, a few preferred easing. Score ~22-28.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Declining equity prices discussed at moderate length -- effects on wealth, business investment, consumer confidence. "Declining equity prices and stricter credit terms for many business borrowers." Financial conditions are discussed meaningfully but as context for the economic slowdown, not as a standalone stability concern. Score ~35-42.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Shift to "risks weighted toward economic weakness." "The Committee should be prepared to respond promptly to indications of further weakness in the economy." "The intention of the Committee to act promptly in coming weeks, including the possibility of an easing move early in the intermeeting period." This is a clear easing signal forward. Score ~28-35. Easing lean with data dependence.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The extent to which such developments would persist and perhaps foster more aggressive retrenchment in consumer spending clearly was uncertain." "The critical issue was whether the expansion would stabilize near its recent growth rate or was continuing to slow." "Currently available information bearing on this issue was not sufficient" -- genuine uncertainty about the trajectory. "Prevailing uncertainties about the prospective performance of the economy." Somewhat elevated uncertainty. Score ~42-48.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (52) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (67): Diverge somewhat -- this makes sense because the economy is decelerating from above potential but labor markets lag. The output gap is closing but labor remains tight. Consistent with late-cycle dynamics.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (40) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (32): Both dovish-leaning. Forward guidance is somewhat more dovish because the explicit signal is toward easing. Current action is a hold. This alignment makes sense -- hold now but ease soon.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says pressure diminishing but not gone. 46 = slightly below midpoint. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Economy still above potential but closing. 53 = slightly above midpoint. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Conditions are tightening. 62 = above midpoint. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Committee is holding but leaning dovish. 40 = below midpoint. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 46 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Against the backdrop of slowing economic growth, core inflation had remained quiescent."
- "All the members agreed that the risks of higher inflation had diminished materially."
- "Labor markets in general were still very tight and likely would remain taut relative to historical experience" -- keeping some upside risk alive.
- "Subdued inflation was a reasonable prospect."

**Center of gravity**: Members see inflation as slightly below concerning levels -- recent pressures easing thanks to the slowdown, but tight labor markets and energy costs keep some residual upside risk.

**Minority views**: "Some members" noted risks from labor costs, medical costs, energy pass-through, and dollar depreciation, which keeps uncertainty moderate.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 53 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The deceleration in the economy had occurred from an unsustainably high growth rate in the first half of the year."
- "High level of resource utilization" still referenced as a factor.
- "Moderate economic expansion" still the baseline, but risks shifting to downside.

**Center of gravity**: The economy had been running well above potential but is decelerating back toward it. Still slightly above potential at the time of this meeting, given the "high level of resource utilization" language, but gap is closing fast.

**Minority views**: Some see growth stabilizing near current pace; the downside risk is that it continues to slow further below potential.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 67 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Labor markets in general were still very tight and likely would remain taut relative to historical experience."
- "Many of the factory workers losing their jobs were readily finding employment elsewhere in what generally continued to be characterized as very tight labor markets across the country."
- "Some members noted...a modest reduction in labor market strains in some areas and industries."

**Center of gravity**: Labor markets are clearly very tight, consistent with the late-1990s/2000 boom. Some softening at the margin in manufacturing, but the overall characterization is unambiguously tight.

**Minority views**: The modest softening in some areas and industries introduces a small downward band.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 37 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Slowing in the growth of consumer spending...consumer sentiment seemed to have deteriorated appreciably...retail sales were widely indicated to have softened."
- "Growth in business expenditures for equipment and software had moderated substantially in recent months from very high rates of increase."
- "Cutbacks in inventory investment or runoffs of existing inventories accounted for a significant part of the recent moderation."

**Center of gravity**: Spending and investment are clearly below trend and weakening. Consumer and business spending both decelerating meaningfully, though not contracting outright. The word "substantially" for the investment slowdown and "appreciably" for consumer sentiment declines place this well below the midpoint.

**Minority views**: Underlying employment/income trends still support expansion; nonresidential construction "well maintained" in many areas.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 62 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Tightening financial conditions in the form of declining equity prices and stricter credit terms for many business borrowers."
- "Sharp deterioration in the equity prices of high-tech companies."
- "Negative wealth effects of further declines in stock market prices" affecting consumers.

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions have tightened meaningfully through equity declines and tighter credit. This goes beyond neutral into clearly restrictive territory, though not at crisis levels.

**Minority views**: Mortgage rates declining, which provides some offset in the housing sector.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 40 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All the members indicated that they could support an unchanged policy stance, consistent with a federal funds rate averaging about 6-1/2 percent."
- "Most members believed the risks were now weighted toward conditions that could generate economic weakness."
- "Those few members who expressed a preference for easing at this meeting" accepted delay.
- "The Committee should be prepared to respond promptly to indications of further weakness."

**Center of gravity**: Hold steady with clear dovish/easing bias. The tightening cycle is over; the question is timing of the first ease. Most want to wait for confirming data, but the directional lean is unmistakably toward easing.

**Minority views**: "A few" wanted immediate easing (more dovish, ~30). "One view" preferred balanced risks (less dovish, ~48-50).

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 25 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All the members indicated that they could support an unchanged policy stance."
- "Those few members who expressed a preference for easing at this meeting...could accept a delay."
- "In one view...a statement of balanced risks would be preferable."

**Center of gravity**: Broad agreement on the hold decision with no formal dissents. A few preferred easing and one preferred a different risk statement, but all ultimately supported the consensus. Minor differences in emphasis and preferred timing.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 40 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Declining equity prices and stricter credit terms for many business borrowers" discussed as key factor affecting investment.
- "Negative wealth effects of further declines in stock market prices" discussed regarding consumers and housing.
- "The re-evaluation of prospects was most pronounced in the high-tech industries...disappointing sales and a better appreciation of risks had resulted in...sharp deterioration in the equity prices."

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are discussed meaningfully by several participants as a channel through which the economy is weakening. The tech bubble deflation is a significant topic. However, this is framed as an economic transmission channel rather than a systemic stability concern per se.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 32 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Shift from inflation-risk bias to "risks weighted toward conditions that could generate economic weakness."
- "The Committee should be prepared to respond promptly to indications of further weakness in the economy."
- "The possibility of an easing move early in the intermeeting period, should confirming information on weakening trends...emerge."

**Center of gravity**: Clear easing signal going forward, with data dependence. The forward guidance is explicitly toward easing, with even the possibility of intermeeting action discussed. This is a mild easing bias with data-dependent conditionality.

**Minority views**: "One view" preferred balanced risks statement, which would be a less dovish forward signal.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 45 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The critical issue was whether the expansion would stabilize near its recent growth rate or was continuing to slow."
- "The extent to which such developments would persist and perhaps foster more aggressive retrenchment in consumer spending clearly was uncertain."
- "Prevailing uncertainties about the prospective performance of the economy."
- "The currently available information bearing on this issue was not sufficient to warrant an easing at this point."

**Center of gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty. The Committee genuinely does not know whether the slowdown will stabilize or deepen. This is the primary reason for holding rather than easing -- they need more data to resolve the uncertainty.
