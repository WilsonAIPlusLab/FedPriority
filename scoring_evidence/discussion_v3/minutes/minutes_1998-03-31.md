# FOMC Meeting Scoring — 1998-03-31 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format — staff and participant content are mixed. I will score only portions with participant language cues ("members commented," "members observed," "several members," etc.) and ignore staff-attributable content.

**Era context**: Late 1990s boom. Strong economic growth, tight labor markets, low inflation due to favorable supply shocks (Asian crisis, dollar appreciation, low energy prices, productivity gains). Fed funds rate at 5.5%. Asian financial crisis is an ongoing concern but effects on the U.S. appear limited so far.

**Quantifier distribution**: "members" (general, implying most/all), "a number" (several), "some," "several," "one member" (Jordan dissent). The dominant tone is collective agreement with minor variation.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as currently very low — "price inflation remained quite low — in fact, it was still declining by some measures." However, they express growing concern about future inflation risks: "the risk that inflation might move higher over the longer run seemed to have increased." Special factors (oil prices, dollar, health costs) are temporarily holding inflation down. The center of gravity is: inflation is low NOW but risks are tilting upward. Current conditions are clearly below target/trend with forward concern. Score should reflect current low readings with acknowledged upside risk — approximately 38-42. Direction: below midpoint because inflation IS low, but upside risks pull it somewhat toward center.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "unusually favorable economic conditions, characterized by strong growth"; "Domestic demand was exceeding expectations and was likely to continue to increase rapidly"; members worried that foreign trade restraint "might not exert enough restraint on aggregate demand to slow the expansion to a sustainable pace in line with the growth of the economy's potential." This is clearly above-potential language. The bright-line rule requires > 50. The economy is running hot with strong domestic demand — score around 60-63.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "exceptionally tight labor markets and growing indications of somewhat faster increases in labor compensation"; "tightening labor markets"; "pressures on labor resources." Unemployment at 4.6% equaling expansion low. This is very tight. Score around 68-72.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumer expenditures, especially for durable goods, had risen sharply"; "little reason to anticipate substantial slowing in the growth of consumer or business expenditures"; "Attractive financing conditions and favorable business confidence also were expected to support substantial further growth in business investment." This is clearly above trend — robust, strong. Score around 65-68.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "accommodative conditions in key segments of financial markets"; "ample liquidity and accommodative financial conditions"; "ebullient equity markets and narrow risk spreads in credit markets as additional evidence that financial conditions were not restraining final demands very much"; "the elevated level of financial wealth and the low cost of capital." Conditions are clearly loose/accommodative. Higher = tighter, so score should be low — around 18-22.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Hold at current rate with asymmetric tilt toward tightening. "all but one of the members indicated... they preferred or could accept a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance that also included a shift from the current symmetrical directive to an asymmetrical directive tilted toward restraint." "Most saw little urgency to tighten policy at this meeting." One dissent (Jordan) for immediate tightening. "several suggested that the need for some policy tightening could well materialize in the near future." This is a lean-hawkish hold — not acting yet but signaling tightening bias. Score around 55-58.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal dissent (Jordan). The majority agreed, though with different emphases on urgency. "some" thought tightening was "not inevitable" while "several" thought it could come in the "near future." Moderate differences with one dissent. Score around 30-35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Stock market discussed by "several members" as "somewhat puzzling," with concern about speculative construction, equity valuations, and the possibility of a market correction. Asian financial turmoil discussed at length. "ebullient equity markets and narrow risk spreads" noted. Financial conditions are a meaningful part of the discussion but not the dominant focus. Score around 40-45.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Asymmetric directive tilted toward tightening. "the Committee's judgment that the information... had altered the inflation risks enough to make some tightening a likely prospect in the not too distant future." "Several suggested that the need for some policy tightening could well materialize in the near future." This is a mild tightening bias signal. Score around 62-66.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "the factors that had produced the favorable inflation results of recent years were not all well understood"; Asian developments "still very much in doubt"; "the ultimate extent of retarding effects from the financial turmoil in Asia was still uncertain"; "a number of members commented that... they might turn out to be less negative than had been expected earlier." Moderate-to-somewhat elevated uncertainty. Score around 38-42.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (62) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70): Both above 50, consistent with late-90s boom. Labor tightness higher because of "exceptionally tight" language while output gap is moderated by foreign trade restraint. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (56) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (64): Forward guidance is more hawkish than current action, which makes sense — they held but signaled future tightening. The divergence is explained and coherent.

### Polarity verification:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says low now with rising risk. Score 40 = slightly below midpoint. Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says accommodative/loose. Score 20 = loose. Matches.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Hold with tightening bias = lean hawkish. Score 56. Matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 40 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "price inflation remained quite low — in fact, it was still declining by some measures — and there was little evidence of any potential acceleration in current price data or in anecdotal reports"
- "the risk that inflation might move higher over the longer run seemed to have increased"
- "any upsurge in inflation seemed unlikely in the nearer term"

**Center-of-gravity**: Members see current inflation as low and benign, held down by temporary favorable factors, but acknowledge rising risks over the longer run. The score reflects low current readings pulled modestly toward midpoint by forward concerns.

**Minority views**: Jordan dissented on grounds that money growth was inconsistent with continued inflation progress and that "recent price statistics understated the trend rates of inflation." This widens the band somewhat.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 62 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Domestic demand was exceeding expectations and was likely to continue to increase rapidly"
- Members concerned that foreign trade restraint "might not exert enough restraint on aggregate demand to slow the expansion to a sustainable pace in line with the growth of the economy's potential"
- "economic activity continued to grow rapidly during the early months of 1998"

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is clearly operating above potential with strong domestic demand, though foreign trade and inventory adjustments provide partial offset.

**Minority views**: None notable — broad agreement on above-potential growth.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 70 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Anecdotal reports from across the nation continued to suggest exceptionally tight labor markets and growing indications of somewhat faster increases in labor compensation"
- "Despite tightening labor markets" / "adding to pressures on labor resources"
- Unemployment at 4.6%, equaling expansion low

**Center-of-gravity**: Members describe labor markets as exceptionally tight with acceleration in compensation, consistent with "very tight" range (66-80).

**Minority views**: No disagreement on tightness assessment — only on policy response.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 66 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Consumer expenditures, especially for durable goods, had risen sharply thus far this year"
- "little reason to anticipate substantial slowing in the growth of consumer or business expenditures"
- "Attractive financing conditions and favorable business confidence also were expected to support substantial further growth in business investment, especially in 'high-tech' equipment"

**Center-of-gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment are well above trend, fueled by wealth effects, confidence, and favorable financing. Housing also at a "high level." Clearly in the "well above trend" range.

**Minority views**: Inventory moderation and trade deficit noted as partial offsets, but center of gravity is strongly positive.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 20 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "accommodative conditions in key segments of financial markets"
- "ample liquidity and accommodative financial conditions"
- "ebullient equity markets and narrow risk spreads in credit markets as additional evidence that financial conditions were not restraining final demands very much"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly loose/accommodative — low cost of capital, elevated equity wealth, narrow credit spreads, strong money growth. Score in the "loose" range.

**Minority views**: No disagreement on the assessment; the debate was about what to do about it.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 56 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all but one of the members indicated... they preferred or could accept a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance... with a shift from the current symmetrical directive to an asymmetrical directive tilted toward restraint"
- "Most saw little urgency to tighten policy at this meeting"
- "several suggested that the need for some policy tightening could well materialize in the near future"

**Center-of-gravity**: Hold with a hawkish tilt. The majority supports patience now but acknowledges tightening is increasingly likely. One member (Jordan) dissented for immediate tightening. This is a lean-hawkish posture.

**Minority views**: Jordan favored immediate tightening (hawkish outlier). "Some" noted tightening "was not inevitable" and could prove "premature" (dovish minority). The band reflects this spread.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 32 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- One formal dissent (Jordan) for immediate tightening
- "some" thought tightening was "not inevitable" while "several" thought it could come "in the near future"
- "all but one member supported" the directive

**Center-of-gravity**: One formal dissent with broad agreement among remaining members, though with varying degrees of urgency. Consistent with "broad agreement with minor emphasis differences and one dissent."

**Minority views**: Jordan's dissent is well-articulated but isolated.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 42 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The recent further increases in equity prices from already high levels played an important role in the assessments of several members. The stock market's rise was viewed as somewhat puzzling"
- "several members again reported indications of speculative nonresidential construction"
- "ebullient equity markets and narrow risk spreads in credit markets"
- Asian financial turmoil discussed at length

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability issues receive moderate attention — equity market valuations, speculative construction, Asian turmoil, and narrow risk spreads are all discussed meaningfully by multiple members. Not the dominant topic but a significant secondary theme.

**Minority views**: None — broadly shared concern.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 64 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Shift to asymmetric directive "tilted toward restraint"
- "the Committee's judgment that the information... had altered the inflation risks enough to make some tightening a likely prospect in the not too distant future"
- "several suggested that the need for some policy tightening could well materialize in the near future"

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear forward signal toward tightening, though data-dependent. The asymmetric directive is an explicit bias signal. Score in the "mild tightening bias" range.

**Minority views**: "Some" members noted tightening "was not inevitable," tempering the signal slightly.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 40 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the factors that had produced the favorable inflation results of recent years were not all well understood, and consequently expectations of greater price pressures had to be tentative"
- "outcomes for economic growth and exchange rates were still very much in doubt" (re: Asia)
- "the ultimate extent of retarding effects from the financial turmoil in Asia was still uncertain"

**Center-of-gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty, primarily around the inflation outlook (why is inflation so low despite tight labor markets?) and Asian crisis spillovers. Not extreme — members seem reasonably confident in the domestic picture but uncertain about external risks and the inflation process.

**Minority views**: None notable.
