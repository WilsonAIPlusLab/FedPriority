# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1994-03-22 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format -- staff and participant content are mixed. I will score only portions with participant language cues ("members noted," "members observed," etc.).

**Era context**: March 1994. The Fed had just begun its tightening cycle in February 1994 with the first rate hike in years. The economy was expanding at a pace above potential after a sluggish early-1990s recovery. Inflation was still low but the Committee was focused on preemptive tightening to prevent future inflation.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all the members" (supporting further tightening), "many members" (concerned about inflation pickup, noting rates need to rise sizably), "a majority" (preferred small move, preferred symmetric directive), "a number of members" (accommodative lending, improving regions), "some" (concerned about overreaction, noted limiting factors on consumer spending), "a few" (expected slowdown, cited NAFTA effects), "one member" (reserves growth). Two formal dissents (Broaddus and Jordan) favoring a larger move.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as currently subdued but with rising risks. "The immediate outlook for inflation remained favorable" and "broad measures of prices...currently did not suggest any increase in inflation." However, "many of the members expressed concern about the potential for a pickup in inflation," citing commodity price increases, early-stage production price pressures, and narrowing output gap. "Warning signs had emerged of the prospect of greater inflation." This is a forward-looking concern about inflation that hasn't materialized yet. Current inflation is low; the worry is prospective. Score should be below 50 but not far -- perhaps 42-48. The concern is real but inflation itself is "subdued." I'll score 44: slightly below midpoint since actual inflation is contained, but the concern pulls it up.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Appreciable momentum in the economic expansion and decreasing margins of unemployed labor and other producer resources." "Growth...perhaps for a time at a rate somewhat above the economy's potential." "The degree of slack in the economy clearly had diminished considerably...to relatively low levels and likely would shrink further." "Production levels in many industries approaching or reaching full capacity utilization." Members see the economy near or modestly above potential and heading further above. Bright-line rule: "above the economy's potential" language -> must be > 50. Score 55-58. The gap has "diminished considerably" but some slack remains. I'll say 56.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Decreasing margins of unemployed labor." "Reduced the rate of unemployment to a level that could well be not far from the natural rate." "Shortages of skilled construction workers...were reported in many areas." This is approaching tight but not fully tight. Near NAIRU. Score 52-55. I'll say 53.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "relatively well maintained" with "considerable strength in expenditures for motor vehicles and other consumer durables." Business investment "on a solid uptrend." "Residential construction was described as quite strong in numerous areas." Some limiting factors on consumption noted (low saving rate, high indebtedness). Overall: above trend. Score 58-62. I'll say 60.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Financial conditions were still seen as supportive of solid economic expansion." "More accommodative lending policies of many depository institutions." However, "sharp changes in bond and...equity prices," "backup in interest rates," and "weakness and volatility in financial markets." Members view conditions as still supportive (loose side) but tightening from market moves. "A still-accommodative monetary policy." Score should be below 50 -- conditions are still accommodative/supportive despite the bond market backup. I'll say 38.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All the members supported a further move toward a less accommodative policy stance." "Members still viewed monetary policy as too stimulative." "The key issue facing the Committee was not whether but how promptly the necessary policy adjustment should be completed." Two dissents for a LARGER move. This is clearly hawkish. A majority preferred a small (25bp) move, but the entire committee wanted tightening, and the debate was about doing more. Score 68-72. I'll say 70.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Two formal dissents (Broaddus and Jordan) wanting a bigger move. Additionally, debate between those wanting small vs. larger moves, and debate about symmetric vs. asymmetric directive. "Views differed on how much further current monetary policy should be adjusted." But the disagreement is about pace, not direction -- all agreed on tightening. Score 35-42. Two dissents with same direction = moderate disagreement. I'll say 38.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Members discussed bond market volatility, equity price declines, "considerable uncertainty and volatility in those markets," risk of "overreaction in financial markets," "speculative excesses." The announcement discussion was partly motivated by financial market concerns. This is more than passing reference but not the dominant topic. Score 35-45. I'll say 40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Monetary policy needed to move fairly quickly toward...a more neutral position." "Such a policy stance was still some distance away." "Whether further tightening beyond that point would be needed later could not be determined." Clear signal of further tightening ahead. Score 72-78. I'll say 75.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The amount of resources that could be mobilized readily to meet this demand was subject to substantial uncertainty." "Considerable uncertainty and volatility" in markets. Some uncertainty about whether slowdown would be more pronounced. But overall the direction is fairly clear to members -- they're quite confident about the need to tighten. Score 35-42. I'll say 38.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (56) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (53): Both modestly above 50, consistent -- economy near/slightly above potential with labor near NAIRU. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (70) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (75): Both hawkish, with forward guidance slightly more hawkish because of "still some distance away" from neutral. Coherent.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 44: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says current inflation subdued but rising concern. 44 captures "slightly below midpoint with upward risk." Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP 56: Higher = more above potential. "Somewhat above potential." Correct, >50.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS 38: Higher = tighter. Conditions "still supportive," "accommodative lending." Below 50 = loose. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 44 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The immediate outlook for inflation remained favorable: costs and prices were being contained by moderate wage increases, continuing pressures for productivity-enhancing investment, and competitive prices from abroad"
- "Many of the members expressed concern about the potential for a pickup in inflation if...margins of unemployed resources narrowed further or disappeared"
- "Warning signs had emerged of the prospect of greater inflation, though perhaps not over the nearer term"

**Center-of-gravity**: Current inflation is acknowledged as subdued and favorable, but forward-looking concern about building pressures pulls the score toward midpoint. The inflation risk is prospective, not realized.

**Minority views**: Dissenters (Broaddus, Jordan) saw "rising inflationary expectations" as an urgent signal, which would push higher. Some members noted inflation "retained a downward tilt," which pulls lower. Band reflects this spread.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 56 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Members continued to expect moderate economic growth, though perhaps for a time at a rate somewhat above the economy's potential"
- "The degree of slack in the economy clearly had diminished considerably in recent quarters to relatively low levels and likely would shrink further"
- "Production levels in many industries approaching or reaching full capacity utilization"

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is viewed as modestly above potential and heading further above. Slack has diminished to "relatively low levels." The "somewhat above" language puts this 5-8 points above midpoint.

**Minority views**: "A few" expected considerable slowdown that might be more pronounced than projected, which could pull gap back.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 53 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Decreasing margins of unemployed labor and other producer resources"
- "Reduced the rate of unemployment to a level that could well be not far from the natural rate"
- "Shortages of skilled construction workers and building materials were reported in many areas"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is near full employment (NAIRU) with some sector-specific tightness. Not yet clearly tight overall, but approaching and slightly past the balanced point.

**Minority views**: Construction sector shortages suggest pockets of significant tightness, but broad labor market is described as near NAIRU rather than clearly past it.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending to be relatively well maintained, buttressed by considerable strength in expenditures for motor vehicles and other consumer durables"
- "Business investment expenditures remained on a solid uptrend"
- "Residential construction was described as quite strong in numerous areas"

**Center-of-gravity**: Both consumption and investment are above trend. Business investment is on a "solid uptrend," consumer spending is "well maintained" with durable goods strength, and residential construction is "quite strong." Some limiting factors noted but the net picture is clearly above trend.

**Minority views**: "Some members observed that a number of factors were likely to limit the potential strength of consumer spending" (low saving rate, high indebtedness), moderating the upside.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 38 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Financial conditions were still seen as supportive of solid economic expansion"
- "A number of members referred in particular to the more accommodative lending policies of many depository institutions"
- "Sharp changes in bond and, to a lesser extent, equity prices over the intermeeting period" and "weakness and volatility in financial markets"

**Center-of-gravity**: Despite the bond market selloff and equity volatility, members still characterize overall financial conditions as supportive/accommodative. Lending policies are loosening. The market turbulence introduces some tightening but is not seen as dominant.

**Minority views**: "Some commented on the risk...that weakness and volatility in financial markets could at some point have a significantly inhibiting effect" -- this pushes the band wider.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 70 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All the members supported a further move toward a less accommodative policy stance"
- "Members still viewed monetary policy as too stimulative"
- "The key issue facing the Committee was not whether but how promptly the necessary policy adjustment should be completed"
- Two dissents (Broaddus, Jordan) for a stronger/bigger move

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous support for tightening with debate only on pace. The majority chose a small move but acknowledged more was needed. Two dissents on the hawkish side. This is clearly in the moderate-to-strong hawkish range.

**Minority views**: The majority preferred gradualism ("another small move"), tempering the hawkishness somewhat. Dissenters wanted "more promptly and decisively."

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All but two of the members indicated that they could accept a directive that called for a slight increase"
- "Views differed on how much further current monetary policy should be adjusted at this meeting"
- Debate between symmetric vs. asymmetric directive: "Members who favored an asymmetric directive observed that such a directive seemed more consistent with the current thrust"

**Center-of-gravity**: Two formal dissents, both on the same (hawkish) side. Disagreement is about pace/magnitude, not direction. Additional debate on symmetric vs. asymmetric directive adds some friction. Moderate disagreement.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 40 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Sharp changes in bond and, to a lesser extent, equity prices over the intermeeting period"
- "Concern about a possible overreaction in financial markets that had become quite sensitive and volatile"
- "Considerable uncertainty and volatility in those markets" drove the announcement decision
- April 18 call: "sharp declines in bond and stock prices suggested that speculative excesses had been reduced"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial market conditions were a meaningful part of the discussion -- bond volatility, equity declines, market sensitivity all received attention. The announcement decision was partly driven by financial stability concerns. More than passing reference, moderate discussion.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 75 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Monetary policy needed to move fairly quickly toward what might be characterized as a more neutral position"
- "Such a policy stance was still some distance away"
- "Money market interest rates would have to rise by a relatively sizable amount from current levels"

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear signal that further tightening is ahead and that the Committee is "still some distance away" from neutral. The forward signal is firmly hawkish -- more rate increases are coming, and the only question is timing.

**Minority views**: "Whether further tightening beyond that point would be needed later could not be determined" introduces some data-dependence at the margin.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 38 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The amount of resources that could be mobilized readily to meet this demand was subject to substantial uncertainty"
- "A few also placed some emphasis on their expectations of a considerable slowdown...and the possibility that the moderation of the expansion might prove to be somewhat more pronounced than was currently projected"
- "Considerable uncertainty and volatility in those markets"

**Center-of-gravity**: There is some genuine uncertainty about the output gap level and the degree of coming slowdown, plus financial market uncertainty. However, the policy direction is clear to all members, and most seem fairly confident in the outlook. Somewhat elevated but not dramatically so.
