# FOMC Meeting Scoring: December 11, 2019

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented format — staff sections are clearly labeled and separated from "Participants' Views" section.

**Era context**: December 2019 — late-cycle expansion. The Fed had cut rates three times in 2019 (July, September, October) as "mid-cycle adjustments" amid trade uncertainty and global weakness. Unemployment at 50-year lows. Inflation persistently running below 2%. This meeting held rates steady after the three insurance cuts. The macro backdrop is a healthy labor market and consumer sector but weak business investment and manufacturing due to trade tensions.

**Quantifier distribution**: "Participants agreed" (consensus), "participants generally" (near-consensus), "many" (substantial majority), "a number of" (significant minority), "several" (notable group), "some" (smaller group), "a few" (small minority), "a couple of" (very small minority). The text uses "participants agreed" and "participants generally" frequently, suggesting broad agreement on key assessments.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target. "Overall and core PCE inflation... had continued to run below 2 percent." "Inflation pressures remained muted." "Participants generally expected that inflation would return to the 2 percent objective" but "weakness abroad and subdued global inflation pressures were cited as sources of risk." Some participants expressed less confidence inflation would return to 2%. This is consistently below target but not dramatically so — the discussion is about persistent undershooting, not deflationary alarm. Direction: below midpoint. Magnitude: "running below 2 percent" and "muted" = moderately below. Score should be in the 36-42 range, consistent with 2013-19 anchor of 38-42. Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says muted/below target. Score below 50 — correct.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic activity had risen at a moderate rate." "Labor market had remained strong." Unemployment at 50-year low. Household spending "strong pace." But "business fixed investment and exports remained weak." The economy is near potential — strong labor market and consumer spending offset by weak business investment. Participants describe resilience but moderate growth. The staff forecast output "at a rate a little above the staff's estimate of its potential rate of growth." Direction: modestly above potential. Bright-line rule: "above potential" language from the staff forecast and the very low unemployment suggest slightly above potential. Score should be 51-55. Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says near/slightly above potential. Score slightly above 50 — correct.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor market had remained strong." "Unemployment rate at a 50-year low." "Job gains remaining solid." "Some measures of labor force participation increasing further." But "various participants remarked that there were some indications that further strengthening in overall labor market conditions was possible without creating undesirable pressures on resources." "Measures of wage growth had generally remained moderate." The labor market is tight by historical standards (50-year low unemployment) but participants see room for further tightening without inflationary consequences, and wage growth is moderate. This suggests tight but not severely tight. Direction: above midpoint. Score 55-60. Anchor: 50 for 2016-17; this is tighter than that period. Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says strong/tight. Score above 50 — correct.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Split picture. "Household spending had risen at a strong pace." Residential investment picking up. But "business fixed investment and exports remained weak." "Trade developments and concerns about the global economic growth outlook as the main factors contributing to weak business investment." Energy sector "especially weak." Agriculture facing difficulties. The NET position: consumer strength partially offset by business investment weakness. Consumer side gets more positive emphasis ("strong pace") while business investment is clearly weak. Net: roughly at trend, maybe slightly above given the strength of the consumer side. Score 48-52. Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Mixed evidence, net roughly balanced. Score near 50 — correct.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: The document discusses financial conditions mostly in staff sections. From participants' discussion: "a couple of participants cautioned that maintaining accommodative financial conditions could be counterproductive." "A few participants raised the concern that keeping interest rates low over a long period might encourage excessive risk-taking." The staff review notes "financing conditions for businesses and households remained supportive of spending." Conditions described as accommodative/supportive — this means loose, not tight. Direction: below midpoint (conditions are loose). Score around 30-35. Anchor: 45-55 for 2017-19 normal. But this meeting comes after three rate cuts — conditions are described as more accommodative than normal. However, energy sector has "tight financing conditions." Score 33-38. Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says accommodative/supportive. Score below 50 — correct.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Participants hold rates steady after three cuts. "Participants judged that it would be appropriate to maintain the target range." "Participants regarded the current stance of monetary policy as likely to remain appropriate for a time." "Participants generally expressed concerns regarding inflation continuing to fall short of 2 percent." The stance is hold with a mild easing bias — they are concerned about below-target inflation and downside risks, but are comfortable pausing. The expected path "remained essentially flat through 2020" with slightly more probability on cuts than hikes. This is balanced to mildly dovish. Score 43-48. Anchor: 40-50 for 2014-15 pre-liftoff. This is a hold-and-assess stance with easing bias. Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says balanced-to-dovish hold. Score slightly below 50 — correct.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "By unanimous vote." The discussion shows broad agreement — "participants agreed," "participants generally." The only minority views are "a few" raising financial stability concerns about low rates, and some who had less confidence inflation would return to 2%. These are modest differences in emphasis, not fundamental disagreements. Score 15-20. Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Evidence says broad consensus. Low score — correct.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: "A few participants raised the concern that keeping interest rates low over a long period might encourage excessive risk-taking, which could exacerbate imbalances in the financial sector." "A couple of participants cautioned that maintaining accommodative financial conditions could be counterproductive if doing so fueled financial imbalances." This is a modest discussion — only "a few" participants, brief treatment. The staff review of financial conditions is detailed but that's staff, not participants. Score 25-30. Anchor: 20-35 for 2013-19 typical. Polarity check: Higher = more attention. Evidence says limited discussion by a few. Score in low-moderate range — correct.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Current stance of monetary policy as likely to remain appropriate for a time as long as incoming information about the economy remained broadly consistent with the economic outlook." "The expected path of the federal funds rate... remained essentially flat through 2020." Survey respondents placed "higher probability on a reduction in the target range over 2020 than an increase." This is a neutral-to-mildly-dovish forward signal — hold with data dependence, slightly more likelihood of easing than tightening. Score 40-45. Anchor: 35-45 for 2019 mid-cycle cuts. Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence says neutral/slightly dovish lean. Score slightly below 50 — correct.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Global developments... continued to pose some risks to the outlook." "Many saw the risks as tilted somewhat to the downside, some risks were seen to have eased over recent months." "Tentative signs that trade tensions with China were easing." "Probability of a no-deal Brexit was judged to have lessened further." "New uncertainties had emerged regarding trade policy with Argentina, Brazil, and France." Uncertainty is present but somewhat diminished from earlier in 2019. Risks are tilted to downside but are easing. This is moderately elevated uncertainty. Score 35-42. Anchor: 40-50 for Brexit/trade tensions. The text suggests uncertainty is easing from previous levels. Score 38.

**Cross-dimension coherence check**:
- OUTPUT_GAP (52) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (57): Both above 50, labor tighter than output gap. Consistent — strong labor market but moderate overall growth due to weak business investment pulling down the output gap.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (45) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (42): Both mildly dovish, aligned. Consistent — hold with slight easing bias.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 39 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Overall inflation and inflation for items other than food and energy were running below 2 percent."
- "Inflation pressures remained muted."
- "Participants generally expected that inflation would return to the 2 percent objective as the economic expansion continued" but "various participants were concerned that indicators were suggesting that the level of longer-term inflation expectations was too low."
**Center of gravity**: Participants see inflation persistently running below the 2% target with muted pressures; expectations of return to target exist but confidence is not high.
**Minority views**: "A few participants commented on factors that may temporarily exert upward pressure" (mild upside), while others "expressed less confidence that inflation would return promptly to the 2 percent objective" (downside). Modest spread.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 52 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity had risen at a moderate rate."
- "Labor market had remained strong... unemployment rate had remained low."
- Staff forecast: output "at a rate a little above the staff's estimate of its potential rate of growth in 2019 through 2021."
**Center of gravity**: Economy operating near or slightly above potential — strong labor market and consumer spending, but weak business investment and exports moderate the overall picture.
**Minority views**: Minimal divergence on this assessment.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 57 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Conditions in the labor market remained strong, with the unemployment rate at a 50-year low, job gains remaining solid, and some measures of labor force participation increasing further."
- "Various participants remarked that there were some indications that further strengthening in overall labor market conditions was possible without creating undesirable pressures on resources."
- "Measures of wage growth had generally remained moderate."
**Center of gravity**: Labor market is tight by historical standards but not generating excessive wage pressure; participants believe there is still some room for further tightening.
**Minority views**: "A few participants commented that increases in the labor force would likely moderate as slack in the labor market diminished" — slight concern about tightening further.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 49 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Household spending had risen at a strong pace" and "consumption spending would likely remain on a firm footing."
- "Business fixed investment and exports remained weak." "Trade developments and concerns about the global economic growth outlook as the main factors contributing to weak business investment."
- "Activity in the energy sector was especially weak."
**Center of gravity**: Strong consumer spending offset by clearly weak business investment and exports, netting to roughly trend-level overall spending and investment.
**Minority views**: "Some participants observed that recent data on retail sales or motor vehicle spending had decelerated slightly" (mild downside on consumption).

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 35 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "A couple of participants cautioned that maintaining accommodative financial conditions could be counterproductive if doing so fueled financial imbalances."
- Staff review (context): "Financing conditions for businesses and households remained supportive of spending and economic activity."
- "Tight financing conditions for energy-producing firms" noted by a few participants.
**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions broadly accommodative/loose following three rate cuts in 2019, though participants did not extensively discuss financial conditions as a group.
**Minority views**: Energy sector tightness noted as a sectoral exception.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 45 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants judged that it would be appropriate to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 1-1/2 to 1-3/4 percent."
- "Participants regarded the current stance of monetary policy as likely to remain appropriate for a time."
- "Participants generally expressed concerns regarding inflation continuing to fall short of 2 percent."
**Center of gravity**: Consensus to hold rates after mid-cycle cuts, with a mild easing bias driven by below-target inflation concerns — but no active push for further cuts.
**Minority views**: "A few participants raised the concern that keeping interest rates low over a long period might encourage excessive risk-taking" (mildly hawkish minority).

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 17 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Unanimous vote to hold rates.
- "Participants agreed that the labor market had remained strong" and "participants generally expected sustained expansion."
- Only modest differences: "a few" raised financial stability concerns; "some" vs "various" on inflation outlook confidence.
**Center of gravity**: Broad consensus on both the economic assessment and the policy action. Minor differences in emphasis on inflation and financial stability risks.
**Minority views**: Financial stability concerns from "a few" participants represent the main divergence from consensus.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 27 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "A few participants raised the concern that keeping interest rates low over a long period might encourage excessive risk-taking, which could exacerbate imbalances in the financial sector."
- "A couple of participants cautioned that maintaining accommodative financial conditions could be counterproductive if doing so fueled financial imbalances and exacerbated the next economic downturn."
**Center of gravity**: Financial stability received limited but explicit attention from a small group of participants. Not a central topic of discussion.
**Minority views**: The "a few" raising these concerns are themselves the minority; most participants did not engage this topic.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 42 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants regarded the current stance of monetary policy as likely to remain appropriate for a time as long as incoming information about the economy remained broadly consistent with the economic outlook."
- Market expectations: "essentially flat through 2020" with "higher probability on a reduction in the target range over 2020 than an increase."
- "If developments emerged that led to a material reassessment of the outlook, the stance of policy would need to adjust."
**Center of gravity**: Data-dependent hold with a slight easing bias — the forward signal is neutral to mildly dovish, with markets pricing slightly more probability of cuts than hikes.
**Minority views**: Minimal divergence on the forward outlook.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 38 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Global developments, related to both persistent uncertainty regarding international trade and weakness in economic growth abroad, continued to pose some risks to the outlook."
- "Many saw the risks as tilted somewhat to the downside, some risks were seen to have eased over recent months."
- "There were some tentative signs that trade tensions with China were easing, and the probability of a no-deal Brexit was judged to have lessened further."
- "New uncertainties had emerged regarding trade policy with Argentina, Brazil, and France."
**Center of gravity**: Elevated but easing uncertainty — downside risks remain from trade and global developments, but several key risks have diminished. Net: somewhat above normal uncertainty levels.
**Minority views**: Spread between those seeing continued significant risks and those noting recent easing.
