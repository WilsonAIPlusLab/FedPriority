# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1997-05-20

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is **minutes** (synthesized summary of participant views). It is an unsegmented pre-2009 format, so I must focus on participant-attributable language ("members agreed," "members observed," "some also expressed") and ignore staff-reported content.

### Era Context
May 1997 -- mid-to-late 1990s expansion. The economy had surged in late 1996/early 1997 and is now showing signs of slowing from that rapid pace. Unemployment is low (4.9%), labor markets are tight, yet inflation remains surprisingly subdued. This is the period of the "new economy" productivity debate. The Committee had tightened in March 1997 (25bp hike). At this meeting they hold rates but adopt an asymmetric tightening bias.

### Quantifier Distribution
- "members agreed" / "all but one" -- near-consensus language used frequently
- "many noted" -- used for upside growth risks and consumer confidence
- "a number of members" -- used for above-full-employment labor view, upside inventory risk, real interest rates observation
- "some" -- used for preference for tightening, areas of construction activity
- "one" -- Mr. Broaddus dissented

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Members observe "increases in prices had remained subdued despite the rapid expansion." Inflation is currently benign -- "few indications of accelerating price inflation." However, members "continued to express concern that, perhaps sooner rather than later, growing pressures on productive resources would be reflected in some upturn in overall inflation." Labor compensation shows a "clear uptrend." The center of gravity is: current inflation is below concerning levels BUT members see rising risks. This is a forward-leaning concern, not realized inflation. Score should be in the 41-49 range -- slightly below the alarm threshold, but with upward pressure recognized. I'll score 47. Higher = more pressure. Evidence says current inflation is subdued but risks are building. 47 captures "not yet a problem but close."

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Members describe an economy that had been growing very rapidly and is now slowing. Key language: "economy retained considerable underlying strength," growth expected "at a pace close to, or a little above, the estimated growth of the economy's long-run potential." Members describe the economy as "operating at a level approximating full employment--indeed, possibly somewhat above sustainable full employment." The bright-line rule: "above potential" language --> must be > 50. "A number of members" see labor markets above full employment. Score 56. This is modestly above potential -- the economy surged and is now settling near/slightly above potential.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Very clear: "utilization of productive resources, notably labor, already at particularly high levels," "the economy had been operating at a level approximating full employment--indeed, possibly somewhat above sustainable full employment in labor markets in the view of a number of members, especially taking into consideration the recent further decline in the unemployment rate." Unemployment at 4.9%. "Increased tightness in labor markets." This is clearly tight. Score 65. Anchors: 65-75 for 1999-2000. We're approaching that range but not yet at peak late-90s tightness.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending: "favorable underlying factors" including "solid growth in consumer incomes, large increases in financial wealth, and currently high levels of consumer confidence." Expected to moderate from robust pace but remain solid. Business investment: "retained a good deal of momentum," investments "highly profitable," nonresidential construction increasing. Risks tilted to upside. This is clearly above trend. Score 60 -- "robust" investment momentum, solid consumption, risks to the upside.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Supportive financial conditions" mentioned. "Ready availability of financing" for commercial construction. Housing: "availability of financing on still generally favorable terms." However, "many banking institutions had tightened lending terms and conditions at least for their more marginal consumer borrowers." Equity markets "risen considerably." On net, conditions are accommodative/loose. Score 32 -- somewhat loose, with minor tightening at the margins for consumer credit.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The Committee held rates with an asymmetric tightening bias. "All but one" supported holding. "Some also expressed a preference for some tightening at this meeting." Those endorsing steady policy "agreed that some tightening might well be needed later." One dissent (Broaddus) favoring tightening. The asymmetric directive was supported by "all the members." This is a clear lean-hawkish posture: hold now, but bias toward tightening. Score 58. The current action is a hold, but the forward tilt and minority favoring immediate action pushes above 50.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal dissent (Broaddus). "Some" preferred tightening but accepted the hold. There's a clear minority wanting to move now vs. majority preferring to wait. This is moderate -- one dissent, some reservations, but broad agreement on the asymmetric directive. Score 32.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Equity prices mentioned briefly ("share prices in equity markets have risen considerably" -- but this is in the directive/staff section). Members note "supportive financial conditions" and "speculative" building activity but say "a building boom reminiscent of the 1980s did not appear to be under way." Financial conditions discussed as context for policy, not as a stability concern per se. Limited emphasis. Score 18.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Asymmetric directive tilted toward tightening adopted unanimously. "Risks were in the direction of a potential need for some tightening in monetary policy to counter rising inflationary pressures, and that they might be required to make such a decision in the not-too-distant future." This is a mild tightening bias forward signal. Score 63.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Subject to substantial uncertainty," "considerable uncertainty" about inflation outlook, "persisting uncertainties about behavior in labor markets and the level and growth of the economy's sustainable potential," members "found it very difficult to account for the surprisingly benign behavior of inflation." Historical experience "not a fully reliable guide." This is genuinely elevated uncertainty, particularly about the productivity/inflation nexus. Score 50.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (56) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (65): Both above 50, labor tighter than output gap. Makes sense -- labor markets were tighter than overall output measures suggested, which was a key puzzle of this era.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (58) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (63): Both lean hawkish, with forward guidance slightly more hawkish than current action. Consistent -- they held but signaled tightening ahead.

### Polarity Verification
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 47: Higher = more pressure. Evidence: subdued but building. 47 = slightly below midpoint. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP 56: Higher = more above potential. Evidence: at or slightly above. 56 matches.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS 65: Higher = tighter. Evidence: tight, "particularly high levels." 65 matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS 32: Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence: supportive/accommodative. 32 = somewhat loose. Matches.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 58: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence: hold with tightening bias. 58 matches.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 63: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence: asymmetric tightening directive, "not-too-distant future." 63 matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 47 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "increases in prices had remained subdued despite the rapid expansion in economic activity in recent quarters"
- "members continued to express concern that, perhaps sooner rather than later, growing pressures on productive resources would be reflected in some upturn in overall inflation"
- "increases in labor compensation had been trending higher, these pressures were muted and had not shown through to prices"

**Center-of-gravity**: Members see current inflation as benign but recognize building cost pressures that have not yet materialized into price inflation. The score sits just below the midpoint -- inflation is not yet a problem, but upside risks are clearly acknowledged.

**Minority views**: None significantly divergent; near-consensus that inflation is subdued now but risks are rising.

---

### OUTPUT_GAP: 56 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "economy retained considerable underlying strength" with growth expected "at a pace close to, or a little above, the estimated growth of the economy's long-run potential"
- "operating at a level approximating full employment--indeed, possibly somewhat above sustainable full employment"
- "utilization of productive resources, notably labor, already at particularly high levels in relation to the economy's potential"

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is at or modestly above potential, with growth expected near trend going forward. The "above" language triggers the bright-line rule (>50).

**Minority views**: Some members may see the gap as wider (more above potential) given labor market tightness, while the slowing from Q1 surge tempers the overall assessment.

---

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 65 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "utilization of productive resources, notably labor, already at particularly high levels"
- "possibly somewhat above sustainable full employment in labor markets in the view of a number of members"
- "increased tightness in labor markets" and unemployment at 4.9%

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor markets are clearly tight and described as at or above full employment. Multiple references to resource constraints being binding.

**Minority views**: The uncertainty band reflects that some members emphasize unmeasured productivity gains that could imply more sustainable capacity than standard measures suggest.

---

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 60 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "favorable underlying factors in the outlook for consumer spending" including "solid growth in consumer incomes, large increases in financial wealth, and currently high levels of consumer confidence"
- "Spending for business fixed investment seemed to have retained a good deal of momentum even after the large increases in such expenditures in recent years"
- "increasing strength in nonresidential construction" and risks tilted to upside

**Center-of-gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment are described as strong, with risks tilted to the upside. Consumer spending is expected to moderate somewhat, but investment retains strong momentum.

**Minority views**: Large consumer debts and tightened lending terms for marginal borrowers noted as offsetting factors.

---

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 32 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "supportive financial conditions" cited as factor for stronger growth
- "ready availability of financing" for commercial construction; housing financing on "still generally favorable terms"
- "many banking institutions had tightened lending terms and conditions at least for their more marginal consumer borrowers"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are broadly accommodative/supportive. The only tightening noted is at the margins of consumer lending. Overall conditions are loose.

**Minority views**: The marginal consumer credit tightening prevents scoring as extremely loose.

---

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 58 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all but one of the members indicated that they could support a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance, although some also expressed a preference for some tightening at this meeting"
- "Those who endorsed a steady policy at this time agreed that some tightening might well be needed later to contain potentially rising inflation"
- Mr. Broaddus dissented, favoring tightening

**Center-of-gravity**: The Committee held but with a clear hawkish lean. The majority prefers patience now but acknowledges tightening may be needed soon. One formal dissent for immediate tightening.

**Minority views**: "Some" preferred immediate tightening, while the majority endorsed a cautious wait-and-see approach.

---

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 32 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- One formal dissent (Broaddus) favoring tightening
- "some also expressed a preference for some tightening at this meeting" but accepted the hold
- Unanimous support for the asymmetric tightening directive

**Center-of-gravity**: One dissent plus some reluctant holders creates moderate but contained disagreement. The direction of disagreement is one-sided (all toward tightening), and all agreed on the tightening bias.

**Minority views**: Broaddus's dissent was principled but isolated.

---

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 18 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "supportive financial conditions" mentioned as context for growth outlook
- "Anecdotal reports of nonresidential building activity undertaken on a speculative basis had increased, but a building boom reminiscent of the 1980s did not appear to be under way"
- Equity market gains noted in the directive section (not participant discussion)

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability receives only passing mentions. The speculative building reference is noted and dismissed. No sustained discussion of financial stability risks.

**Minority views**: None significant.

---

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 63 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all the members supported a shift from the symmetric directive...to an asymmetric directive tilted toward tightening"
- "risks were in the direction of a potential need for some tightening in monetary policy to counter rising inflationary pressures"
- "they might be required to make such a decision in the not-too-distant future"

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear mild tightening signal via the asymmetric directive, with unanimous support. The language "not-too-distant future" signals near-term action is plausible.

**Minority views**: The forward signal is unanimously in the same direction; disagreement is only about timing of actual action.

---

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 50 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the prospects for subsequent quarters were subject to substantial uncertainty"
- "the inflation outlook remained subject to considerable uncertainty"
- Members "found it very difficult to account for the surprisingly benign behavior of inflation"
- "historical experience was not a fully reliable guide to the prospective behavior of prices"

**Center-of-gravity**: Genuinely elevated uncertainty, particularly around the productivity-inflation nexus. Members explicitly acknowledge their models may not apply. Multiple uses of "considerable" and "substantial" uncertainty.

**Minority views**: The uncertainty is broadly shared, not confined to a few members.
