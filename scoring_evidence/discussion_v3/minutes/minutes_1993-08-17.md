## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the fundamentals bearing on the outlook for inflation were consistent with further disinflation, and the members drew some encouragement from consumer and producer price developments in recent months"
- "Several cautioned, however, that recent price measures probably overstated the reduction in inflation"
- "Members also referred to the persistence of inflationary expectations among business executives and consumers"
- "some slack in labor and capital resources would persist" keeping inflation down

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Members see inflation as contained and below target, with disinflation as the trend, though persistent expectations provide a floor. The environment is consistent with further disinflation, placing this moderately below the midpoint.

**Minority views**: Several members cautioned against reading too much into favorable recent data, and the persistence of inflation expectations is noted as a risk. This keeps the band moderate.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 40 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "outlook for moderate and sustained growth in economic activity"
- "some slack in labor and capital resources would persist in the context of projections that pointed to a relatively moderate rate of economic expansion"
- "relatively slow economic expansion in the first half of the year"
- Regional descriptions: "slow to moderate growth in most regions"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is expanding at a moderate pace but remains below potential with persistent slack in labor and capital. Growth is positive but not fast enough to close the gap quickly.

**Minority views**: None significantly diverge; regional variation exists (California weaker) but the overall assessment is fairly uniform.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "consumer attitudes continued to be inhibited by concerns about employment opportunities, especially given further reductions in defense spending, the ongoing restructuring and related downsizing of many business operations"
- "growth in employment thus far this year, while tending to involve many low paying jobs, had greatly exceeded the rate of expansion in 1992"
- "some slack in labor and capital resources would persist"
- Unemployment at 6.8% (directive data)

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The labor market is slack with elevated unemployment and restructuring concerns, though job growth has improved from 1992. The level remains well below full employment.

**Minority views**: "At least some members" expected "appreciable further growth" in employment as firms found it harder to rely on outsourcing and temporary workers, suggesting a mildly more optimistic minority.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 47 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "overall consumer spending and business investment appeared to be reasonably well maintained"
- "consumer expenditures had strengthened in recent months after a pause earlier in the year"
- "relative strength in business investment spending in the first half of this year"
- "business sentiment was widely described as cautious or negative"
- Housing: "sales of new and existing homes were brisk in many regions"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Spending and investment are near trend -- "reasonably well maintained" with some recent strengthening in consumer spending and housing, offset by cautious/negative business sentiment and headwinds from tax increases and defense cutbacks.

**Minority views**: No significant divergence on this dimension.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 27 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "real short-term interest rates were at very low levels, indeed slightly negative by some calculations"
- "monetary policy was in an accommodative posture"
- "favorable effects on spending of reduced interest rates in intermediate- and long-term debt markets"
- "somewhat better availability of loans from financial intermediaries"
- "greater willingness to supply credit by banks, other financial intermediaries, and investors in securities markets"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions are clearly accommodative with low real rates, improving credit availability, and supportive long-term rate declines. Well below neutral.

**Minority views**: "No compelling evidence that current monetary policy was fostering credit flows usually associated with speculative excesses" suggests conditions are loose but not dangerously so.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 48 +/-4 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "members agreed that recent developments pointed to the desirability of a steady policy course"
- Committee moved to unbiased directive, removing prior tightening presumption: "it now appeared less likely than at the time of the May and July meetings that the Committee needed to bias its consideration...toward possible tightening"
- "monetary policy probably would have to move in the direction of restraint at some point to resist any incipient tendency for inflationary pressures to intensify"
- One member: "monetary policy had been overly stimulative for some time and...the Committee should move toward restraint at the first favorable opportunity"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The Committee is in a hold posture, having just moved to a symmetric/unbiased directive from a previous tightening bias. The current lean is essentially neutral with a slight dovish shift from prior meetings, though future tightening is acknowledged as likely eventually needed.

**Minority views**: One member wanted to tighten at the first opportunity, but still supported the current hold decision.

- "all the members expressed a preference for a directive that called for maintaining the existing degree of pressure"
- One member expressed a desire to tighten at first opportunity but still agreed with current action

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Near-unanimous consensus with zero dissents. One member expressed a mildly hawkish preference but voted with the majority. Very low disagreement.

**Score: 53 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

- "monetary policy probably would have to move in the direction of restraint at some point"
- Shift away from prior tightening bias: "less likely than at the time of the May and July meetings that the Committee needed to bias...toward possible tightening"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The formal forward signal is symmetric/neutral (moved from tightening bias to no bias). However, the acknowledged expectation that policy will eventually need to tighten nudges the forward tone slightly above neutral.

**Minority views**: One member explicitly wanted restraint at first opportunity, adding a mild hawkish minority pull.

**Evidence**:

- "The timing of that effect was uncertain" (regarding tax increases)

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Standard-level uncertainty with several specific unknowns mentioned (tax timing, NAFTA, vehicle demand, whether disinflation is real), but no participant signals deep or unusual uncertainty about the outlook. Views are expressed with reasonable confidence.

**Minority views**: None significantly diverge on uncertainty level.
