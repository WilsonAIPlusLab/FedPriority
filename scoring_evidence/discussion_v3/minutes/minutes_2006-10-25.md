# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2006-10-25

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format, but participant language cues are clear throughout ("participants noted," "members agreed," "many participants," etc.). Staff content is minimal and clearly distinguishable.

**Era context**: October 2006 — the Fed had paused its tightening cycle at 5.25% after 17 consecutive hikes. Housing was correcting sharply, but core economy appeared resilient. Core inflation had been running above the implicit comfort zone. This is the period before the subprime crisis fully materialized.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all" (3 uses — inflation concern, risk to inflation, risks of greatest concern), "nearly all" (2 — favored holding, core inflation uncomfortably high), "most" (3 — expected core inflation to moderate, downside risks diminished, expected near-potential growth), "many" (3 — core inflation undesirably high, housing comfort, concern about housing spillover), "several" (3 — strain on construction firms, transportation softness, nonresidential robust), "some" (2 — expectations higher than consistent, upside risks declined), "one" (2 — uncertainty delaying investment, oil price weakness).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as ABOVE target. "All meeting participants expressed concern about the outlook for inflation." "Nearly all participants viewed the current rates of core inflation as uncomfortably high." "All participants emphasized that the risks around the desired downward path to inflation remained to the upside." However, "most participants expected core inflation to edge lower" and recent data showed "some slowing." This is clearly elevated but with expectation of moderation. The language "uncomfortably high" and "upside risks" pushes this well above 50. Direction: above midpoint. Magnitude: "uncomfortably high" is strong language, roughly 15-20 points above midpoint. Score ~65. Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says elevated. Matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as near potential. "Nearly all members expected that the economy would expand close to or a little below its potential growth rate." Growth had slowed in Q3 but largely due to "temporary influences." Housing drag offset by resilient consumer spending and business investment. The phrase "close to or a little below potential" implies slightly below 50. Score ~48. Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says at/slightly below. Score below 50. Matches.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see labor market as TIGHT. "Both data and reports from businesses indicated that the labor market remained tight." "Many of their contacts were increasingly concerned about the difficulty of recruiting suitably qualified workers." "Shortages were most pronounced for certain types of professional and skilled workers." Clear tightness language. Score ~60. Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says tight. Matches.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "appeared to have grown at a steady pace." "Retail activity in most Districts had been relatively robust." "Investment spending also appeared to be holding up well." Business contacts "generally optimistic." However, housing is a major drag and auto sector weakness. Net: consumer and business investment solid, housing pulling down. The rubric says not to separately score housing construction. Consumer spending at steady pace, investment holding up well — roughly at or modestly above trend. Score ~52. Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Evidence says steady/holding up. Matches.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Favorable financial conditions" mentioned as supporting consumer spending. "Strength of corporate balance sheets and profits" supporting investment. No discussion of tight credit or stressed markets. Conditions appear generally loose/supportive. Score ~30. Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says favorable/supportive = loose. Below 50. Matches. The 2005-06 anchor is 10-20; however, the discussion here is quite limited — a brief mention of "favorable" conditions — so a score of ~28-30 seems appropriate.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Nearly all members favored keeping the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent." On hold but with clear inflation concern. "All members agreed that the risks to achieving the anticipated reduction in inflation remained of greatest concern." "Additional policy firming was possible." This is a hold with a tightening bias. Score ~58. Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says hold with tightening bias and dominant inflation concern. Matches.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Nearly all members favored keeping the target" — the "nearly all" suggests perhaps one would have preferred otherwise. "Most members judged downside risks had diminished a little." Views are largely aligned. Broad agreement with minor nuances on risk assessment. Score ~22. Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Evidence shows broad alignment with minor differences. Low score. Matches.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Housing correction discussed extensively but as an economic activity issue, not financial stability per se. Brief mention of "favorable financial conditions." Concern about house prices impacting consumer spending. Some mention of strain on construction firms. Limited direct financial stability discussion. Score ~35. The 2006-07 pre-crisis anchor is 40-55, but this meeting has less financial stability focus than some — mostly housing as real-economy drag, not systemic risk. Score ~35.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Statement should continue to convey that inflation risks remained the dominant concern and that additional policy firming was possible." "A significant amount of data would be published before the next Committee meeting... giving the Committee ample scope to refine its assessment... before judging whether any additional firming was needed." This signals a tightening bias but with data dependence — not committing to hike but keeping the door open. Score ~62. Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence says possible further tightening. Matches.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Most participants expected core inflation to moderate gradually, but they were quite uncertain as to the likely pace and extent of that moderation." "Substantial uncertainty continued to attend that outlook." "Many participants expressed concern that ongoing developments in the housing market could have a more pronounced impact." Explicit "quite uncertain" language and "substantial uncertainty." Score ~50. Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. Evidence shows elevated uncertainty. Matches.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (48) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (60): These diverge somewhat. Explanation: The economy is growing near/slightly below potential pace, but the labor market remains tight from the prior expansion — a lagging indicator. The text explicitly describes this: growth slowing but labor market still tight. This divergence is well-supported.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (58) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (62): These align — both show a mild tightening bias. The forward guidance is slightly more hawkish because the statement explicitly mentions "additional firming was possible."

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 65 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All meeting participants expressed concern about the outlook for inflation."
- "Nearly all participants viewed the current rates of core inflation as uncomfortably high and stressed the importance of further moderation."
- "All participants emphasized that the risks around the desired downward path to inflation remained to the upside."

**Center-of-gravity**: Universal concern about elevated core inflation, though most expect gradual moderation; risks firmly to the upside.

**Minority views**: None dissent from the inflation concern — the uncertainty band reflects differing views on pace of moderation (some noted recent slowing "helped to allay their fears").

---

### OUTPUT_GAP: 48 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Nearly all members expected that the economy would expand close to or a little below its potential growth rate."
- "The ongoing adjustment in the housing market was likely to depress real activity in the near term."
- "Private final domestic purchases had held up well in recent months."

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy viewed as near potential but growth trajectory is slightly below potential pace, with housing drag offset by resilient private demand.

**Minority views**: One participant noted possible broader weakness from oil price decline possibly reflecting weak global demand; most saw temporary factors.

---

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 60 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Both data and reports from businesses indicated that the labor market remained tight."
- "Many of their contacts were increasingly concerned about the difficulty of recruiting suitably qualified workers."
- "The possibility that the tightness of the labor market could lead to a sustained increase in wage pressure was viewed by participants as an upside risk."

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear consensus that labor market is tight with specific reports of worker shortages, though aggregate compensation data giving "contradictory signals."

**Minority views**: Limited; the contradiction in wage data creates some uncertainty but not disagreement on tightness.

---

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 52 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending... appeared to have grown at a steady pace in recent months. Retail activity in most Districts had been relatively robust."
- "Investment spending also appeared to be holding up well... business contacts were generally optimistic."
- "Housing activity was likely to remain a substantial drag on economic growth over the next few quarters."

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer and business investment spending at a steady, modestly positive pace, with housing acting as drag but not spilling over.

**Minority views**: "Many participants expressed concern that ongoing developments in the housing market could have a more pronounced impact on consumer and other spending."

---

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 28 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Consumer spending "supported by favorable financial conditions."
- "The strength of corporate balance sheets and profits was seen as likely to help maintain a solid profile for investment spending."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions described as favorable and supportive, consistent with loose conditions.

**Minority views**: None articulated. Limited discussion of financial conditions specifically.

---

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 58 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Nearly all members favored keeping the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent at this meeting."
- "All members agreed that the risks to achieving the anticipated reduction in inflation remained of greatest concern."
- "The statement... should continue to convey that inflation risks remained the dominant concern and that additional policy firming was possible."

**Center-of-gravity**: Hold with a clear tightening bias; inflation is the dominant concern and further firming remains on the table.

**Minority views**: "Some members felt that the upside risks to inflation had declined, albeit only slightly" — modestly less hawkish voices exist but do not change the center of gravity.

---

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 22 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Nearly all members favored keeping the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent."
- "All members agreed that the risks to achieving the anticipated reduction in inflation remained of greatest concern."
- "Most members judged that the downside risks to economic activity had diminished a little."

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on holding rates and on inflation as primary concern; "nearly all" rather than "all" on rate decision suggests at most one dissenter or leaner.

**Minority views**: Minor differences in degree of concern about growth vs. inflation risks.

---

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 35 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Housing correction discussed extensively as economic drag, with "considerable strain on some small- and medium-sized residential construction firms."
- "Favorable financial conditions" mentioned briefly as supportive factor.
- Concern about house price declines impacting spending.

**Center-of-gravity**: Housing discussed primarily as real-economy drag rather than systemic financial risk; limited direct financial stability discussion.

**Minority views**: None specifically on financial stability.

---

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 62 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The statement... should continue to convey that inflation risks remained the dominant concern and that additional policy firming was possible."
- "A significant amount of data would be published before the next Committee meeting... giving the Committee ample scope to refine its assessment... before judging whether any additional firming was needed."

**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal is mildly hawkish — additional firming possible but data-dependent, not pre-committed.

**Minority views**: The data-dependence language ("ample scope to refine") tempers the hawkish lean modestly.

---

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 50 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "They were quite uncertain as to the likely pace and extent of that moderation."
- "Substantial uncertainty continued to attend that outlook."
- "Many participants expressed concern that ongoing developments in the housing market could have a more pronounced impact on consumer and other spending, especially if house prices declined significantly."

**Center-of-gravity**: Elevated uncertainty particularly around the inflation moderation path and housing spillover risks; explicit "quite uncertain" and "substantial uncertainty" language.

**Minority views**: "Most members judged that the downside risks to economic activity had diminished a little" — some felt more confident than others.
