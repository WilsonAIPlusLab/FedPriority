# FOMC Meeting Scoring — 2007-12-11 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Pre-2009 unsegmented format — staff and participant content mixed. I will score only portions with clear participant attribution language ("participants noted," "members judged," "some participants," etc.). The first 16 lines appear to be staff forecast content and are excluded.

**Era context**: December 2007 — the very onset of the Great Recession (NBER later dated the recession start to December 2007). The subprime mortgage crisis was intensifying, housing was in steep decline, and financial markets were experiencing renewed stress. The Committee had already cut rates 75bp (from 5.25% to 4.50%) in September and October. This meeting cut another 25bp to 4.25%.

**Quantifier distribution**: "participants generally" (outlook weaker), "participants agreed" (housing correction deeper, downside risks, inflation expectations), "most participants" (continued to anticipate moderation in inflation), "nearly all members" (25bp cut appropriate), "some participants" (concerns about household spending, cited weaker dollar, more-positive financial aspects), "several participants" (commercial real estate solid, strong foreign growth might not persist), "a few" — not prominent. The dominant quantifier pattern is "participants" (collective) with "some" as the notable minority marker.

**Core dimension analysis**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as somewhat above target with upside risks. "Recent readings on inflation generally were seen as slightly less favorable than in earlier months." Energy/commodity prices and weaker dollar put upward pressure. However, "most participants continued to anticipate some moderation in core and especially headline inflation over the next few years." There is concern about upside risks but expectations of moderation. This places inflation as modestly elevated but expected to moderate — around 55-58. Direction: above midpoint but not strongly so.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: "Incoming information pointed to a somewhat weaker outlook for spending." Growth "likely to be somewhat more sluggish." Economy expected to "gradually recover" and "expand at about its trend rate in 2009." Still, "some data, including readings on the labor market, suggested that the economy retained forward momentum." The economy is at or slightly below potential, slowing toward below potential. Score around 44-46 — slightly below potential and deteriorating.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Job growth rebounded somewhat in October and November" and "readings on the labor market suggested that the economy retained forward momentum." Participants expected "continuing gains in employment and income" but "slower growth of jobs." "Few business contacts had reported unusually large wage increases." Labor market still functional but softening. Score around 46-48 — near full employment but easing.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Marked deceleration in consumer spending" — "real personal consumption expenditures had shown essentially no growth in September and October." Business investment showed "solid but appreciably less rapid growth." Housing "weaker than participants had expected." Consumer confidence "dropped by a sizable amount." Exports strong. Net: clearly below trend. Score around 33-37.

5. **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Extensive discussion of financial stress. "Resurgence of stresses in financial markets," "lenders to tighten further the terms on new extensions of credit," "pronounced widening in term spreads," "diminished liquidity in interbank markets." But "credit to most nonfinancial firms remained relatively low" and "consumer credit remained readily available for most households." Tightening is significant but not yet at crisis levels for the broader economy. Score around 68-72.

6. **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Nearly all members judged that a 25 basis point reduction... would be appropriate." Already eased 75bp. Members saw downside risks. But members also noted "some inflation pressures and risks remained" and that "a reversal of some of the rate cuts might become appropriate" if financial conditions improved. Moderate dovish lean with inflation caution. Score around 32-36.

7. **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Nearly all members" suggests one or possibly two did not favor the 25bp cut. The text notes tension between those worried about downside growth risks (wanting more easing) and those concerned about inflation. "Members also recognized that financial market conditions might improve more rapidly... in which case a reversal of some of the rate cuts might become appropriate." Some divergence but broad agreement on the 25bp action. Score around 30-35.

8. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: A very large portion of the discussion — an entire multi-paragraph section — is devoted to financial market stress. "Participants discussed in detail the resurgence of stresses in financial markets." Financial conditions dominate the meeting discussion alongside the economic outlook. Score around 72-78.

9. **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is easing-leaning but cautious. "Some members noted the risk of an unfavorable feedback loop... such an adverse development could require a substantial further easing of policy." But "members also recognized that financial market conditions might improve more rapidly than members expected, in which case a reversal of some of the rate cuts might become appropriate." The statement language was largely unchanged on inflation. This is a dovish lean with data dependence. Score around 30-35.

10. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The economic outlook unusually uncertain." "Heightened financial stress... made the outlook for the economy considerably more uncertain." "The situation was quite fluid." Genuine elevated uncertainty. Score around 68-72.

**Cross-dimension coherence checks**:
- OUTPUT_GAP (45) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (47): Both slightly below midpoint, consistent — economy at the cusp of weakening but labor market not yet deeply impaired. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (34) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (32): Both moderately dovish, with some caution. Coherent.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (70) and FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (75): High tightness with high attention. Coherent.

**Polarity checks**:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says modestly elevated with upside risks. Score 57 = above midpoint. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says slightly below potential and weakening. Score 45 = below midpoint. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says significant tightening in credit. Score 70 = well above midpoint. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says dovish (cutting rates, easing). Score 34 = below midpoint. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 57 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Recent readings on inflation generally were seen as slightly less favorable than in earlier months, partly due to upward revisions to previously published data."
- "Participants remained concerned about upside risks to inflation stemming from elevated prices of energy and non-energy commodities; some also cited the weaker dollar."
- "Most participants continued to anticipate some moderation in core and especially headline inflation over the next few years."

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants perceive inflation as modestly above target with upside risks from commodities and the dollar, but expect gradual moderation. Current readings slightly worse than expected but not alarming.

**Minority views**: "Some participants said that higher input costs and rising prices of imports were leading more firms to seek price increases" — these voices push the uncertainty band upward.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 45 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Incoming information pointed to a somewhat weaker outlook for spending than at the time of the October meeting."
- "Growth in late 2007 and during 2008 was likely to be somewhat more sluggish than participants had indicated in their October projections."
- "Some data, including readings on the labor market, suggested that the economy retained forward momentum."

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is at or slightly below potential, with clear deceleration underway. Not yet in deep recession territory but weakening, with participants expecting sluggish growth.

**Minority views**: The range spans from those seeing retained forward momentum to those fearing an unfavorable feedback loop leading to sharper contraction.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 47 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Job growth rebounded somewhat in October and November, and participants expected continuing gains in employment and income to support rising consumer spending."
- "They anticipated slower growth of jobs, income, and spending than in recent years."
- "Few business contacts had reported unusually large wage increases."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market still near full employment but with clear signs of softening. Jobs still growing but at a slower pace, wage pressures moderate.

**Minority views**: Limited divergence on labor market assessment.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 35 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants noted the marked deceleration in consumer spending... Real personal consumption expenditures had shown essentially no growth in September and October."
- "The housing correction was likely to be both deeper and more prolonged than they had anticipated in October."
- "Solid but appreciably less rapid growth in business fixed investment during the fourth quarter than the third."

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending has stalled, housing is in steep decline, and business investment is decelerating. Exports provide a partial offset. Overall, spending is clearly below trend.

**Minority views**: "Retailers saw solid growth" in some regions; "contacts in other parts of the country or industries reported no such change" in capital spending — regional variation widens the band.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 70 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Lenders to tighten further the terms on new extensions of credit, not just in the troubled markets for nonconforming mortgages but, in some cases, for other forms of credit as well."
- "Pronounced widening in term spreads for periods extending through year-end" and "diminished liquidity in interbank markets."
- "The cost of credit to most nonfinancial firms remained relatively low" and "consumer credit remained readily available for most households."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions have tightened significantly, especially in mortgage and interbank markets, with widening credit spreads. However, the broader economy has not yet experienced a full credit freeze — nonfinancial firms and most consumers still have access to credit.

**Minority views**: "Some participants cited more-positive aspects of recent financial developments" including capital raises by large financial intermediaries and modest losses at regional banks.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 34 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Members judged that the softening in the outlook for economic growth warranted an easing of the stance of policy at this meeting."
- "Nearly all members judged that a 25 basis point reduction... would be appropriate."
- "Some inflation pressures and risks remained, including pressures from elevated commodity and energy prices and the possibility of upward drift in the public's expectations of inflation."

**Center-of-gravity**: Moderately dovish — the Committee is cutting rates but cautiously, balancing downside growth risks against lingering inflation concerns. The 25bp cut (rather than 50bp) and attention to inflation risks temper the dovishness.

**Minority views**: Some members noted risk of needing "substantial further easing," while others noted rate cuts "might" need to be reversed — creating a wider range than the action suggests.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 33 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Nearly all members judged that a 25 basis point reduction... would be appropriate" — implying one or two did not fully agree.
- Tension between those emphasizing downside growth risks and those emphasizing inflation risks.
- "Members also recognized that financial market conditions might improve more rapidly... in which case a reversal of some of the rate cuts might become appropriate."

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on the policy action with moderate differences about the balance of risks and appropriate forward posture. "Nearly all" suggests at least one member preferred a different action.

**Minority views**: The text implies at least one member may have preferred either a larger cut or no cut, but the disagreement is not sharply characterized.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 75 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants discussed in detail the resurgence of stresses in financial markets in November" — dedicating multiple paragraphs.
- Discussion covers mortgage-related losses, bank balance sheet pressures, interbank liquidity, term spreads, credit tightening, commercial paper, leveraged loans, and the new Term Auction Facility.
- "Heightened financial stress posed increased downside risks to growth and made the outlook for the economy considerably more uncertain."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability is a prominent, arguably dominant topic. The discussion is extensive and detailed, rivaling the economic assessment in length and depth.

**Minority views**: Minimal — virtually all participants engaged with the financial stability topic.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 32 +/-10 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Some members noted the risk of an unfavorable feedback loop... such an adverse development could require a substantial further easing of policy."
- "Members also recognized that financial market conditions might improve more rapidly than members expected, in which case a reversal of some of the rate cuts might become appropriate."
- "The situation was quite fluid and the economic outlook unusually uncertain."

**Center-of-gravity**: The forward signal leans dovish — further easing is on the table if conditions deteriorate — but with significant data dependence and explicit acknowledgment that reversal is possible. The wide uncertainty band reflects the fluid, two-sided language.

**Minority views**: Range from "substantial further easing" to possible "reversal" — an unusually wide range of forward scenarios.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 70 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The situation was quite fluid and the economic outlook unusually uncertain."
- "Heightened financial stress... made the outlook for the economy considerably more uncertain."
- "Uncertainties about values of mortgage-related assets and related losses, and consequently strains in financial markets, could persist for quite some time."

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants express genuinely elevated uncertainty — the word "unusually" is a strong intensifier beyond routine caveats. Multiple sources of uncertainty are cited: financial market duration, housing depth, consumer behavior, and feedback loops.

**Minority views**: Uncertainty is broadly shared; no participant is described as expressing high confidence in the outlook.
