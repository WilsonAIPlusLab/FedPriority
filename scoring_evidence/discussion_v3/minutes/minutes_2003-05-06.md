# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2003-05-06

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is **minutes** (synthesized summary of participant views). It is unsegmented (pre-2009), meaning staff and participant content may be mixed. I will score ONLY portions with participant language cues ("members commented," "members noted," "members anticipated," etc.) and IGNORE staff-attributed content.

### Era Context
May 2003: The economy is in the aftermath of the 2001 recession and the Iraq War. The fed funds rate is at 1.25%. The economy is experiencing sluggish growth with substantial excess capacity. Deflation risk is a genuine concern. The stock market has begun recovering. I will let the text, not the era backdrop, drive scores.

### Quantifier Distribution
- "all the members" — support unchanged policy stance
- "members" (general) — used frequently for consensus views on economy, inflation, outlook
- "many private-sector forecasters" — predicted upturn
- "some members" — cautioned about prolonged inaction
- "two members" — saw merit in balanced risks assessment
- "most business decisionmakers" — preferred fuller utilization over expansion

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW target and falling further. Key evidence: "Members anticipated that inflation would remain at a low level for an extended period and indeed that the probability of further disinflation was higher than that of a pickup in inflation, given the current high levels of excess capacity." Also: "substantial additional disinflation would be unwelcome because of the likely negative effects on economic activity." There is explicit concern about deflation risk, though members agreed "only a remote possibility" of extended price-level decline. This is clearly in the 15-25 range: meaningful deflation risk, well below target, "subdued." The word "disinflation" appears repeatedly and is the dominant inflation concern. Score: ~20.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as clearly BELOW potential. "Current high levels of excess capacity in labor and product markets, which seemed likely to diminish only gradually." "Widespread excess capacity" is mentioned repeatedly. "Persisting weakness" in business investment. "Near-term sluggishness in economic growth." The bright-line rule: "excess capacity" = below potential, score MUST be < 50. This is substantial slack, but not catastrophic contraction — the economy is growing, just below potential. Score: ~30. Members anticipate improvement but slack persists.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: The labor market is clearly slack. "High levels of excess capacity in labor... markets." "Disappointing" employment reports. "High degree of caution in the business community with regard to... hiring decisions." This indicates significant slack, well below full employment. Score: ~28.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed but below trend overall. Business investment: "persisting weakness," "widespread excess capacity as a reason for holding down business capital spending." Consumer spending: "appreciable decline in sales of motor vehicles and slower growth in other consumer spending." "Members did not see any firm indications of significant acceleration in consumer spending." Some positive signs (improving confidence, stock market gains) but current conditions are weak. Housing "remained at a high overall level" but "further impetus... likely to be limited." Net position: below trend, sluggish. Score: ~34.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Financial conditions have improved notably. "Marked strengthening of domestic financial markets, a sizable decline in oil prices." "Gains in the stock market." "Decline in the cost of business capital." "Favorable financing" for housing. Monetary policy described as "accommodative." However, there's mention of past "corporate governance and credit problems" being unwound. Overall, conditions are loose/accommodative. Score: ~28 (somewhat loose, supportive conditions).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All members support maintaining the current stance (hold at 1.25%). A "case could be made for easing policy immediately" was acknowledged. "Some members cautioned that persisting uncertainty... should not provide a basis for prolonged inaction... an easing move might be desirable in the near term, perhaps at the June meeting." The lean is clearly dovish — they held but with an easing bias. This is in the 35-42 range: hold steady with mild easing bias. Score: ~38.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All the members indicated that they could support a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance." Unanimous support. However, "some members" wanted to ease soon, and "two members" saw merit in a different risk assessment. There are mild differences about timing/framing but no formal dissent and broad agreement on the action. Score: ~22.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial conditions are mentioned in the context of economic outlook (stock market recovery, financial market strengthening, corporate governance issues being unwound). There's mention of "the functioning of financial institutions and markets" in the context of disinflation risk. But financial stability per se is not a prominent standalone topic — it's mentioned as background. Score: ~25.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is dovish. The new language: "the probability of some disinflation from an already low level exceeded that of a pickup in inflation" and "balance of risks to the Committee's dual goals was tilted toward the downside." Some members explicitly mentioned "an easing move might be desirable in the near term, perhaps at the June meeting." This is a mild easing bias with data dependence. Score: ~30.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The timing and vigor of a pickup in economic activity remained uncertain, especially in the context of a persistently high degree of caution in the business community." "Not enough time had elapsed since the end of the Iraqi war to sort out the underlying forces at work." "Persisting uncertainty regarding economic trends." Uncertainty is a genuine theme but not extreme — they expect improvement, they just can't confirm it yet. Score: ~50 (elevated uncertainty, multiple participants emphasize it).

### Polarity Checks
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says disinflation/deflation risk. Low score (20). Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says substantial excess capacity. Low score (30). Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says significant labor slack. Low score (28). Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence says conditions are loose/accommodative. Low score (28). Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says hold with easing bias. Score 38 (below 50). Correct.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence says downside risk tilt, possible near-term easing. Score 30. Correct.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (30) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (28): Both below 50, both showing slack. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (38) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (30): Both dovish-leaning. Forward guidance slightly more dovish because of the explicit downside risk language and mention of possible June easing. Coherent — the forward signal is slightly more dovish than the current hold action.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 20 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Members anticipated that inflation would remain at a low level for an extended period and indeed that the probability of further disinflation was higher than that of a pickup in inflation, given the current high levels of excess capacity in labor and product markets."
- "Even assuming a pickup in the expansion of economic activity... excess capacity in labor and product markets would remain elevated and might well foster further disinflation over coming quarters."
- "Members also agreed that there was only a remote possibility that the process of disinflation would cumulate to the point of a decline for an extended period in the general price level."

**Center of gravity**: Members see inflation as already low and likely to fall further, with deflation risk acknowledged though deemed remote. The dominant concern is unwelcome disinflation, not inflation pressure.

**Minority views**: None — consensus view that disinflation is the risk. Dollar depreciation and worker benefit costs cited as limited offsets.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Current high levels of excess capacity in labor and product markets, which seemed likely to diminish only gradually."
- "Anecdotal reports by business contacts tended to emphasize widespread excess capacity as a reason for holding down business capital spending."
- "Near-term sluggishness in economic growth would give way to more vigorous expansion as the year progressed."

**Center of gravity**: The economy is operating well below potential with substantial excess capacity that is expected to persist even with anticipated growth improvement. Members expect recovery but the gap remains large.

**Minority views**: None — all acknowledge significant slack. Disagreement is about how quickly it closes.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 28 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "High levels of excess capacity in labor... markets."
- "The latest reports on economic activity generally were disappointing, notably those relating to employment."
- "A persistently high degree of caution in the business community with regard to investment and hiring decisions."

**Center of gravity**: The labor market has significant slack with disappointing employment reports and businesses reluctant to hire. Well below full employment.

**Minority views**: None evident — consensus on labor slack.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 34 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Business fixed investment remained a key factor... persisting weakness in such spending in association with gloomy sentiment and a high degree of risk aversion among business decisionmakers."
- "An appreciable decline in sales of motor vehicles and slower growth in other consumer spending in the first quarter."
- "The members did not see any firm indications of significant acceleration in consumer spending."
- Housing "remained at a high overall level" but "any further impetus to growth from that sector was likely to be limited."

**Center of gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment are below trend. Business investment is particularly weak due to excess capacity and risk aversion. Some forward-looking positives (improving confidence, stock gains, fiscal stimulus) but current conditions are sluggish.

**Minority views**: Members note positive forward signals but agree current conditions are weak.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 28 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The successful prosecution of the war had served to reduce geopolitical uncertainties and in turn had helped to foster a marked strengthening of domestic financial markets."
- "Favorable factors in the outlook mentioned by members included an accommodative monetary policy."
- "A decline in the cost of business capital."
- "Favorable financing would help to sustain the housing sector."

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are notably loose and accommodative. Stock market has strengthened markedly, borrowing costs have declined, and monetary policy is described as accommodative. Past credit/governance problems are being worked through.

**Minority views**: Past corporate governance and credit problems are noted as constraints being unwound, suggesting conditions were tighter recently but are now improving.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All the members indicated that they could support a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance."
- "The members acknowledged that a case could be made for easing policy immediately... Nonetheless, they concluded that, on balance, an easing action was not desirable at this time."
- "Some members cautioned that persisting uncertainty regarding economic trends should not provide a basis for prolonged inaction... an easing move might be desirable in the near term, perhaps at the June meeting."

**Center of gravity**: Unanimous hold but with a clear easing bias. Members explicitly acknowledge the case for easing and some signal it may come soon. This is a dovish hold — maintaining accommodation with readiness to ease further.

**Minority views**: "Some members" pushed more explicitly for near-term easing. "Two members" preferred a different framing of the risk assessment. The center of gravity is a patient but dovish hold.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 22 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All the members indicated that they could support a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance."
- "Some members cautioned that persisting uncertainty regarding economic trends should not provide a basis for prolonged inaction."
- "Two members saw merit in adopting a balanced risks assessment at this meeting."

**Center of gravity**: Unanimous on the policy action. Mild differences on communication framing and timing of potential future easing. No formal dissent. Broad agreement with minor emphasis differences.

**Minority views**: Two members preferred different risk language; some wanted to signal more urgency about possible easing. These are tactical, not fundamental, disagreements.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 25 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Substantial additional disinflation would be unwelcome because of the likely negative effects on economic activity and the functioning of financial institutions and markets."
- "Steps taken to address corporate governance and credit problems were also working to strengthen the expansion."
- Stock market recovery discussed as positive economic factor.

**Center of gravity**: Financial stability is mentioned in passing — the functioning of financial institutions in the context of deflation risk, and the resolution of corporate governance issues. It is not a major standalone topic of discussion.

**Minority views**: None on this dimension.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 30 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The balance of risks to the Committee's dual goals was tilted toward the downside over the foreseeable future."
- "The probability of some disinflation from an already low level exceeded that of a pickup in inflation."
- "In the absence of convincing indications of an appreciable pickup in economic growth, an easing move might be desirable in the near term, perhaps at the June meeting."

**Center of gravity**: The forward signal is clearly dovish. The new risk assessment language explicitly tilts downside, and some members signal possible easing at the next meeting. This is a mild easing bias with data dependence.

**Minority views**: Two members preferred balanced risk assessment, suggesting slightly less dovish forward signal from a minority.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 50 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The timing and vigor of a pickup in economic activity remained uncertain, especially in the context of a persistently high degree of caution in the business community."
- "Not enough time had elapsed since the end of the Iraqi war to sort out the underlying forces at work in the economy."
- "Persisting uncertainty regarding economic trends."
- "It was not clear at this point how some state and local governments would resolve their current budgetary crises."

**Center of gravity**: Uncertainty is elevated and explicitly acknowledged as a reason for the hold decision. Members emphasize that the post-war environment makes assessment difficult, but they do have a base case expectation of improvement. This is genuine elevated uncertainty, not just rhetorical hedging.

**Minority views**: None — uncertainty itself is a consensus view, though members differ on whether it justifies inaction.
