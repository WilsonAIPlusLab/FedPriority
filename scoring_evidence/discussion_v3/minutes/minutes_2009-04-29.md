# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2009-04-29

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). This is a post-2009 segmented document, so participant and staff content are reasonably separated.

**Era context**: April 2009 — deep in the Great Financial Crisis. The economy is in severe recession but the first tentative signs of stabilization are emerging. The Fed is at the zero lower bound and conducting large-scale asset purchases (QE). Unemployment is rising sharply.

**Quantifier distribution**: "participants agreed" (consensus level), "most" (inflation subdued), "many" (inflation expectations stable), "several" (balance sheet concerns), "some" (various observations), "a few" (global signs of abatement), "a number of" (stress test concerns, regional surveys).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target. "Most participants expected inflation to remain subdued over the next few years" and saw risk that "elevated unemployment and low capacity utilization could cause inflation to remain persistently below" appropriate rates. However, deflation risk was seen as having "diminished" — monthly readings were above the low rates of late 2008, and survey expectations were "reasonably stable." Several participants flagged upside risks from the balance sheet. Direction: below target but deflation fears receding. Score should be in the 18-25 range. Higher = more pressure. Low inflation = low score. Score ~20.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: The economy is in severe contraction. "Pace of contraction in real economic activity was starting to diminish" — but still contracting. "Acceleration in final demand...over the next few quarters would be modest." Business investment "would probably shrink further." Multiple restraining factors listed for medium-term recovery. This is deep below potential. Score ~18.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Labor market is extremely weak. "Labor market conditions were still deteriorating." "Unemployment claims were exceptionally elevated." "Ratio of permanent job cuts to temporary layoffs was substantially higher than in previous economic downturns." Staff reductions even at hospitals/nonprofits. Large part-time-for-economic-reasons. This is near the worst end. Score ~14.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "firmed somewhat" in Q1, a glimmer of improvement. But recovery expected to be "slow." Business investment "would probably shrink further." "Magnitude of the retrenchment in production and capital spending" cited. Some forward-looking optimism from surveys but current conditions very weak. Net assessment: deeply below trend but stabilizing. Score ~22.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Mixed picture. Financial markets improved: equity prices up, volatility down, risk spreads narrowed, corporate bond issuance "relatively brisk." BUT: "risk spreads remained unusually wide," "markets continued to be fragile," "credit conditions stayed quite restrictive for many households and businesses," banks still tightening terms, credit volume still contracting, securitization markets limited. Net: still tight, though improving from crisis peak. Score ~70.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Members agreed that large-scale asset purchases were "providing financial stimulus" and should continue. The entire discussion is about maintaining maximum accommodation. No hint of tightening. The concern is whether they're doing enough, not whether they're doing too much. Firmly dovish. Score ~12.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Committee members agreed" on continuing purchases. There's no mention of formal dissent. There are differences in emphasis — some worried about deflation, several worried about balance sheet expansion and future inflation — but the policy direction has broad agreement. Score ~25.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Substantial discussion. An entire paragraph on financial market developments. Discussion of stress test results and potential market volatility. Commercial real estate losses and consequences for banks. Global banking system fragility. Financial system "remained vulnerable to further shocks." This is a prominent topic but not the sole focus. Score ~65.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Members agreed to "continue making purchases" of securities. The entire forward signal is about maintaining accommodation. No discussion of exit timing as imminent. Several mentioned concerns about reversing the balance sheet "in a sufficiently timely manner" — but this was contacts' concerns, not participants calling for tightening. The forward lean is firmly dovish/accommodative. Score ~10.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Participants "emphasized the tentative nature of the incoming data, which are volatile and subject to revision." They noted challenges of identifying recovery onset, that financial-crisis recessions are "more severe and protracted," "continued to see significant downside risks," "global financial system remained vulnerable to further shocks," stress test results might "trigger volatility." Also, some noted pitfalls of using slack-based inflation projections. Substantial uncertainty expressed. Score ~68.

### Cross-dimension coherence check:
- OUTPUT_GAP (18) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (14): Both deep below 50 — consistent with deep recession. Labor slightly lower reflects that labor is explicitly described as "still deteriorating" even as output contraction pace is diminishing.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (12) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (10): Both firmly dovish — consistent. QE is ongoing and continuing.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Low score for subdued inflation. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Low score for deep below potential. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter conditions. High score for restrictive credit. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Low score for strong dovishness/QE. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 20 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Most participants expected inflation to remain subdued over the next few years, and they saw some risk that elevated unemployment and low capacity utilization could cause inflation to remain persistently below the rates that they judged as most consistent with sustainable economic growth"
- "the risk of a protracted period of deflation had diminished" — deflation fears receding but still present
- "Several participants referred to contacts who had expressed concerns that the expansion of the Federal Reserve's balance sheet might not be reversed in a sufficiently timely manner and hence that inflation could rise"

**Center of gravity**: Most see inflation subdued with some lingering downside risk; deflation risk diminished but not eliminated. Meaningful deflation risk zone.
**Minority views**: Several flagged upside inflation risk from balance sheet expansion — informs the +7 band upward.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 18 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "participants agreed that the information received since the March meeting provided some tentative evidence that the pace of contraction in real economic activity was starting to diminish"
- "the acceleration in final demand and economic activity over the next few quarters would be modest"
- "Business investment spending would probably shrink further"

**Center of gravity**: Economy deep below potential; contraction pace diminishing but still contracting, with only modest recovery anticipated.
**Minority views**: Narrow range of views — all agree on deep contraction.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 14 +/-4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Labor market conditions were still deteriorating. Unemployment claims were exceptionally elevated"
- "the ratio of permanent job cuts to temporary layoffs was substantially higher than in previous economic downturns"
- "Staff reductions were under way even at traditionally stable employers such as hospitals and nonprofit institutions"

**Center of gravity**: Near-depression-level labor weakness, still worsening. Exceptionally elevated claims, unprecedented permanent layoff ratios.
**Minority views**: No dissent on this assessment — all agree labor market is extremely weak.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 22 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending firmed somewhat during the first quarter despite the rising unemployment rate" but "recovery in consumer demand over the next few quarters would be slow"
- "the magnitude of the retrenchment in production and capital spending" — business investment still shrinking
- "Growth of consumption expenditures was likely to be restrained by the weakness in labor markets and the lagged effects of past reductions in household wealth"

**Center of gravity**: Deep below trend — consumption showing faint stabilization but investment still contracting; overall spending outlook weak.
**Minority views**: Some saw early manufacturing sentiment improvement as a positive signal, but not enough to shift the center significantly.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 70 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "credit conditions stayed quite restrictive for many households and businesses"
- "a large net fraction of banks had tightened their terms and standards for credit during the previous three months"
- "the volume of credit extended to households and businesses was still contracting"
- Offsetting: "Equity prices increased, money markets were functioning better, and corporate issuance of bonds and convertible securities was relatively brisk"

**Center of gravity**: Still tight — credit conditions restrictive, banks tightening, credit contracting — but improving from peak crisis levels. Spreads narrowing but "remained unusually wide."
**Minority views**: The improving market conditions partially offset the tight credit picture, informing the downward side of the band.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 12 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Committee members agreed that the Federal Reserve's large-scale securities purchases were providing financial stimulus that would contribute to the gradual resumption of sustainable economic growth"
- "Members also agreed that it would be appropriate to continue making purchases" (document ends mid-sentence confirming ongoing QE)
- No mention of any dissent or calls to tighten

**Center of gravity**: Strong dovish consensus — actively conducting QE and agreeing to continue. Firmly in "maintaining max accommodation" territory.
**Minority views**: Several contacts (not participants directly) worried about balance sheet risks, but this did not translate into hawkish policy preferences by participants.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 25 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Committee members agreed" on continuing asset purchases — no formal dissent mentioned
- Difference in emphasis: "most" saw subdued inflation but "several" flagged balance sheet/inflation risks — some intellectual disagreement on inflation outlook
- Broad consensus on economic assessment and policy action

**Center of gravity**: Broad agreement with minor differences in emphasis on inflation risks. No formal dissent visible.
**Minority views**: The "several" flagging inflation concerns from balance sheet expansion represents a meaningful minority view on risks, though not on current policy.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 65 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Full paragraph on financial market developments (equity prices, spreads, volatility, corporate issuance)
- "credit conditions stayed quite restrictive for many households and businesses" — detailed credit discussion
- "the global financial system remained vulnerable to further shocks"
- Stress test discussion: "investors were concerned that the upcoming publication of stress test results might trigger volatility"
- "mounting losses in commercial real estate, which could have substantial adverse consequences for regional banks"

**Center of gravity**: Financial stability is a prominent topic — extensive discussion of credit conditions, market fragility, bank stress, and CRE risks. Not quite dominant (the economic outlook discussion is equally or more prominent).
**Minority views**: None — broad agreement on the importance of financial conditions.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 10 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Members agreed to "continue making purchases" — ongoing QE commitment
- No discussion of exit timing or normalization
- Entire forward posture is about maintaining accommodation and supporting recovery
- Several mentioned concern that balance sheet might not be reversed "in a sufficiently timely manner" — but this was contacts' concern, not a policy signal

**Center of gravity**: Strong easing bias with commitment to ongoing large-scale purchases. Forward signal is firmly accommodative.
**Minority views**: The document is truncated, so some nuance about the forward guidance language may be missing — reflected in moderate confidence.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 68 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "participants emphasized the tentative nature of the incoming data, which are volatile and subject to revision"
- "The experience of previous recessions underscored the challenges of identifying the onset of economic recovery"
- "economic downturns that had been triggered by financial crises generally concluded that such contractions tended to be more severe and protracted"
- "participants continued to see significant downside risks to the economic outlook"
- "the global financial system remained vulnerable to further shocks"

**Center of gravity**: High uncertainty — participants explicitly flag data tentative, identification challenges, financial system vulnerability, and significant downside risks. Multiple sources of uncertainty emphasized.
**Minority views**: No one expressed high confidence in the outlook — uncertainty was broadly shared.
