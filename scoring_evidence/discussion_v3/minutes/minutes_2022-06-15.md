# FOMC Meeting Scoring — 2022-06-15 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented format — clear separation between staff review and participant discussion sections.

**Era context**: June 2022 — peak inflation cycle. CPI at 8.6% (May), PCE at 6.3%. The Committee is in the midst of an aggressive tightening cycle, having just delivered a 75bp hike (the first since 1994). Russia-Ukraine war ongoing, China lockdowns exacerbating supply chains.

**Quantifier distribution**: Heavy use of strong quantifiers — "participants" (general/all), "all participants," "almost all participants," "most," "many," and "several." Dissenting language is limited: "one participant" preferred 50bp, "a few" and "some" used for minority views. The text is dominated by consensus language.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as far above target. "Inflation remained much too high" and "continued to run well above the Committee's longer-run 2 percent objective." PCE at 6.3%, CPI at 8.6% in May — "came in above expectations." "Inflation pressures had yet to show signs of abating." Broadening across goods and services. "Many participants raised the concern that longer-run inflation expectations could be beginning to drift up." This is clearly in the 82-88 anchor range. Direction: well above midpoint. Score ~85.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Overall economic activity appeared to have picked up after edging down in the first quarter." GDP rebounding. Staff projected "the level of real GDP was still expected to remain well above potential." Supply-demand imbalances across wide range of product markets. Demand outstripping supply. Bright-line rule: "above potential" language means score MUST be > 50. The language "well above potential" plus supply constraints binding suggests 59-65 range. Score ~60.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor market was very tight" — used multiple times. "Unemployment rate near a 50-year low, job vacancies at historical highs, and elevated nominal wage growth." "Demand for labor continued to outstrip available supply across many parts of the economy." "Most business contacts had continued to report persistent wage pressures as well as difficulties in hiring and retaining workers." This matches the 66-80 range ("very tight; severe shortages; unsustainable wage growth"). Score ~73.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed signals. Consumer spending "remained robust" and "remaining strong." But "several participants indicated that some of their contacts reported that the pace of consumer spending, though strong, was beginning to moderate." Business investment "appeared to be slowing." Housing "appeared to be softening." A few contacts "postponing investment or construction projects." Net position: still strong but moderating. Above trend but not booming. Score ~58.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Financial conditions tightened, on net." "Equity prices moved substantially lower." "Corporate bond spreads widened notably." Mortgage rates at highest since 2010. But "credit remained widely available, particularly to higher-rated firms." "Financing conditions in domestic credit markets remained generally accommodative." So conditions have tightened considerably but not yet restrictive/stressful. Score ~62.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Almost all participants agreed that it was appropriate to raise the target range for the federal funds rate 75 basis points." They "concurred that the economic outlook warranted moving to a restrictive stance of policy" and "recognized the possibility that an even more restrictive stance could be appropriate." Forward guidance: 50 or 75bp at next meeting. "All participants reaffirmed their strong commitment to returning inflation to the Committee's 2 percent objective." Only one preferred 50bp. This is strongly hawkish — 75bp hike with consensus for more. Anchor: 80-85 for late 2022 75bp hikes. Score ~83.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Very high consensus. "Almost all participants agreed" on 75bp. Only "one participant favored a 50 basis point increase." No deep philosophical splits. Some nuance on inflation expectations (a few noted market-based measures fell vs. many worried about drift up). Minor differences on forward pace (50 or 75 at next meeting). This is broad agreement with one mild dissent. Score ~22.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial conditions are discussed meaningfully — tightening conditions, equity declines, corporate spreads widening, mortgage rate increases. "Many participants noted that the Committee's credibility... had been helpful in shifting market expectations... and had already contributed to a notable tightening of financial conditions." But financial stability per se (systemic risk, fragility) is not a dominant theme. Moderate attention. Score ~40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Strong tightening signal. "Participants continued to anticipate that ongoing increases in the target range... would be appropriate." "50 or 75 basis points would likely be appropriate at the next meeting." "Economic outlook warranted moving to a restrictive stance of policy." "Even more restrictive stance could be appropriate if elevated inflation pressures were to persist." This is clearly hawkish forward guidance. Score ~87.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants judged that uncertainty about economic growth over the next couple of years was elevated." "GDP and gross domestic income had been giving conflicting signals." Risks to growth "skewed to the downside," risks to inflation "skewed to the upside." Timing and magnitude of supply resolution "uncertain." But the policy direction is clear and the uncertainty is acknowledged rather than paralyzing. Score ~55.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (60) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (73): Both above 50, consistent. Labor tightness higher because the labor market is described in more extreme terms ("very tight," "50-year low unemployment") than overall output gap language.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (83) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (87): Aligned — both strongly hawkish. Forward guidance slightly higher because the signal explicitly points to continued aggressive tightening.

### Polarity verification:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 85: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says "much too high," "well above." Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP 60: Higher = more above potential. "Well above potential." Matches.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS 62: Higher = tighter. Conditions tightened, spreads widened, equity prices fell. Matches.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 83: Higher = more hawkish. 75bp hike, restrictive stance. Matches.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 85 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants noted that inflation remained much too high and observed that it continued to run well above the Committee's longer-run 2 percent objective, with total PCE prices having risen 6.3 percent over the 12 months ending in April."
- "Participants were concerned that the May CPI release indicated that inflation pressures had yet to show signs of abating, and a number of them saw it as solidifying the view that inflation would be more persistent than they had previously anticipated."
- "Many participants raised the concern that longer-run inflation expectations could be beginning to drift up to levels inconsistent with the 2 percent objective."

**Center of gravity**: Near-unanimous view that inflation is far too high, broadening, and more persistent than expected — squarely in the peak-inflation anchor range.

**Minority views**: None meaningfully dovish on inflation. A few noted market-based medium-term inflation compensation fell, providing slight tempering.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 60 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Overall economic activity appeared to have picked up after edging down in the first quarter."
- Staff projected "the level of real GDP was still expected to remain well above potential over the projection period."
- "The imbalance between supply and demand across a wide range of product markets was contributing to upward pressure on inflation."

**Center of gravity**: Economy operating above potential with demand outstripping supply, though growth is moderating and participants revised down GDP growth projections for the year.

**Minority views**: "A couple of them noted that GDP and gross domestic income had been giving conflicting signals recently regarding the pace of economic growth."

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 73 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants noted that the demand for labor continued to outstrip available supply across many parts of the economy... an unemployment rate near a 50-year low, job vacancies at historical highs, and elevated nominal wage growth."
- "Most business contacts had continued to report persistent wage pressures as well as difficulties in hiring and retaining workers."
- "Several participants observed that labor force participation remained below its pre-pandemic level... and judged that the labor force participation rate was unlikely to move up considerably in the near term."

**Center of gravity**: Very tight labor market with broad-based shortages, elevated wage growth, and limited near-term relief from participation improvements.

**Minority views**: "Some contacts reported that, because of previous wage hikes, hiring and retention had improved and pressure for additional wage increases appeared to be receding."

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 58 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Consumption spending had remained robust, in part reflecting strong balance sheets in the household sector and a tight labor market."
- "Several participants indicated that some of their contacts reported that the pace of consumer spending, though strong, was beginning to moderate."
- "Growth in business fixed investment appeared to be slowing, and activity in the housing sector appeared to be softening."

**Center of gravity**: Spending still above trend but with clear early signs of moderation. Consumer spending robust but softening; business investment slowing; housing weakening.

**Minority views**: "A few participants noted that... contacts reported that they were postponing investment or construction projects because of rising input and financing costs."

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 62 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Financial conditions tightened, on net, as market participants assessed incoming information about the economy."
- "Spreads on investment-grade and, to a greater extent, speculative-grade corporate bonds widened notably, on net, reaching levels comparable with those at the end of 2018."
- "Borrowing costs for residential mortgage loans increased significantly over the intermeeting period... reaching their highest levels since 2010."
- However: "Financing conditions for most businesses and households remained generally accommodative... Credit remained widely available, particularly to higher-rated firms."

**Center of gravity**: Conditions have tightened meaningfully — higher rates, wider spreads, equity declines — but credit remains available and functioning. Tight but not restrictive/stressed.

**Minority views**: None significantly divergent.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 83 ±4 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Almost all participants agreed that it was appropriate to raise the target range for the federal funds rate 75 basis points at this meeting."
- "Participants concurred that the economic outlook warranted moving to a restrictive stance of policy, and they recognized the possibility that an even more restrictive stance could be appropriate."
- "All participants reaffirmed their strong commitment to returning inflation to the Committee's 2 percent objective."

**Center of gravity**: Strong hawkish consensus for aggressive tightening — 75bp hike with near-unanimity and clear intent to continue.

**Minority views**: "One participant favored a 50 basis point increase in the target range at this meeting instead of 75 basis points."

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 22 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Almost all participants agreed" on 75bp; only "one participant favored a 50 basis point increase."
- Forward guidance showed modest range: "an increase of 50 or 75 basis points would likely be appropriate at the next meeting."
- Minor divergence on inflation expectations interpretation (market-based measures vs. survey-based).

**Center of gravity**: Broad agreement on diagnosis, prescription, and forward path. One mild dissent on magnitude (50 vs 75bp). Very little fundamental disagreement.

**Minority views**: The lone 50bp preference and some variation in reading inflation expectations measures.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 40 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Many participants noted that the Committee's credibility... had already contributed to a notable tightening of financial conditions that would likely help reduce inflation pressures."
- Extensive staff discussion of equity declines, spread widening, and credit conditions. Participants referenced tighter financial conditions as a transmission mechanism.
- No significant discussion of systemic risk, financial fragility, or stability threats per se.

**Center of gravity**: Moderate discussion of financial conditions primarily as a policy transmission channel rather than as a stability concern in its own right.

**Minority views**: None notable.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 87 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants continued to anticipate that ongoing increases in the target range for the federal funds rate would be appropriate."
- "An increase of 50 or 75 basis points would likely be appropriate at the next meeting."
- "The economic outlook warranted moving to a restrictive stance of policy, and they recognized the possibility that an even more restrictive stance could be appropriate if elevated inflation pressures were to persist."

**Center of gravity**: Strong hawkish forward signal — continued aggressive rate increases ahead, with the possibility of going even further if needed.

**Minority views**: Data dependence language provides slight softening: "pace of rate increases and the extent of future policy tightening would depend on the incoming data."

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants judged that uncertainty about economic growth over the next couple of years was elevated."
- "The timing and magnitude of these effects were uncertain."
- "Most participants assessed that the risks to the outlook for economic growth were skewed to the downside."
- "A couple of them noted that GDP and gross domestic income had been giving conflicting signals recently."

**Center of gravity**: Elevated uncertainty about growth and supply dynamics, but not paralyzing — the policy direction is clear. Two-sided risks acknowledged.

**Minority views**: None — uncertainty was broadly shared.

### Optional Dimensions

**INFLATION_GOODS: 75**
- "Several participants judged that a shift in spending from goods to services was likely to be associated with less upward pressure on prices in the goods sector." Goods inflation still elevated but expected to moderate. Score reflects still-high goods prices with early rotation away.

**INFLATION_SERVICES: 72**
- "An intensification of upward pressure on prices in the services sector" as spending shifts from goods to services. Services inflation building but described less extremely than headline.

**INFLATION_HOUSING: 55**
- "Housing activity appeared to be softening, in part as a result of a sharp rise in mortgage rates." Housing discussed primarily as a demand/activity issue rather than a shelter inflation issue. Limited direct housing inflation discussion. Borderline N/A but the housing cost references in hardship context ("housing" as essential cost) push it slightly above neutral.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 62 ±8**
- "Measures of longer-term inflation expectations... were generally judged to be broadly consistent with the Committee's longer-run 2 percent inflation objective." But "many participants raised the concern that longer-run inflation expectations could be beginning to drift up to levels inconsistent with the 2 percent objective." Mixed: anchored but showing signs of drift. Above 50 given the concern about unanchoring.

**CREDIT_STRESS: 55**
- "Spreads on investment-grade and, to a greater extent, speculative-grade corporate bonds widened notably." "Market indicators of future default expectations of businesses deteriorated to some extent." But "credit quality of business loans on banks' books remained sound" and "credit remained widely available." Slightly above normal.

**EQUITY_STRESS: 60**
- "Broad domestic equity price indexes declined considerably." "Stock price declines were largely associated with mixed corporate earnings news... and increasing concerns about the economic outlook." VIX "elevated relative to its historical distribution." Moderate stress but not panic.

**BANKING_STRESS: N/A**
- No meaningful participant discussion of banking system health or stress.

**TREASURY_STRESS: N/A**
- Market participants reported "overall market functioning had held up" despite liquidity challenges. Staff observation, not participant focus. N/A.

**DOLLAR_CONCERN: N/A**
- "The broad dollar edged up" mentioned in staff review but no participant discussion of dollar concerns.

**PRODUCTIVITY: N/A**
