# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2001-11-06

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Unsegmented (pre-2009), so I must focus on participant-attributable language ("members commented," "members noted," "members anticipated") and ignore staff-reported content. This document is entirely participant discussion, so all content is scorable.

**Era context**: November 2001 -- the economy is in recession (NBER dated March-November 2001). The September 11 terrorist attacks occurred less than two months prior, adding enormous uncertainty. The Fed had already been cutting rates aggressively throughout 2001. This meeting resulted in a 50bp cut to 2.00%.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all the members" (support 50bp, retain risk statement), "most members" (favored 50bp), "a number of members" (close call between 25/50), "three" (preferred 25bp but accepted 50bp), "several" (risk statement not a commitment), "some" (decline in confidence less than expected; concerned about market expectations), "many" (depressed holiday sales).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW target and declining. "Core consumer price inflation was projected to remain subdued and quite possibly edge lower." "Members expected underlying consumer price inflation to remain benign and possibly to drift lower." "Core inflation, which was already modest, would decelerate further." There is even concern about potential "downward pressures on prices that could be difficult to counter." This is clearly in the 20-30 range -- meaningful disinflation with some deflation concern emerging. Score: 25.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: The economy is clearly contracting and well below potential. "The economy was contracting," "business fixed investment currently seemed to be declining at an even faster rate," "considerable slack in labor markets." This is recession territory with significant slack. The rubric anchors 16-30 as "deep recession; far below potential; substantial slack." The contraction is real but members still see it as "fairly brief and limited" with recovery in 2002, so this isn't the deepest recession. Score: 25.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Considerable slack in labor markets, evidenced by both statistical and widespread anecdotal reports, was expected to exert appreciable downward pressure on wage increases." "Further job losses" anticipated. This is clearly loose labor market territory. Not depression-level but significant slack. Score: 28.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Investment is collapsing: "Business fixed investment currently seemed to be declining at an even faster rate than earlier." "Sharp decrease in new orders." Consumer spending held up somewhat due to motor vehicle sales but that was temporary. "Consumer spending was likely to be held back." Net position is clearly below trend and contracting. Investment is in freefall; consumption is holding but weakening. Score: 22.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "A variety of factors had limited the passthrough of lower short-term interest rates into long-term rates, equity prices, bank lending rates, and the foreign exchange value of the dollar." "Heightened degree of uncertainty and risk aversion." Financial conditions are somewhat tight despite policy easing -- the passthrough is impaired. But there's no discussion of credit freezing or systemic stress per se. Score: 60.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All members supported 50bp cut. "Most" favored 50bp, three preferred 25bp but accepted 50bp. The bias statement retained "risks tilted toward further weakness." This is clearly dovish -- active easing. The 50bp cut with a dovish tilt forward is in the 15-25 range. Score: 20.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: All supported 50bp ultimately, but "a number of members noted that the choice between 25 and 50 basis points was a close call." Three preferred smaller move but accepted larger. This is moderate internal tension but no formal dissents. Score: 30.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial conditions are mentioned but not as a dominant topic. The passthrough discussion is brief. Most discussion is about real economy. Some attention to stock market wealth effects, risk aversion, and consumer confidence post-9/11. Score: 30.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "All the members indicated that with the risks to the economy clearly tilted toward further weakness, they could vote in favor of retaining a statement to that effect." But "several stressed that such a statement did not constitute a commitment by the Committee to ease policy further." "A decision in that regard would depend entirely on the nature of future economic and financial developments." This is an easing bias but with data dependence. Score: 22.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Marked degree of uncertainty," "key uncertainty in the outlook," "heightened degree of uncertainty," "substantial uncertainty about the course of the economy." Uncertainty about fiscal legislation, terrorism, foreign economies. This is elevated to high uncertainty. Score: 65.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (25) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (28): Both well below 50, consistent -- recession with slack.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (20) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (22): Both dovish, consistent -- actively easing with easing bias forward.
- CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT (22) is very weak, consistent with OUTPUT_GAP (25).

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says declining, benign, possibly lower. 25 < 50. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Economy contracting, substantial slack. 25 < 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Passthrough impaired, risk aversion elevated. 60 > 50. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. 50bp cut with easing bias. 20 < 50. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 25 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Members expected underlying consumer price inflation to remain benign and possibly to drift lower over coming quarters, abetted by the indirect effects of generally weaker energy prices."
- "Core consumer price inflation was projected to remain subdued and quite possibly edge lower."
- "Insufficient monetary policy stimulus would risk... possibly even downward pressures on prices that could be difficult to counter."
**Center-of-gravity**: Members uniformly see inflation as subdued and declining, with some emerging concern about potential deflationary pressures -- placing this in the meaningful disinflation zone.
**Minority views**: None dissenting on inflation; broad agreement.

### OUTPUT_GAP: 25 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The economy was contracting, and they saw no significant evidence that overall business conditions were in the process of stabilizing prior to recovering."
- "Considerable slack in labor markets, evidenced by both statistical and widespread anecdotal reports."
- Members still expected "a fairly brief and limited decrease in economic activity" with recovery in 2002.
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy clearly in recession with substantial slack, but members view it as temporary, not catastrophic -- consistent with the upper end of the "deep recession" range.
**Minority views**: None; broad agreement on contraction.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 28 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The considerable slack in labor markets, evidenced by both statistical and widespread anecdotal reports, was expected to exert appreciable downward pressure on wage increases over the forecast period."
- "Further job losses could undermine consumer confidence and spending."
**Center-of-gravity**: Significant labor market slack with ongoing job losses and downward wage pressure. Clearly below full employment but not depression-level.
**Minority views**: None apparent.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 22 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Business fixed investment currently seemed to be declining at an even faster rate than earlier in the year, and the sharp decrease in new orders of capital goods in September pointed to marked additional weakness over the months ahead."
- "Consumer spending had held up reasonably well thus far" but "consumer spending was likely to be held back by the persistence of widespread caution among households."
- "Business firms were continuing to cut back production in efforts to adjust output to faltering demand and to pare excess inventories."
**Center-of-gravity**: Investment is in sharp contraction; consumer spending is holding but deteriorating. The net position is clearly contracting, weighted heavily by the investment collapse.
**Minority views**: None; broad agreement.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 60 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "A variety of factors had limited the passthrough of lower short-term interest rates into long-term rates, equity prices, bank lending rates, and the foreign exchange value of the dollar."
- "The heightened degree of uncertainty and risk aversion following the terrorist attacks seemed to be having a pronounced effect on business and household spending."
**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are somewhat tight -- policy easing is not fully transmitting to broader financial conditions due to risk aversion and impaired passthrough. Not crisis-level but clearly restrictive relative to policy intent.
**Minority views**: Limited direct discussion.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 20 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All the members indicated that they could support a proposal calling for further easing in reserve conditions consistent with a 50 basis point reduction in the federal funds rate to a level of 2 percent."
- "Most members, however, favored a 50 basis point reduction."
- Three members "favored a smaller move on balance, although they could accept the larger decrease."
**Center-of-gravity**: Strongly dovish -- unanimously supporting easing with most favoring the larger 50bp cut. Active easing cycle with further easing bias.
**Minority views**: Three preferred 25bp but accepted 50bp, slightly less dovish but still firmly in the easing camp.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "A number of members noted that the choice between 25 and 50 basis points of easing was a close call."
- "Three favored a smaller move on balance, although they could accept the larger decrease."
- All ultimately supported the 50bp cut; no formal dissents.
**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on direction (ease) with moderate tactical disagreement on magnitude (25 vs 50bp). No formal dissents. This is typical one-shade-of-disagreement territory.
**Minority views**: Three preferring 25bp creates moderate but contained tension.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 30 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Discussion of impaired passthrough of monetary policy to financial conditions.
- "Decline in stock market wealth over the last year or so" mentioned as headwind to consumption.
- "Heightened degree of uncertainty and risk aversion" noted.
**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability/conditions receive limited but meaningful discussion, primarily as context for how policy transmits to the real economy. Not a focal topic of the meeting.
**Minority views**: None.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 22 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All the members indicated that with the risks to the economy clearly tilted toward further weakness, they could vote in favor of retaining a statement to that effect."
- "Several stressed that such a statement did not constitute a commitment by the Committee to ease policy further at the next meeting."
- "Significant further weakness in the economy might indeed warrant additional easing."
**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal is dovish -- retaining a risk-tilted-to-downside statement -- but with explicit caveats about data dependence and no commitment. This is mild easing bias with data dependence.
**Minority views**: Several want to be clear the statement is not a commitment.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 65 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The strength and timing of the eventual recovery remained subject to question especially in light of the marked degree of uncertainty that surrounded the prospects for further fiscal policy legislation, developments in the war against terrorism, and weakness in foreign economies."
- "Substantial uncertainty about the course of the economy and the appropriate stance of policy."
- "A key uncertainty in the outlook for investment spending was the outcome of the ongoing Congressional debate."
**Center-of-gravity**: High uncertainty driven by multiple sources: terrorism, fiscal policy prospects, foreign weakness, timing of recovery. Multiple participants emphasize genuine uncertainty across several domains.
**Minority views**: None; uncertainty is widely shared.
