### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as transitioning from below-target toward balance. "Many members indicated that the surprisingly large advances had substantially reduced the odds of further disinflation and also had increased their uncertainty about prospective price trends. The center of gravity is: inflation was very low, recent data have pushed it up, but most still see low inflation ahead. However, businesses are increasingly able to pass through costs. This is moving from below-target toward target -- roughly 43-48 range. The upside risks to inflation are noted as having "moved into balance." I'll score 46 -- slightly below target but with upward momentum acknowledged.

The phrase "margins of unutilized resources" = below potential. "Resource use slack" is explicitly stated. Growth is strong and accelerating, but slack remains. This is clearly below potential but closing. Score: 42 (below potential, recovering, gap narrowing).

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "After a protracted period of meager gains in employment, conditions in the U.S. labor market evidently were improving." "Considerable slack seemed to remain in the labor market overall, and wage gains on the whole were moderate." Hiring is picking up but from a very weak base, with slack still considerable. Some pockets of tightness in specialized skills. Score: 35 (below full employment, slack remains, but improving).

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumer spending was rising at a brisk rate, housing activity remained at a high level, and business fixed investment was growing vigorously." Nonresidential structures sluggish. Net position is clearly above trend. Score: 62 (above trend, robust spending and investment).

"The appreciable rise in real long-term interest rates over the intermeeting period implied that financial market conditions had already tightened on balance." Conditions started loose/accommodative but have tightened somewhat with the rise in long rates. The fed funds rate is 1% -- very low. Net: still accommodative but tightening at the margin. Score: 30 (loose, accommodative, but some tightening noted).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All members favor holding at 1%. But "the time at which policy firming appropriately would commence might be closer than previously had seemed most probable." Score: 55 (lean hawkish: hold steady with tightening bias, approaching time to begin).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All of the members favored maintenance of the existing target." "All of the members agreed" on dropping patience language and accepting "measured." This is broad agreement with moderate internal discussion about communication. Score: 22 (broad agreement, minor emphasis differences).

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Limited direct discussion of financial stability. The backup in mortgage rates is mentioned, long-term rates rising, accommodative conditions. But no prominent discussion of financial stability risks, asset bubbles, credit conditions as a systemic concern. A few passing references. Score: 18 (brief mentions, not a focus).

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The committee explicitly drops "patient" and replaces with "measured" pace of accommodation removal. This is a clear signal that tightening is coming, but will be gradual. "Policy tightening, once it began, probably could proceed at a pace that would be 'measured.'" This is a mild tightening bias -- normalization is signaled but gradual. Score: 62 (mild tightening bias, "begin normalization" language).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Many members indicated that the surprisingly large advances... had increased their uncertainty about prospective price trends." Members express uncertainty about inflation outlook specifically. Some uncertainty about whether growth could falter if rates rise sharply. But overall outlook is described with reasonable confidence -- expansion "firmly established." Score: 40 (somewhat elevated, inflation uncertainty specifically noted).
