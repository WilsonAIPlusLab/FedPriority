# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2015-09-17

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Post-2009 segmented format -- participant views clearly distinguished from staff content.

**Era context**: September 2015, the meeting immediately preceding the eventual December 2015 liftoff. The economy had been recovering for six years; unemployment was approaching estimates of its longer-run normal rate. Inflation remained below 2% due to energy price declines and dollar appreciation. The China growth scare and global financial volatility were fresh concerns. The Committee held rates at 0-0.25% at this meeting with one dissent favoring a hike.

**Quantifier distribution**: "most" (used several times for majority views), "many" (used for significant-but-not-majority groups), "several" (minority concerns), "some" (notable faction), "a few" (small faction), "one member" (single dissenter).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target. "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's longer-run objective." "Almost all participants anticipated that inflation would continue to run below 2 percent in the near term." Further downward pressures from oil and dollar. Core PCE "well below" 2%. A few noted core had been running at 1.7% annualized since January. Overall direction: clearly below target but not alarming -- expected to return gradually to 2%. This maps to the 26-40 range ("below target; slack keeping prices in check; disinflation"). With new downside risks from global developments, I score toward the lower end of that range. Score: 32. Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says below target with further downward pressures. 32 is below midpoint. Correct.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic activity was expanding moderately." Growth was "stronger than participants expected." Pace of expansion "somewhat above its longer-run rate." Unemployment rate had fallen to near longer-run normal rate. But net exports remained soft, and global developments added some downside risk. "Many" judged effects on domestic activity "likely to be small." The language says growth is "somewhat above" its longer-run rate, meaning the gap is closing and near potential. Unemployment near its natural rate suggests near-potential operation. Score: 48. The economy is essentially at potential or very slightly below, with pace of growth slightly above trend suggesting the gap is closing. Bright-line check: "somewhat above its longer-run rate" refers to growth rate, not level relative to potential. The text notes underutilization had "diminished" but some slack remained. This suggests slightly below potential. Score: 47. Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says near potential with residual slack. 47 is slightly below midpoint. Correct.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor market conditions had improved considerably." Unemployment "close to most participants' estimates of its longer-run normal rate." "Most participants thought that the underutilization of labor resources had been substantially reduced, and a few of them expressed the view that underutilization had been eliminated." But "some others believed that labor market slack in addition to that measured by the unemployment rate remained." No broad-based wage acceleration. Direction: near full employment but some residual slack. This maps to 44-48 range. The center of gravity is close to but not quite at full employment. Score: 46. Polarity check: Higher = tighter labor market. Near NAIRU but some remaining slack. 46 is near midpoint, slightly below. Correct.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumer spending was rising at a solid rate." "Household spending and business fixed investment were increasing moderately." Housing improving with sales and construction trending higher. Business spending was "mixed" -- auto strong but dollar hurting exports, energy/agriculture weak with "worsening" energy outlook. Net effect: consumer side solid, business side mixed. Score: 50. "Moderate" growth maps to the 46-54 range. Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending. "Solid" consumer + "mixed" business = moderate overall. 50 is midpoint. Correct.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Equity prices fell, on balance, amid significant volatility, and risk spreads for businesses widened." Global developments from China contributed to tighter conditions. However, "many" judged effects "likely to be small" and "a couple noted that volatility had begun to subside." Participants did not see asset price changes "as bearing significantly on their policy choice." Some tightening from global events but not severe. Score: 55. Conditions tightened somewhat from the China scare and equity volatility, but not dramatically. Polarity check: Higher = tighter conditions. Spreads widened, equity decline, volatility. 55 is somewhat tight. Correct.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Most participants continued to anticipate that... the conditions for policy firming had been met or would likely be met by the end of the year." But the Committee decided to hold. "All but one member" voted to hold. One dissent in favor of hiking. Many saw conditions being met "later this year." "Some participants were concerned that the downside risks to inflation could be realized if the target range was increased before it was clear that economic growth would remain at an above-trend pace." "Some other participants expressed concerns about delaying the start of normalizing." Center of gravity: lean hawkish -- most expect liftoff soon (by year-end) but decided to wait one more meeting. This is the pre-liftoff deliberation phase. Score: 52. Just above neutral -- they held but signaled liftoff was imminent. The anchor for 2014-15 pre-liftoff is 40-50, and for Dec 2015 liftoff is 60-65. September sits between: clearly leaning toward firming but not ready yet. Score: 53. Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence: most expect liftoff by year-end, one dissent for hiking now, but chose to wait. 53 is mildly hawkish. Correct.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal dissent (member wanting to hike). Clear camps visible: "some" wanted to wait for more data, "some other" participants wanted to begin firming soon. Notable debate about timing. But "all but one" voted to hold, suggesting broad (though not unanimous) agreement on the action. The debate was about timing (soon vs. now), not fundamental direction. Score: 38. Moderate differences with one dissent -- maps to 31-45 range. Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. One dissent, two visible camps but with broad agreement on hold. 38 fits.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Participants discussed China spillovers, equity price declines, volatility, and risk spreads at moderate length. Several paragraphs devoted to global financial developments and their potential impact. But participants explicitly stated asset prices did not "bear significantly on their policy choice except insofar as they affected the outlook." "Some other participants expressed concerns about... an undesired buildup of... economic and financial imbalances." Moderate attention. Score: 40. Maps to the 40-60 range for moderate discussion. Not the primary focus but meaningfully addressed. Polarity check: Higher = more attention. Multiple paragraphs discussed. 40 is at the lower end of "moderate discussion." Correct.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Most participants continued to anticipate that... the conditions for policy firming had been met or would likely be met by the end of the year." "Many expected those conditions to be met later this year." Strong signal that liftoff is coming soon but not at this meeting. This is a mild tightening bias forward signal. Score: 60. Maps to the 60-75 range for "begin normalization" language. The lower end because they didn't actually hike yet. Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Most expect firming by year-end. 60 is mild tightening bias. Correct.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Many acknowledged that recent global economic and financial developments may have increased the downside risks to economic activity somewhat." "Several members were concerned about downside risks to the outlook for real activity and inflation." The Committee "decided that it was prudent to wait for additional information confirming that the economic outlook had not deteriorated." Elevated uncertainty from China/global developments, but not extreme. Score: 48. Maps to 46-60 range for "elevated" uncertainty with multiple participants emphasizing unusual factors. Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. Global risks, China, volatile markets created genuine uncertainty about timing. 48 fits.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (47) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (46): Both slightly below midpoint, coherent -- economy near but not quite at full employment.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (53) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (60): Forward guidance is more hawkish than current lean -- this makes sense because they held steady now but signaled liftoff by year-end. Divergence is explained by the "wait one more meeting" posture.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 32 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's longer-run objective, partly reflecting declines in energy prices and in prices of non-energy imports."
- "Almost all participants anticipated that inflation would continue to run below 2 percent in the near term."
- "The 12-month changes in both the total and core PCE price indexes for August were expected to still be well below the Committee's 2 percent objective."

**Center-of-gravity**: Near-universal agreement that inflation was well below target with further downside pressures from oil and the dollar, though expected to gradually return to 2%.

**Minority views**: "A few participants pointed out that since January... core PCE prices had risen at an annual rate of 1.7 percent, closer to the Committee's objective" -- slightly less pessimistic reading, informing the upper bound of the uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 47 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity was expanding moderately" with the "pace of expansion of real activity... somewhat above its longer-run rate."
- "Growth in real GDP over the first half of the year was stronger than participants expected."
- "Many acknowledged that recent global economic and financial developments may have increased the downside risks to economic activity somewhat."

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is near potential with growth somewhat above trend, suggesting the small remaining gap is closing. Modest residual slack remains.

**Minority views**: Narrow band -- broad agreement on the characterization.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 46 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The unemployment rate had fallen to a level close to most participants' estimates of its longer-run normal rate."
- "Most participants thought that the underutilization of labor resources had been substantially reduced, and a few of them expressed the view that underutilization had been eliminated."
- "Some others believed that labor market slack in addition to that measured by the unemployment rate remained."
- "The incoming information on wages and labor compensation... showed no broad-based acceleration."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is near full employment but with residual slack visible in participation rates and part-time employment. Absence of wage acceleration suggests not yet tight.

**Minority views**: "A few" said underutilization eliminated (would score higher); "some others" saw meaningful remaining slack (would score lower). This disperses the uncertainty band.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 50 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending was rising at a solid rate after a modest increase in the first quarter."
- "Household spending and business fixed investment were increasing moderately."
- "The information on business spending from District contacts was mixed." Energy sector outlook "appeared to be worsening."

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending solid and housing improving, but business investment was mixed with energy-sector weakness offsetting strength elsewhere. Net position is at trend.

**Minority views**: Little disagreement on the characterization.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 55 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Equity prices fell, on balance, amid significant volatility, and risk spreads for businesses widened."
- "Concerns associated with developments in China and other emerging market economies had contributed to a further appreciation of the dollar."
- "Many of them saw the likely effects of recent developments on the path of economic activity and inflation as small or transitory."
- "A couple noted that volatility had begun to subside."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions tightened somewhat due to global volatility and the China scare, but participants largely viewed the effects as limited and transitory.

**Minority views**: "Some participants commented that the recent decline in equity prices needed to be viewed in the context of overall valuation levels, which they saw as relatively high" -- suggesting conditions were less worrisome than headline moves implied.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 53 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Most participants continued to anticipate that... the conditions for policy firming had been met or would likely be met by the end of the year."
- "All but one member concluded that... economic conditions did not warrant an increase in the target range for the federal funds rate at this meeting."
- "One member, however, preferred to raise the target range for the federal funds rate at this meeting."
- "Some participants were concerned that the downside risks to inflation could be realized if the target range was increased before it was clear that economic growth would remain at an above-trend pace."

**Center-of-gravity**: The Committee held rates but with a clear lean toward imminent liftoff. The decision to wait was driven by caution about global risks, not by a view that the economy wasn't ready.

**Minority views**: One hawkish dissent; "some" wanted to wait longer for more confidence on inflation. Fairly wide spread of views on timing.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 38 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All but one member concluded that... economic conditions did not warrant an increase" -- one formal dissent.
- Two visible camps on timing: "Some participants were concerned that the downside risks to inflation could be realized if the target range was increased" vs. "Some other participants expressed concerns about delaying the start of normalizing."
- "Several members were concerned about downside risks to the outlook."

**Center-of-gravity**: One formal dissent and clear camps about timing of liftoff, but broad agreement on the hold decision and the overall economic assessment.

**Minority views**: N/A (this dimension captures the spread itself).

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 40 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Extended discussion of China spillovers, equity volatility, and risk spreads (multiple paragraphs).
- "Participants indicated that they did not see the changes in asset prices during the intermeeting period as bearing significantly on their policy choice except insofar as they affected the outlook."
- "A significant delay risked an undesired buildup of... economic and financial imbalances."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions received moderate attention as context for the policy decision, driven by China/global volatility, but participants explicitly downplayed financial stability as a direct policy input.

**Minority views**: Minimal -- most agreed the financial developments were secondary to the macro outlook.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 60 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Most participants continued to anticipate that... the conditions for policy firming had been met or would likely be met by the end of the year."
- "Many expected those conditions to be met later this year."
- "Most of them expecting that beginning the process before long would allow the target range for the federal funds rate to be increased gradually."

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear forward signal that normalization was imminent, with most expecting liftoff by year-end. Gradual pace emphasized.

**Minority views**: "Several members were concerned about downside risks to the outlook for real activity and inflation" -- these would pull the forward signal lower, but they were a minority.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 48 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Many acknowledged that recent global economic and financial developments may have increased the downside risks to economic activity somewhat."
- "The Committee decided that it was prudent to wait for additional information confirming that the economic outlook had not deteriorated."
- "Several members were concerned about downside risks to the outlook for real activity and inflation."

**Center-of-gravity**: Elevated uncertainty driven by China slowdown, global financial volatility, and unclear inflation path. The decision to hold despite being near liftoff conditions itself signals meaningful uncertainty.

**Minority views**: Some participants saw the effects of global developments as "small or transitory" -- less uncertain than others.
