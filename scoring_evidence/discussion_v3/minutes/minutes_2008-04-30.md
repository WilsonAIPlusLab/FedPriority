# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2008-04-30 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Pre-2009 unsegmented format, so I must carefully distinguish participant-attributed language from staff-attributed language. The text uses quantifier language throughout ("most," "many," "several," "some," "a few," "a number of").

**Era context**: April 2008 -- early-to-mid phase of the Global Financial Crisis. The Bear Stearns rescue occurred in March 2008. The economy was weakening, housing was in freefall, and financial markets were under stress but had seen some improvement from the March acute episode. The Fed had been cutting rates aggressively. Oil and commodity prices were rising sharply.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all participants" (GDP at/above trend by 2010; total PCE inflation 1.5-2% by 2010), "most members" (25bp cut), "many participants" (GDP likely to contract slightly in H1), "several participants" (firms deferring spending; concern about global slowdown; pass-through of input costs; wage inflation lagging), "some participants" (fed funds rate low by historical standards; tax rebates used to pay down debt), "a few" (commercial real estate firm in some areas; tentative housing pickup signs), "a number of participants" (financial headwinds through much of next year; inflation expectations drift concern).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as above target currently because of elevated energy and food prices, with mixed signals on core. "Total PCE inflation was projected to moderate from its current elevated level." "Crude oil prices to record levels, together with rapid increases in food and import prices." But core had "improved somewhat" (partly transitory). Several reported business contacts with "growing concerns about increase in input costs" and firms "seeking to pass on these higher costs." However, others noted "pass-through appeared relatively limited" and wage gains "remained moderate." The center of gravity is: currently elevated headline inflation, with concern about persistence, but core is somewhat better and expected moderation. This is clearly above target but not extreme -- score in the 61-70 range. Direction: above midpoint. Score: 64.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says elevated inflation with upside risks. Score above 50 matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants clearly see the economy as below potential. "Many participants judging that real GDP was likely to contract slightly in the first half of 2008." "Slack in resource utilization was likely to increase." "Outlook for growth and employment remained weak." GDP expected to recover in H2 but only return to trend by 2010. This is clearly below potential -- significant negative output gap forming. Score in the 30-38 range. Direction: below midpoint. Score: 34.

Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says below potential with slack increasing. Score below 50 matches.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Nonfarm payroll employment had fallen for the third consecutive month" and "unemployment rate had moved up." "Participants projecting the unemployment rate to pick up further this year and to remain elevated in 2009." "Weakening labor markets" mentioned multiple times. Labor market is loosening/softening but not yet at deep recession levels. Score in the 31-40 range. Direction: below midpoint. Score: 35.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter labor market. Evidence says softening, rising unemployment. Score below 50 matches. Coherent with OUTPUT_GAP at 34.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Growth in consumer spending appeared to have slowed to a crawl" and "consumer sentiment had fallen sharply." "Outlook for business spending remained decidedly downbeat." "Firms were scaling back their capital spending plans." "Spending on nonresidential construction projects continued to slow." The one bright spot: export strength, which "partially insulated" output. But domestic consumption and investment are clearly weak. Score in the 25-33 range. Direction: below midpoint. Score: 28.

Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending/investment. Evidence says crawl/downbeat. Score below 50 matches.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Very prominent discussion. Some improvement from March: "Conditions across a number of financial markets had improved." But "financial markets remained under considerable stress," "functioning of many markets remained impaired," "credit conditions were seen as remaining tight," banks "tightened lending standards," "availability of both consumer credit and home equity lines had tightened considerably further." Several participants: "easing in monetary policy since last fall had not as yet led to a loosening in overall financial conditions." This is clearly tight -- improvement from crisis peak but still very stressed. Score in the 72-78 range. Direction: well above midpoint. Score: 74.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter financial conditions. Evidence says tight/stressed despite some improvement. Score above 50 matches.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Most members judged that policy should be eased by 25 basis points at this meeting." The outlook for growth was weak and "slack in resource utilization was likely to increase." But there's a clear tension -- some noted the fed funds rate was "relatively low by historical standards" and inflation concerns were significant. The document ends mid-discussion of the policy decision, but the clear center of gravity is a 25bp cut, which is dovish. However, this is a step-down from the larger 75bp cuts earlier -- suggesting the easing cycle is nearing its end. The inflation concerns and language about "difficulty of gauging appropriate stance" suggest moderate dovishness, not strong. Score: 32.

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says majority favors 25bp cut but with significant inflation concern tempering further easing. Score below 50 matches but not far below.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Most members" favored 25bp cut, but significant disagreement about the stance of policy exists. "Some participants noted" the rate was low, while "other participants observed" that financial strains meant real rate measures were unreliable. "Views varied about the likely pace and vigor of the recovery." There's a real tension between inflation hawks and those focused on financial/growth risks. The document suggests notable minority disagreement. Score: 42.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Extremely prominent. Multiple long paragraphs devoted to financial conditions -- credit markets, banking, liquidity facilities, mortgage markets, credit availability. Financial conditions dominate the discussion and are discussed as the primary lens through which the economy and policy are viewed. Score: 75.

Polarity check: Higher = more attention. Evidence clearly shows extensive discussion. Score well above midpoint matches.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The document is truncated and doesn't fully capture forward guidance discussion. However, the text available shows: "most members judged that policy should be eased by 25 basis points" (current action is dovish), but there's substantial concern about inflation expectations drifting up and "appropriate monetary policy, combined with effective communication of the Committee's commitment to price stability." This suggests the forward lean is shifting toward pause/hold rather than further easing. The 25bp cut is a step-down from prior larger cuts, suggesting easing nearing an end. Score: 38 (easing lean but with data dependence and inflation caution).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants stressed the difficulty of gauging the appropriate stance of policy in current circumstances." "Pronounced strains in banking and financial markets imparted much greater uncertainty to such assessments." "Considerable uncertainty attending the likely path of commodity prices." "Several participants reported that uncertainty about the economic outlook was leading firms to defer spending projects." Views varied about recovery pace. This is clearly elevated uncertainty. Score: 65.

**Cross-dimension coherence check**:
- OUTPUT_GAP (34) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (35): Both below 50, coherent -- economy weakening, labor softening.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (32) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (38): Both lean dovish, coherent. Forward guidance slightly less dovish because of inflation concern suggesting the end of the easing cycle is near.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 64 +/-8 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "crude oil prices to record levels, together with rapid increases in food and import prices in recent months, was likely to put upward pressure on inflation over the next few quarters"; "total PCE inflation was projected to moderate from its current elevated level"; "Several participants reported that business contacts had expressed growing concerns about the increase in their input costs and that there were signs that an increasing number of firms were seeking to pass on these higher costs"
- Center of gravity: Participants view headline inflation as clearly elevated due to energy/food prices, with meaningful upside risk from cost pass-through and potential inflation expectations drift, though core has improved somewhat and moderation is expected.
- Minority views: "Other participants noted...the extent of the pass-through...appeared relatively limited" and wage gains "remained moderate" -- some see less pressure.

### OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 34 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "many participants judging that real GDP was likely to contract slightly in the first half of 2008"; "slack in resource utilization was likely to increase"; "GDP growth was expected to begin to recover in the second half of this year"
- Center of gravity: Economy clearly below potential with a negative output gap that is widening, though recovery anticipated in H2 2008. Not a deep recession yet but meaningful contraction.
- Minority views: "Views varied about the likely pace and vigor of the recovery through 2009" -- some more pessimistic, some more optimistic about the recovery path.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "nonfarm payroll employment had fallen for the third consecutive month in March and the unemployment rate had moved up"; "participants projecting the unemployment rate to pick up further this year and to remain elevated in 2009"; "weakening labor markets"
- Center of gravity: Labor market clearly softening with rising unemployment, but not yet at deep recession levels. Expected to continue weakening.
- Minority views: None noted in text -- broad agreement on labor market direction.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 28 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Growth in consumer spending appeared to have slowed to a crawl"; "consumer sentiment had fallen sharply"; "The outlook for business spending remained decidedly downbeat"; "firms were scaling back their capital spending plans"
- Center of gravity: Both consumer spending and business investment are weak. Consumer spending at a crawl, business sentiment low, capex plans being cut. Export strength is the only offset.
- Minority views: Fiscal stimulus expected to provide temporary support; "A few participants reported that the commercial real estate market in some areas remained relatively firm."

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 74 +/-8 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "financial markets remained under considerable stress"; "credit conditions were seen as remaining tight"; "banks had tightened lending standards and pricing terms on loans to both businesses and households"; "availability of both consumer credit and home equity lines had tightened considerably further"
- Center of gravity: Despite some improvement from the March acute stress period, financial conditions remain very tight with broad-based credit tightening and ongoing market dysfunction.
- Minority views: The improvement since March ("better tone," equity prices up, spreads narrowed somewhat) keeps this from scoring higher, but participants "emphasized" the remaining stress.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 32 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "most members judged that policy should be eased by 25 basis points at this meeting"; "the outlook for growth and employment remained weak and slack in resource utilization was likely to increase"; "Some participants noted that the level of the federal funds target...was relatively low by historical standards"
- Center of gravity: Majority favors continued easing (25bp cut), but this is a deceleration from prior larger cuts, and significant inflation concerns create tension. Moderate dovish lean.
- Minority views: Some see rates as already low relative to inflation, suggesting less room/appetite for further easing. Document is truncated so full dissent picture is incomplete.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 42 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Views varied about the likely pace and vigor of the recovery"; "Some participants noted" vs. "other participants observed" on policy stance assessment; tension between inflation hawks and financial stability/growth doves
- Center of gravity: Notable disagreement about the appropriate policy framework and economic assessment, though the policy action itself commands "most" support. The tension between inflation concerns and growth/financial worries creates meaningful internal disagreement.
- Minority views: The "some" who note rates are already low represent a meaningful minority pulling hawkish.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 75 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: Multiple extended paragraphs on financial market conditions, credit stress, bank lending, liquidity facilities, housing market as financial stability risk. "The various liquidity facilities introduced by the Federal Reserve"; "financial headwinds would probably continue to restrain economic activity through much of next year"
- Center of gravity: Financial stability/conditions is a dominant topic rivaling the economic assessment in emphasis. Nearly all discussion is framed through the lens of financial stress.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 38 +/-10 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: "most members judged that policy should be eased by 25 basis points" (continued easing direction); "participants stressed that the expected moderation in inflation was dependent on the continued stability of inflation expectations"; "appropriate monetary policy, combined with effective communication of the Committee's commitment to price stability"
- Center of gravity: Forward lean is toward potential further easing but with significant caution about inflation. The step-down to 25bp signals the easing cycle may be nearing its end. Data-dependent easing posture.
- Note: Document is truncated before forward guidance discussion may have been completed, reducing confidence.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 65 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Participants stressed the difficulty of gauging the appropriate stance of policy in current circumstances"; "pronounced strains in banking and financial markets imparted much greater uncertainty to such assessments"; "considerable uncertainty attending the likely path of commodity prices"
- Center of gravity: Participants express high uncertainty about the outlook, policy transmission, and commodity prices. Multiple explicit references to unusual difficulty in assessment.
