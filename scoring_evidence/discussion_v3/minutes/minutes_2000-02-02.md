# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2000-02-02

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). This is an unsegmented pre-2009 document where staff and participant content are mixed. I will focus on portions with participant language cues ("members commented," "members were concerned," "members noted," "several cited," etc.) and ignore staff forecast/review content.

**Era context**: February 2000 -- the height of the late-1990s/early-2000s technology boom. The economy was growing rapidly, labor markets were very tight, productivity was accelerating, and the Fed was in a tightening cycle (funds rate at 5.5%, about to hike to 5.75%). Inflation was subdued but risks were tilted to the upside.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all agreed" (inflation risk from tighter labor), "members were concerned," "members commented," "some members," "several cited," "one member" (monetary ranges dissent). Broad agreement on most themes.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as currently subdued but with rising risks. "core inflation remained subdued" BUT "risks as primarily tilted toward rising inflationary pressures." "Several cited recent statistical and anecdotal evidence of larger increases in labor compensation." "some nonlabor input prices already were rising faster." Energy passthrough concerns. "all agreed that a significant further tightening of labor resource utilization would appreciably raise the risk of deterioration in the underlying inflation picture." The center of gravity is: inflation currently contained but pressure building. This is modestly above neutral -- they see rising pressure but not yet manifested in core inflation. Score should be 53-58. Direction: above midpoint because risks tilted upward and some input prices rising, but core still subdued.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "the economy still seemed to be growing very vigorously" -- "demand for output had been growing even more strongly" than potential. "the economy's potential to produce goods and services had been accelerating over time, but the demand for output had been growing even more strongly." This is clearly above potential. The bright-line rule: "above potential" language = must be >50. "If this imbalance continued" -- demand outpacing supply. Score 60-65. This matches the late-90s boom anchor of 57-63.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "very tight labor markets" -- "already very tight labor resource utilization" -- "all agreed that a significant further tightening of labor resource utilization would appreciably raise the risk." Unemployment projected at 4-4.25%. "shortages of skilled construction workers." This is clearly tight. Anchor for 1999-2000 is 65-75. Score 68-72.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Retail sales appeared to have strengthened further" -- "Motor vehicle sales... remarkably high level" -- "Consumption was being supported by robust growth in jobs and incomes, very high levels of consumer confidence" -- business investment "robust further growth." "economy still seemed to be growing very vigorously." This is well above trend. Score 65-70.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "higher interest rates" mentioned as offsetting stock market gains. "Relatively high real interest rates" needed. Markets expected "substantial Federal Reserve tightening." Mortgage rates up. But equity markets resilient, "remarkable resilience to higher interest rates." Credit conditions aren't described as restrictive per se -- rates are higher but the economy is booming. Financial conditions are tightening from the rate side but stock market wealth is offsetting. Score around 48-53 -- roughly neutral, perhaps slightly tight given rate increases but offset by equity wealth.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The Committee was clearly in tightening mode. "the Committee was especially concerned about the potential for inflation pressures to increase." Members questioned "whether rates would be high enough without policy tightening to bring the growth of demand in line with that of supply." "all agreed that a significant further tightening of labor resource utilization would appreciably raise the risk." The previous meeting set up a tightening expectation. This meeting appears to have hiked 25bp. Forward lean is toward more tightening. Score 68-72 (moderate to strong hawkish).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All but one of the members favored" the monetary ranges (that's a minor procedural dissent). On the main economic assessment, there's broad agreement: "all agreed" on labor market risks. Minor variations ("some members," "several cited") but no fundamental disagreement on direction. Score 18-22.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Stock market wealth effects discussed as a macroeconomic driver ("sharp increases in stock market wealth"), but not from a financial stability/systemic risk perspective. "the future course of stock market prices was highly uncertain, and equity markets had shown a remarkable resilience to higher interest rates." Some mention of portfolio risk ("global portfolios... weighted toward dollar assets"). This is moderate -- stock market is discussed as an economic channel, not as a stability risk per se. Score 30-35.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The December statement noted the Committee "was especially concerned about the potential for inflation pressures to increase and would want to consider at its February meeting whether policy action would be needed." Members see need for tighter conditions: "Relatively high real interest rates... would be needed to help restore balance." "members expected that inflation pressures would gather some momentum over time unless financial conditions became tighter." Clear tightening signal forward. Score 72-76.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "the outlook for inflation remained subject to a marked degree of uncertainty" -- "the future course of stock market prices was highly uncertain" -- uncertainties about productivity growth dimensions. But overall confidence in the economic outlook is reasonably high; the uncertainty is more about specific variables (stock market, productivity trend). Score 42-48.

### Cross-dimension coherence check:
- OUTPUT_GAP (62) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (70): Both above 50, labor tighter than output gap which makes sense -- labor markets were historically tight even relative to the output gap.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (70) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (74): Both hawkish, aligned. Forward guidance slightly more hawkish reflects the explicit signaling.

### Polarity verification:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says rising risks, some pressures emerging. Score above 50. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says demand exceeding supply. Score above 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Rates up but equity wealth strong, credit flowing. Near neutral. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 56 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "core inflation remained subdued" (current state)
- "the members continued to see the risks as primarily tilted toward rising inflationary pressures"
- "all agreed that a significant further tightening of labor resource utilization would appreciably raise the risk of deterioration in the underlying inflation picture over time"
- "several cited recent statistical and anecdotal evidence of larger increases in labor compensation"
- "some nonlabor input prices already were rising faster"

**Center-of-gravity**: Members see core inflation currently contained but a mounting set of upside risks from tight labor, energy passthrough, and strong demand -- placing the score modestly above neutral.

**Minority views**: None explicitly dovish on inflation; the range is between "not yet a problem" and "building pressures," with all agreeing on the risk direction.

---

### OUTPUT_GAP: 62 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the economy still seemed to be growing very vigorously as it entered the new year"
- "the economy's potential to produce goods and services had been accelerating over time, but the demand for output had been growing even more strongly"
- "If this imbalance continued, inflationary pressures were likely to build"

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants clearly see demand exceeding potential supply, consistent with an economy running above potential. The gap is meaningful but not extreme because productivity growth is also accelerating.

**Minority views**: None; broad agreement on this assessment.

---

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 70 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "already very tight labor markets"
- "all agreed that a significant further tightening of labor resource utilization would appreciably raise the risk"
- Unemployment projected at "4 to 4-1/4 percent"
- "shortages of skilled construction workers and scarcities of some building supplies"
- "several cited recent statistical and anecdotal evidence of larger increases in labor compensation"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor markets are described as "very tight" with shortages and rising compensation -- firmly in the tight range. Matches the 1999-2000 anchor of 65-75.

**Minority views**: None dissenting; broad agreement on tightness.

---

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 67 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Retail sales appeared to have strengthened further during the opening weeks of the new year after a surge during the holiday season"
- "Motor vehicle sales in particular had continued to hold up at a remarkably high level"
- "Consumption was being supported by robust growth in jobs and incomes, very high levels of consumer confidence, and the lagged wealth effects"
- "Opportunities to enhance profits by using new technology were likely to lead to robust further growth in business fixed investment"

**Center-of-gravity**: Both consumption and investment are well above trend, with broad-based strength across consumer spending and business equipment. Some offset from softness in nonresidential structures.

**Minority views**: Minor -- some expected moderation ahead, but current conditions are uniformly strong.

---

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 50 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "higher interest rates apparently offset those of unexpectedly high corporate earnings, and most broad stock market indexes fell slightly"
- "Reflecting market expectations of substantial Federal Reserve tightening"
- "equity markets had shown a remarkable resilience to higher interest rates as earning prospects continued to be marked up"
- Higher mortgage rates noted, but "many homebuyers were shifting... to currently lower-cost adjustable rate mortgages"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are roughly neutral. Interest rates are higher and markets expect tightening, but equity wealth effects remain powerful, credit is flowing, and the economy is booming. No stress language.

**Minority views**: No meaningful divergence on financial conditions assessment.

---

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 70 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the Committee was especially concerned about the potential for inflation pressures to increase"
- "members questioned whether rates would be high enough without policy tightening to bring the growth of demand in line with that of supply"
- "members expected that inflation pressures would gather some momentum over time unless financial conditions became tighter"

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear consensus for tightening. Members see tighter policy as necessary to prevent overheating. Consistent with the 25bp hike at this meeting and expectation of more to come.

**Minority views**: No evident dovish dissent on the policy direction.

---

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 18 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all agreed that a significant further tightening of labor resource utilization would appreciably raise the risk"
- "All but one of the members favored" the monetary ranges (procedural, not policy dissent)
- No mention of policy dissents or significant disagreement on the economic outlook or policy direction

**Center-of-gravity**: Near-consensus on both the economic assessment and policy direction. The only dissent is one member on the monetary aggregate ranges, a procedural matter.

**Minority views**: One member wanted to raise monetary growth ranges to reflect higher productivity growth -- minor and procedural.

---

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 32 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Stock market wealth effects discussed at length as a macroeconomic transmission channel
- "the future course of stock market prices was highly uncertain, and equity markets had shown a remarkable resilience to higher interest rates"
- "as global portfolios came to be increasingly weighted toward dollar assets, expected returns on those assets would need to rise"
- No explicit "financial stability" or "systemic risk" language

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions (equity prices, interest rates, dollar) are discussed as economic drivers but not framed as stability concerns. Limited but present discussion of asset price dynamics.

**Minority views**: None.

---

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 74 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- December statement: "the Committee was especially concerned about the potential for inflation pressures to increase and would want to consider at its February meeting whether policy action would be needed"
- "Relatively high real interest rates... would be needed to help restore balance"
- "members expected that inflation pressures would gather some momentum over time unless financial conditions became tighter"
- "members questioned whether rates would be high enough without policy tightening"

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear forward signal toward continued tightening. The language explicitly conditions the outlook on achieving tighter financial conditions, signaling further rate increases ahead.

**Minority views**: None opposing the tightening trajectory.

---

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 45 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the outlook for inflation remained subject to a marked degree of uncertainty"
- "the future course of stock market prices was highly uncertain"
- "The prospects for energy prices were very difficult to predict"
- But overall economic assessment is quite confident in direction and strength

**Center-of-gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty, primarily around specific variables (stock prices, energy, productivity trend) rather than the overall direction of the economy. "Marked degree of uncertainty" on inflation pushes this above normal.

**Minority views**: Uncertainty about productivity growth dimensions noted.

**DOLLAR_CONCERN**: 55 +/-10 [Low confidence]

- "structural productivity growth had increased substantially"
- Clearly above-trend productivity growth perceived.
