# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2018-11-08 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Post-2009 segmented format with clear "Participants' Views" section separated from staff content.

**Era context**: November 2018 -- the Fed is in a gradual hiking cycle (target range 2.00-2.25%), economy growing above trend, unemployment at historically low levels, inflation near 2% target. Trade tensions with China are escalating. This is one of the final meetings before the December 2018 hike and the subsequent January 2019 dovish pivot.

**Quantifier distribution**: "participants agreed" (multiple), "almost all" (2 instances on further gradual hikes), "most" (residential weakness), "many" (wage growth, statement language), "several" (fiscal diminishing, leveraged loans concern), "some" (trade/tariffs factors, downside risks from abroad, upside inflation risk), "a few" (uncertainty increased, pass-through limited, timing uncertainty on hikes), "a couple" (near neutral rate, labor force participation both sides, investment volatility).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants observe both overall and core PCE "remained near 2 percent." Views are "consistent with their expectation that inflation would remain near the Committee's symmetric 2 percent objective on a sustained basis." Some firming in input costs due to tariffs but "pass-through of these higher costs to consumer prices was limited." Direction: at target. Score should be near 50. The tariff/input cost discussion creates mild upside concern but limited pass-through keeps it balanced. Score ~50-52.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "economic activity had been rising at a strong rate," "above-trend economic growth expected to continue." The text explicitly says "above-trend" growth. Bright-line rule: must be > 50. Participants point to strong employment, fiscal stimulus, consumer/business confidence. Expected to slow to trend over medium term. Current assessment is clearly above potential. Score ~56-58.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "labor market had continued to strengthen," "unemployment rate remained at a historically low level," "labor market tightness such as rates of job openings and quits continued to be elevated," "contacts in many Districts continued to report tight labor markets with difficulties finding qualified workers," "labor shortages, particularly for skilled labor, might be constraining activity." This is clearly tight. Nominal wage growth "picking up" but "broadly consistent with trends in productivity growth and inflation" -- so tight but not overheating wage spiral. Score ~62-65.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumption "strong" per participants, supported by District contacts "mostly upbeat." However, business fixed investment "slowed in the third quarter following several quarters of rapid growth" with trade/tariff concerns. "Most participants noted weakness in residential investment." Net: consumer spending strong, business investment moderating, housing weak. The mixed picture pulls from pure strength. Score ~55-58.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "financial conditions tightened over the intermeeting period, as equity prices declined, longer-term yields and borrowing costs for most sectors increased, and the foreign exchange value of the dollar rose." But "a number of participants judged that financial conditions remained accommodative relative to historical norms." So tightening from accommodative levels -- still on the loose-to-neutral side but moving toward neutral. Score ~42-46.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "almost all participants reaffirmed the view that further gradual increases...would likely be consistent with sustaining the Committee's objectives." "almost all participants expressed the view that another increase in the target range...was likely to be warranted fairly soon." This is a hold meeting with a clear signal for a December hike. However, "a couple of participants noted that the federal funds rate might currently be near its neutral level" and "many participants indicated that it might be appropriate at some upcoming meetings to begin to transition to statement language that placed greater emphasis on the evaluation of incoming data" -- signaling a shift toward more data-dependent/flexible stance. The dominant view is still hiking, but seeds of caution are present. Score ~67-70.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No formal dissents mentioned (unanimous hold). Views are broadly aligned -- "almost all" agree on further gradual increases. Minor differences: "a couple" think near neutral, "a few" express timing uncertainty. The spread is modest. Score ~22-26.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: "Among those who commented on financial stability, a number cited possible risks related to elevated CRE prices, narrow corporate bond spreads, or strong issuance of leveraged loans." Several participants concerned about leveraged loans. A couple noted the upcoming Financial Stability Report. This is a moderate level of discussion -- not dominant but more than passing. Score ~35-40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "almost all" see another hike "fairly soon" (December). But "many participants indicated that it might be appropriate at some upcoming meetings to begin to transition to statement language that placed greater emphasis on the evaluation of incoming data" -- i.e., moving away from pre-committed "further gradual increases." This is a hawkish near-term signal (December hike) but with a softening forward outlook. Score ~67-70.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "a few participants indicated that uncertainty had increased recently." "Participants also commented on how the Committee's communications...might need to be revised" suggesting awareness of changing conditions. Various risks noted (trade, global, fiscal fade) but "participants agreed that risks to the outlook appeared roughly balanced." Uncertainty is present but not dominant. Score ~32-38.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. At target = 50. Check: correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Above-trend = >50. Check: correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Very tight = high score. Check: correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter conditions. Tightened but still accommodative = below 50. Check: correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Almost all favor further gradual hikes = hawkish. Check: correct.

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (57) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (63): Both above 50, labor tighter than output gap -- consistent with late-cycle economy where labor is the binding constraint.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (68) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (68): Aligned -- both moderately hawkish. The forward guidance is slightly softening (data dependence language emerging) but still pointing to further hikes.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 51 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "both overall and core PCE price inflation remained near 2 percent on a 12-month basis"
- "participants viewed recent price developments as consistent with their expectation that inflation would remain near the Committee's symmetric 2 percent objective"
- "contacts in many Districts indicated that input costs had risen and that increased tariffs were raising costs" but "the pass-through of these higher costs to consumer prices was limited"

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation is essentially at target with mild upside pressures from tariffs/input costs that are not yet passing through to consumers. Essentially balanced around 2%.

**Minority views**: "Some participants raised the concern that tightening resource utilization...could generate undesirable upward pressure on inflation" (upside risk). "A couple of participants expressed the concern that measures of inflation expectations would remain low" (downside risk).

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 57 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "economic activity had been rising at a strong rate"
- "above-trend economic growth expected to continue before slowing to a pace closer to trend over the medium term"
- "strong employment gains, expansionary federal tax and spending policies, and continued high levels of consumer and business confidence"

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is running clearly above potential with above-trend growth, but expected to converge back to trend. The "above-trend" language puts this solidly above the midpoint.

**Minority views**: None -- broad agreement on above-trend characterization.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 63 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the unemployment rate remained at a historically low level"
- "measures of labor market tightness such as rates of job openings and quits continued to be elevated"
- "contacts in many Districts continued to report tight labor markets with difficulties finding qualified workers"
- "labor shortages, particularly for skilled labor, might be constraining activity in certain industries"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is clearly tight with historically low unemployment, elevated job openings/quits, and widespread reports of difficulty finding workers. Wage growth picking up but in line with productivity/inflation trends -- tight but not spiraling.

**Minority views**: "A couple of participants saw scope for further increases in the labor force participation rate" (suggesting some remaining slack), while "a couple of other participants judged that there was little scope for significant further increases."

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 56 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "participants generally continued to characterize consumption growth as strong" with District contacts "mostly upbeat"
- "growth in business fixed investment slowed in the third quarter following several quarters of rapid growth"
- "most participants noted weakness in residential investment"

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending is clearly strong (above trend), but business investment is moderating and residential investment is weak. Net position is above trend but not uniformly so.

**Minority views**: "A couple of others noted that business investment growth can be volatile...factors such as the recent cuts in corporate taxes...were expected to support investment going forward."

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 44 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "financial conditions tightened over the intermeeting period, as equity prices declined, longer-term yields and borrowing costs for most sectors increased, and the foreign exchange value of the dollar rose"
- "a number of participants judged that financial conditions remained accommodative relative to historical norms"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are tightening but remain accommodative by historical standards. The level is still on the loose side of neutral, even though the direction of change is toward tightness.

**Minority views**: None explicitly stated on this dimension.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 68 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "almost all participants reaffirmed the view that further gradual increases in the target range for the federal funds rate would likely be consistent with sustaining the Committee's objectives"
- "almost all participants expressed the view that another increase in the target range...was likely to be warranted fairly soon"
- "many participants indicated that it might be appropriate at some upcoming meetings to begin to transition to statement language that placed greater emphasis on the evaluation of incoming data"

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear hawkish lean with near-consensus for continued gradual hikes and imminent December hike. However, the emerging data-dependence language and awareness of crosscurrents temper the hawkishness from a stronger level.

**Minority views**: "A couple of participants noted that the federal funds rate might currently be near its neutral level and that further increases in the federal funds rate could unduly slow the expansion."

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 24 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- No formal dissents mentioned for this hold decision.
- "almost all participants" language on further gradual hikes indicates near-consensus.
- "A couple of participants noted that the federal funds rate might currently be near its neutral level" -- a minor dovish contingent.

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on the policy action and general direction, with only minor differences about timing and proximity to neutral rate. Low disagreement overall.

**Minority views**: The "couple" near neutral rate and "few" with timing uncertainty represent mild dissent from the dominant view.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 37 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Among those who commented on financial stability, a number cited possible risks related to elevated CRE prices, narrow corporate bond spreads, or strong issuance of leveraged loans"
- "several participants were concerned that the high level of debt in the nonfinancial business sector, and especially the high level of leveraged loans, made the economy more vulnerable to a sharp pullback in credit availability"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability receives a dedicated paragraph of discussion with specific concerns identified (CRE, leveraged loans, corporate bond spreads). It is more than passing but not a dominant topic of the meeting.

**Minority views**: "A few participants suggested that some of these financial vulnerabilities might not currently represent risks to financial stability so much as they represent downside risks to the economic outlook."

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 68 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "almost all participants expressed the view that another increase in the target range for the federal funds rate was likely to be warranted fairly soon"
- "many participants indicated that it might be appropriate at some upcoming meetings to begin to transition to statement language that placed greater emphasis on the evaluation of incoming data"
- "Monetary policy was not on a preset course; if incoming information prompted meaningful reassessments of the economic outlook and attendant risks...their policy outlook would change"

**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal is moderately hawkish -- pointing to a December hike and continued gradual normalization. But the emerging emphasis on data dependence and flexibility signals the beginning of a shift away from pre-committed tightening. This is a pivotal meeting in the transition.

**Minority views**: "A couple of participants noted that the federal funds rate might currently be near its neutral level" -- suggesting the hiking cycle may be near its end.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 35 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "a few participants indicated that uncertainty had increased recently, pointing to the high levels of uncertainty regarding the effects of fiscal and trade policies"
- "participants agreed that risks to the outlook appeared roughly balanced"
- Multiple risk factors noted (trade, global developments, leveraged loans, fiscal fade) but risk balance described as "roughly balanced"

**Center-of-gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty -- more risk factors cited than typical (trade, global, fiscal, financial vulnerabilities) and "a few" flag increased uncertainty. But the center of gravity sees risks as balanced, placing this in the mildly-above-normal range.

**Minority views**: Some see more downside risks from global developments/dollar; others see upside from fiscal stimulus/confidence.
