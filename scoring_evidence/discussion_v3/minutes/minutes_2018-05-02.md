# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2018-05-02 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **minutes** document (synthesized summary of participant views). It is segmented, with a clear "Participants' Views on Current Conditions and the Economic Outlook" section followed by a "Committee Policy Action" section. Quantifier language is used throughout ("all," "most," "many," "several," "some," "a few").

### Era Context
May 2018: mid-hiking cycle. The Fed had been gradually raising rates since December 2015. The economy was in an expansion with unemployment below estimates of the longer-run normal rate. Inflation had been persistently below 2% for years but was recently moving up to around the 2% target. Tax cuts passed in late 2017 were expected to boost growth. Trade policy uncertainty (tariffs) was emerging as a concern.

### Quantifier Distribution
- "All" / "agreed": economy rising at moderate rate; appropriate to leave rate unchanged at May meeting; adjustments depend on outlook
- "Participants generally": labor market strengthened further; expected further gradual increases appropriate; above-trend growth; risks roughly balanced
- "Most": recent inflation firming providing reassurance; if incoming info confirms outlook, likely soon appropriate to take another step
- "Many": overall wage pressures still moderate; emphasized inflation returning to 2% on sustained basis; tight resource utilization supporting projections
- "Several": recent wage developments provided little evidence of overheating; yield curve monitoring important; fiscal policy timing uncertain
- "Some": inflation likely to modestly overshoot 2%; supply constraints could develop; trade uncertainty could damp sentiment; revise forward guidance language soon
- "A few": underlying inflation trend little changed; premature to conclude inflation at 2%; longer-term expectations possibly drifted below 2%; federal funds rate could be at or above longer-run normal before too long; need to build financial sector resilience

### Core Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as approximately AT target. The 12-month PCE hit 2%, core at 1.9%. "Most participants viewed the recent firming in inflation as providing some reassurance that inflation was on a trajectory to achieve the Committee's symmetric 2 percent objective." "Some participants noted that inflation was likely to modestly overshoot 2 percent for a time." However, "several participants suggested that the underlying trend in inflation had changed little" and "various measures of underlying inflation...remained relatively stable at levels below 2 percent." The center of gravity is: inflation has arrived at or very near the 2% target, with modest upside risks. This is right around 50, perhaps slightly above. Score: 52. Polarity check: Higher = more pressure. Inflation at/near target with some seeing modest overshoot = slightly above 50. Correct.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as operating ABOVE potential. Key: "above-trend economic growth" is stated explicitly. "The unemployment rate was unchanged, but at a level below most estimates of its longer-run normal rate." The economy is "already operating above its potential level" (staff framing, but participants echo this with "above-trend" language). Bright-line rule: "above trend" language requires > 50. The language is moderate -- "above trend" but not "running hot" or "overheating." Score: 55. Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Above-trend growth, unemployment below longer-run normal = above 50. Correct.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see labor markets as TIGHT. "Labor market had continued to strengthen," "unemployment rate...at a level below most estimates of its longer-run normal rate," "contacts in a number of Districts reported shortages of workers," "in some cases, labor shortages were contributing to upward pressure on wages." However, "many participants commented that overall wage pressures were still moderate" and "several of them noted that recent wage developments provided little evidence of general overheating in the labor market." This is tight but not extremely so -- tight conditions with still-moderate wage follow-through. Score: 58. Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Low unemployment, worker shortages, but moderate wage pressures = moderately tight, above 50. Correct.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Economic activity had been rising at a moderate rate." Consumer spending moderated from strong Q4 pace (but expected temporary), business fixed investment "continued to grow strongly." Fundamentals supporting "continued above-trend economic growth" including tax policy, confidence, favorable financial conditions. However, trade/tariff concerns could cause "postponing or pulling back on capital spending." Net: solid, above-trend but not booming. Score: 56. Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Above-trend growth, strong business investment, temporary consumer moderation = somewhat above trend. Correct.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Financial conditions tightened somewhat over the intermeeting period but remained accommodative overall." Equity prices near historical highs, yield curve flattening, dollar up modestly. "Financial conditions, although somewhat tighter than at the time of the March FOMC meeting, had stayed accommodative overall." The clear characterization is: somewhat tightened but still accommodative. Score: 35. Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Conditions described as "accommodative overall" despite some tightening = below neutral. Correct.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All agreed to hold rates unchanged at this meeting. "Most participants judged that if incoming information broadly confirmed their current economic outlook, it would likely soon be appropriate for the Committee to take another step in removing policy accommodation." "Participants generally agreed...continuing to raise the target range for the federal funds rate gradually would likely be appropriate." This is a hold with clear expectation of hiking soon (June) -- firmly in the gradual tightening camp. "A few participants observed that the neutral level of the federal funds rate might currently be lower than their estimates of its longer-run level" suggesting the cycle may be nearing its end. Score: 67. Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Hold with clear signal of imminent hike and continued gradual tightening path = moderately hawkish. Correct.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: No dissents. "All participants expressed the view that it would be appropriate...to leave the target range...unchanged." There are some differing views on inflation trajectory (some see overshoot, several see underlying trend unchanged, a few see expectations drifted below) and on how close rates are to neutral. But the range of disagreement is modest -- it's about nuance within a shared framework. Score: 25. Polarity check: Higher = more disagreement. Unanimous hold, modest differences about outlook details = low-to-moderate disagreement. Correct.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: A couple of participants noted reduced prevalence of low-volatility strategies after February volatility. Asset valuations and nonfinancial corporate leverage "remained elevated." Several commented on regulatory reforms strengthening capital positions. A few emphasized need to build additional resilience. Yield curve flattening discussed by meeting participants at moderate length. This is a limited but present discussion. Score: 30. Polarity check: Higher = more attention. Limited but meaningful discussion by a few/several participants = below-moderate attention. Correct.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Forward signal is clearly hawkish-leaning. "Most participants judged...it would likely soon be appropriate...to take another step in removing policy accommodation." "Participants generally agreed...continuing to raise the target range...gradually would likely be appropriate." Members expected "further gradual increases." However, "a few" noted rates could be at or above longer-run normal "before too long," and some discussed modifying forward guidance language about rates being "below levels expected to prevail in the longer run." This is a moderate tightening forward signal -- further gradual hikes expected. Score: 68. Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Clear expectation of continued gradual rate increases = hawkish forward lean. Correct.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants generally viewed the risks to the economic outlook to be roughly balanced." However, there is notable uncertainty around trade policy: "a number of participants viewed the range of possible outcomes for economic activity and inflation to be particularly wide, depending on what actions were taken." Fiscal policy effects also uncertain. But the overall tone is measured -- standard caveats plus somewhat elevated trade uncertainty. Score: 35. Polarity check: Higher = more uncertainty. Roughly balanced risks but elevated trade policy uncertainty = somewhat above normal. Correct.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence
- OUTPUT_GAP (55) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (58): Both above 50, coherent with an economy running modestly above potential with tight labor markets.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (67) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (68): Closely aligned -- moderately hawkish current lean matches moderately hawkish forward signal, consistent with mid-hiking cycle gradual tightening.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 52 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The 12-month changes in overall and core PCE prices moved up in March, to 2 percent and 1.9 percent, respectively."
- "Most participants viewed the recent firming in inflation as providing some reassurance that inflation was on a trajectory to achieve the Committee's symmetric 2 percent objective on a sustained basis."
- "Some participants noted that inflation was likely to modestly overshoot 2 percent for a time." vs. "several participants suggested that the underlying trend in inflation had changed little."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Inflation has essentially reached the 2% target after years of undershooting; the center of gravity sees it at target with modest upside risk but no alarm.

**Minority views**: A few participants cautioned that underlying measures remained below 2% and it was premature to declare victory; some saw modest overshoot ahead. Narrow band reflects broad agreement that inflation is near target.

---

### OUTPUT_GAP: 55 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "A number of economic fundamentals were currently supporting continued above-trend economic growth."
- "The unemployment rate was unchanged, but at a level below most estimates of its longer-run normal rate."
- "Above-trend growth in real GDP in recent quarters" (Policy Action section).

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is operating modestly above potential, with above-trend growth and unemployment below its longer-run normal rate, but without language suggesting significant overheating.

**Minority views**: Some participants saw scope for labor supply to expand, potentially moderating the above-potential reading. Band is narrow given broad agreement.

---

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 58 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Labor markets were generally strong, and contacts in a number of Districts reported shortages of workers in specific industries or occupations."
- "The unemployment rate was unchanged, but at a level below most estimates of its longer-run normal rate."
- "Many participants commented that overall wage pressures were still moderate or were strong only in industries and occupations experiencing very tight labor supply."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Labor markets are clearly tight with low unemployment and scattered worker shortages, but wage pressures remain moderate overall, placing conditions above full employment but not at extreme tightness.

**Minority views**: Several noted little evidence of general labor market overheating; several saw scope for labor supply increases. Narrow band.

---

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 56 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity had been rising at a moderate rate... business fixed investment had continued to grow strongly."
- "A number of economic fundamentals were currently supporting continued above-trend economic growth; these included a strong labor market, federal tax and spending policies, high levels of household and business confidence, favorable financial conditions."
- "Contacts expressed concern about the possible adverse effects of tariffs and trade restrictions, including the potential for postponing or pulling back on capital spending."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Spending is solid and above trend, driven by strong business investment and supportive fundamentals, though consumer spending moderated temporarily and trade uncertainty poses a downside risk to investment.

**Minority views**: Trade concerns create some downside risk to the investment outlook. Band is narrow.

---

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Financial conditions tightened somewhat over the intermeeting period but remained accommodative overall."
- "Equity prices were about unchanged, on net, since the beginning of the year but were still near their historical highs."
- "Financial conditions, although somewhat tighter than at the time of the March FOMC meeting, had stayed accommodative overall."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions remain accommodative despite some intermeeting tightening, placing them on the loose side of neutral.

**Minority views**: None of significance; broad agreement on characterization.

---

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 67 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All participants expressed the view that it would be appropriate for the Committee to leave the target range for the federal funds rate unchanged at the May meeting."
- "Most participants judged that if incoming information broadly confirmed their current economic outlook, it would likely soon be appropriate for the Committee to take another step in removing policy accommodation."
- "Participants generally agreed...continuing to raise the target range for the federal funds rate gradually would likely be appropriate if the economy evolves about as expected."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants are in a moderately hawkish posture -- holding this meeting but clearly signaling the next hike is imminent and the gradual tightening path continues.

**Minority views**: A few noted rates could soon be at or above longer-run neutral, suggesting the cycle may be nearing its end; a few emphasized caution given inflation history. Band is narrow.

---

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 25 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All participants expressed the view that it would be appropriate for the Committee to leave the target range for the federal funds rate unchanged."
- Differences are on nuance: some vs. several on inflation trajectory, a few on proximity to neutral rate, a few vs. many on inflation expectations anchoring.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Broad agreement on the hold decision and general economic assessment, with only modest variation in emphasis on inflation trajectory and proximity to neutral rate.

**Minority views**: None that rise to formal dissent level.

---

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 30 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "A couple of participants noted that after the bout of financial market volatility in early February, the use of investment strategies predicated on a low-volatility environment may have become less prevalent."
- "Asset valuations across a range of markets and leverage in the nonfinancial corporate sector remained elevated relative to historical norms."
- "A few participants emphasized the need to build additional resilience in the financial sector at this point in the economic expansion."
- Extended discussion of yield curve flattening and its implications.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial stability receives a limited but meaningful discussion, with a couple/few participants addressing asset valuations, leverage, and regulatory resilience, plus a broader yield curve discussion. This is somewhat above the typical 2013-19 baseline.

**Minority views**: Most of the discussion is from a few participants; it does not dominate the meeting.

---

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 68 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Most participants judged that if incoming information broadly confirmed their current economic outlook, it would likely soon be appropriate for the Committee to take another step in removing policy accommodation."
- "Members expected that economic conditions would evolve in a manner that would warrant further gradual increases in the federal funds rate."
- "Some participants noted it might soon be appropriate to revise the forward-guidance language...indicating that the 'federal funds rate is likely to remain, for some time, below levels that are expected to prevail in the longer run.'"

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal is moderately hawkish -- continued gradual rate increases expected, with some discussing removing accommodative language, indicating the tightening cycle is well underway.

**Minority views**: A few noted proximity to neutral may limit further hikes; a few emphasized caution on inflation expectations. Band is narrow.

---

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants generally viewed the risks to the economic outlook to be roughly balanced."
- "A number of participants viewed the range of possible outcomes for economic activity and inflation to be particularly wide, depending on what actions were taken by the United States and how U.S. trading partners responded."
- "Several participants continued to note the challenge of assessing the timing and magnitude of the effects of recent fiscal policy changes."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Overall uncertainty is somewhat elevated above normal, driven primarily by trade and fiscal policy uncertainty, though the baseline risk assessment is balanced.

**Minority views**: Trade policy creates wider-than-normal uncertainty for some participants, but the overall framing is measured.
