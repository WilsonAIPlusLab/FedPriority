**Era context**: Mid-2015, pre-liftoff period. Fed funds rate at 0-0.25%. Economy in extended recovery from GFC, with labor market improving but inflation persistently below 2%. Committee approaching first rate hike since 2006. China concerns and dollar appreciation in the background.

**Quantifier distribution**: "generally" (frequent), "most" (several uses), "many" (several uses), "several" (several uses), "some" (several uses), "a few" (a couple uses), "a couple" (a couple uses), "one member" (once). This is a well-distributed minutes document with good granularity.

"Some participants cited downside risks to inflation." "Most participants still expected that the downward pressure on inflation from the previous declines in energy prices and the effects of past dollar appreciation would prove to be temporary." The center of gravity is clearly below target but with expectation of gradual return. Some alternative measures (trimmed mean PCE, median CPI) "continued to run at higher levels" and "core CPI inflation had picked up." This tempers somewhat but the dominant message is below target. ## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score**: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective, partly reflecting earlier declines in energy prices and further decreases in prices of non-energy imports."
- "Some participants cited downside risks to inflation, pointing to the absence of any noticeable response of inflation to the reduction in resource slack over the past several years."
- "Most participants still expected that the downward pressure on inflation from the previous declines in energy prices and the effects of past dollar appreciation would prove to be temporary."
**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation running below target with some downside risks, but most participants expected a gradual return to 2% -- placing this in the "below target; slack keeping prices in check" range.
**Minority views**: Some cited alternative measures (trimmed mean, median CPI, core CPI) running higher, slightly tempering the below-target signal.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score**: 44 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity had been expanding moderately in recent months."
- "Most members saw room for some additional progress in reducing labor market slack, although several viewed current labor market conditions as at or very close to those consistent with maximum employment."
- "A few participants observed that although GDP growth appeared to have picked up in recent months relative to the first-quarter pace, the level of GDP remained lower than had been projected earlier in the year."
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy slightly below potential but closing the gap; "underutilization" still cited but "diminishing."
**Minority views**: Several viewed labor conditions as already at or near maximum employment, pulling the range upward.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score**: 46 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Underutilization of labor resources had continued to diminish" and "several participants contended that... some noticeable margins of slack remained."
- "Anecdotal reports of firms having concerns about retaining workers and facing difficulties in filling even medium- and lower-skilled jobs."
- "Several viewed current labor market conditions as at or very close to those consistent with maximum employment."
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market approaching full employment with remaining pockets of slack; near NAIRU but not clearly past it.
**Minority views**: Split between those seeing remaining slack (high part-time, discouraged workers) and those seeing near-full employment with hiring difficulties.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score**: 48 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants generally expected consumer spending to rise moderately over the near term" with "continued gains in employment and income, high household net worth, and low gasoline prices" as supports.
- "Business fixed investment remained soft even as the drag from the sharp contraction in drilling rigs over the first half of this year appeared to be fading."
- "Net exports to continue to subtract from GDP growth over the second half of the year."
**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending moderate, business investment soft, net exports a drag -- net position roughly at trend, slightly below.
**Minority views**: A couple concerned about consumer spending disappointment despite favorable conditions.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score**: 35 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Consumer credit conditions were also seen as favorable, with business contacts pointing to steady loan growth, especially for auto loans and credit cards."
- "The easing of lending standards for residential mortgages evidenced in the most recent SLOOS."
- Mention of "deterioration in bond market liquidity reported by market participants."
**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions described as generally accommodative/favorable, with easy credit and loosening lending standards. Bond market liquidity a minor concern. Somewhat loose overall.
**Minority views**: Bond market liquidity concerns introduce some tightening element.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score**: 54 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Most judged that the conditions for policy firming had not yet been achieved, but they noted that conditions were approaching that point."
- "Some participants... viewed the economic conditions for beginning to increase the target range for the federal funds rate as having been met or were confident that they would be met shortly."
- "Almost all members indicating that they would need to see more evidence that economic growth was sufficiently strong."
**Center-of-gravity**: Hold steady with clear tightening bias -- "approaching" first hike, majority not quite ready but the direction is unmistakably toward tightening.
**Minority views**: Some ready to hike now; several concerned about premature tightening given low inflation.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score**: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "One member... indicated a readiness to take that step at this meeting but was willing to wait for additional data."
- "Some participants expressed the view that the incoming information had not yet provided grounds for reasonable confidence that inflation would move back to 2 percent."
- "Some participants... emphasized that the economy had made significant progress... and viewed the economic conditions for beginning to increase the target range... as having been met."
**Center-of-gravity**: Notable camps (ready-to-hike vs. need-more-evidence) but no formal dissent. The one near-dissenter deferred. Moderate disagreement about timing but broad agreement on direction.
**Minority views**: The ready-to-hike camp vs. the inflation-concerned camp create a meaningful spread.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score**: 37 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants discussed a range of topics associated with financial market developments and financial stability" including bond market liquidity, leveraged loan migration, valuation risks, and Puerto Rico.
- "A couple of others thought that an appreciable delay in beginning the process of normalization might... have adverse consequences for financial stability."
**Center-of-gravity**: A dedicated paragraph of discussion covering multiple financial stability topics, plus a few participants linking normalization timing to financial stability -- moderate but not dominant emphasis.
**Minority views**: None divergent; this is about attention level.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score**: 57 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Conditions were approaching that point" for policy firming.
- "The expected path for policy, not the initial increase, would be the most important determinant of financial conditions."
- "It likely would be appropriate to adjust the federal funds rate gradually after the first increase."
- "Policy would continue to be accommodative to support progress toward the Committee's dual objectives."
**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal points toward normalization beginning soon but gradually -- mild tightening bias with emphasis on data dependence and gradual path.
**Minority views**: Some wanted to signal more readiness; others cautioned about premature tightening.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score**: 40 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Considerable uncertainty remained about when wages might begin to accelerate and whether that development might translate into increased price inflation."
- "Several members... underscored the uncertainty surrounding the inflation process as well as the role and dynamics of inflation expectations."
- "Participants generally viewed the risks to the outlook for domestic economic activity and the labor market as nearly balanced" but "many continued to see some downside risks arising from economic and financial developments abroad."
**Center-of-gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty, particularly around the inflation process and international risks (China, dollar), but domestic risks seen as "nearly balanced." Above normal hedging but not dramatically uncertain.
**Minority views**: International risks (China, dollar) cited by several as sources of added uncertainty.
