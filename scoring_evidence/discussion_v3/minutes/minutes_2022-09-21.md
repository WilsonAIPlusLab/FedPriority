# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2022-09-21 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented -- participant discussion is clearly separated from staff content.

**Era context**: September 2022. The Fed is in the midst of its most aggressive tightening cycle in decades. CPI inflation peaked near 9% in June 2022. The Committee had already delivered 75bp hikes in June and July. This meeting delivers another 75bp hike. The macro backdrop is high inflation, very tight labor markets, and incipient slowdown in interest-sensitive sectors.

**Quantifier distribution**: Heavy use of strong quantifiers -- "participants" (all/most implied) appears throughout as the dominant framing. "Many" appears several times for key hawkish views. "Several" used for specific observations. "A few" and "a couple" for minority positions. "All participants agreed" on the 75bp hike. The center of gravity is firmly hawkish with near-consensus.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as far above target. "Inflation remained unacceptably high and well above the Committee's longer-run goal of 2 percent." "Recent inflation data generally had come in above expectations." "Price pressures had remained elevated and had persisted across a broad array of product categories." Risks "weighted to the upside." This is peak-era 2022 language. Direction: very high. Per anchor, 2022 Q2-Q3 peak = 82-88. The language here -- "unacceptably high," "well above," "above expectations," "broad-based," upside risks -- squarely fits 84-87.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Recent indicators had pointed to modest growth in spending and production." Economy growing but "below-trend pace" anticipated. "Interest-sensitive sectors weakened appreciably." But labor market "very tight," unemployment "historically low," and "several participants noted... current GDP data could understate the strength in economic activity." The economy is still above potential (tight labor market, demand outpacing supply in key areas) but momentum is clearly decelerating. The bright-line rule: labor market "very tight" and operating above potential signals score >50. But "below-trend" growth is anticipated/beginning. Current conditions still above potential but moderating. Score: 55-58. Anchor: 57-63 for 2022.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor market had remained very tight, as evidenced by a historically low unemployment rate, elevated job vacancies and quit rates, a low pace of layoffs, robust employment gains, and high nominal wage growth." "A few participants remarked that employers facing particularly acute labor shortages." "Some participants noted... developments consistent with the labor market moving toward better balance" but this is incipient. Current level: very tight. Anchor: 65-75 for 2022-23. This fits 70-73.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumer spending grew moderately." "Spending appeared to have held up relatively well." But "notable slowdown in residential investment and other interest-sensitive spending." "Growth in investment spending appeared modest." "Manufacturing activity had slowed." Mixed picture: consumer spending moderate/resilient, but investment weak and housing declining. Net: slightly below trend to at trend. Score: 43-47.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Committee's commitment to restoring price stability... had contributed to a notable tightening of financial conditions over the past year." "Substantial increases in real interest rates across the maturity spectrum." "Some interest-sensitive categories of spending... had already started to respond to the tightening of financial conditions." "Higher financing costs" for businesses. This describes clearly tightened conditions. Anchor: 60-70 for 2022 rate hikes. Score: 65-68.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All participants agreed... appropriate to raise the target range for the federal funds rate 75 basis points." "Many participants emphasized that the cost of taking too little action to bring down inflation likely outweighed the cost of taking too much action." "Many stressing the importance of staying on this course even as the labor market slowed." "Many participants... had raised their assessment of the path of the federal funds rate." "Several participants underlined the need to maintain a restrictive stance for as long as necessary." This is very strong hawkish consensus with a 75bp hike. Anchor: 80-85 for late 2022 75bp hikes. A few participants note emerging two-sided risks, but this is clearly minority. Score: 83.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All participants agreed" on the 75bp hike. No formal dissents mentioned. The range of views is narrow on the action. "Several participants noted... it would be important to calibrate the pace of further policy tightening with the aim of mitigating the risk of significant adverse effects." "Several participants observed that as policy moved into restrictive territory, risks would become more two-sided." These are moderate cautionary notes from a minority. This is broad agreement with minor tactical differences. Score: 22-28.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial conditions are discussed but primarily as a transmission mechanism for policy, not as a stability concern per se. "Notable tightening of financial conditions" -- mentioned in context of policy effects. "Heightened risk of recession in Europe" and global headwinds mentioned. "Several participants noted that the monetary policy tightening under way in many other economies would affect global financial markets." No direct discussion of systemic risk, market dysfunction, or stability threats. Moderate background discussion. Score: 30-35.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Participants continued to anticipate that ongoing increases in the target range for the federal funds rate would be appropriate." "Many participants... had raised their assessment of the path of the federal funds rate that would likely be needed." "Many participants indicated that, once the policy rate had reached a sufficiently restrictive level, it likely would be appropriate to maintain that level for some time." "Several participants noted... it would become appropriate at some point to slow the pace of policy rate increases." The forward signal is strongly hawkish -- more hikes coming, hold at restrictive level, but slowing pace eventually. Anchor: 80-95 for 2022 aggressive hiking. Score: 85-88.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants agreed that the uncertainty associated with their economic outlooks was high." "Risks to their inflation outlook were weighted to the upside." "Risks to real GDP growth... weighted to the downside." "A few participants particularly stressed the high uncertainty associated with the expected future path of the unemployment rate." "Highly uncertain global economic and financial environment." Explicit "high" uncertainty language from participants as a group. Anchor: 61-75 for "many emphasize difficulty." Score: 65-68.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (56) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (72): Divergence is explained by labor market lagging the slowdown in output. Participants explicitly note that GDP is slowing while labor remains very tight -- this is the documented condition.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (83) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (86): Aligned. Both strongly hawkish. Forward guidance slightly more hawkish due to "ongoing increases" and "maintain restrictive level for some time."

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says very high pressure. Score 85. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Economy still above potential but decelerating. Score 56. Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. "Very tight." Score 72. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. "Notable tightening." Score 67. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 85 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants observed that inflation remained unacceptably high and well above the Committee's longer-run goal of 2 percent."
- "Recent inflation data generally had come in above expectations and... inflation was declining more slowly than they had previously been anticipating."
- "Price pressures had remained elevated and had persisted across a broad array of product categories."
- Risks "weighted to the upside."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Near-universal view that inflation is far above target, broadening, and declining more slowly than expected, with upside risks -- consistent with peak 2022 inflation concern.

**Minority views**: None meaningfully dissent on the inflation assessment; uncertainty band reflects the range between those emphasizing medium-term deceleration vs. those stressing upside risks.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 56 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Recent indicators had pointed to modest growth in spending and production."
- "Activity in interest-sensitive sectors weakened appreciably."
- "Several participants noted that the continued strength in the labor market... raised the possibility that the current GDP data could understate the strength in economic activity."
- "Participants generally anticipated that the U.S. economy would grow at a below-trend pace."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is still modestly above potential given labor tightness and residual demand strength, but the output gap is narrowing as interest-sensitive sectors slow and below-trend growth is anticipated.

**Minority views**: Some see GDP as understating strength (pushing score higher); the anticipated below-trend growth suggests the gap will close. The +-6 band captures this tension.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 72 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The labor market had remained very tight, as evidenced by a historically low unemployment rate, elevated job vacancies and quit rates, a low pace of layoffs, robust employment gains, and high nominal wage growth."
- "A few participants remarked that employers facing particularly acute labor shortages were those associated with professional occupations, service industries, skilled trades, and smaller firms."
- "Some participants noted a number of developments consistent with the labor market moving toward better balance, including a lower rate of job turnover, a moderation in employment growth."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The labor market is very tight by all standard measures, though nascent signs of rebalancing are emerging at the margin.

**Minority views**: "Some" note early signs of easing; "several" see limited scope for participation improvement. Narrow band reflects broad agreement on the "very tight" assessment.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 45 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending grew moderately, reflecting strength in the labor market."
- "Spending appeared to have held up relatively well, especially among higher-income households."
- "The notable slowdown in residential investment and other interest-sensitive spending had continued."
- "Growth in investment spending appeared modest." "Manufacturing activity had slowed."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumer spending is moderate but business investment is modest and housing is declining sharply, netting to slightly below trend overall.

**Minority views**: Higher-income consumer resilience vs. lower-income spending shifts create some dispersion. The divergence between consumption (holding up) and investment/housing (weakening) widens the band.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 67 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The Committee's commitment to restoring price stability... had contributed to a notable tightening of financial conditions over the past year."
- "This tightening had led to substantial increases in real interest rates across the maturity spectrum."
- "Some interest-sensitive categories of spending... had already started to respond to the tightening of financial conditions."
- Businesses faced "higher financing costs."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions have tightened notably and are now clearly restrictive, with real rates up substantially and credit conditions affecting interest-sensitive spending, though no crisis-level stress is described.

**Minority views**: Minimal disagreement on this assessment; participants uniformly acknowledge tightening.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 83 +/-4 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All participants agreed that it was appropriate to raise the target range for the federal funds rate 75 basis points at this meeting."
- "Many participants emphasized that the cost of taking too little action to bring down inflation likely outweighed the cost of taking too much action."
- "Many stressing the importance of staying on this course even as the labor market slowed."
- "Several participants underlined the need to maintain a restrictive stance for as long as necessary."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Very strong hawkish consensus supporting a 75bp hike with a risk-management framework tilted toward doing more rather than less, consistent with peak 2022 hawkishness.

**Minority views**: "Several participants noted... it would be important to calibrate the pace of further policy tightening with the aim of mitigating the risk of significant adverse effects on the economic outlook" -- a modest cautionary minority. "Several" see two-sided risks emerging.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 25 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All participants agreed" on the 75bp hike -- no formal dissents.
- "Several participants noted... it would be important to calibrate the pace of further policy tightening with the aim of mitigating the risk of significant adverse effects."
- "Several participants observed that as policy moved into restrictive territory, risks would become more two-sided."
- "A few participants... noted that this possibility was heightened by factors beyond the Committee's actions."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Broad agreement on the current action with minor tactical differences about the forward pace and risk calibration -- a typical low-dissent meeting with modest "several" minority cautionary notes.

**Minority views**: The cautionary "several" constitute real but contained disagreement about the forward path, not the current action.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 32 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Financial conditions discussed primarily as policy transmission: "notable tightening of financial conditions over the past year."
- "Several participants noted that the monetary policy tightening under way in many other economies would affect global financial markets... with the potential for spillovers."
- "Heightened risk of recession in Europe" and global headwinds noted.
- No dedicated discussion of systemic risk, market dysfunction, or financial stability as a standalone concern.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions are discussed in a meaningful but secondary role, mainly as a channel through which policy restrains demand and as a source of global spillover risk, not as a primary stability concern.

**Minority views**: None on this dimension specifically.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 86 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants continued to anticipate that ongoing increases in the target range for the federal funds rate would be appropriate."
- "Many participants... had raised their assessment of the path of the federal funds rate that would likely be needed."
- "Many participants indicated that, once the policy rate had reached a sufficiently restrictive level, it likely would be appropriate to maintain that level for some time."
- "It would become appropriate at some point to slow the pace of policy rate increases while assessing the effects of cumulative policy adjustments."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal is strongly hawkish -- more rate increases ahead, higher terminal rate than previously expected, and a commitment to hold at restrictive levels for an extended period, with the pace-slowing signal only a minor offset.

**Minority views**: The "slow the pace" language and emerging two-sided risk acknowledgment provide a modest dovish lean from some participants on the forward path, captured in the +-5 band.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 66 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants agreed that the uncertainty associated with their economic outlooks was high."
- "Risks to their inflation outlook were weighted to the upside."
- "Participants broadly judged the risks to real GDP growth to be weighted to the downside."
- "Several participants noted... particularly in the current highly uncertain global economic and financial environment."
- "A few participants particularly stressed the high uncertainty associated with the expected future path of the unemployment rate."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants explicitly characterize uncertainty as "high" with asymmetric risks on both inflation (up) and growth (down), reflecting genuine elevated uncertainty about the outlook.

**Minority views**: Broad agreement that uncertainty is high; the band reflects the range between those with somewhat more confident policy prescriptions and those emphasizing deep uncertainty about unemployment path.
