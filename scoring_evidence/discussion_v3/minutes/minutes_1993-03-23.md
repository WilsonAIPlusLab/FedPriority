# FOMC Discussion Scoring - 1993-03-23

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is **minutes** (synthesized summary of participant views). It is unsegmented (pre-2009), mixing staff and participant language. I will score only participant-attributable content identified by cues such as "the members," "most members," "several members," "some members," "a number of members," etc.

### Era Context
March 1993: The U.S. economy is in the early-to-mid phase of recovery from the 1990-91 recession. Growth picked up in late 1992 but is expected to moderate. Unemployment is 7.0% -- still elevated. The Fed funds rate is low (around 3%). Inflation is low but recent CPI/PPI readings have ticked up, generating some concern. The macro backdrop is a sluggish recovery with significant remaining slack.

### Quantifier Distribution
- "most members" / "majority": endorsed unchanged policy; concluded recent prices did not confirm inflation resurgence; favored symmetric directive
- "many members": concluded recent prices not persuasive evidence of inflation change; gave more weight to unit labor costs
- "several members": acknowledged low real rates could have inflationary consequences if expansion strengthened
- "some members": cautioned about uncertainty in either direction; commented further disinflation was reasonable expectation
- "a number of members": commented policy was quite accommodative
- "one member": observed faster expansion might intensify inflation; commented about narrower monetary measures
- "two members" (Angell, Lindsey): dissented, favoring immediate tightening

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as slightly below midpoint but with rising concerns. "Many members" concluded recent price upticks were NOT persuasive evidence of inflation change. "Most" concluded evidence did not confirm a resurgence. "Some commented that further modest disinflation remained a reasonable expectation." However, there was "a degree of unease" about recent CPI/PPI increases, commodity price upticks. The center of gravity: inflation is low/subdued, but there are upside warning signs. Members generally gave more weight to favorable unit labor cost trends and "considerable slack." This points to below-midpoint but not deeply so -- around 35-40. The two dissenters see more inflation risk, which widens uncertainty.

Direction: Below midpoint. Recent price data raises some concern but majority sees it as transitory/not yet confirmed. Score ~37.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Members describe "sustained economic expansion" but "at a pace appreciably below" Q4 1992. Unemployment at 7.0% -- still high. "Considerable slack in the utilization of labor and capital resources." Balance sheet restructuring and defense cutbacks are ongoing drags. Growth is moderate but below potential. Clearly below potential language. Score must be < 50. The economy is recovering but slack remains substantial. Score ~38.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 7.0%. "Considerable slack in the utilization of labor and capital resources." "Absence of any firming in wage pressures." Firms relying on overtime rather than hiring new workers due to uncertainty, though "an increasing number of contacts were reporting worker recalls or new hires." "Increases in wages have remained subdued." This is clearly a slack labor market -- below full employment with elevated unemployment. Score ~32.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending had been strong in late 1992 but was expected to moderate. "Consumer sector was viewed as likely to play a key role in sustaining the expansion." Consumer balance sheets improved. Business equipment spending providing "appreciable stimulus." But nonresidential construction "remained soft" due to overcapacity. Housing "held up relatively well." Net assessment: moderate growth, slightly below trend given the mixed picture (strong equipment, weak nonresidential structures, moderating consumer). Score ~45.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Greatly reduced interest rates" are a positive factor. Policy described as "quite accommodative" with "low short-term interest rates, especially on an inflation-adjusted basis." "Continuation of balance sheet restructuring activities by financial institutions and the associated caution on the part of these institutions with regard to extending loans still appeared to be exerting a significantly inhibiting effect." So rates are very low (loose) but credit supply is still constrained by bank restructuring. Net: somewhat loose overall due to very low rates, but credit channel is impaired. Score ~40 (somewhat loose to neutral, leaning loose due to low rates but partially offset by bank caution in lending).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Most members (10-2 vote) favored unchanged policy. Two members dissented hawkish. "Several members acknowledged" low real rates could have inflationary consequences but didn't favor action now. The majority saw current stance as "appropriately balanced." This is a hold with some hawkish undertones from a minority. The center of gravity is hold/slightly dovish to balanced -- the expansion is still fragile, policy is accommodative by design. Score ~47 (lean slightly dovish to balanced -- holding an accommodative stance, not yet ready to tighten, but acknowledging eventual need).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Two formal dissents (Angell, Lindsey) both favoring tightening. The majority was fairly cohesive around holding. "Several members acknowledged" risks of current low rates. This is a clear two-dissent meeting with substantive disagreement about inflation outlook. Score ~40.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Very minimal discussion of financial stability per se. References to "financial markets" are about rates and conditions, not stability concerns. Bank balance sheet restructuring is mentioned but as a credit channel issue, not a stability concern. Bond market functioning referenced briefly. This is a 1993 meeting -- financial stability emphasis was typically very low. Score ~10.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Symmetric directive -- no presumption about direction. "Some observed that a policy change during the intermeeting period, if any, might well be in the direction of a tightening move." But "no compelling case" for action now. "A symmetric directive was warranted." This is basically neutral/data-dependent with a very mild hawkish tilt acknowledged by some. Score ~52.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Some members cautioned that...a considerably different result--in either direction--could not be ruled out." "Current expansion differed in important respects from earlier cyclical recoveries." Business contacts "uncertain about the outlook." "Considerable uncertainty" around deficit reduction outlook. "Price developments were notably difficult to predict." Monetary growth relative to spending had "considerable uncertainty." This is moderately elevated uncertainty language. Score ~42.

### Cross-Dimension Coherence Check
- OUTPUT_GAP (38) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (32): Both below 50, consistent with slack economy. Labor slightly lower reflecting 7% unemployment.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (47) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (52): Close together. Slight divergence explained by the forward signal that "if any" change would be toward tightening, while current action is firmly hold/slightly dovish.

### Polarity Checks
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (37): Higher = more pressure. Evidence says low but with upside unease. 37 < 50. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP (38): Higher = more above potential. Economy below potential with slack. 38 < 50. Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS (32): Higher = tighter. Labor slack, 7% unemployment. 32 < 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (40): Higher = tighter conditions. Very low rates but impaired credit channel. Net somewhat loose. 40 < 50. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (47): Higher = more hawkish. Majority holds accommodative stance. 47 < 50. Correct.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (52): Higher = more hawkish forward. Neutral directive but acknowledged next move likely tightening. 52 slightly > 50. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 37 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "the members generally gave more weight to the behavior of unit labor costs, which indicated that much of the economy's underlying cost structure did not reflect any signs of a pickup in inflationary pressures"
- "most of the members concluded that the evidence at this point did not confirm a resurgence in inflationary pressures, and some commented that further modest disinflation remained a reasonable expectation"
- "the faster increases in consumer prices in recent months and a sharp upturn in the prices of certain producer materials tended to raise concerns, or at least a degree of unease"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The majority of members see inflation as contained and supported by favorable unit labor costs and slack, though recent price upticks have introduced some unease. The center of gravity is below-target inflation with upside risk flags.

**Minority views:** Two dissenters (Angell, Lindsey) see inflation risk as more pressing, citing commodity prices, low real rates, and dollar weakness. This widens uncertainty.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 38 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "sustained economic expansion, though at a pace appreciably below that now indicated for the fourth quarter of 1992"
- "considerable slack in the utilization of labor and capital resources"
- "a number of factors were likely to limit its strength, including ongoing balance sheet and business restructuring activities, the outlook for a more restrictive federal budget, and continuing weakness in key foreign markets"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The economy is recovering but remains meaningfully below potential, with considerable slack in both labor and capital utilization and multiple headwinds limiting growth momentum.

**Minority views:** None significant -- broad agreement on slack.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "considerable slack in the utilization of labor and capital resources"
- "absence of any firming in wage pressures" and "increases in wages have remained subdued"
- "continuing reliance of numerous firms on overtime work to meet growing demand rather than incurring the considerable costs of adding new workers"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The labor market has significant slack with 7.0% unemployment. Firms are reluctant to hire, relying on overtime instead. Wage pressures are absent. Some worker recalls and new hires are emerging, but the market is clearly below full employment.

**Minority views:** No meaningful disagreement on labor conditions.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 45 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "the consumer sector was viewed as likely to play a key role in sustaining the expansion this year" but with expected moderation from Q4 pace
- "Business spending on producers' durable equipment also was believed likely to continue to provide appreciable stimulus"
- "business spending for nonresidential structures probably would continue to be held back by weakness in office construction stemming from widespread overcapacity"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Consumer spending is moderating from a strong Q4 but remains a pillar of the expansion. Business equipment investment is solid. However, nonresidential construction is weak due to overcapacity, and housing starts were "somewhat disappointing." Net assessment is slightly below trend but moderately positive.

**Minority views:** Limited disagreement; views are fairly uniform on spending patterns.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 40 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "greatly reduced interest rates" cited as a positive factor; "a number of members commented that other indicators suggested that current monetary policy was in fact quite accommodative"
- "continuation of balance sheet restructuring activities by financial institutions and the associated caution on the part of these institutions with regard to extending loans still appeared to be exerting a significantly inhibiting effect on the overall growth in spending"
- "extensions of credit and growth in overall nonfinancial sector debt were not consistent with an economy that was generating significant inflationary pressures"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Interest rates are very low, making conditions generally loose/accommodative. However, bank balance sheet restructuring and caution in lending partially offset this, creating a somewhat unusual environment of low rates but impaired credit transmission. Net conditions are somewhat loose but not fully accommodative due to credit channel constraints.

**Minority views:** Dissenters view policy as "overly accommodative," which would imply even looser financial conditions from their perspective.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 47 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "most of the members endorsed a proposal to maintain an unchanged degree of pressure on reserve positions"
- "the current degree of reserve pressure continued to represent a policy stance that was appropriately balanced in light of the opposing risks of a faltering economic expansion and a resurgence of inflation"
- "two members supported an immediate move to tighten reserve conditions" citing "concerns about the outlook for inflation"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The majority (10-2) favors holding the accommodative stance, viewing it as balanced between growth and inflation risks. The lean is very slightly dovish -- they are consciously maintaining low rates to support a fragile expansion. The two hawkish dissenters pull the center slightly toward neutral.

**Minority views:** Angell and Lindsey favor immediate tightening, arguing policy is "overly accommodative" and risks higher inflation. This creates meaningful but minority hawkish pressure.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 40 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Votes against this action: Messrs. Angell and Lindsey" -- two formal dissents
- Dissenters offered detailed reasoning: "monetary policy currently was overly accommodative as suggested by various indicators such as recent data on consumer and producer prices, the upswing in commodity prices, the low level of real short-term interest rates"
- Mr. Angell provided an extended, detailed rationale including historical precedent arguments

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Two formal dissents with substantive, well-articulated disagreement about inflation risks and policy direction. The majority was fairly cohesive but "several members acknowledged" some of the dissenters' concerns about low real rates. This is moderate disagreement -- more than typical one-dissent meetings.

**Minority views:** The disagreement is substantive (inflation framework) not merely tactical.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 10 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- Brief references to "financial markets" and bond yields but no substantive discussion of financial stability risks
- Bank restructuring discussed as a credit channel issue, not a stability concern
- One mention: "a shift in the direction of policy...would be likely to have an especially pronounced impact on financial markets" -- but this is about policy signaling, not stability

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial stability is essentially not a topic of discussion at this meeting. References to financial conditions are about monetary policy transmission, not stability concerns. Consistent with the low-attention 1993 era anchor.

**Minority views:** None.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 52 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "a preference for retaining the symmetry of the existing directive" -- no presumption about direction
- "Some observed that a policy change during the intermeeting period, if any, might well be in the direction of a tightening move"
- "a tilt in the directive toward restraint would give a misleading indication of the Committee's current intentions"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The forward signal is essentially neutral (symmetric directive) with the faintest hawkish lean -- some members acknowledged the next move, if any, would likely be tightening. But this was explicitly not strong enough to warrant an asymmetric directive. Score is just barely above neutral.

**Minority views:** The two dissenters obviously favor a hawkish forward signal, but they were overruled on both the action and the directive language.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 42 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "some members cautioned that...the current expansion differed in important respects from earlier cyclical recoveries and in light of the attendant uncertainties a considerably different result--in either direction--could not be ruled out"
- "considerable uncertainty that continued to surround the outlook for broad money relative to spending"
- "many business contacts were uncertain about the outlook for demand in their own industries or the potential strength of the overall expansion"

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Uncertainty is somewhat elevated. Members note the unusual nature of this recovery, two-sided risks, difficulty predicting prices, and considerable uncertainty around money-spending relationships. This goes beyond routine hedging but is not at the "particularly uncertain" level. The word "considerable" appears multiple times.

**Minority views:** Fairly uniform expression of elevated uncertainty.
