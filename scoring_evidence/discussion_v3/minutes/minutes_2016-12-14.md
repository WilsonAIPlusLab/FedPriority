# FOMC Scoring: 2016-12-14 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Segmented -- participant discussion is clearly delineated from staff content. Quantifier language is used throughout ("participants agreed," "most," "many," "several," "some," "a few," "a couple").

**Era context**: December 2016. The second rate hike of the post-GFC cycle (one year after liftoff in Dec 2015). Economy near full employment, inflation still modestly below 2%. Post-election uncertainty about fiscal policy is a prominent theme. The committee raised the fed funds rate 25bp at this meeting.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target but rising toward it. Headline PCE at 1.4%, core at 1.7%. Market-based inflation compensation "moved up considerably but still were low." "Inflation had increased since earlier in the year but was still below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective." The center of gravity is below target but with improving trajectory. Direction: below 50 but approaching. Score ~43. Polarity check: higher = more pressure. Inflation is below target but increasing -- slightly below midpoint. Correct.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic activity had been expanding at a moderate pace." "Most participants viewed the cumulative progress in the labor market as having brought labor market conditions to or close to those consistent with the Committee's maximum-employment objective." "Almost all anticipating that the unemployment rate over the next couple of years would run below their estimates of its longer-run normal level." The economy is at or very near potential, with expectations of running modestly above. Score ~51. Bright-line rule: at/near potential language with expectation of modest overshoot = 50-53 range.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Most participants viewed the cumulative progress in the labor market as having brought labor market conditions to or close to those consistent with the Committee's maximum-employment objective." Unemployment dropped to 4.6%. "Firms were increasingly finding their job openings hard to fill." "Some participants commented that some businesses in their Districts were experiencing shortages of skilled workers." BUT "aggregate measures of wages were still rising at a subdued pace." Near full employment with pockets of tightness. Score ~52. Anchor: 50 for 2016-17 range.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumer spending had been rising at a moderate rate." Business fixed investment "remained soft" but "the outlook for the business sector improved." Equipment spending flat but orders turning up. Housing "firming." Net: moderate consumption, weak-to-improving investment. "Moderate pace" language = at trend. Score ~49. The softness in business investment pulls slightly below trend.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Substantial changes in financial markets: longer-term interest rates increased, dollar strengthened, but equity prices rose and credit spreads narrowed. These are mixed signals -- higher rates tighten, but rising equities and narrower spreads loosen. Net effect: roughly neutral to slightly looser given the equity/credit signal. The minutes note these changes were attributed to expectations of expansionary fiscal policy. Score ~42. Conditions still broadly accommodative with mixed cross-currents.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Participants agreed that sufficient evidence had accumulated... to warrant an increase of 25 basis points." "Most participants judged that a gradual pace of rate increases was likely to be appropriate." "Many participants judged that the risk of a sizable undershooting of the longer-run normal unemployment rate had increased somewhat and that the Committee might need to raise the federal funds rate more quickly than currently anticipated." This is a hiking meeting with gradual forward lean and some participants flagging potential need for faster pace. Score ~65. Anchor: 60-65 for Dec 2015 liftoff; this is one year later with a similar but slightly more confident stance.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Participants agreed" on the 25bp hike -- no dissent mentioned. There are differing views on the pace going forward (some want gradual, many flag risk of needing faster), and different views on inflation risks and labor market implications. But these are moderate differences about outlook, not sharp disagreements on action. Score ~25. Broad agreement with minor emphasis differences.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Financial conditions discussed as context (equity prices, credit spreads, dollar, interest rates), but not framed as stability concern. "Financial vulnerabilities in some foreign economies" mentioned as a downside risk. Brief, contextual. Score ~25. Limited discussion, mentioned as context not as central worry.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Most participants judged that a gradual pace of rate increases was likely to be appropriate." "Many participants judged that... the Committee might need to raise the federal funds rate more quickly than currently anticipated." "Several participants noted circumstances that might warrant changes to the path." Forward signal is gradual tightening with upside risk to the pace. Score ~68. Mild-to-moderate tightening bias with emphasis on data dependence.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants emphasized their considerable uncertainty about the timing, size, and composition of any future fiscal and other economic policy initiatives." "Members agreed that there was heightened uncertainty about possible changes in fiscal and other economic policies." "Many participants emphasized that the greater uncertainty about these policies made it more challenging to communicate." The word "considerable uncertainty" is used explicitly. Score ~52. Elevated but focused on fiscal policy uncertainty; economic outlook itself described as "roughly balanced."

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (51) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (52): Both near 50 -- consistent, economy at/near potential.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (65) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (68): Aligned -- current hike with gradual further tightening expected.

### Quantifier distribution:
- "participants agreed" / "all": used for the rate decision and balanced near-term risks
- "most": labor market at/near max employment; gradual pace appropriate
- "many": need for caution on financial market implications; risk of undershooting unemployment; uncertainty about policies
- "several": upside risks to growth; downside risks from dollar; faster pace possibility
- "some": shortages; wage subdued; moderate undershooting benign
- "a few": unemployment decline might reverse; other inflation readings

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 43 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Inflation had increased since earlier in the year but was still below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective, partly reflecting earlier declines in energy prices and in prices of non-energy imports."
- "Core PCE price inflation had also moved up moderately over the past year, and, over the 12 months ending in October, it was 1.7 percent."
- "Market-based measures of inflation compensation had moved up considerably... but still were low."

Center-of-gravity: Participants see inflation as below target but on a rising trajectory toward 2%, with improving inflation compensation. The "still below" language keeps this below 50.

Minority views: "Several... pointed out that market-based measures of inflation compensation were still low or that downside risks to inflation remained." Some saw upside risks from fiscal policy and oil prices. Band reflects this two-way dispersion.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 51 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Economic activity had been expanding at a moderate pace since midyear."
- "Most participants viewed the cumulative progress in the labor market as having brought labor market conditions to or close to those consistent with the Committee's maximum-employment objective."
- "Almost all anticipating that the unemployment rate over the next couple of years would run below their estimates of its longer-run normal level."

Center-of-gravity: Economy essentially at potential with modest expectation of running slightly above. "Moderate pace" growth and near-full employment signal balanced output.

Minority views: "Several anticipated a more substantial undershoot" of the unemployment rate, suggesting some saw more above-potential conditions ahead.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 52 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Most participants viewed the cumulative progress in the labor market as having brought labor market conditions to or close to those consistent with the Committee's maximum-employment objective."
- "National surveys reported that job availability was high and that firms were increasingly finding their job openings hard to fill."
- "Aggregate measures of wages were still rising at a subdued pace, suggesting that upward pressure on wages had not become widespread."

Center-of-gravity: Labor market at or very near full employment. Pockets of tightness (skilled worker shortages) but subdued wage growth moderates the signal. Near NAIRU.

Minority views: Some reported worker shortages and need for higher wages; others noted subdued aggregate wage growth. Band reflects this.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 49 +/-5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Consumer spending had been rising at a moderate rate, on balance, since midyear."
- "Nonresidential investment was still weak and equipment spending had been flat in the third quarter."
- "The outlook for the business sector improved over the intermeeting period... orders for nondefense capital goods and the number of drilling rigs in operation had both turned up recently."

Center-of-gravity: Moderate consumer spending at trend, offset by still-weak business investment. Improving outlook but current levels are mixed. Net position is approximately at trend.

Minority views: "A couple of participants reported plans for a pickup in capital spending"; agricultural sector "remained depressed." Modest dispersion.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 42 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Most participants attributed the substantial changes in financial market conditions over the intermeeting period -- including the increase in longer-term interest rates, the strengthening of the dollar, the rise in equity prices, and the narrowing of credit spreads -- to expectations for more expansionary fiscal policies."
- "The stance of policy would remain accommodative."

Center-of-gravity: Mixed signals -- higher long-term rates and stronger dollar tighten conditions, but rising equities and narrower credit spreads loosen them. Net: somewhat loose to neutral, with policy still described as accommodative.

Minority views: "Many participants expressed the need for caution in evaluating the implications of recent financial market developments." Uncertainty about net impact widens the band.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 65 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Participants agreed that sufficient evidence had accumulated of continued progress toward the Committee's objectives... to warrant an increase of 25 basis points."
- "Most participants judged that a gradual pace of rate increases was likely to be appropriate."
- "Many participants judged that the risk of a sizable undershooting of the longer-run normal unemployment rate had increased somewhat and that the Committee might need to raise the federal funds rate more quickly than currently anticipated."

Center-of-gravity: Unanimous support for 25bp hike with gradual further tightening expected. Many flagged possible need for faster pace, adding to hawkish lean. But "gradual" remains the baseline.

Minority views: "Some members saw... only modest risk of a scenario in which an undershooting of the longer-run normal rate of unemployment would create a sharp acceleration in prices" -- a more dovish minority cautioning against overreaction. "Several members noted that if the labor market appeared to be tightening significantly more than expected, it might become necessary to adjust... consistent with the possibility that a less gradual pace of increases could become appropriate" -- hawkish minority.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 25 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:

- Differences on forward path: "many" flagged risk of faster pace vs. "some" who saw modest risk of undershooting.
- No formal dissents mentioned.

Center-of-gravity: Broad agreement on action with moderate differences about the pace and risks going forward. No sharp divisions.

Minority views: Forward-looking disagreement on pace (gradual vs. potentially faster) but this is about emphasis, not fundamental splits.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 25 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- Financial market changes discussed as economic context (rates, dollar, equities, credit spreads) but not framed as stability concern.
- "Financial vulnerabilities in some foreign economies" noted as a downside risk.
- "Many participants expressed the need for caution in evaluating the implications of recent financial market developments."

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions discussed as backdrop for economic outlook, not as a stability worry. Limited emphasis relative to economic fundamentals.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 68 +/-7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Most participants judged that a gradual pace of rate increases was likely to be appropriate."
- "Many participants judged that... the Committee might need to raise the federal funds rate more quickly than currently anticipated."
- "A couple of participants expressed concern that the Committee's communications about a gradual pace of policy firming might be misunderstood as a commitment to only one or two rate hikes per year."

Center-of-gravity: Clear signal of continued gradual tightening, with notable upside risk to the pace. The forward signal is moderately hawkish.

Minority views: Some emphasized downside inflation risks and the benign nature of modest undershooting, pulling the forward signal slightly less hawkish.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 52 +/-6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Participants emphasized their considerable uncertainty about the timing, size, and composition of any future fiscal and other economic policy initiatives."
- "Members agreed that there was heightened uncertainty about possible changes in fiscal and other economic policies."
- "Near-term risks to the economic outlook appeared roughly balanced."

Center-of-gravity: Elevated uncertainty driven primarily by fiscal policy uncertainty post-election. However, the economic outlook itself is described as roughly balanced with modest changes to forecasts. "Considerable uncertainty" is genuine but narrowly focused on one factor.

Minority views: Broadly shared concern about fiscal uncertainty; less dispersion here.
