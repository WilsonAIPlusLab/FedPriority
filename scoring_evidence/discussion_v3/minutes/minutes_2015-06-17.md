# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2015-06-17

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Post-2009 segmented format with clear participant sections.

**Era context**: June 2015 -- mid-recovery period. The economy was rebounding from a weak Q1 (partly weather/seasonal adjustment issues). The Committee was in pre-liftoff deliberations, having held rates at the zero lower bound since December 2008. Inflation was running persistently below 2%. The labor market was steadily improving but had not yet reached full employment by most participants' assessment.

**Quantifier distribution**: "most" appears several times (judging further progress needed in labor market, conditions for firming not yet met). "Many" used for consumer spending outlook support, concern about Greece, need for additional information. "Several" used for uncertainty about Greece/China, labor trends, wage subdued remarks. "Some" used for various minority views. "A few" / "one or two" appear for outlier hawkish positions. "All" used only for the implementation note proposal.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW target. "Total PCE inflation continued to run below the Committee's objective." "Core inflation was still well well below 2 percent." Energy prices stabilizing, dollar stabilizing -- reducing downward pressure. Several noted 3-month core firming and alternative measures running higher. A couple worried low inflation might persist and feed into expectations. Center of gravity: inflation is below target but with some signs of stabilization. This is solidly in the 30-40 range. Score ~35. Higher = more pressure; evidence says below target with mild stabilization signals. Matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as BELOW potential but improving. Q1 was weak (transitory factors), second half expected to be moderate growth. "Underutilization of labor resources diminished somewhat" but "most participants judged that further progress would be required to eliminate underutilization." The economy is approaching potential but not there yet. This maps to 41-49 range ("slightly below potential; approaching full capacity"). Score ~44. Bright-line check: "further progress would be required to eliminate underutilization" = below potential, score < 50. Confirmed.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Below full employment but closing. "Most participants judged that further progress would be required to eliminate underutilization of labor resources." "Several other participants indicated that, in their view, labor market slack had already been largely eliminated." "Some of them anticipated that the utilization gap would close around the end of the year." Wage increases firming but "even with the recent upturn, wage increases remain subdued." Center of gravity: approaching full employment but not there yet, with a hawkish minority seeing it already there. Score ~43. Gotcha check: even though job creation is strong, scoring the LEVEL of tightness, not rate of improvement. Matches.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending was "rising at a moderate pace" in Q1 with "strong rebound in motor vehicle sales" and "solid gain in retail sales in May." Many participants anticipated "a solid pace of consumer spending going forward." Housing starts and permits "rose considerably" but still below trend. Manufacturing mixed -- some stabilization but drags from energy/dollar. Business investment outlook "remained soft." Net: moderate consumer spending, mixed investment. Score ~48. At-trend to slightly below.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Several participants commented on rise in 10-year Treasury yield. Volatility in U.S. fixed-income was "still somewhat below pre-crisis levels." Many expressed concern about Greece spillovers. Housing credit "still relatively tight." Overall, conditions are roughly neutral with some specific areas of tightness. Score ~45. Somewhat loose to neutral given the low-rate environment but with housing credit tight and some yield rise.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Most participants judged that the conditions for policy firming had not yet been achieved." "Many participants emphasized that... they would need additional information." "Some participants viewed the economic conditions for increasing the target range... as having been met or were confident that they would be met shortly." One member indicated readiness to raise rates at this meeting. Committee concluded conditions "had not yet been met." This is pre-liftoff, lean dovish to balanced with building hawkish momentum. Anchor: 40-50 for 2014-15 pre-liftoff. Score ~48. The majority favors holding but the discussion shows they are actively approaching the point of liftoff.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One member indicated readiness to raise at this meeting. Most agreed to hold. "A couple of members indicated that they viewed the unemployment rate as very close or essentially identical to its mandate-consistent level." There is moderate disagreement -- a clear minority wants to go but the majority holds firm. The disagreement extends to the assessment of labor market conditions. Score ~35. Broad agreement with notable minority views; 0 formal dissents.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Several commented on Treasury yield rise. Many expressed concern about Greece spillovers. Some reiterated importance of Committee communications to avoid outsized market reactions. One mention of "adverse consequences for financial and macroeconomic stability." Moderate but not dominant discussion. Score ~35. Limited to moderate discussion by several participants.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is tilting toward tightening. "Members thus saw economic conditions as continuing to approach those consistent with warranting a start to the normalization." Data-dependent posture with building tightening bias. "Many expected that labor market underutilization would be largely eliminated around year-end." Several indicated Committee "should remain data dependent." Anchor: 25-40 for 2015 pre-liftoff. Score ~45. Leaning toward tightening but not yet committing.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Participants generally saw the risks... as balanced, they gave a number of reasons to be cautious." Several mentioned Greece uncertainty and China growth. Uncertainty about productivity trends. "Some members were more uncertain about the extent of progress in the labor market." Somewhat elevated uncertainty with specific identifiable risk factors. Score ~40.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (44) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (43): Both below 50, consistent -- economy below potential, labor market not yet at full employment. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (48) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (45): Both near 50, leaning slightly dovish to balanced with tightening approaching. Coherent -- holding now but signaling approaching normalization.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Total PCE inflation continued to run below the Committee's objective."
- "Core inflation was still well below 2 percent."
- "Several participants pointed out that the 3-month change in that index had firmed recently, signaling some improvement in the inflation outlook."

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation is running below target, with energy price and dollar stabilization reducing downward pressure, but core remains well below 2%. Participants expect a gradual rise back toward target.

**Minority views**: A couple concerned low inflation might persist and feed into expectations (more dovish on inflation); several noted firming signals in alternative measures (suggesting somewhat less concern).

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 44 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity was expanding moderately after little change in the first quarter of the year."
- "Most participants judged that further progress would be required to eliminate underutilization of labor resources."
- "Participants generally expected real GDP to expand at a rate sufficient to continue to move labor market conditions toward levels judged consistent with the Committee's dual mandate."

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is operating modestly below potential, recovering from a weak Q1, with moderate growth expected to close the remaining gap.

**Minority views**: Some saw the economy as potentially lacking momentum; others saw labor market strength signaling stronger bounceback.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 43 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Most participants judged that further progress would be required to eliminate underutilization of labor resources; some of them anticipated that the utilization gap would close around the end of the year."
- "Several other participants indicated that, in their view, labor market slack had already been largely eliminated."
- "Even with the recent upturn, wage increases remain subdued."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market improving steadily but most see remaining slack. Wages firming but still subdued. Approaching but not yet at full employment.

**Minority views**: Several participants believed slack was largely eliminated (pushing score higher); some were concerned about slow improvement (pulling lower). Wider uncertainty band reflects this spread.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 48 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending was somewhat better than previously reported, rising at a moderate pace in the first quarter."
- "Many participants anticipated that these factors would support a solid pace of consumer spending going forward."
- "The outlook for business investment remained soft, and net exports were likely to continue to be restrained."

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending at a moderate pace with positive fundamentals, but business investment soft and net exports weak. Net position is roughly at trend with mixed signals across components.

**Minority views**: Others concerned consumers not responding to energy price drop as expected.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 45 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Several participants commented on the rise in the 10-year Treasury yield."
- "Volatility in U.S. fixed-income markets was still somewhat below pre-crisis levels."
- "Credit availability was still relatively tight" (housing context).
- "Many participants expressed concern that a failure of Greece and its official creditors to resolve their differences could result in disruptions in financial markets."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are roughly neutral to slightly loose overall. Treasury yields rose but not dramatically. Housing credit remains tight. Greece risk looms as potential tightening catalyst.

**Minority views**: None clearly articulated in opposing direction.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 48 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Most participants judged that the conditions for policy firming had not yet been achieved; a number of them cautioned against a premature decision."
- "Some participants viewed the economic conditions for increasing the target range for the federal funds rate as having been met or were confident that they would be met shortly."
- "One member, however, indicated a readiness to take that step at this meeting."

**Center-of-gravity**: Majority favors continued hold with data-dependent approach, but the Committee is clearly approaching the point of normalization. Lean is slightly dovish to balanced with growing hawkish wing.

**Minority views**: Some ready to hike now or soon -- creates notable upside to the uncertainty band.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All members but one indicated that they would need to see more evidence."
- "One member, however, indicated a readiness to take that step at this meeting but also expressed a willingness to wait another meeting or two."
- "Most members saw room for additional progress in reducing labor market slack, while a couple of members indicated that they viewed the unemployment rate as very close or essentially identical to its mandate-consistent level."

**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate differences, with one near-dissenter ready to hike and a couple seeing labor market already at mandate-consistent levels. No formal dissent but meaningful disagreement about the assessment of conditions.

**Minority views**: The "some" who saw conditions as met represents a meaningful minority creating moderate tension.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 35 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Many participants expressed concern that a failure of Greece and its official creditors to resolve their differences could result in disruptions in financial markets in the euro area, with possible spillover effects on the United States."
- "Some participants reiterated the importance of effective Committee communications in reducing the likelihood of an outsized financial market reaction."
- One mention of "adverse consequences for financial and macroeconomic stability" as risk of delayed normalization.

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability receives limited but meaningful discussion, primarily through the Greece risk lens and communication around liftoff. Not a dominant topic but addressed by several participants.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 45 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Members thus saw economic conditions as continuing to approach those consistent with warranting a start to the normalization of the stance of monetary policy."
- "Many expected that labor market underutilization would be largely eliminated around year-end."
- "Several indicating that the Committee should remain data dependent in making adjustments to the target range."

**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal is data-dependent with a building tightening bias. The language clearly points toward approaching normalization but does not commit to a specific timeline. Neutral with mild tightening lean.

**Minority views**: Some ready to move imminently vs. others wanting considerable more evidence.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 40 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "While participants generally saw the risks to their projections of economic activity and the labor market as balanced, they gave a number of reasons to be cautious in assessing the outlook."
- "Several mentioned their uncertainty about whether Greece and its official creditors would reach an agreement and about the likely pace of economic growth abroad, particularly in China."
- "Other concerns were related to whether the apparent weakness in productivity growth recently would be reversed or continue."

**Center-of-gravity**: Risks seen as balanced overall but with specific identifiable concerns (Greece, China, productivity, Q1 interpretation) elevating uncertainty somewhat above normal.
