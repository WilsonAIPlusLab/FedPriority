# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2012-12-12 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized participant views). Segmented — participant discussion section is clearly separated from staff content. Quantifier language is used throughout ("most," "many," "several," "a few," etc.).

**Era context**: December 2012. Mid-recovery from the GFC. Unemployment still elevated (~7.7%). QE3 (MBS purchases) had launched in September 2012. The maturity extension program (Operation Twist) was ending. Fiscal cliff negotiations were the dominant source of uncertainty. Inflation was running below the 2% target.

**Quantifier distribution**: "many" used frequently (economic pace, household balance sheets, housing, deleveraging); "most" (inflation risks balanced, favored thresholds); "several" (hiring rates depressed, noted thresholds transition); "a few" (financial imbalance concerns, labor force exits, fiscal breakdown risk, preferences for qualitative guidance, views on asset purchase duration); "almost all" (fiscal uncertainty affecting activity); "one participant" used for individual outlier views.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW target. "Consumer price inflation had been running somewhat below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent." "Incoming information pointed to stable, low inflation that was running a little below the Committee's longer-run goal." "Compensation pressures had remained subdued." "Most participants saw the risks to the inflation outlook as broadly balanced." "Many noted that longer-term inflation expectations were well anchored." This is clearly below target but not alarmingly so — "somewhat below," "a little below." Direction: below midpoint. Magnitude: "somewhat" = 5-10 points below 50. Score ~38. Anchors: 38-42 for 2013-19 below target; this is late 2012, similar environment. Score 38.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy as BELOW potential. "Economic activity and employment continued to expand at a moderate pace." "The unemployment rate had declined somewhat since the summer but remained elevated." "Growth in economic activity continued to be restrained by several persistent headwinds." Fiscal cliff uncertainty described as "a major headwind." "Without sufficient policy accommodation, economic growth might not be strong enough to generate sustained improvement in labor market conditions." The recovery is ongoing but clearly below potential with "substantial" headwinds. Not deep recession, but persistent slack. Anchors: 35-42 for 2010-14 recovery. Score 37.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see the labor market as BELOW full employment. "The unemployment rate remained elevated." "Rates of hiring remained depressed relative to those observed before the financial crisis." "Part of the decline in unemployment in November was attributable to a drop in labor force participation." Only "a couple of participants" noted vacancies suggesting some tightness in specific areas. Center of gravity is clearly slack labor market. Anchors: 35-40 for 2011-14. Score 35.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Participants see spending as BELOW trend but mixed. "A recent drop in consumer sentiment and a softening in consumer spending." "Growth in business fixed investment had slowed." Firms "postponed plans to expand capacity." However: "purchases of autos and other durables remained relatively strong," household balance sheets improving, housing sector recovering. Net: below trend with some pockets of strength. "Household spending had continued to advance" but "growth in business sector had slowed." Anchors: 35-42 for 2011-13 "moderate." Score 38.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Mixed signals. "Still-tight credit conditions for some borrowers" and "ongoing deleveraging." But "firms' balance sheets were generally strong and liquidity was ample." "A few participants, observing that low interest rates had increased the demand for riskier financial products." Financial conditions are somewhat loose due to QE but credit still tight for some. Net: slightly below neutral — accommodative overall but with pockets of tightness. Score 40. Direction: somewhat loose to neutral, reflecting QE-driven accommodation but persistent deleveraging headwinds.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Strongly dovish. "All members but one judged that continued provision of monetary accommodation was warranted." Committee authorized $40B/month MBS + $45B/month Treasuries. Adopting quantitative thresholds (Evans Rule) for forward guidance — this is a dovish innovation. "Most participants favored replacing the calendar-date forward guidance with economic thresholds." "A few members expressed the view that ongoing asset purchases would likely be warranted until about the end of 2013." However, "a number of participants expressed the concern that additional purchases could complicate the Committee's efforts to eventually withdraw monetary policy accommodation." One dissent. Per rubric: QE discussion IS the dovish signal, placing this in 15-25 range. Score 20.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal dissent ("all members but one"). Some range of views on asset purchase pace and duration — "a few" wanted purchases until end of 2013, "a few others" [text truncated but implied different view]. "A few" preferred qualitative over quantitative guidance. But "most" favored thresholds; broad agreement on overall direction. Anchors: 25-35 for typical one-dissent meeting. Score 32.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Moderate attention. "A few participants, observing that low interest rates had increased the demand for riskier financial products, pointed to the possibility that holding interest rates low for a prolonged period could lead to financial imbalances and imprudent risk-taking." One participant referenced historical episodes. One mentioned money market mutual fund reform. "Participants noted that the Committee would need to continue to assess whether large purchases were having adverse effects on market functioning and financial stability." This is limited discussion — a few participants, not the center of gravity. Anchors: 20-35 for 2013-19 typical. Score 30.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Very dovish. This meeting introduced the Evans Rule — threshold-based forward guidance tying rate increases to 6.5% unemployment and 2.5% inflation. "Most participants favored replacing the calendar-date forward guidance with economic thresholds." This is the strongest form of easing commitment (threshold-based guidance promising near-zero rates). Anchors: 3-8 for 2012-13 Evans Rule/QE3 thresholds. Score 5.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: Elevated. "Almost all indicated that heightened uncertainty about fiscal policy probably was affecting economic activity adversely." "Business contacts in many parts of the country were also said to be highly uncertain about the outlook for U.S. fiscal policy." "Considerable uncertainty surrounded the relationship between unemployment and inflation." "Benefits of ongoing purchases were uncertain." However, most of this uncertainty is about fiscal cliff, not deep macro/model uncertainty. Anchors: 40-50 for Brexit, trade tensions level. Score 50.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (37) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (35): Both below 50, consistent — economy below potential, labor market slack. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (20) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (5): Both strongly dovish. The lean is very dovish (QE expansion) and forward guidance is maximally dovish (Evans Rule thresholds). The gap is explained: forward guidance is even more dovish because the Evans Rule introduction is one of the strongest forms of commitment to low rates. Coherent.

### Polarity verification:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says below target, subdued. Score 38 < 50. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says below potential with slack. Score 37 < 50. Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says elevated unemployment, slack. Score 35 < 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says mixed but generally accommodative. Score 40 < 50. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says strongly dovish (QE expansion, Evans Rule). Score 20. Correct.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence says threshold-based easing commitment. Score 5. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Consumer price inflation had been running somewhat below the Committee's longer-run objective of 2 percent." "Incoming information pointed to stable, low inflation that was running a little below the Committee's longer-run goal." "Compensation pressures had remained subdued, and unit labor costs were little changed." "Most participants saw the risks to the inflation outlook as broadly balanced."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Inflation perceived as modestly below the 2% target with subdued wage pressures, but not dangerously low — stable and anchored expectations.
- **Minority views**: One participant expressed concern about "considerable uncertainty" in the unemployment-inflation relationship, suggesting inflation might not stay restrained. Narrow uncertainty band as most agreed.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 37 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Economic activity and employment continued to expand at a moderate pace." "Growth in economic activity continued to be restrained by several persistent headwinds." "Without sufficient policy accommodation, economic growth might not be strong enough to generate sustained improvement in labor market conditions."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Economy operating below potential with moderate expansion and persistent headwinds from fiscal uncertainty, deleveraging, and global slowdown.
- **Minority views**: "A number of participants suggested that the business sector was well positioned to expand spending and hiring quickly upon a positive resolution of the fiscal cliff negotiations," suggesting upside potential. Does not change current assessment.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "The unemployment rate remained elevated." "Several pointed to indicators suggesting that rates of hiring remained depressed relative to those observed before the financial crisis." "Part of the decline in unemployment in November was attributable to a drop in labor force participation."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Labor market has improved but remains well below full employment with elevated unemployment and depressed hiring rates.
- **Minority views**: "A couple of participants noted that vacancies remained at a high level... suggesting that at least some firms were having a hard time finding suitable workers." This signals modest structural tightness but is a minority observation.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 38 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Many participants noted a recent drop in consumer sentiment and a softening in consumer spending." "Growth in business fixed investment had slowed." "Firms... had shifted toward a higher proportion of part-time employees and postponed plans to expand capacity." But "household spending had continued to advance" and "purchases of autos and other durables remained relatively strong."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Spending below trend with fiscal uncertainty weighing on investment and consumer confidence, but not contracting — "moderate pace" with pockets of strength.
- **Minority views**: Some reported strong holiday activity and improving household balance sheets, widening the band modestly.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 40 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Still-tight credit conditions for some borrowers." "Many noted the ongoing deleveraging in the private nonfinancial sector." But "firms' balance sheets were generally strong and liquidity was ample." "Low interest rates had increased the demand for riskier financial products."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are somewhat loose overall due to QE-driven accommodation, but residual tightness persists for some borrowers and deleveraging continues. Net: slightly below neutral (somewhat loose to neutral).
- **Minority views**: "A few participants" flagged risk of financial imbalances from prolonged low rates, suggesting conditions may be too easy in some segments.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 20 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "All members but one judged that continued provision of monetary accommodation was warranted." Committee authorized $40B MBS + $45B Treasury purchases per month. Adopted Evans Rule thresholds. "Various members stressed the importance of a continuing assessment of labor market developments."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Strongly dovish — expanding QE and introducing threshold-based forward guidance. Per rubric, active QE expansion discussion places this in the 15-25 range.
- **Minority views**: One dissent. "A number of participants expressed the concern that additional purchases could complicate the Committee's efforts to eventually withdraw monetary policy accommodation." But this is a caution about risks, not advocacy for tightening.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 32 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "All members but one" (one formal dissent). Range of views on purchase pace/duration: "a few members expressed the view that ongoing asset purchases would likely be warranted until about the end of 2013, while a few others emphasized" [different timeline — text truncated]. "A few" preferred qualitative over quantitative guidance.
- **Center-of-gravity**: One dissent with moderate range of views on implementation details, but broad agreement on direction. Typical one-dissent meeting with some tactical disagreement.
- **Minority views**: The debate over quantitative vs. qualitative thresholds and the range of views on purchase duration add some dispersion.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 30 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "A few participants, observing that low interest rates had increased the demand for riskier financial products, pointed to the possibility that holding interest rates low for a prolonged period could lead to financial imbalances and imprudent risk-taking." "Participants noted that the Committee would need to continue to assess whether large purchases were having adverse effects on market functioning and financial stability."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Limited but present discussion — a few participants raised financial stability concerns; it was acknowledged as something to monitor but was not a primary focus.
- **Minority views**: Most participants did not dwell on financial stability; this was raised by a minority.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 5 +/-3 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Most participants favored replacing the calendar-date forward guidance with economic thresholds" of 6.5% unemployment and 2.5% inflation. "Thresholds could increase the probability that market reactions to economic developments would move longer-term interest rates in a manner consistent with the Committee's view." This is threshold-based guidance — the rubric's strongest form of easing commitment.
- **Center-of-gravity**: Introduction of the Evans Rule represents maximum dovish forward guidance — threshold-based commitment to near-zero rates.
- **Minority views**: "A few" preferred qualitative guidance, and "a few" worried thresholds could be misinterpreted as triggers, but the center strongly favored thresholds.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 50 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- **Evidence**: "Almost all indicated that heightened uncertainty about fiscal policy probably was affecting economic activity adversely." "Business contacts in many parts of the country were also said to be highly uncertain about the outlook." "The benefits of ongoing purchases were uncertain and that the potential costs could rise." "Considerable uncertainty surrounded the relationship between unemployment and inflation."
- **Center-of-gravity**: Elevated uncertainty, primarily driven by fiscal cliff negotiations and their potential economic impact, plus uncertainty about QE efficacy. Multiple dimensions of uncertainty discussed by many participants.
- **Minority views**: Uncertainty is broadly shared — almost all participants flagged fiscal policy uncertainty.
