# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1993-09-21 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format -- staff and participant content intermixed. I will score ONLY portions with participant language cues ("members commented," "members agreed," "some members," etc.).

**Era context**: September 1993. The U.S. economy is in a moderate expansion following the 1990-91 recession. The fed funds rate is at 3%, which is very low by historical standards. Inflation had surged early in the year but moderated. Unemployment is at 6.7% -- still elevated. Deficit-reduction legislation recently passed. Long-term interest rates have declined considerably.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all" (policy stance unchanged, symmetrical directive), "members generally" (subdued price pressures, moderate growth), "many" (favorable price developments), "some" (disinflationary trend, policy firming concerns, policy easing concerns), "a few" (equity market overvaluation concern), "one member" (M2 adjusted growth, inner city concerns).

**Dimension-by-dimension reasoning**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Members see inflation as below target / subdued. "Many members referred to the more favorable price developments." "Members generally agreed that price pressures were likely to remain subdued given their projections of some continuing slack in resource utilization." Early-year surge acknowledged but recent trend favorable. "Costs of materials... rising only slowly, if at all." Worker benefit costs rising rapidly is a partial offset. Direction: below midpoint. "Subdued" = 26-40 range. Slack keeping prices in check, but with some uncertainty about whether disinflationary trend is established. Score ~35.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: Members describe "moderate and sustained expansion." Economy growing but with "some continuing slack in resource utilization." "Business conditions remained uneven across local areas." "Business conditions continued to be quite weak in some areas, notably in California." Growth is moderate, but slack persists. Direction: below potential. Bright-line rule: "slack remains" language means < 50. The economy is expanding moderately but has not closed the gap. Score ~42.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 6.7% (mentioned in directive). "Appreciable growth in employment thus far this year" but "concerns about employment and income prospects" among consumers. "Indications of fairly tight labor markets in some areas, but wage pressures remained limited even in those markets." Mixed -- some pockets of tightness but overall still slack. Direction: below full employment. Score ~38.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "somewhat mixed" but expected to provide "continued if not strong support." Consumer attitudes "remained hesitant." Housing "quite strong in many parts of the country" with "appreciable and widespread strengthening in demand for single-family housing." Business fixed investment "expected to continue to provide considerable support." Equipment spending anticipated to grow "appreciably." But capacity expansion "likely to remain relatively limited." Net: moderate, slightly below trend due to consumer hesitancy offset by housing and equipment strength. Score ~47.

5. **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Current monetary policy continued quite accommodative." "Short-term interest rates were low, indeed close to zero after adjustment for inflation." "Appreciable further declines in longer-term interest rates." "Bond and stock markets had exhibited considerable strength." Banking institutions "in a better position to accommodate increases in loan demand." Balance sheets improved. Direction: conditions are loose/accommodative. Score ~22.

6. **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All of the members agreed" on unchanged policy. Unanimous vote for symmetrical directive. "Some members were concerned the current stance... was quite accommodative and probably would need to be firmed at some point." "Other members... foresaw the need to maintain an accommodative policy posture and could not rule out the possibility that the next policy move might have to be toward greater monetary stimulus." The center of gravity is hold steady with no lean -- truly symmetrical. The "some" vs "other" split means balanced. But the fact that policy is very accommodative and some see eventual firming suggests a very slight hawkish lean is building. Score ~48 (essentially balanced to very mild dovish lean given the symmetrical directive and active accommodation).

7. **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Unanimous vote (12-0). All endorsed symmetrical directive. But there IS notable disagreement about direction of NEXT move -- "some" want firming eventually while "others" could not rule out easing. This is internal philosophical disagreement but expressed within a consensus framework. Score ~25.

8. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: "A few members commented on the apparently growing concern in financial markets that current equity prices were high relative to earnings and dividends." Discussion of mutual fund risks, but brief (one paragraph). Balance sheet improvements discussed for their macro implications. For 1993, this is a bit more than typical. Anchor: 1-10 for 1993-96 typical. The equity market discussion pushes it slightly above that range. Score ~15.

9. **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Symmetrical directive. "Both the timing and, in the view of at least some members, the direction of the next policy change could not be foreseen at this time." No directional signal. Truly neutral/data-dependent. Score ~50.

10. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The economic outlook clearly remained subject to a variety of uncertainties, including potential developments abroad that were especially difficult to predict." "Difficulty of reconciling the appreciable growth in employment thus far this year with the slow expansion in measured output." "The inflation outlook remained subject to considerable uncertainty." Multiple sources of uncertainty acknowledged: productivity puzzle, foreign developments, inflation trajectory. "Considerable uncertainty" = elevated. Score ~45.

**Polarity checks**:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 35: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says "subdued," "slack keeping prices in check." Low score matches. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP 42: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says "slack remains." Below 50. Correct.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS 38: Higher = tighter. Evidence says unemployment still elevated, some pockets of tightness. Below midpoint. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS 22: Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence says "quite accommodative," near-zero real rates, declining long rates. Low score = loose. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 48: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says balanced with slight dovish lean (maintaining accommodation). Score slightly below 50. Correct.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE 50: Neutral, no directional signal. Correct.

**Cross-dimension coherence**:
- OUTPUT_GAP (42) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (38): Both below 50, consistent with slack remaining. Labor slightly lower reflecting that employment growth hasn't fully translated to tightness. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (48) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (50): Both near midpoint. Balanced/hold stance with no forward lean. Coherent.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (22) is low (loose) while HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (48) is near balanced: accommodative conditions but no urgency to tighten because slack remains. Coherent for early-mid recovery.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 35 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Many members referred to the more favorable price developments that had occurred since the early part of the year when key measures of inflation had surged."
- "The members generally agreed that price pressures were likely to remain subdued given their projections of some continuing slack in resource utilization."
- "The costs of materials purchased by business firms generally were reported to be rising only slowly, if at all."
- "The costs of worker benefits continued to rise fairly rapidly."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Members see inflation as subdued and likely to remain so given slack, though some lingering uncertainty and benefit cost pressures prevent a lower score.

**Minority views**: "Some members suggested that the prospects for continued slack in resource utilization were consistent with a disinflationary trend" -- a few may see even less pressure than the center.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 42 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Members commented that recent developments had not altered their outlook for moderate and sustained expansion in economic activity."
- "Their projections of some continuing slack in resource utilization."
- "Business conditions remained uneven across local areas and industries... ranging from little change to moderate growth."
- "Business conditions continued to be quite weak in some areas, notably in California."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is expanding moderately but still below potential with slack remaining. Growth is closing the gap gradually but has not eliminated it.

**Minority views**: Uneven conditions -- some areas weak, others stronger. Uncertainty band reflects regional dispersion.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 38 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The appreciable growth in employment thus far this year" alongside "the civilian unemployment rate declined to 6.7 percent."
- "There were indications of fairly tight labor markets in some areas, but wage pressures remained limited even in those markets."
- "Concerns about employment and income prospects" among consumers.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Labor market is improving from recession but still below full employment. Pockets of tightness exist but do not characterize the overall market, and wage pressures remain limited.

**Minority views**: Regional variation -- some areas tight, others weak. The uncertainty band captures this.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 47 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Members gave somewhat mixed reports on retail sales in recent weeks, but they generally anticipated that consumer spending would provide continued if not strong support to sustained economic expansion."
- "Housing markets were described as quite strong in many parts of the country."
- "Business fixed investment was expected to continue to provide considerable support to the economic expansion."
- "Consumer attitudes remained hesitant."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Spending is roughly at trend -- consumer spending hesitant but persistent, housing strengthening notably, business equipment investment solid. Offsets from fiscal restraint and weak exports keep the net assessment near trend.

**Minority views**: Uneven retail reports and regional variation contribute to the uncertainty band.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 22 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The current monetary policy continued quite accommodative. Short-term interest rates were low, indeed close to zero after adjustment for inflation, and there had been appreciable further declines in longer-term interest rates."
- "Bond and stock markets had exhibited considerable strength."
- "Banking institutions had bolstered their capital positions and were in a better position to accommodate increases in loan demand."
- "Business firms and consumers had made substantial progress in strengthening their balance sheets."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions are clearly loose/accommodative -- near-zero real short rates, declining long rates, strong asset markets, improved bank and household balance sheets. Well below neutral.

**Minority views**: None significant; broad agreement on accommodative conditions.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 48 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All of the members agreed that recent economic and financial developments pointed to the desirability of an unchanged policy stance."
- "Some members were concerned the current stance... was quite accommodative and probably would need to be firmed at some point."
- "Other members... foresaw the need to maintain an accommodative policy posture and could not rule out the possibility that the next policy move might have to be toward greater monetary stimulus."
- "All of the members endorsed a proposal to retain a symmetrical directive."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Unanimous hold with a truly symmetrical directive. Internal views split: some lean toward eventual firming, others toward potential easing. The center is essentially balanced with a very marginal dovish tilt given the emphasis on maintaining accommodation.

**Minority views**: "Some" lean hawkish (eventual firming needed), "other" members lean dovish (cannot rule out easing). This split widens the uncertainty band.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 25 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Votes for this action: [12 names]. Votes against this action: None."
- Split views on future direction: "some members were concerned... would need to be firmed" vs. "other members... could not rule out the possibility that the next policy move might have to be toward greater monetary stimulus."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Unanimous vote with no dissents, but meaningful philosophical disagreement about the future direction of policy. This is a typical one-or-fewer-dissent meeting with some internal tension.

**Minority views**: The hawk-dove split on future direction is notable but does not produce current disagreement on action.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 15 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "A few members commented on the apparently growing concern in financial markets that current equity prices were high relative to earnings and dividends."
- "A correction in U.S. equity markets could trigger cumulative selling, especially by mutual funds."
- Balance sheet and banking discussion focused more on macro implications than stability per se.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Limited financial stability discussion. The equity valuation concern from "a few members" is notable for 1993 but brief. Most financial discussion is about favorable conditions supporting growth rather than stability risks.

**Minority views**: Only "a few" raised equity concerns; most members focused on the positive aspects of financial conditions.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 50 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Both the timing and, in the view of at least some members, the direction of the next policy change could not be foreseen at this time."
- "All of the members endorsed a proposal to retain a symmetrical directive."
- "Did not include a presumption about the likely direction of any adjustment to policy during the intermeeting period."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Maximally neutral forward guidance -- explicitly no presumption about direction. The symmetrical directive and acknowledgment that even the direction of the next move is unknown define pure neutrality.

**Minority views**: Some lean toward eventual firming, others toward easing, but no directional signal was embedded.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 45 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The economic outlook clearly remained subject to a variety of uncertainties, including potential developments abroad that were especially difficult to predict."
- "The difficulty of reconciling the appreciable growth in employment thus far this year with the slow expansion in measured output."
- "The inflation outlook remained subject to considerable uncertainty."
- "Neither the pace of the economic expansion nor the uncertain progress toward price stability reflected a wholly satisfactory economic performance."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants express elevated uncertainty on multiple fronts -- the productivity puzzle, foreign developments, and inflation trajectory. The word "considerable" is used for inflation uncertainty specifically. This is somewhat above normal for a mid-expansion meeting.

**Minority views**: Uncertainty appears broadly shared rather than concentrated in a minority.
