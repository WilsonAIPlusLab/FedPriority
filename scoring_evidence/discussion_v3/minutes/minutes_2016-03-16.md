# FOMC Meeting Scoring — March 15–16, 2016 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented — participant discussion section clearly separated from staff content.

**Era context**: March 2016 — three months after the first rate hike in December 2015 (liftoff from zero). Global concerns (China slowdown, commodity price decline, financial market turbulence in early 2016) had rattled markets. U.S. economy growing moderately; inflation below 2% target; labor market steadily improving. The Committee held rates steady at this meeting.

**Quantifier distribution**: "most" (used for holding rates, foreign growth slower), "many" (downside risks from global, lower rate path appropriate), "several" (downside risks abroad, cautious approach, concern about inflation expectations), "some" (at or near max employment, inflation firming, inflation expectations stable vs. moving lower, next meeting hike possible), "a few" (upside inflation risks, actions by foreign central banks helpful), "a couple" (preferred to raise rates), "one" (dissent for hike).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants note inflation "picked up in recent months" but "continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective." "Participants continued to anticipate that inflation would run below the Committee's 2 percent objective in the near term." Some saw recent increase as "consistent with a firming trend," some others saw it as "unlikely to be sustained." Several noted "persistence of global disinflationary pressures." Market-based inflation compensation "remained low." Direction: below target but with some recent improvement. Score should be in the 35-42 range (below target, some pickup noted). I'll score 38.

Polarity check: Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says below target but picking up modestly. 38 = below target with some firming. Matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic activity had been expanding moderately." Participants shared assessment that "real GDP would continue to increase at a moderate rate." No language about being "above potential." Some participants judged "current labor market conditions were at or near those consistent with maximum sustainable employment" while "some other participants judged that the economy had not yet reached maximum employment." The bright-line rule: no clear "above potential" language. Mixed views cluster around "near" potential. Score: 48 (just below potential, approaching).

Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Mixed signals, center of gravity is near potential but with some viewing remaining slack. 48 fits.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Strong payroll gains, further tick down in unemployment. "Some participants judged that current labor market conditions were at or near those consistent with maximum sustainable employment, noting that the unemployment rate was at or below their estimates of its longer-run normal level and citing anecdotal reports of labor shortages or increased wage pressures." But "some other participants judged that the economy had not yet reached maximum employment" citing "still-high rate of involuntary part-time employment" and "surprisingly limited extent to which aggregate data indicated upward pressure on wage growth." Center of gravity: near full employment with split views. Score: 49.

Polarity check: Higher = tighter labor market. Split between at/near max employment and some remaining slack. 49 is appropriate — just shy of full tightness.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "increasing at a moderate rate," "motor vehicle sales remained particularly strong," participants "generally expected consumer spending to continue to rise moderately." But "business fixed investment and net exports had been soft," "capital spending plans appeared to remain soft," "sluggish growth would continue" for business investment. Net exports expected to "continue to subtract from real GDP growth." Housing mixed but "fundamentals for housing activity were seen as strong." Net: moderate consumer spending offset by soft investment and weak exports. Score: 44 (slightly below trend due to investment/export drag).

Polarity check: Higher = stronger spending/investment. Consumer spending moderate but investment soft and exports weak. 44 = slightly below trend. Matches.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Financial conditions deteriorated" early in intermeeting period with "equity prices falling and credit spreads on riskier corporate bonds widening." Then "investor sentiment rebounded, and domestic and global financial conditions eased on net." But "longer-term interest rates and market participants' expectations for the future path of the federal funds rate remained lower." Participants "also cited wider credit spreads as a factor that was likely to restrain growth in demand." Many saw "global economic and financial situation still posed appreciable downside risks." Net: conditions had tightened then partially reversed; still somewhat tight relative to before. Score: 55 (somewhat tight; conditions tightened then partially eased but still tighter than pre-turbulence).

Polarity check: Higher = tighter financial conditions. Evidence of tightening that partially reversed but credit spreads still wider. 55 matches.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Most participants judged it appropriate to maintain the target range" at current level. "Many participants expressed the view that a somewhat lower path for the federal funds rate than they had projected in December now seemed most likely to be appropriate." "Several participants also argued for proceeding cautiously in reducing policy accommodation." "Many participants noted that, with the target range for the federal funds rate only slightly above zero, the FOMC continued to have little room to ease" — asymmetry argument for patience. Only "a couple of participants" saw a hike as appropriate; one member dissented for a hike. This is clearly a dovish-leaning hold. Score: 44 (lean dovish — hold with cautious bias, lower projected path).

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says hold with dovish lean, lower projected path. 44 fits in "lean dovish to balanced" range.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "The Committee expected that economic conditions would evolve in a manner that would warrant only gradual increases in the federal funds rate." "Several members noted that their current projections of the path for the federal funds rate that would likely be appropriate this year and next were lower than they had projected in December." "Several expressed the view that a cautious approach to raising rates would be prudent or noted their concern that raising the target range as soon as April would signal a sense of urgency they did not think appropriate." But "some other participants indicated that an increase in the target range at the Committee's next meeting might well be warranted." Forward signal is gradual tightening but with a more cautious/lower pace than previously. Score: 55 (mild tightening bias — still on hiking path but very gradual and lowered).

Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Still in a hiking cycle but signaling patience and lower path. 55 = mild tightening bias. Matches.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal dissent (for a hike). "A couple of participants" preferred a hike. Split views on labor market (at max employment vs. not yet). Range of views on inflation persistence. "Several" vs. "some" on various risk assessments. "Participants expressed a range of views about the likelihood that incoming information would make an adjustment appropriate at the time of their next meeting." Moderate differences with one dissent. Score: 33.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Significant discussion of global financial developments and their implications. "Many participants expressed a view that the global economic and financial situation still posed appreciable downside risks." "Recent financial market turbulence provided an important reminder." Discussion of equity prices falling, credit spreads widening, financial conditions deteriorating then easing. Multiple paragraphs devoted to financial conditions and their interaction with policy. Score: 48 (moderate discussion — several participants address meaningfully).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Global economic and financial developments as continuing to pose risks." "Several participants expressed the view that the underlying factors abroad that led to a sharp, though temporary, deterioration in global financial conditions earlier this year had not been fully resolved and thus posed ongoing downside risks." "Heightened global risks and the asymmetric ability of monetary policy to respond to them warranted caution." Many saw "appreciable downside risks." Explicit discussion of asymmetric policy capacity. But not "uncharted territory" language. Score: 48 (elevated uncertainty, especially around global risks and asymmetry).

### Cross-dimension coherence:
- OUTPUT_GAP (48) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (49): Both near 50, consistent with economy approaching potential. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (44) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (55): Slight divergence — current action is dovish (hold with lower projected path) but forward signal still points to gradual tightening. This is explained by the fact that participants held steady now but still expected gradual hikes ahead, just at a lower pace. The gap is logical.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 38 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Inflation picked up in recent months, but it continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective"
- "Participants continued to anticipate that inflation would run below the Committee's 2 percent objective in the near term"
- "Several participants indicated that the persistence of global disinflationary pressures or the possibility that inflation expectations were moving lower continued to pose downside risks to the inflation outlook"

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation below target with recent pickup noted, but most expect it to remain below 2% near-term with downside risks from global disinflation.

**Minority views**: "Some participants saw the increase as consistent with a firming trend"; "a few others expressed the view that there were also risks that could lead to inflation running higher than anticipated." This widens the band modestly.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 48 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity had been expanding moderately despite the global economic and financial developments"
- "Some participants judged that current labor market conditions were at or near those consistent with maximum sustainable employment"
- "Some other participants judged that the economy had not yet reached maximum employment"

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy near potential, with split views on whether slack remains. Center of gravity is just below potential.

**Minority views**: Some see at/above maximum employment; some see remaining slack. Narrow band reflects views clustering near potential.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 49 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Some participants judged that current labor market conditions were at or near those consistent with maximum sustainable employment, noting that the unemployment rate was at or below their estimates of its longer-run normal level and citing anecdotal reports of labor shortages or increased wage pressures"
- "Some other participants judged that the economy had not yet reached maximum employment... the still-high rate of involuntary part-time employment and the low level of the employment-to-population ratio for prime-age workers"
- "The surprisingly limited extent to which aggregate data indicated upward pressure on wage growth also suggested some remaining slack"

**Center-of-gravity**: Near full employment with genuine split on whether slack remains. Limited wage pressure keeps the score just under 50.

**Minority views**: Clear split between "at/near max employment" camp and "not yet there" camp.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 44 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Household spending had been increasing at a moderate rate" and "motor vehicle sales remained particularly strong"
- "Business fixed investment and net exports had been soft"; "capital spending plans appeared to remain soft"
- "Declines in net exports to continue to subtract from real GDP growth"

**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate consumer spending offset by soft business investment and weak net exports, placing the net slightly below trend.

**Minority views**: Some Districts reported positive commercial construction; housing fundamentals strong. Limited upside variation.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 55 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Financial conditions deteriorated, with equity prices falling and credit spreads on riskier corporate bonds widening. Subsequently, investor sentiment rebounded, and domestic and global financial conditions eased on net over the intermeeting period"
- "Also cited wider credit spreads as a factor that was likely to restrain growth in demand"
- "Longer-term interest rates and market participants' expectations for the future path of the federal funds rate remained lower"

**Center-of-gravity**: Conditions tightened then partially reversed. Net: somewhat tighter than before the turbulence, with credit spreads still wider. Lower rate expectations partially offset.

**Minority views**: "A few noted that the actions taken by several foreign central banks... had helped mitigate downside risks."

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 44 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Most participants judged it appropriate to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at ¼ to ½ percent at this meeting"
- "Many participants expressed the view that a somewhat lower path for the federal funds rate than they had projected in December now seemed most likely to be appropriate"
- "A couple of participants, however, saw an increase in the target range to ½ to ¾ percent as appropriate at this meeting"

**Center-of-gravity**: Hold with dovish lean — most favor patience, many see lower rate path than December. Still in gradual normalization framework but slower pace.

**Minority views**: A couple/one member preferred hike, citing continued expansion and inflation firming. Keeps the score from going lower.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 33 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "One member, however, preferred to raise the target range for the federal funds rate"
- Split views on labor market slack: "some" at max employment vs. "some other" not yet reached
- "Participants expressed a range of views about the likelihood that incoming information would make an adjustment appropriate at the time of their next meeting"

**Center-of-gravity**: One formal dissent with moderate differences on labor market assessment and timing of next move. Typical for the era.

**Minority views**: The single dissenter and a couple who favored hiking represent a visible but small minority.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 48 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Many participants expressed a view that the global economic and financial situation still posed appreciable downside risks to the domestic economic outlook"
- "Recent financial market turbulence provided an important reminder that the ability of central banks to offset the effects of adverse economic shocks might be limited"
- Multiple paragraphs discussing financial conditions, global financial developments, and their implications for policy

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions and global financial risks receive moderate-to-substantial attention, woven throughout the discussion and directly influencing policy deliberations.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 55 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The Committee expected that economic conditions would evolve in a manner that would warrant only gradual increases in the federal funds rate"
- "Several members noted that their current projections of the path for the federal funds rate that would likely be appropriate this year and next were lower than they had projected in December"
- "Several expressed the view that a cautious approach to raising rates would be prudent or noted their concern that raising the target range as soon as April would signal a sense of urgency they did not think appropriate"

**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal is mild tightening — still on a gradual hiking path but at a lower and more cautious pace than previously projected.

**Minority views**: "Some other participants indicated that an increase in the target range at the Committee's next meeting might well be warranted" — somewhat more hawkish forward lean from a minority.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 48 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Global economic and financial developments as continuing to pose risks to the outlook for economic activity and the labor market"
- "The underlying factors abroad that led to a sharp, though temporary, deterioration in global financial conditions earlier this year had not been fully resolved and thus posed ongoing downside risks"
- "The heightened global risks and the asymmetric ability of monetary policy to respond to them warranted caution"

**Center-of-gravity**: Elevated uncertainty driven by global risks, unresolved factors abroad, and asymmetric policy capacity. Beyond normal hedging but not extreme.
