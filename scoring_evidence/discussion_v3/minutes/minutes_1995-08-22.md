# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1995-08-22

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented (pre-2009), so staff and participant content are mixed. I will score only portions with clear participant language cues ("members noted," "members generally viewed," "some members," etc.).

**Era context**: Mid-1995. The Fed had completed a tightening cycle in early 1995 (funds rate raised from 3% to 6% over 1994-95). The economy had slowed notably in the spring of 1995, and the question was whether a soft landing was achievable. Inflation was moderate but not at target. Budget deficit reduction debates were prominent in Congress.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all the members" (unanimous hold vote), "members" (general), "some members" (optimistic on inflation), "others/other members" (concerned about inflation persistence), "several" (questioned export potential), "a number of members" (limited export growth). The document features balanced "some vs. others" splits on inflation outlook specifically.

**Dimension-by-dimension directional reasoning**:

1. **INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Members note the "near-term outlook for inflation as more encouraging," the pause "eased pressures on resources," and inflation "experienced during the early months of the year seemed unlikely to persist." Some are optimistic about "modest further decreases in inflation" with "labor costs remaining subdued." However, others cite "persisting inflationary expectations," "recent weakness of the bond markets," and the economy "operating in the vicinity of its potential." The center of gravity is that inflation pressure has eased somewhat but is still present -- slightly below the midpoint. Direction: modestly below 50.

2. **OUTPUT_GAP**: Members describe "some strengthening in the expansion" after "limited growth during the spring." The economy is described as having "regained at least moderate expansionary momentum." Crucially, members refer to the economy "operating in the vicinity of its potential" -- this is near-potential language. The pause eased pressures on resources, suggesting a slight step back from potential. Direction: near potential, very slightly below. Score should be around 48-50.

3. **LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Members note "lessening labor shortages in some areas and reduced use of overtime work by some firms." Labor costs are "remaining subdued." The pause eased resource pressures. But the economy is near potential, so tightness isn't extremely low. Unemployment at 5.7% (from the directive text, which is staff/factual). The labor market had loosened from earlier tightness. Direction: near NAIRU but slightly loose side. Around 46-48.

4. **CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending shows "strengthening retail sales in numerous areas" with "relatively high levels of confidence." Housing shows "considerable gains" after weakness. Business equipment spending remains "considerable" though slowing from "exceptional" rates. Nonresidential construction expected to slow. Inventory adjustment nearly complete. Overall: moderate growth, roughly at trend or slightly above. Direction: at trend to modestly above. Around 52-55.

5. **FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Long-term rates "risen appreciably." Bond market weakness noted. But mortgage rates had declined from highs, supporting housing. Dollar appreciated substantially. Fiscal restraint prospects "contributing to reduced long-term interest rates" potentially. Members note current policy stance is "slightly to the restrictive side at least in terms of the inflation-adjusted federal funds rate." This is about conditions resulting from policy. The mix is somewhat tight -- rates still elevated, but some loosening in mortgages. Direction: somewhat tight. Around 55-58.

6. **HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Unanimous vote to hold. Symmetric directive (no bias either direction). Policy stance characterized as "slightly to the restrictive side." Members see a potential need for easing "especially if substantial fiscal restraint were to be enacted" -- this is a conditional easing bias. But they want to wait for clarity on fiscal policy. Direction: hold with very mild easing lean. Around 46-48.

7. **DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Unanimous vote (11-0). "All the members" accepted the proposal. No dissents. Some differences on inflation outlook ("some... others"), but no policy disagreement. Direction: very low dissent. Around 12-15.

8. **FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Minimal discussion of financial stability per se. Some mentions of stock market wealth effects and bond market weakness, but these are in the context of economic outlook, not financial stability concerns. Direction: very low. Around 8-12.

9. **FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Members "generally believed that consideration would need to be given to an adjustment in the Committee's policy stance, especially if substantial fiscal restraint were to be enacted." The word "adjustment" combined with the existing "slightly restrictive" stance and the fiscal restraint trigger implies a potential easing direction. But it's conditional and uncertain. Symmetric directive adopted. Direction: neutral to mild easing lean. Around 43-47.

10. **UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Risks were still sizable in both directions." "Uncertainties about federal budget policies and their effects on the economy remained substantial." The external sector "remained subject to particular uncertainty." Federal budget developments "might well differ considerably from current forecasts." This is elevated uncertainty language. Direction: somewhat elevated. Around 42-48.

**Polarity checks**:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says pressure easing but not absent. Score ~43-45. Matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says near potential, slight easing of resource pressure. Score ~48. Matches.
- LABOR_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says lessening shortages, subdued costs. Score ~46. Matches.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says hold, conditional easing lean. Score ~47. Matches.
- FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. Evidence says potential easing ahead. Score ~44. Matches.

**Cross-dimension coherence**:
- OUTPUT_GAP (48) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (46): Both near midpoint, slightly below. Consistent -- economy near potential with slight slack. Good.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (47) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (44): Both lean very mildly dovish. The forward tone is slightly more dovish because of the explicit mention of potential future easing. Coherent.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 44 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Members generally viewed the near-term outlook for inflation as more encouraging than it had appeared to be earlier this year."
- "Some members were optimistic that growth of the economy at a pace in line with their expectations would be consistent with modest further decreases in inflation."
- "Other members expressed reservations about the prospects for an improved inflation performance over coming quarters. They cited indications of persisting inflationary expectations such as the recent weakness of the bond markets."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The majority view is that inflation pressures have eased thanks to the spring slowdown, with labor costs subdued, but a meaningful minority worries about persistent inflationary expectations and near-potential output levels preventing further progress.

**Minority views**: "Other members" concerned about persisting inflationary expectations and bond market signals push the uncertainty band upward.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 49 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Some strengthening in the expansion of economic activity after a period of limited growth during the spring."
- "The economy appeared to be in a better position to accommodate moderate growth over the forecast horizon without adding to inflation."
- "An economy that was operating in the vicinity of its potential."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is described as operating near its potential -- having recovered from spring weakness but not overheating. The "vicinity of its potential" language places this squarely near the midpoint.

**Minority views**: None notable; broad agreement on this assessment.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 46 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Anecdotal reports of lessening labor shortages in some areas and reduced use of overtime work by some firms."
- "Labor costs remaining subdued."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The labor market has loosened modestly from tighter conditions earlier in the year. Shortages are easing and labor costs are subdued, placing conditions just below the full-employment level.

**Minority views**: Some concern that "favorable labor cost developments would not persist indefinitely in an economy that was operating in the vicinity of its potential."

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 53 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Strengthening retail sales in numerous areas... relatively high levels of confidence among consumers and many retailers."
- "Considerable gains in housing activity after a period of pronounced weakness."
- "Current trends pointed to further sizable increases in outlays for office and computing equipment... still considerable overall growth in spending for business equipment."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumer spending is picking up, housing is rebounding, and business equipment investment remains solid though slowing from exceptional levels. The net picture is moderate growth at or slightly above trend.

**Minority views**: Motor vehicle sales were notably weak; consumer indebtedness could inhibit spending. These push the lower bound of the band.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 56 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Members characterized policy stance as "slightly to the restrictive side at least in terms of the inflation-adjusted federal funds rate."
- "Intermediate- and long-term rates have risen appreciably."
- Mortgage rates had declined from highs, supporting housing recovery.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions are somewhat tight, with elevated real interest rates and rising long-term rates, though some offset from declining mortgage rates. The real funds rate is described as slightly restrictive.

**Minority views**: The fiscal restraint prospect could ease long-term rate pressures, adding uncertainty.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 47 +/-4 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "All the members accepted a proposal to maintain an unchanged degree of pressure in reserve markets and to adopt a directive that was not biased in either direction."
- "Over the longer term, the members generally believed that consideration would need to be given to an adjustment in the Committee's policy stance, especially if substantial fiscal restraint were to be enacted."
- Policy stance "characterized by some members as slightly to the restrictive side."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Unanimous hold with a symmetric directive, but with a subtle easing lean conditional on fiscal restraint. The acknowledgment that the current stance is "slightly restrictive" and that future "adjustment" may be needed (implying easing) places this just below the midpoint.

**Minority views**: No members advocated tightening or immediate easing; very tight consensus.

**Score: 12 +/-4 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Votes for this action: [all 11 members listed]. Votes against this action: None."
- "All the members indicated that they would vote for a directive."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Near-total consensus on both the policy action and the symmetric directive. The only differences are in the inflation outlook assessment (some optimistic, others less so), but these did not translate into policy disagreement.

**Minority views**: Inflation outlook differences are mild and did not produce dissent.

- "Recent weakness of the bond markets" cited as evidence of inflationary expectations, not financial stability concern.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial stability is not a topic of participant discussion. References to financial markets are incidental to economic outlook assessments, not framed as stability concerns.

**Minority views**: None.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 44 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Over the longer term, the members generally believed that consideration would need to be given to an adjustment in the Committee's policy stance, especially if substantial fiscal restraint were to be enacted."
- "The extent to which an adjustment might be needed later in the stance of monetary policy -- characterized by some members as slightly to the restrictive side."
- Symmetric directive: "slightly greater or slightly lesser reserve restraint would be acceptable."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal is nominally symmetric but with a conditional easing lean. The explicit discussion of future "adjustment" from a "slightly restrictive" stance in the context of fiscal restraint implies the next move is more likely to be easing than tightening, though highly data/fiscally dependent.

**Minority views**: The symmetric directive and uncertainty about fiscal outcomes keep this near neutral.

**Score: 48 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Risks were still sizable in both directions."
- "Uncertainties about federal budget policies and their effects on the economy remained substantial."
- "The external sector of the economy remained subject to particular uncertainty."
- "Federal budget developments... might well differ considerably from current forecasts."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Uncertainty is elevated, driven primarily by fiscal policy uncertainty (the 1995 budget battles) and the external sector. The word "substantial" applied to budget uncertainty and "sizable" risks in both directions place this above the normal range.

**Minority views**: None -- broad agreement on elevated uncertainty.
