# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2013-03-20

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Segmented -- participant views are clearly separated from staff views with a "Participants' Views on Current Conditions and Economic Outlook" header.

**Era context**: March 2013 -- mid-recovery from the GFC. The Fed is conducting QE3 ($85 billion/month in MBS and Treasury purchases). The unemployment rate remains elevated. Inflation is running below the 2% target. Sequestration has just begun. Threshold-based forward guidance (Evans Rule) is in place.

**Quantifier distribution**: "generally" (frequent), "most" (several uses), "many" (several uses), "several" (a few uses), "some" (frequent), "a few" (frequent), "a couple" (several uses), "one" (several uses), "nearly all" (once, on inflation). Good breadth of discussion across topics.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target. "Inflation had been running below the Committee's 2 percent objective for some time, and nearly all of the participants anticipated that it would run at or below 2 percent over the medium term." "Participants saw little near-term inflationary pressure." "Some participants saw downside risks to inflation." A few noted upside risk if policy stayed accommodative too long, but these are minority views. Direction: clearly below midpoint. "Subdued" language aligns with 26-40 range. Anchor for 2013 below-target is 38-42. Given "little near-term inflationary pressure" and "some saw downside risks," this is in the lower part of that range. Score: 35.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "the economy had returned to moderate growth following a pause late last year." "fiscal policy was exerting significant near-term restraint on the economy." "unemployment rate remained elevated." "slack in resource utilization remaining." Growth is moderate but clearly below potential with significant slack. The bright-line rule: "slack remains" language means score MUST be < 50. Anchor for 2010-14 recovery is 35-42. Growth has resumed but is moderate with fiscal drag. Score: 38.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "labor markets had shown signs of improvement, although the unemployment rate remained elevated." "participants generally saw the unemployment rate as still elevated and were not yet confident that the recent progress... would be sustained." "firms reportedly remained cautious about hiring." Long-term unemployment is "especially high." The level of tightness is low -- unemployment elevated, hiring cautious. Anchor for 2011-14 is 35-40. Some improvement from worst but still well below full employment. Score: 35.

**Polarity check**: OUTPUT_GAP (38) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (35) both below 50, consistent with each other. Good.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "most participants noted that the data on spending were somewhat encouraging, particularly with regard to spending on automobiles, other consumer durables, and housing." "the moderate acceleration in spending might in part reflect pent-up demand." Business contacts seeing "some further improvement"; "increased planning for capital expenditures." But "overall consumer spending was likely still held back" by some participants. Housing has "improved further." Net: moderately positive but below trend. "Somewhat encouraging" and "moderate acceleration" suggest movement toward trend but not yet there. Anchor for 2011-13 is 35-42. Score: 42.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Accommodative monetary policy was likely providing important support." "investors in some financial markets were taking on additional risk -- either credit risk or interest rate risk -- in an effort to boost returns." "U.S. banks had expanded their capital positions and were generally in sound financial condition." The discussion emphasizes easy financial conditions driven by accommodative policy, with reach-for-yield behavior. This is loose conditions. Credit is becoming more available. Score should be below 50 -- conditions are loose/accommodative. Score: 28.

**Polarity check**: Higher = tighter conditions. Evidence says conditions are loose/accommodative. 28 < 50. Correct.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "all but one member judged that a highly accommodative stance of monetary policy was warranted." QE3 continues at $85B/month. Threshold-based forward guidance maintained. One dissent (hawkish). Discussion of when to taper: "a few" want to end soon; "a few" see risks increasing and want to reduce "before long"; "many" would slow "beginning at some point over the next several meetings" given improvement; "a few" want to continue at current pace "at least until late in the year." The center of gravity is maintaining full accommodation now with openness to tapering later if conditions improve. This is firmly in QE territory, which is 15-25 per the rubric. But there's active taper discussion, which pushes it slightly higher. Score: 25.

**Polarity check**: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence: maintaining QE, highly accommodative, but beginning to discuss tapering. 25 is in the "strong dovish" range. The taper discussion nudges it above the floor of QE-era dovishness. Correct.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Threshold-based forward guidance retained (Evans Rule). "Many participants... expressed the view that continued solid improvement in the outlook for the labor market could prompt the Committee to slow the pace of purchases beginning at some point over the next several meetings." But "purchases might well continue at the current pace at least through the end of the year" (two members). The forward signal is: accommodation continues, but tapering is on the horizon contingent on improvement. The Evans Rule thresholds are in place. Anchor for 2012-13 Evans Rule/QE3 thresholds is 3-8. But the active taper discussion introduces a forward signal that is less dovish than pure threshold guidance. Score: 12.

**Coherence check**: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (25) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (12) -- both dovish, with the forward tone slightly more dovish because the thresholds are still operative and the taper is conditional/future. The slight divergence is explained by the current action being fully accommodative while the forward discussion introduces the possibility of future tapering. Reasonable.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal dissent (hawkish). Views on taper timing range widely: "a few" want to end soon, "many" see slowing later, "a few" want to continue all year. "A range of views was expressed regarding the economic and labor market conditions that would call for an adjustment." There's meaningful disagreement on the pace/timing of purchases, but broad agreement on the overall accommodative stance. One dissent plus a wide range of views on taper timing. Score: 40.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Substantial discussion. An entire section devoted to "assessments of risks to financial stability." "Many participants noted that in the current low-interest rate environment, investors in some financial markets were taking on additional risk." Extensive discussion of costs and risks of asset purchases, including financial stability implications. "participants pointed to possible risks to the stability of the financial system." "a number of participants remained concerned about the potential for financial stability risks to build." This is a prominent topic discussed at length. Anchor for 2013-19 typical is 20-35, but this meeting has an unusually detailed financial stability review. Score: 55.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "estimates of the economic effects were necessarily imprecise and covered a wide range." "a few participants indicated that their contacts saw the level of uncertainty about the economic outlook as having declined recently." "the outlook for the sector was quite uncertain" (housing, one participant). "were not yet confident that the recent progress... would be sustained." Some uncertainty about fiscal effects and QE efficacy, but not extreme. The discussion reflects moderate uncertainty -- standard caveats plus some specific uncertainty about QE effects and fiscal impacts. Anchor for 2013-14 is 25-35. Score: 35.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Inflation had been running below the Committee's 2 percent objective for some time, and nearly all of the participants anticipated that it would run at or below 2 percent over the medium term."
- "Participants saw little near-term inflationary pressure."
- "some participants saw downside risks to inflation, especially if economic activity did not pick up as projected."

**Center-of-gravity**: Nearly all participants see inflation below target with little near-term pressure; the center of gravity is clearly below the 2% objective with some downside risk.

**Minority views**: "a few participants noted that the risk remained that inflationary pressures could rise as the expansion continued, especially if monetary policy remained highly accommodative for too long." This is a small minority and widens the band slightly.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the economy had returned to moderate growth following a pause late last year"
- "fiscal policy was exerting significant near-term restraint on the economy"
- "slack in resource utilization remaining"

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy is growing moderately but below potential, with fiscal headwinds and significant remaining slack. Recovery in progress but output gap still negative.

**Minority views**: "a few noted that the downside risks may have diminished" -- modest optimism at the margin but no one suggests the gap is closing.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the unemployment rate remained elevated"
- "participants generally saw the unemployment rate as still elevated and were not yet confident that the recent progress toward the Committee's employment objective would be sustained"
- "firms reportedly remained cautious about hiring"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market improving from weak levels but unemployment still elevated with cautious hiring; well below full employment.

**Minority views**: Some noted signs of improvement, and one noted payroll gains "might be more meaningful than would first appear," but the consensus is that the market remains slack.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 42 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "most participants noted that the data on spending were somewhat encouraging, particularly with regard to spending on automobiles, other consumer durables, and housing"
- "Firms reported increased planning for capital expenditures, supported by low interest rates and substantial cash holdings"
- "a couple of other participants thought that overall consumer spending was likely still held back"

**Center-of-gravity**: Spending is improving and "somewhat encouraging" with moderate acceleration, but remains below trend with fiscal headwinds and cautious consumers.

**Minority views**: Some see payroll tax and student debt as restraints; others emphasize pent-up demand support.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 28 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Accommodative monetary policy was likely providing important support to these developments"
- "investors in some financial markets were taking on additional risk -- either credit risk or interest rate risk -- in an effort to boost returns"
- "easing of credit constraints allowed more borrowers to take advantage of lower interest rates"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly accommodative/loose, with reach-for-yield behavior indicating easy credit and low rates supporting activity.

**Minority views**: Some participants note "episodic strains in European markets" but domestic conditions described as loose.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 25 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all but one member judged that a highly accommodative stance of monetary policy was warranted"
- Committee agreed to continue purchases of $40B MBS + $45B Treasuries per month
- "Many participants... expressed the view that continued solid improvement in the outlook for the labor market could prompt the Committee to slow the pace of purchases beginning at some point over the next several meetings"

**Center-of-gravity**: Firmly accommodative with QE continuing at full pace, but the beginning of taper discussions introduces a modest hawkish lean relative to peak QE dovishness.

**Minority views**: One formal dissent (hawkish); "a few" want to end purchases soon; "a few" want to continue at least through year-end. This range widens uncertainty.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 40 +/-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "One member dissented from the Committee's policy decision"
- "A range of views was expressed regarding the economic and labor market conditions that would call for an adjustment in the pace of purchases"
- Views range from "bring the program to a close relatively soon" to "purchases might well continue at the current pace at least through the end of the year"

**Center-of-gravity**: One formal dissent plus a wide spectrum of views on taper timing. Agreement on the general accommodative stance but notable disagreement on the trajectory of asset purchases.

**Minority views**: The dissenter and those wanting early taper closure form one camp; those wanting purchases through year-end form another.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 55 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants discussed their assessments of risks to financial stability, particularly in light of the Committee's highly accommodative stance of monetary policy."
- "Many participants noted that in the current low-interest rate environment, investors in some financial markets were taking on additional risk"
- Extensive multi-paragraph discussion of costs/risks of asset purchases to financial stability, market functioning, and exit risks

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability received prominent attention, with a dedicated review of QE costs and risks. More attention than typical 2013 meetings due to the formal efficacy/costs review.

**Minority views**: N/A -- most participants engaged with the topic, varying in degree of concern.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 12 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The Committee also retained its forward guidance about the federal funds rate, including the thresholds on the unemployment and inflation rates."
- "continued solid improvement in the outlook for the labor market could prompt the Committee to slow the pace of purchases beginning at some point over the next several meetings"
- "were the outlook to deteriorate, the pace of purchases could be increased"

**Center-of-gravity**: Threshold-based forward guidance (Evans Rule) fully in place, signaling extended accommodation. Conditional taper discussion introduces a mild hint of future normalization, but the dominant signal remains "low for long" with QE continuing.

**Minority views**: Some want taper soon; others want purchases through year-end.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 35 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "participants recognized that estimates of the economic effects were necessarily imprecise and covered a wide range"
- "were not yet confident that the recent progress toward the Committee's employment objective would be sustained"
- "a few participants indicated that their contacts saw the level of uncertainty about the economic outlook as having declined recently"

**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate, somewhat elevated uncertainty -- standard for this period. Uncertainty about QE effects and sustainability of improvement, but some contacts report declining uncertainty. Balanced overall.

**Minority views**: One participant sees housing outlook as "quite uncertain and potentially subject to rapid changes."
