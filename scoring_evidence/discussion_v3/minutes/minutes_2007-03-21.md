# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2007-03-21

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format — staff and participant content somewhat mixed, but participant language cues are clear ("participants agreed," "members favored," "most participants," etc.).

**Era context**: March 2007 — pre-financial crisis. Fed funds rate at 5.25%. Subprime mortgage problems are emerging but seen as contained. Housing correction ongoing. Economy growing moderately. Core inflation "uncomfortably high." This is the period when the Committee is holding rates steady, caught between inflation concerns and emerging growth risks.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all members" (hold rate, inflation concern), "most participants" (gradual inflation decline), "many" (manufacturing softness), "a number of" (inventory progress), "some" (upside inflation risk increased). Strong consensus language on holding, with broad concern about inflation.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as ABOVE the comfortable level. "Prevailing level of inflation remained uncomfortably high." "All meeting participants expressed concern about the risks." "Latest readings on core inflation were higher than expected." Core inflation described as "elevated." Risks "mainly to the upside." "Some noted that upside risks to inflation appeared to have increased slightly." This is clearly above target — not dramatically so, but persistently and uncomfortably. Score range: 61-68. The word "uncomfortably" and "elevated" with "upside risks" pushes this to the upper end of "clearly above target."

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Economy "likely to expand at a moderate pace" close to or "modestly below the economy's trend growth rate." Resource utilization "near the high end of historical experience." This is a mixed signal — growth near but slightly below trend, yet resource utilization high. The resource utilization language suggests operating near or slightly above potential. "Rates of resource utilization that were near the high end of historical experience" clearly indicates above potential. Score: 52-55. The growth is at/below trend but the level of resource utilization is elevated.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor markets remained relatively tight." "Shortages of skilled workers in technical and professional fields, with significant wage pressures in some occupations." "Scarcity of less skilled and unskilled workers in some areas." This is clearly tight — "relatively tight" with reported shortages. Score: 57-62.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending supported by employment/income gains, but housing weakness, oil price increases, and equity declines warrant "modest downward adjustment." Business investment "surprisingly weak of late" but expected to firm. Manufacturing soft. Net: slightly below trend with some supports. Score: 42-47.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Subprime market tightening noted — "increase in interest rates and tightening of credit standards in the subprime mortgage market." But "no sign of spillovers from the subprime market to the overall mortgage market." "Financing conditions and other fundamentals remained favorable." "No indication that recent market volatility had prompted a reduction in the availability of financing for business investment." Net: mostly accommodative conditions with a pocket of tightening in subprime. Score: 38-43. Conditions still generally loose/supportive with emerging stress in one sector.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "All members favored keeping the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent." "Predominant policy concern remains the risk that inflation will fail to moderate." "Further policy firming might prove necessary." But they softened forward language — "future policy adjustments will depend on the evolution of the outlook for both inflation and economic growth." This is a hold with a hawkish tilt that is being modestly softened. Score: 57-62. Lean hawkish — holding with tightening bias but acknowledging two-sided risks more.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All members favored keeping the target." Broad agreement on assessment. The main nuance is the shift in statement language to acknowledge two-sided risks. No dissents. Very broad agreement. Score: 15-20.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Subprime discussed meaningfully as a risk factor. Housing market conditions discussed. Market volatility mentioned. But financial stability is not the dominant theme — it's discussed as one factor among several. "Several" mentions of subprime, housing market implications. Score: 42-48.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Key shift: "the statement should no longer cite only the possibility of further firming" and instead "future policy adjustments will depend on the evolution of the outlook for both inflation and economic growth." This is a shift from hawkish to more balanced forward guidance. But "further policy firming might prove necessary" still retained. Score: 55-60. Mild tightening bias but notably softened from prior meetings.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Increased uncertainty about the outlook for both growth and inflation." "Downside risks relative to the expectation of moderate growth had increased." "Greater uncertainty that the expected gradual decline in core inflation would materialize." "Increased the odds that inflation would fail to moderate." Multiple references to increased uncertainty. Score: 48-55.

### Cross-dimension coherence check:
- OUTPUT_GAP (53) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (59): Both above 50, consistent — economy near/above potential with tight labor. Labor a bit higher reflects the specific shortage reports.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (59) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (57): Aligned — lean hawkish but softening. Forward guidance slightly less hawkish as they removed the one-directional firming language.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says "uncomfortably high," "elevated," upside risks. Score 64 matches.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says resource utilization at "high end" but growth at/below trend. Score 53 matches — slightly above potential on net.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says mostly favorable conditions with subprime stress only. Score 40 matches — somewhat loose to neutral.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 64 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The prevailing level of inflation remained uncomfortably high, and the latest information cast some doubt on whether core inflation was on the expected downward path."
- "All meeting participants expressed concern about the risks to this outlook. The latest readings on core inflation were higher than expected."
- "Participants agreed that risks around the expected and desired path of a gradual decline in core inflation remained mainly to the upside; some noted that upside risks to inflation appeared to have increased slightly."

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants unanimously see inflation as uncomfortably elevated with upside risks increasing, though most still expect gradual moderation — placing this in the "clearly above target" range.

**Minority views**: None truly dovish on inflation; the range is from "expect gradual decline" to "upside risks increased slightly."

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 53 ±6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The economy was likely to expand at a moderate pace in coming quarters... close to or modestly below the economy's trend growth rate."
- "Rates of resource utilization that were near the high end of historical experience suggested a possibility that inflation pressures could build."

**Center-of-gravity**: Growth near trend with resource utilization at the high end of historical experience places the economy modestly above potential in level terms, even as growth is near/below trend.

**Minority views**: Downside risks emphasized by several participants regarding investment weakness and subprime.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 59 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Anecdotal and statistical evidence suggested that labor markets remained relatively tight."
- "Business contacts continued to report shortages of skilled workers in technical and professional fields, with significant wage pressures in some occupations, as well as a scarcity of less skilled and unskilled workers in some areas."
- "Aggregate measures of labor compensation were showing only moderate increases."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor markets clearly tight with reported shortages across skill levels, though aggregate wage growth still moderate — consistent with a tight but not extremely tight market.

**Minority views**: The moderation in aggregate compensation suggests the tightness may not be as severe as anecdotes imply; this informs the uncertainty band.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 43 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Business fixed investment spending had been surprisingly weak of late."
- "Growth in consumer spending would likely continue to be supported by gains in employment and incomes."
- "The recent increase in oil prices and the reduction in household net worth... warranted a modest downward adjustment in projected growth of consumer spending."
- "Many participants again reported softness in manufacturing."

**Center-of-gravity**: Business investment surprisingly weak, manufacturing soft, consumer spending sustained but facing headwinds — net assessment is modestly below trend.

**Minority views**: Fundamentals for investment remain favorable and a rebound is expected; consumer spending upside risk noted.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 40 ±8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "There was no sign of spillovers from the subprime market to the overall mortgage market; indeed, interest rates on prime mortgage loans had declined somewhat."
- "No indication that recent market volatility had prompted a reduction in the availability of financing for business investment."
- "The increase in delinquencies on subprime adjustable-rate mortgage loans and the ensuing increase in interest rates and tightening of credit standards in the subprime mortgage market."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions broadly accommodative with one emerging pocket of stress in subprime lending. Overall conditions still supportive — somewhat loose to neutral.

**Minority views**: The subprime tightening is noted as a potential constraint on housing recovery, widening the uncertainty band.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 59 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All members favored keeping the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent."
- "The Committee's predominant policy concern remains the risk that inflation will fail to moderate as expected."
- "The Committee agreed that further policy firming might prove necessary... but... the statement should no longer cite only the possibility of further firming."

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous hold with inflation as the predominant concern and a retained firming bias, but notable softening of forward language to acknowledge two-sided risks — lean hawkish but less so than prior meetings.

**Minority views**: No dissenting votes. The range runs from those emphasizing downside growth risks to those emphasizing upside inflation risks, but all support the hold.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 17 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All members favored keeping the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent."
- Broad agreement on statement language. The main debate was over the wording of the forward-looking risk assessment.

**Center-of-gravity**: Near-consensus on policy action and assessment. Minor differences in emphasis on growth vs. inflation risks, but these led to a unified agreement to broaden the risk language rather than a split.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 45 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Extended discussion of subprime mortgage market stress, potential spillovers, and housing market implications.
- "Turmoil in the subprime mortgage market did not appear to be generating any diminution in the availability of other types of household credit."
- Discussion of market volatility effects on business financing.

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability/conditions received moderate attention — subprime stress was discussed meaningfully as a risk factor, and spillover analysis was conducted, but it did not dominate the meeting.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 57 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The Committee agreed that further policy firming might prove necessary to foster lower inflation."
- "The statement should no longer cite only the possibility of further firming. Instead, the statement should indicate that future policy adjustments will depend on the evolution of the outlook for both inflation and economic growth."

**Center-of-gravity**: A notable shift from one-directional tightening signal to a more balanced, data-dependent stance — but still retaining a mild tightening bias given the inflation emphasis.

**Minority views**: The shift itself was consensual, suggesting all participants agreed the forward signal should be softened.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 52 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The combination of generally weaker-than-expected economic indicators and uncomfortably high readings on inflation suggested increased downside risks to economic growth and greater uncertainty."
- "Increased uncertainty about the outlook for both growth and inflation."
- "It was difficult to discern whether the apparent downward trend in core inflation during the past few quarters was continuing."

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants explicitly noted increased uncertainty on both sides — growth and inflation — placing this above normal levels but not at extreme uncertainty.
