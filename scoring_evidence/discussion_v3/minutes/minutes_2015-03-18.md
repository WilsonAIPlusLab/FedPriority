# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2015-03-18 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). Segmented -- participant discussion section is clearly labeled starting at "Participants' Views on Current Conditions and the Economic Outlook." Staff content appears in the first ~18 lines and is excluded from scoring.

**Era context**: March 2015 -- mid-recovery period. Fed funds rate still at 0-1/4%, committee actively debating "liftoff" timing. Oil prices had fallen sharply. Dollar appreciating. Inflation well below 2% target. Labor market improving but debate about remaining slack. This is the meeting where "patient" language was removed from forward guidance.

**Quantifier distribution**: "most" (1x -- risks nearly balanced), "many" (2x -- slack remains, inflation data in line), "several" (4x -- dollar appreciation, labor improvement, gradual normalization, June liftoff likely), "some" (3x -- softness transitory, consumer spending view, stance accommodative), "a few" (5x -- housing optimistic, wage growth prompting estimates revision, wages not useful yardstick, risks of early/late liftoff, appreciation from foreign CBs), "a couple" (3x -- oil stock limits, oil/compensation misalignment, liftoff in 2016), "one" (3x -- rising wages, against guidance change, pre-signal before liftoff), "almost all" (2x -- foreign risks noted, removing "patient").

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as well BELOW the midpoint. Inflation "declined further below the Committee's longer-run objective, largely reflecting declines in energy prices." Expected to "stay near its recent low level in the near term." Market-based inflation compensation "remained low." Many judged inflation data "about in line with their expectations that inflation would move temporarily further below the Committee's goal." One participant noted inflation derivatives suggest "substantial increase in the probability that inflation would remain well below the Committee's target over the next decade." Direction: clearly below 50. The language is "further below," "low level," "near-term low" -- this is solidly in the 26-40 range. Energy price declines and dollar appreciation are suppressing inflation. Expected to rise "gradually" back toward 2%. Score ~30.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Pace of economic activity had moderated somewhat." "Underutilization of labor resources was continuing to diminish" but still present. "Many judged that some degree of labor market slack remained." "Considerable progress achieved toward the Committee's objective of maximum employment." The economy is below potential but closing the gap. "Slack remains" language per the bright-line rule must be < 50. But "continuing to diminish" and "considerable progress" suggest approaching potential. Score ~43.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor market conditions continued to improve, with strong job gains and a lower unemployment rate." BUT "many judged that some degree of labor market slack remained, as evidenced by the low rate of labor force participation, still-elevated involuntary part-time employment, or subdued growth in wages." Several judged "further progress toward maximum employment" but not there yet. "Wage pressures generally reported to be modest." This is below full employment but improving -- the 38-42 range. Score ~40.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Household spending appeared to have slowed somewhat." "Widespread reports of a slowdown in growth during the first quarter across a range of industries." Winter weather and port disruptions noted as transitory. "Business contacts continued to express optimism about prospects for future sales or investment." Housing "remained sluggish." Auto sales and service sector "reasonably strong." Net picture: moderation from weather/transitory but underlying conditions supportive. Below trend currently with optimism for medium term. Score ~40.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Very limited direct discussion of financial conditions as such. Dollar appreciation discussed (tightening effect). One participant warned removing "patient" "could lead to a tightening of financial conditions." Market-based inflation compensation low. No discussion of credit spreads, lending conditions, or market stress. Financial conditions appear relatively accommodative given zero lower bound, but dollar appreciation is a mild tightening channel. Score ~38 (somewhat loose, reflecting ongoing accommodation with dollar as modest tightening factor).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: This is the pivotal "removing patient" meeting. "Almost all participants favored removing from the forward guidance... the indication that the Committee would be patient." They kept rates at 0-1/4% but opened the door to liftoff. "Several participants judged that the economic data and outlook were likely to warrant beginning normalization at the June meeting." But "others anticipated... conditions likely would not be appropriate to begin raising rates until later in the year" and "a couple suggested... not until 2016." The center of gravity is: hold now, but moving toward liftoff, data-dependent. The anchor for 2014-15 pre-liftoff is 40-50. Removing "patient" is a step toward hawkish but they explicitly noted April was off the table. "Several" for June, others later. This is lean hawkish -- the Committee is signaling approaching readiness. Score ~52.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Removing "patient" is the key forward guidance change. New language: "anticipates that it will be appropriate to raise the target range... when it has seen further improvement in the labor market and is reasonably confident that inflation will move back to its 2 percent objective." "Several" expected June. "Several noted they expected... a fairly gradual pace of normalization." This is a mild tightening bias with strong data dependence. The anchor for 2015 pre-liftoff is 25-40, but removing "patient" actively moves toward normalization. Score ~55 (mild tightening direction, consistent with "begin normalization" language range of 60-75 but tempered by data dependence and explicit statement that April was unlikely).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Almost all" favored removing "patient" -- one did not. No formal vote dissent is recorded in this section. "Several" for June, others for later, "a couple" for 2016 -- a range of views on timing but broad agreement on direction. "Participants expressed a range of views about how they would assess the outlook for inflation and when they might deem it appropriate to begin removing policy accommodation." Moderate differences on timing, not on direction. Score ~32.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Very minimal financial stability discussion. Dollar appreciation discussed in foreign/trade context, not financial stability per se. One participant mentioned tightening of financial conditions as a risk of changing guidance. No discussion of asset valuations, leverage, systemic risk, bubbles, etc. Score ~15.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE vs HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN coherence**: HD at ~52, FG at ~55. Both lean mildly hawkish, with FG slightly more hawkish because removing "patient" is an explicit forward signal. Coherent.

**OUTPUT_GAP vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS coherence**: OG at ~43, LT at ~40. Both below 50, reflecting remaining slack. Coherent.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Almost all participants noted potential risks to the economic outlook resulting from foreign economic and financial developments." "Most saw the risks to the outlook for economic growth and the labor market as nearly balanced." Discussion of whether market-based inflation compensation signals are reliable -- multiple competing explanations offered. "Participants pointed to a number of risks to the international economic outlook, including the slowdown in growth in China, fiscal and financial problems in Greece, and geopolitical tensions." "In light of the uncertainties attending the outlook for inflation." Moderate uncertainty -- foreign risks noted broadly, inflation path uncertain, but risks "nearly balanced." Score ~40.

### Optional dimensions:

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Energy price declines discussed extensively. "Non-oil import prices" mentioned. But this is more about overall inflation drivers than goods specifically. Borderline -- energy/import prices are goods prices. Score ~28 (below-target goods price pressure from energy and imports).

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Not specifically discussed as a distinct category by participants.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Housing sector "remained sluggish" but this is activity, not housing inflation. Not discussed as inflation sub-component.

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Discussed prominently. "Survey-based measures of longer-term inflation expectations had remained stable." "Market-based measures of inflation compensation remained low" and "about unchanged" but one participant noted derivatives suggest "substantial increase in the probability that inflation would remain well below target over the next decade." Another noted low compensation "could reflect increased concern... about adverse outcomes." Split between anchored surveys and low market-based measures. Score ~40 (mostly anchored but market measures raising questions, slightly below target anchoring).

**DOLLAR_CONCERN**: "Several participants noted that the dollar's further appreciation over the intermeeting period was likely to restrain U.S. net exports and economic growth for a time." "A few participants suggested that accommodative policy actions by a number of foreign central banks could lead to a further appreciation of the dollar." Dollar strength is a concern. Score ~62.

**CREDIT_STRESS, EQUITY_STRESS, BANKING_STRESS, TREASURY_STRESS**: Not discussed.

**PRODUCTIVITY**: "A number of participants noted that slow growth of productivity or the labor force could reconcile the moderation in economic growth with the solid performance of some labor market indicators." Brief mention -- productivity perceived as low. Score ~35.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 30 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Inflation had declined further below the Committee's longer-run objective, largely reflecting declines in energy prices, and was expected to stay near its recent low level in the near term." "Many participants judged that the inflation data received over the intermeeting period had been about in line with their expectations that inflation would move temporarily further below the Committee's goal." One participant cited "a substantial increase in the probability that inflation would remain well below the Committee's target over the next decade."
- Center-of-gravity: Participants broadly agree inflation is well below the 2% target, driven by energy and dollar, but expect it to rise gradually. Current readings are low with near-term low persistence.
- Minority views: One participant highlighted downside risk from inflation derivatives; another noted inflation compensation could reflect concern about adverse low-inflation outcomes. These widen uncertainty slightly downward.

### OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 43 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "The pace of economic activity had moderated somewhat." "Participants judged that underutilization of labor resources was continuing to diminish." "Many judged that some degree of labor market slack remained."
- Center-of-gravity: Economy is below potential but closing the gap. Growth moderated somewhat in Q1 (weather, ports), but underlying trajectory is toward potential.
- Minority views: "A number of participants noted that slow growth of productivity or the labor force could reconcile the moderation in economic growth with the solid performance of some labor market indicators" -- suggesting gap may be smaller than headline GDP implies.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 40 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Labor market conditions continued to improve, with strong job gains and a lower unemployment rate." "Many judged that some degree of labor market slack remained, as evidenced by the low rate of labor force participation, still-elevated involuntary part-time employment, or subdued growth in wages." "Wage pressures generally reported to be modest."
- Center-of-gravity: Improving labor market but not yet at full employment. Slack remains in participation, part-time, and wages.
- Minority views: One participant "saw some evidence of rising wage growth and suggested that compositional changes in the labor force could be masking underlying wage pressures." A few noted wages may not be a useful yardstick for slack.

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 40 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Household spending appeared to have slowed somewhat over the intermeeting period." "Widespread reports of a slowdown in growth during the first quarter across a range of industries, partly reflecting severe winter weather." "Business contacts in many parts of the country continued to express optimism about prospects for future sales or investment."
- Center-of-gravity: Current spending and investment below trend due to transitory factors, but forward-looking signals are more positive. Net assessment is modestly below trend.
- Minority views: Some more optimistic about consumer spending supported by labor market, wealth, lower gas prices.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 38 +/-8 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: "Several participants noted that the dollar's further appreciation over the intermeeting period was likely to restrain U.S. net exports." One participant warned that "the announcement of a meeting-by-meeting approach to policy could lead to a tightening of financial conditions."
- Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions remain generally accommodative (zero lower bound, ongoing reinvestment) with dollar appreciation as a modest tightening channel. Very limited direct discussion.
- Minority views: Limited evidence makes this uncertain.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 52 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Almost all participants favored removing from the forward guidance... the indication that the Committee would be patient." "Several participants judged that the economic data and outlook were likely to warrant beginning normalization at the June meeting." "Others anticipated... conditions likely would not be appropriate to begin raising rates until later in the year, and a couple of participants suggested... not until 2016."
- Center-of-gravity: Hold rates now but actively preparing for liftoff. Removing "patient" is a concrete step toward tightening. Center of gravity is lean hawkish but not yet at the point of action.
- Minority views: One participant opposed removing "patient" (dovish side); several wanted June (hawkish side); a couple wanted to wait until 2016 (dovish side).

### DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 32 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Almost all participants favored removing" patient -- one did not. On timing: "Several judged... June meeting" vs "others anticipated... later in the year" and "a couple... suggested... not until 2016." "Participants expressed a range of views about how they would assess the outlook for inflation and when they might deem it appropriate to begin removing policy accommodation."
- Center-of-gravity: Broad directional agreement on moving toward normalization, with moderate differences on timing. No formal dissents recorded.
- Minority views: Range from June 2015 to 2016 on timing, but direction is shared.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 15 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: One participant mentioned "tightening of financial conditions" as a risk of guidance change. Dollar appreciation discussed in trade/growth context. No substantive discussion of asset valuations, systemic risk, or financial stability as a dedicated topic.
- Center-of-gravity: Financial stability was not a meaningful focus of this meeting's discussion.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 55 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: Removed "patient" language. New guidance: "anticipates that it will be appropriate to raise the target range... when it has seen further improvement in the labor market and is reasonably confident that inflation will move back to its 2 percent objective." "Several noted that they expected economic developments would call for a fairly gradual pace of normalization." "An increase in the target range for the federal funds rate remained unlikely at the April FOMC meeting."
- Center-of-gravity: Forward signal is mildly hawkish -- moving toward normalization but data-dependent, gradual, with April explicitly off the table. The removal of "patient" is a concrete step toward tightening forward guidance.
- Minority views: Several wanted June; a couple wanted 2016. Forward signal is deliberately ambiguous on exact timing.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 40 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Almost all participants noted potential risks to the economic outlook resulting from foreign economic and financial developments." "Most saw the risks to the outlook... as nearly balanced." "Participants pointed to a number of risks to the international economic outlook, including the slowdown in growth in China, fiscal and financial problems in Greece, and geopolitical tensions." Multiple competing explanations for market-based inflation compensation.
- Center-of-gravity: Somewhat elevated uncertainty, particularly around foreign developments and inflation path, but risks seen as "nearly balanced" by most. Not extreme uncertainty.

### INFLATION_GOODS
**Score: 28 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Declines in energy prices" discussed extensively as driver of below-target inflation. "Lower prices of non-oil imports" also cited. "Persistently low oil prices had resulted in declines in drilling."
- Center-of-gravity: Goods price pressures are clearly low, driven by energy and import price declines.

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS
**Score: 40 +/-8 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Survey-based measures of longer-term inflation expectations had remained stable." "Market-based measures of inflation compensation over the longer term were about unchanged... although they had exhibited some volatility." One participant noted derivatives suggest "substantial increase in the probability that inflation would remain well below the Committee's target over the next decade."
- Center-of-gravity: Mostly anchored per surveys, but market-based measures low and raising questions. Slightly below midpoint.

### DOLLAR_CONCERN
**Score: 62 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Several participants noted that the dollar's further appreciation over the intermeeting period was likely to restrain U.S. net exports and economic growth for a time." "A few participants suggested that accommodative policy actions by a number of foreign central banks could lead to a further appreciation of the dollar."
- Center-of-gravity: Notable concern about dollar strength weighing on exports and growth; discussed by several participants with clear directional concern.

### PRODUCTIVITY
**Score: 35 +/-8 [Low confidence]**
- Evidence: "A number of participants noted that slow growth of productivity or the labor force could reconcile the moderation in economic growth with the solid performance of some labor market indicators."
- Center-of-gravity: Productivity perceived as below historical trend, mentioned briefly as an explanatory factor.
