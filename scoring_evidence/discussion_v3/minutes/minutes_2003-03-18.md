# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2003-03-18

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented (pre-2009), so I must focus on participant language cues ("members commented," "many observed," "some members") and ignore staff-attributable content.

**Era context**: March 2003 — the economy is in a sluggish post-2001-recession recovery with the Iraq War about to begin. The fed funds rate is at 1.25%. Growth is below potential, labor markets are weak, and inflation is low and declining. Geopolitical uncertainty dominates.

**Quantifier distribution**: "members" (collective) used frequently, "many" used several times, "some" used several times, "unanimously" on policy decision. No "most/majority" or "a few" language.

### Dimension-by-dimension directional assessment:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target and declining. Key: "the likelihood of continued low or even declining inflation was high," "further disinflation in core prices as a distinct possibility," "well-entrenched expectations of low inflation." Oil prices add some upward uncertainty but members note they are "more of an effect in damping demand than in raising inflation." Direction: clearly below midpoint, in the disinflation/deflation-concern zone.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Economy is operating below potential. Key: "persisting slack in resource utilization," "continued low levels of capital and labor utilization," "continued growth below potential as the primary risk." The economy had been "faltering in recent weeks." Direction: well below 50.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "weak labor markets" mentioned explicitly. "continued low levels of...labor utilization." No discussion of hiring or tightness. Direction: well below 50, significant slack.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending weakened recently — motor vehicle sales declined substantially, retail sales weakened. Business capital expenditures held back with "no persuasive evidence that business fixed investment would provide the needed support." Commercial construction "especially downbeat." Some high-tech spending picking up. Housing "held up well." Direction: below trend, sluggish.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Members refer to "accommodative financial and bank lending markets" and "stimulative monetary...policies." Low mortgage rates mentioned. Equity prices lower but credit markets appear supportive. Direction: conditions are loose/accommodative, so FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS is below 50.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Members unanimously supported a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance." They note monetary policy is "positioned to accommodate a strengthening economic performance." Some members saw "continued growth below potential as the primary risk" and cautioned the Committee "might well need to adjust its policy" (implying possible easing). No one suggested tightening. Direction: dovish lean — hold with easing bias.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Unanimously supported" the unchanged stance. While "some members" had different emphasis on risks, there is broad agreement on holding steady. Direction: low disagreement.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Brief mentions of equity prices being lower and financial conditions being accommodative, but no substantive discussion of financial stability risks, systemic concerns, or market dysfunction. Direction: low emphasis.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is dovish-leaning: "readiness to respond promptly," "might well need to adjust its policy," combined with acknowledgment that growth below potential is the primary risk. No hint of tightening. Direction: easing lean with data dependence.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "An unusually high degree of uncertainty" — this phrase is strong and explicit. Iraq war uncertainty dominates. "Very difficult to assess the factors underlying the performance of the economy." "Some clarification of the Iraqi situation...should improve the Committee's ability to assess." "Substantial degree of uncertainty." Direction: very elevated uncertainty.

### Cross-dimension coherence check:
- OUTPUT_GAP (~32) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (~28): Both well below 50, consistent with slack economy. Coherent.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (~38) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (~32): Both dovish-leaning. Coherent — hold with easing bias, forward guidance suggests readiness to ease.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says low/declining. Score below 50. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says below potential. Score below 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says "accommodative." Score below 50. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Evidence says dovish lean. Score below 50. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 22 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "the likelihood of continued low or even declining inflation was high, especially given expectations of persisting slack in resource utilization"
- "members saw further disinflation in core prices as a distinct possibility over the next several quarters"
- "well-entrenched expectations of low inflation"

**Center of gravity**: Members collectively perceive inflation as low and declining, with disinflation a real possibility. This places them in the "meaningful deflation risk / subdued / muted" range. Not quite at deflation alarm (10), but clearly well below target.

**Minority views**: Some concern about oil prices pushing higher, but members dismiss this as more demand-dampening than inflationary.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 32 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:

- "continued low levels of capital and labor utilization"
- "some members saw continued growth below potential as the primary risk for the near term"

**Center of gravity**: The economy is clearly below potential with persistent slack. Recent data shows further weakening. Members see possible strengthening ahead but only after geopolitical resolution.

**Score: 32 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Center of gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment are below trend and weakening. Housing is a bright spot but overall the picture is sluggish with multiple headwinds. Some high-tech spending picking up provides a modest offset.
