# FOMC Meeting Scoring: January 30-31, 2018 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Segmented format (post-2009) -- participant discussion section is clearly separated from staff content.

**Era context**: January 2018 -- mid-hiking cycle. Tax Cuts and Jobs Act recently enacted (December 2017). Economy growing above trend, unemployment at 4.1% (near 20-year low), but inflation still running below 2% target (core PCE at 1.5%). Global economy strengthening. Equity markets rallying. The Fed had been gradually raising rates (25bp at a time) and was expected to continue.

**Quantifier distribution**: "participants agreed" (consensus), "many participants" (used for labor tightness, inflation data, financial conditions easing, inflation expectations anchored), "several" (tax cut uncertainty, dollar decline helping inflation, increased confidence in inflation, upside risks), "a few" (slack remaining, tax effects on wages, tax cuts dragging inflation, inflation expectations slipped), "a couple" (saving rate, labor tightening risks), "some" (inflation falling short risk, neutral rate comments), "a number of" (marked up growth forecasts, districts boosting production/investment), "a majority" (stronger outlook raising likelihood of further firming), "almost all" (inflation moving to 2% over medium term).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target but gradually rising. Core PCE at 1.5%, total PCE at 1.7%. "Many participants noted that inflation data... had generally pointed to a gradual rise in inflation." "Almost all participants continued to anticipate that inflation would move up to the Committee's 2 percent objective over the medium term." But "some participants saw an appreciable risk that inflation would continue to fall short." Current reading is clearly below target, but there's confidence it's heading toward target. Score should be in the 41-49 range -- slightly below target but moving up. The "gradual rise" and expectation of reaching 2% keeps it from being lower.
Direction: Below target but not concerning. Score ~44.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants describe the economy as operating above potential. Key phrase: "the economy continued to operate above potential" and "above-trend economic growth." The rate of growth in 2018 "would exceed their estimates of its sustainable longer-run pace." This is clearly above potential. Bright-line rule: must be > 50. "Continued above-trend economic growth" with "substantial underlying economic momentum" and tax cuts boosting activity further. This is modestly above potential.
Direction: Above potential. Score ~55.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Many participants reported that labor market conditions were tight in their Districts, evidenced by low unemployment rates, difficulties for employers in filling open positions or retaining workers." Unemployment at 4.1% -- "lowest level seen in the past 20 years." U-6 and involuntary part-time back to pre-recession levels. "A few participants judged that while the labor market was close to full employment, some margins of slack remained." But the center of gravity is clearly tight. Wage growth still muted though -- "few signs of a broad-based pickup in wage growth." This is tight but not extremely tight (wage pressures not yet fully manifesting).
Direction: Tight. Score ~58.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Solid growth in consumer spending" expected to continue. Business contacts "generally upbeat." "Businesses in a number of Districts reported plans to further increase investment." "Gains in employment, household spending, and business fixed investment were solid." "Substantial underlying economic momentum." This is above trend -- solid and supported by tax cuts, wealth effects, confidence. Not "robust/booming" but clearly solid and above trend.
Direction: Above trend. Score ~58.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Many participants noted that financial conditions had eased significantly over the intermeeting period." The decline in dollar and rise in equity prices more than offset rise in Treasury yields. "Accommodative financial conditions" cited as supporting growth. This is clearly on the loose side.
Direction: Loose/accommodative. Score ~25. Financial conditions have "eased significantly" -- this is clearly loose.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Participants agreed that a gradual approach to raising the target range... remained appropriate." "A majority of participants noted that a stronger outlook for economic growth raised the likelihood that further gradual policy firming would be appropriate." No rate change at this meeting (held steady), but strong signal of further gradual tightening ahead. "Some participants saw an appreciable risk that inflation would continue to fall short" and "could afford to be patient" -- a dovish minority. The center of gravity is gradual tightening continues. This is in the hiking cycle, mid-range hawkish.
Direction: Moderate hawkish lean. Score ~67.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Some participants" saw inflation falling short and wanted patience vs. "a majority" seeing stronger growth warranting further firming. "A few" thought slack remained vs. "many" seeing tight conditions. There are visible differences, especially on inflation outlook and pace, but no evidence of formal dissents. The disagreements are within a relatively narrow band of "gradual tightening yes, but how confident about inflation?"
Direction: Moderate differences. Score ~33.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: "Several participants cautioned that imbalances in financial markets may begin to emerge as the economy continued to operate above potential." Discussion of elevated asset valuations, increased use of debt by nonfinancial corporations, leverage by nonbank financial institutions, yield curve slope, regulatory changes. This is a meaningful segment of discussion -- several participants engage with it, but it's not the dominant topic.
Direction: Moderate attention. Score ~40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "A majority of participants noted that a stronger outlook for economic growth raised the likelihood that further gradual policy firming would be appropriate." "Participants agreed that a gradual approach to raising the target range... remained appropriate." "Several others suggested that the upside risks to the near-term outlook... may have increased." Forward signal is clearly toward continued tightening, but "gradual." This is a tightening bias.
Direction: Mild to moderate tightening signal. Score ~68.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Near-term risks to the economic outlook appeared roughly balanced." "Several participants expressed considerable uncertainty about the degree to which changes to corporate taxes would support business investment." "The outlook for the neutral rate was uncertain." Some uncertainty about tax effects and neutral rate, but overall a fairly confident meeting. Standard-plus uncertainty, not elevated.
Direction: Slightly above normal. Score ~32.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (55) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (58): Both above 50, coherent -- economy above potential with tight labor market.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (67) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (68): Aligned -- current moderate hawkish stance with continued tightening signal.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (25) is loose while HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (67) is hawkish -- coherent because loose financial conditions support the case for continued tightening.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 44: Higher = more pressure. Below 50 because inflation still below target. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP 55: Higher = more above potential. Above 50 because "above potential" language. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS 25: Higher = tighter. Low because conditions are loose/accommodative. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 44 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "On a 12-month basis, both overall inflation and inflation for items other than food and energy continued to run below 2 percent."
- "Almost all participants continued to anticipate that inflation would move up to the Committee's 2 percent objective over the medium term."
- "Some participants saw an appreciable risk that inflation would continue to fall short of the Committee's objective."

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation running below target at 1.5% core PCE, but expected to gradually rise toward 2%. Participants are not alarmed but continue to monitor closely. Slightly below the neutral midpoint.

**Minority views**: "A few other participants pointed to the record of inflation consistently running below the Committee's 2 percent objective over recent years" -- these would push score lower. "Several commented that recent developments had increased their confidence in the outlook for further progress" -- these would push higher.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 55 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Continued above-trend economic growth and a further strengthening in labor market conditions."
- "The rate of economic growth in 2018 would exceed their estimates of its sustainable longer-run pace."
- "The economy continued to operate above potential."

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear consensus that the economy is operating modestly above potential with substantial momentum, boosted by tax cuts and global strength.

**Minority views**: None significant -- broad agreement on above-trend growth.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 58 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Many participants reported that labor market conditions were tight in their Districts, evidenced by low unemployment rates, difficulties for employers in filling open positions or retaining workers."
- "The unemployment rate, at 4.1 percent, had remained near the lowest level seen in the past 20 years."
- "Participants generally noted few signs of a broad-based pickup in wage growth in available data."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market clearly tight by multiple measures, but wage growth still muted, keeping the score from going higher.

**Minority views**: "A few participants judged that while the labor market was close to full employment, some margins of slack remained" -- pointed to prime-age participation rate and employment-to-population ratio still below pre-recession levels.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 58 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Gains in employment, household spending, and business fixed investment were solid... indicating substantial underlying economic momentum."
- "Participants expected the recent solid growth in consumer spending to continue."
- "Businesses in a number of Districts reported plans to further increase investment in coming quarters in order to expand capacity."

**Center-of-gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment are solid and above trend, supported by tax cuts, wealth effects, and global strength. Not "booming" but clearly strong.

**Minority views**: "Several participants expressed considerable uncertainty about the degree to which changes to corporate taxes would support business investment and capacity expansion."

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 25 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Many participants noted that financial conditions had eased significantly over the intermeeting period."
- "Accommodative financial conditions" cited as supporting growth.
- "The economic effects of the decline in the dollar and the rise in equity prices as more than offsetting the effects of the increase in nominal Treasury yields."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly loose and accommodative. Easing significantly despite ongoing rate normalization.

**Minority views**: None on this dimension -- broad agreement that conditions are accommodative.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 67 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants agreed that a gradual approach to raising the target range for the federal funds rate remained appropriate."
- "A majority of participants noted that a stronger outlook for economic growth raised the likelihood that further gradual policy firming would be appropriate."
- "Some participants... judged that the Committee could afford to be patient in deciding whether to increase the target range."

**Center-of-gravity**: Solidly in the gradual tightening camp. No rate change at this meeting but clear majority favoring continued firming. The "gradual" qualifier and patient minority keep this below 70.

**Minority views**: "Some participants" favored patience given inflation shortfall, providing a dovish counterweight that widens the uncertainty band.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 33 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Majority favors further firming vs. "some participants" advocating patience due to inflation risk.
- "A few participants judged that... some margins of slack remained" vs. "many" seeing tight conditions.
- No evidence of formal dissents at this meeting.

**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate differences in emphasis, particularly around inflation outlook and appropriate urgency of further tightening, but within a shared framework of gradual normalization.

**Minority views**: The disagreement is real but contained -- "some" vs. "majority" framing suggests a notable but not sharp division.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 40 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Several participants cautioned that imbalances in financial markets may begin to emerge as the economy continued to operate above potential."
- "Amid elevated asset valuations and an increased use of debt by nonfinancial corporations."
- Discussion of yield curve slope, leverage by nonbank financial institutions, regulatory policy changes.

**Center-of-gravity**: Meaningful discussion of financial stability concerns by several participants. Not the dominant topic but receives a dedicated paragraph with multiple specific concerns raised.

**Minority views**: None -- this is a concern-flagging discussion, not a debate.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 68 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "A majority of participants noted that a stronger outlook for economic growth raised the likelihood that further gradual policy firming would be appropriate."
- "A number of participants indicated that they had marked up their forecasts for economic growth in the near term."
- "Adjustments to the policy path would depend on their assessments of how the economic outlook and risks to the outlook were evolving."

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear forward signal of continued gradual tightening. Data dependence maintained but direction firmly toward further firming. Tax cuts and global strength reinforce the tightening trajectory.

**Minority views**: "Some participants" favoring patience and seeing inflation risks to the downside temper the forward signal somewhat.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 32 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Near-term risks to the economic outlook appeared roughly balanced."
- "Several participants expressed considerable uncertainty about the degree to which changes to corporate taxes would support business investment."
- "The outlook for the neutral rate was uncertain and would depend on the interplay of a number of forces."

**Center-of-gravity**: Standard uncertainty with some elevation around tax effects and neutral rate. Overall, participants express reasonable confidence in the outlook with specific pockets of uncertainty. Not an especially uncertain period.

**Minority views**: None significant -- uncertainty is concentrated on specific topics (tax effects, neutral rate) rather than the broad outlook.
