# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2006-12-12

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format, so I must focus on participant-attributed language ("participants noted," "members agreed," "several judged") and ignore staff-reported content.

**Era context**: December 2006. The Fed had paused its hiking cycle at 5.25% in August 2006 after 17 consecutive rate increases. Housing market was in a significant correction. Core inflation was above the implicit ~2% target. The economy was slowing but labor markets remained solid.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all" (concern about inflation, agreement on inflation as predominant risk), "nearly all" (favored holding rates, viewed core inflation as uncomfortably high), "most" (expected core inflation to moderate), "many" (second half softer than expected, business contacts reported recruitment difficulties), "several" (downside risks increased, contacts in construction), "some" (indicators pointing to subdued picture, contacts at delivery firms, participants highlighted individual price components), "one" (disagreed on statement language direction).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as ABOVE target. "All meeting participants remained concerned about the outlook for inflation." "Nearly all participants viewed core inflation as uncomfortably high." "Risks around this desired downward path remained to the upside." Readings had "improved modestly since the spring" but were "not yet viewed as convincingly on a downward trend." This is clearly elevated — "uncomfortably high" is strong language. Score should be in the 61-75 range. The expectation of gradual moderation tempers it slightly, but the current perception is firmly elevated. Score: 65.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy operating near or slightly below potential. "Participants continued to expect the economy to expand at a rate close to or a little below the economy's long-run sustainable pace." Activity was "a touch softer than expected." Housing was weighing on growth. But labor market was "tight." The language "close to or a little below" sustainable pace suggests near potential but slightly below. Score: 48.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Participants see the labor market as TIGHT. "Incoming data and reports from businesses suggested that the labor market remained tight." Unemployment had "moved slightly lower." "Many business contacts reported difficulties in recruiting suitably qualified workers." "Tightness of the labor market could lead to sustained upward pressure on nominal labor costs." This is clearly tight. Score: 58.

**Coherence check OUTPUT_GAP vs LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: OUTPUT_GAP at 48 and LABOR_TIGHTNESS at 58 diverge somewhat. The text explicitly addresses this: "Solid gains in employment over recent quarters stood in contrast to the softer pace of economic expansion suggested by the spending and production data." Participants attribute this to lags, possible weaker structural productivity, or GDP measurement issues. This divergence is directly discussed and justified.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "expanded at a steady pace," "buoyed by continued gains in employment and by a decline in energy prices." Expected to "expand at a steady pace going forward." Business investment "appeared to have decelerated recently," with "relatively slow rise in equipment and software spending over the next few quarters." Nonresidential construction "moderating considerably." But "optimism among business contacts" and "investment to expand at a solid pace" longer-term. Net: consumption steady/at trend, investment decelerating. Score: 46.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Financial conditions described as "favorable" and "accommodative." "Ready availability of funding from financial markets and institutions." "Accommodative financial conditions" for commercial real estate. Mortgage rates had declined. This is clearly loose. Score: 22.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Nearly all members favored keeping the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent." "All members agreed that the risk that inflation would fail to moderate as desired remained the predominant concern." "The statement should continue to convey that inflation risks remained of greatest concern and that additional policy firming was possible." This is a hold with a tightening bias. The inflation concern is dominant, but they are holding rates. One member wanted language about adjustment in either direction. Score: 60.

**Coherence HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN vs FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Both should align — hold with tightening bias, forward signal is "additional policy firming was possible." Aligned.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "Nearly all members favored keeping the target." One member "did not favor language that referenced only the possibility of additional policy firming." "Several members judged that the subdued tone of some incoming indicators meant that the downside risks to economic growth in the near term had increased." There's broad agreement on the hold decision but some differences on risks and statement language. Score: 25.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Housing market correction discussed extensively (spillover risks, house prices). Financial conditions mentioned as "favorable/accommodative" — but this is background context, not stability concern. No discussion of systemic risk, credit stress, or market dysfunction. The housing discussion is economic, not financial stability per se. Score: 30.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "The statement should continue to convey that inflation risks remained of greatest concern and that additional policy firming was possible." One member wanted bidirectional language. The forward signal is mildly hawkish — they maintain the possibility of tightening, but with a hold in place. Score: 62.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Considerable uncertainty regarding the ultimate extent of the housing market correction." "Considerable uncertainty as to the probable pace and extent of the moderation in core inflation." Mixed signals from data. Multiple sources of uncertainty noted. Score: 48.

### Optional dimensions:

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: "Some participants also highlighted the impact that movements in the prices of individual components of the price index, such as owners' equivalent rent and medical costs, could have on near-term readings on core inflation." This is a brief mention by "some" participants — borderline. Score: N/A (not prominently discussed by 2+).

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: "Participants expressed concern that a failure of inflation to moderate as expected could entail significant costs if an upward drift in inflation expectations ensued." This is concern about potential future drift, not about current state. The concern implies expectations are currently anchored but at risk. Score: 55.

**CREDIT_STRESS**: Not discussed as stressed. Conditions described as accommodative/loose. Score: N/A (no stress discussion, only that conditions are loose — which is captured in FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS).

**PRODUCTIVITY**: "The growth of structural labor productivity could be weaker than currently thought." This was discussed by participants as an explanation for the GDP-employment divergence. Score: 42 (below historical trend).

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 65 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "All meeting participants remained concerned about the outlook for inflation."
- "Nearly all participants viewed core inflation as uncomfortably high and stressed the importance of further moderation."
- "The risks around this desired downward path remained to the upside."

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants unanimously see inflation as uncomfortably above target with upside risks, though they expect gradual moderation, placing this firmly in the "clearly above target" range.

**Minority views**: None meaningful — near-unanimous concern. The modest improvement since spring provides the lower bound of the uncertainty band.

---

### OUTPUT_GAP: 48 ±6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants continued to expect the economy to expand at a rate close to or a little below the economy's long-run sustainable pace."
- "Many participants judged that economic activity in the second half of this year was probably a touch softer than had been expected."
- "Indicators of activity in the services sector implied continued brisk growth."

**Center-of-gravity**: The economy is seen as near potential but slightly below, with mixed signals across sectors — housing dragging, services strong.

**Minority views**: The divergence between spending/production data (softer) and labor data (solid) creates some uncertainty about exact positioning.

---

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 58 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Incoming data and reports from businesses suggested that the labor market remained tight."
- "Many business contacts reported difficulties in recruiting suitably qualified workers, especially for certain types of professional and skilled positions."
- "The possibility that the tightness of the labor market could lead to sustained upward pressure on nominal labor costs was viewed as an upside risk."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is clearly tight with worker shortages reported, though the downward revision to labor compensation data eased some concerns.

**Minority views**: Some participants' concerns were eased by the labor compensation data revision, providing the lower bound.

---

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 46 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Consumer spending "had expanded at a steady pace in recent months, buoyed by continued gains in employment and by a decline in energy prices."
- "Business investment appeared to have decelerated recently, and surveys and orders data pointed to a relatively slow rise in equipment and software spending over the next few quarters."
- "Increases in nonresidential construction spending could be moderating considerably."

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending is steady at trend while business investment is decelerating, pulling the net assessment slightly below trend.

**Minority views**: Business contacts remained optimistic, and longer-term investment outlook was solid, providing upside uncertainty.

---

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 22 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Growth in consumer spending was expected to be supported by favorable financial conditions."
- "Ready availability of funding from financial markets and institutions."
- "Contacts within the construction sector had reported that commercial real estate activity remained robust, encouraged by...accommodative financial conditions."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions are described as clearly loose/accommodative, supporting spending across sectors.

**Minority views**: None noted — descriptions uniformly point to accommodative conditions.

---

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 60 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Nearly all members favored keeping the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent."
- "All members agreed that the risk that inflation would fail to moderate as desired remained the predominant concern."
- "Members agreed that the statement should continue to convey that inflation risks remained of greatest concern and that additional policy firming was possible."

**Center-of-gravity**: A hold with a clear tightening bias — inflation is the predominant concern, and the statement retains the possibility of further firming.

**Minority views**: One member wanted bidirectional language in the statement; several noted increased downside growth risks.

---

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 25 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Nearly all members favored keeping the target federal funds rate at 5-1/4 percent."
- "One member did not favor language that referenced only the possibility of additional policy firming."
- "Several members judged that the subdued tone of some incoming indicators meant that the downside risks to economic growth in the near term had increased a little."

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on the hold decision with minor differences about risk characterization and statement language. One member's dissent on language is notable but limited.

**Minority views**: The one member wanting bidirectional language and several noting increased growth risks represent mild divergence.

---

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 30 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Extensive discussion of the housing market correction and potential spillovers to consumer spending.
- "Accommodative financial conditions" mentioned in context of commercial real estate.
- No discussion of systemic risk, credit market dysfunction, or financial system stress.

**Center-of-gravity**: Housing correction is discussed as an economic drag, not as a financial stability threat. Financial conditions are mentioned as loose/supportive. Limited attention to financial stability as a distinct concern.

**Minority views**: None.

---

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 62 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Members agreed that the statement should continue to convey that inflation risks remained of greatest concern and that additional policy firming was possible."
- "One member...believed that...the statement should emphasize that policy could be adjusted in either direction."

**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal is mildly hawkish — maintaining the tightening bias in the statement while on hold, with inflation as the predominant risk.

**Minority views**: One member pushed for a more balanced, bidirectional signal.

---

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 48 ±7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Considerable uncertainty regarding the ultimate extent of the housing market correction meant that spillovers to consumption could become more evident."
- "Considerable uncertainty as to the probable pace and extent of the moderation in core inflation."
- "Recent indicators provided mixed signals about the strength of near-term activity."

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants express elevated uncertainty across multiple fronts — housing correction magnitude, inflation trajectory, and mixed economic signals — using "considerable uncertainty" language twice.

**Minority views**: None — uncertainty is broadly shared.

---

### INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS: 55 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants expressed concern that a failure of inflation to moderate as expected could entail significant costs if an upward drift in inflation expectations ensued."

**Center-of-gravity**: Expectations are currently near-anchored but with upside risk concern, placing this slightly above the neutral anchor of 50.

---

### PRODUCTIVITY: 42 ±8 [Low confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The growth of structural labor productivity could be weaker than currently thought, helping to reconcile the steady growth in employment with more subdued advances in spending and output."

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants raised the possibility of below-trend structural productivity growth as an explanation for data divergences, though this was presented as a hypothesis rather than a consensus view.
