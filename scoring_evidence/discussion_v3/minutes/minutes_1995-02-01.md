# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1995-02-01 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented pre-2009 format, but participant language cues are clear throughout ("members agreed," "members commented," "most of the members," etc.). Staff content is minimal and clearly separable.

**Era context**: Early 1995. The Fed had been tightening throughout 1994 (the famous 1994 tightening cycle). Economy growing strongly, capacity utilization high, unemployment around 5.4%. Inflation had not yet materialized in final goods prices but was building in raw materials and producer inputs. This meeting resulted in a 50bp rate hike (discount rate increase).

**Quantifier distribution**: "all the members" (endorsed ranges, supported firming), "most of the members" (concluded some rise in inflation probable, convinced prompt action needed), "many members" (saw resource pressures portending higher inflation), "several" (anecdotal evidence not pointing to significant moderation), "some members" (concern about bank lending standards, consumer indebtedness), "a few" (preferred to delay tightening).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as above the midpoint. "Most of the members concluded that some rise in inflation appeared probable over coming quarters." Prices of raw materials rising rapidly. CPI projected at 3-3.5% vs 2.75% in prior years. "The economy had attained levels of labor and capital utilization that implied a strong risk of rising inflation." However, final goods inflation had NOT yet materialized broadly -- "had not led thus far to a broad pickup in inflation as measured by the prices of final goods and services." So this is anticipatory/building pressure, not yet realized. Score should be 58-65 range. The pressure is clearly perceived but not yet fully manifested.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Economy operating at or above potential. "Economy that already seemed to be operating at, and perhaps beyond, sustainable capacity levels." "High levels of resource utilization." "Continuing to expand at a pace above its long-run potential." GDP growth 4% in 1994, expected to moderate to 2-3%. Capacity utilization "continued to move up from already high levels." Bright-line rule: "above potential" language requires > 50. Score 58-63.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 5.4%, projected to stay around 5.5%. "Current pressures on labor and other producer resources." "High levels of resource utilization." Members saw labor utilization as tight enough to portend higher inflation. Score 55-60 -- tight but not severely so.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Mixed picture. Consumer spending showed "sluggish behavior of non-auto retail sales in November and December" but business fixed investment "had strengthened further." Capital goods sector showing "considerable momentum." Housing expected to soften. Inventory accumulation "unsustainable." Overall the economy is growing strongly but expected to moderate. Net position is above trend. Score 58-63.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Little indication that policy tightening actions over the past year were constraining the availability of credit to any observable degree." "Financial conditions remained broadly supportive of further economic expansion." "Accommodative lending policies of banking institutions." "Relatively narrow quality spreads." This is clearly loose/accommodative. Score 25-30.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All members voted for tightening. "Most of the members were convinced that current monetary policy should be adjusted promptly to a more clearly restrictive stance." Only "a few preferred to delay" and even they joined the vote. This is a 50bp hike with strong consensus. Score 70-75 -- strong hawkish but with symmetric directive (no presumption of further tightening).

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Unanimous vote on monetary policy (12-0). "A few preferred to delay" but joined the consensus. Two dissents on Mexico swap line (Lindsey and Melzer) but that's not about monetary policy per se. On the policy action itself, broad agreement with minor timing differences. Score 18-25.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Members discussed bank lending standards, narrow quality spreads, stock market performance, Mexico financial crisis. "Some members expressed concern that a number of banks might have eased their lending standards unduly." Mexico discussion is substantial but primarily about foreign policy/trade implications. For a 1993-96 era meeting, this is somewhat elevated. Score 25-32.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The committee adopted a symmetric directive after tightening -- "did not believe that there should be a presumption toward possible further tightening." "Further monetary restraint might not be needed if... the expansion was moderating." This signals the hike may be the last or near-last, a distinctly less hawkish forward signal than the current action. Score 58-63 -- mildly hawkish forward (they just hiked but signaled possible pause).

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Their current projections were subject to substantial risks." "A key uncertainty in the outlook was whether the slowing in overall economic growth would be sufficient." Fiscal policy outcome "very uncertain." Timing/extent of inventory adjustment "impossible to predict." Consumer spending outlook "uncertain." This is somewhat elevated uncertainty. Score 45-52.

### Cross-dimension coherence check:
- OUTPUT_GAP (60) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (57): Both above 50, aligned. Economy running hot.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (72) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (60): Hawkish current action but more cautious forward signal (symmetric directive). Divergence explained by the "tighten now, then wait and see" stance. Makes sense.

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says building pressure. Score above 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says loose/accommodative. Score below 50. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: Higher = more hawkish. Unanimous tightening vote. Score well above 50. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 62 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "most of the members concluded that some rise in inflation appeared probable over coming quarters, and they were concerned that this upturn would not be reversed and could be extended in the absence of further monetary restraint"
- "the economy had attained levels of labor and capital utilization that implied a strong risk of rising inflation over coming quarters"
- "projections of the rate of inflation, as indexed by the consumer price index, had a central tendency of 3 to 3-1/2 percent"
- Counterbalance: "had not led thus far to a broad pickup in inflation as measured by the prices of final goods and services"

**Center of gravity**: Members broadly perceive building inflation pressure from tight resource utilization, with CPI expected to rise to 3-3.5%. Pressure is anticipated and building rather than fully realized, placing this clearly above neutral but below the "persistent/concerning" range.

**Minority views**: Some noted structural changes and productivity improvements might contain inflation, but this was a minority perspective.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 60 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "an economy that already seemed to be operating at, and perhaps beyond, sustainable capacity levels and to be continuing to expand at a pace above its long-run potential"
- "capacity utilization continued to move up from already high levels"
- "growth in real GDP had a central tendency of 2 to 3 percent, compared with a growth rate of 4 percent estimated for 1994"

**Center of gravity**: Economy clearly operating above potential with strong momentum, though expected to moderate. The "at, and perhaps beyond" language and above-trend growth rate place this solidly above 50.

**Minority views**: Some members noted "scattered signs of some softening" but "several emphasized... anecdotal evidence did not currently point to any significant moderation."

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 57 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "civilian unemployment rate declined to 5.4 percent"
- "projections of the rate in the fourth quarter of 1995 were centered in a narrow range around 5-1/2 percent"
- "The high levels of resource utilization implied by these projections were viewed by most members as likely to foster somewhat greater pressure on wages and prices"
- "current pressures on labor and other producer resources, which many members saw as portending higher inflation"

**Center of gravity**: Labor market tight enough to generate inflation concern, with unemployment at 5.4% and expected to remain low. Moderately tight but not "severe shortages" territory.

**Minority views**: None explicitly stated on labor conditions.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 60 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "real business-fixed investment had strengthened further in the fourth quarter" with "considerable momentum"
- "nonresidential construction was now trending higher, with particular strength evident in industrial and commercial construction"
- "sluggish behavior of non-auto retail sales in November and December" but "consumer confidence was at a high level"
- "The expansion continued to display appreciable momentum"

**Center of gravity**: Business investment is very strong; consumer spending shows mixed signals but overall economy has "appreciable momentum." Net position is above trend, though expected to moderate. The strength in capital goods offsets softness in retail.

**Minority views**: Some noted consumer indebtedness as a potential drag; housing expected to soften.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 27 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "they saw little indication that policy tightening actions over the past year were constraining the availability of credit to any observable degree"
- "financial conditions remained broadly supportive of further economic expansion"
- "relatively narrow quality spreads in debt markets attested to a considerable degree of confidence among investors"
- "accommodative lending policies of banking institutions... had encouraged rapid growth in consumer and business loans"

**Center of gravity**: Despite a year of rate hikes, financial conditions remained clearly accommodative/loose. Credit freely available, spreads narrow, bank lending aggressive. This is well below neutral.

**Minority views**: "Some members expressed concern that a number of banks might have eased their lending standards unduly" -- but this reinforces the loose conditions assessment.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 72 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "all the members indicated that they could support some firming in reserve conditions"
- "Most of the members were convinced that current monetary policy should be adjusted promptly to a more clearly restrictive stance"
- "prompt action was needed to counter inflationary pressures and inflationary expectations"
- "a delay in tightening policy would incur an unacceptable risk of allowing further inflationary momentum"
- 50bp discount rate increase approved unanimously

**Center of gravity**: Strong hawkish consensus for immediate tightening, with only "a few" preferring a short delay (who still voted yes). The 50bp hike with unanimous support reflects strong hawkish conviction, though the symmetric directive moderates the forward lean slightly.

**Minority views**: "A few preferred to delay" citing "favorable news on inflation in the fourth quarter" and Mexico concerns, but they ultimately supported the action.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 22 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Both monetary policy votes were 12-0 unanimous
- "a few preferred to delay such an action" but "indicated that they would join with the other members in voting to tighten"
- "Opinions differed to some degree with regard to both the likely extent of the prospective slowing in economic growth and the outlook for inflation"
- Two dissents on Mexico swap line (Lindsey, Melzer) but this is not monetary policy per se

**Center of gravity**: Broad agreement on policy action with minor differences in preferred timing. The "a few preferred delay" camp is a small minority that joined consensus. Some differences in economic outlook but not sharp.

**Minority views**: The timing preference of "a few" and the Mexico-related dissents suggest some underlying tension but not on core monetary policy.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 28 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Some members expressed concern that a number of banks might have eased their lending standards unduly and thus assumed unwarranted risks in their loan portfolios"
- "The performance of stock market prices and the relatively narrow quality spreads in debt markets attested to a considerable degree of confidence among investors"
- Extensive Mexico financial crisis discussion (contagion concerns, dollar vulnerability)

**Center of gravity**: Financial stability receives limited but present discussion. Bank lending standards concern is a notable mention. Mexico financial crisis is discussed but more as a trade/foreign policy issue than domestic financial stability. For the mid-1990s era, this is slightly elevated.

**Minority views**: None strongly divergent on this topic.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 60 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "they did not believe that there should be a presumption toward possible further tightening in this period"
- "further monetary restraint might not be needed if... the incoming evidence on the performance of the economy suggested that the expansion was moderating sufficiently"
- "the Committee's most difficult decision over the next several quarters was likely to be that of determining when further tightening was no longer desirable"
- Symmetric directive adopted

**Center of gravity**: Forward signal is mildly hawkish -- they just hiked but explicitly adopted a symmetric (neutral) forward posture, suggesting this could be the last hike if the economy moderates as expected. The forward signal is notably less hawkish than the current action.

**Minority views**: None explicitly; unanimous on symmetric directive.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 48 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "their current projections were subject to substantial risks"
- "A key uncertainty in the outlook was whether the slowing in overall economic growth would be sufficient to relieve the current pressures"
- "Fiscal policy was under active debate in the Congress, and the members viewed the outcome of that debate as very uncertain"
- "the precise timing and extent were impossible to predict" (inventory adjustment)
- "the outlook for this sector of the economy... was uncertain with regard to both the timing and the extent of possible slowing"

**Center of gravity**: Multiple sources of uncertainty explicitly acknowledged -- fiscal policy, inventory timing, whether growth will actually slow enough. The word "substantial" applied to risks elevates this. However, members had relatively clear views on the direction of the economy and policy, suggesting the uncertainty is real but bounded.

**Minority views**: None sharply different; uncertainty was broadly shared.
