# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2016-07-27

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Post-2009 segmented format with clear participant vs. staff sections.

**Era context**: Mid-2016. The Fed raised rates in December 2015 (first hike since 2009) and has been on hold since. Moderate growth, labor market nearing full employment, inflation running below 2% target. Brexit vote occurred during the intermeeting period. The broad macro backdrop is a cautious normalization phase.

**Quantifier distribution**: Heavy use of "most," "many," "several," "some," "a few," "a couple," and "one." The language suggests a range of views but with a clear center of gravity favoring patience.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target. "Inflation had continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective." Core PCE at 1.6%. "Most noted that the firming in various indicators of core inflation over the past year... provided support for their forecasts" that inflation would gradually rise to 2%. But "several viewed the risks to their inflation forecasts as weighted to the downside." Market-based inflation compensation "remained low." Direction: below target but gradually improving. The 12-month core at 1.6% and headline well below 2% puts this in the 35-42 range. The upward trajectory in core and H1 annualized rate near 2% nudges it slightly higher. Score ~40.
Polarity check: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says below target but firming. 40 fits (below 50, in the "below target; slack keeping prices in check" range).

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "Economic activity had been expanding at a moderate rate." "Most participants anticipated that economic growth would move up to a rate somewhat above its longer-run trend during the second half of 2016." GDP rising at moderate rate, near potential. No language about being clearly above or below potential overall. The economy is described as operating near potential with moderate growth. Bright-line rule: no explicit "above potential" or "below potential" language for the overall economy. "Moderate rate" and "near potential" suggests ~50. Score 49-51.
Polarity check: Higher = more above potential. Evidence says moderate growth, near trend. Score ~50.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Most participants judged that labor market conditions were at or approaching those consistent with maximum employment." Some believed convergence to moderate job gains needed "to prevent an unwanted increase in inflationary pressures" (suggesting near or at full employment). Others judged "labor utilization remained below that consistent with... maximum-employment objective." The center of gravity is at or approaching full employment. Score ~49. The split between "at" and "approaching" keeps it just below or at the midpoint.
Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Most say at or approaching max employment. 49 fits.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Split picture. "Household spending had been growing strongly" -- consumer spending rebounded. But "business fixed investment appeared to have declined further during the second quarter, with broad-based weakness." Net position: consumer strength partially offset by investment weakness. "Strong" consumer spending is above trend, but "declined further" in business investment is clearly below. Net effect is roughly at or slightly below trend. Score ~46.
Polarity check: Higher = stronger. Mixed signals, consumer strong but investment weak. 46 fits.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "U.S. financial conditions eased over the intermeeting period." "Major equity indexes rose, longer-term interest rates fell, credit spreads narrowed." "Mortgage rates had fallen further." Credit conditions "remained favorable." This clearly describes loose/eased conditions. Score ~30-35.
Polarity check: Higher = tighter. Evidence says conditions eased. Score ~32.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Decision: hold rates at 1/4 to 1/2 percent. "Members generally agreed that... it was prudent to accumulate more data." "Many judged that it was appropriate to wait for additional information." "Some other members anticipated that economic conditions would soon warrant taking another step." "One member preferred to raise the target range... at the current meeting." The center of gravity is hold with a mild tightening bias -- most want to wait but expect to hike eventually, "some" want to move soon, one wanted to hike now. Forward language: "only gradual increases." This is lean hawkish to balanced, with a patient bias. Score ~48-52. The fact that they held, most wanted to wait, but there's a clear expectation of hiking soon puts this at ~50-52.
Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish. Mostly patient but with expectation of future tightening. Score ~51.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One member preferred to raise rates now. "Some other members anticipated conditions would soon warrant" a hike. "Several suggested the Committee would likely have ample time to react" (more dovish lean). "A couple of members preferred also to wait for more evidence that inflation would rise." There's a range of views but broad agreement to hold. One implicit dissent (one member preferred hiking). This is moderate -- 1 dissent, some range of views. Score ~30.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Explicit paragraph on financial stability discussion. "Several participants commented on a few developments, including potential overvaluation in the market for CRE, the elevated level of equity values relative to expected earnings, and the incentives for investors to reach for yield." Also discussion of European banks. "Several expressed concern that an extended period of low interest rates risked intensifying incentives for investors to reach for yield." This is moderate discussion by several participants. Score ~40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "The Committee expected that economic conditions would evolve in a manner that would warrant only gradual increases in the federal funds rate." This signals future tightening but gradual/patient. "Some other members anticipated that economic conditions would soon warrant taking another step." The forward signal is mild tightening -- expect gradual hikes ahead. Score ~58-62.
Polarity check: Higher = more hawkish forward signal. "Gradual increases" expected. Score ~60.

Coherence check: HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (51) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (60) -- current action is hold/balanced but forward signal leans toward tightening. This makes sense: they held but expect to hike gradually. OUTPUT_GAP (50) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (49) -- both near balance, coherent.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Some noted that the Brexit vote had created uncertainty about the medium- to longer-run outlook." Near-term risks diminished but medium-term uncertainty from Brexit, China, global economy. "Some other participants indicated that they did not view the uncertainties attending the outlook to be unusually elevated." Mixed views on uncertainty. Overall somewhat elevated but not extreme. Score ~40.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score**: 40 +-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Inflation had continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective, partly reflecting earlier declines in energy prices and in prices of non-energy imports."
- "Core PCE inflation had risen from 1.3 percent a year earlier" but "continued to be held down by the pass-through of earlier declines in energy prices and by soft prices of imports."
- "Several viewed the risks to their inflation forecasts as weighted to the downside."
**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation running below target at 1.6% core PCE with gradual firming expected, but still clearly short of the 2% objective.
**Minority views**: Several see downside risks; a few caution about overshooting risks from tight labor market, widening the band somewhat.

### OUTPUT_GAP
**Score**: 50 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity had been expanding at a moderate rate."
- "Most participants anticipated that economic growth would move up to a rate somewhat above its longer-run trend during the second half of 2016."
- GDP "rose at a moderate rate in the second quarter."
**Center-of-gravity**: Economy operating near potential with moderate growth, expected to move slightly above trend in H2 2016.
**Minority views**: Some concern about continued weakness in business investment posing downside risks.

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score**: 49 +-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Most participants judged that labor market conditions were at or approaching those consistent with maximum employment."
- "Other participants continued to judge that labor utilization remained below that consistent with the Committee's maximum-employment objective."
- "Some participants noted that recent signs of a moderate step-up in wage increases provided further evidence of improving labor market conditions."
**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market at or approaching full employment, with a meaningful minority arguing slack remains.
**Minority views**: Some see slack remaining (lower score); a few caution about overshooting natural rate (higher score).

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score**: 46 +-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Household spending had been growing strongly" with "solid real income growth, gains in house and equity values, low gasoline prices."
- "Business fixed investment appeared to have declined further during the second quarter, with broad-based weakness in equipment and another steep drop in drilling and mining structures."
- "Continued weakness in business investment and the possibility of slower improvement in the housing sector posed some downside risks."
**Center-of-gravity**: Strong consumer spending offset by declining business investment, netting to roughly at or slightly below trend.
**Minority views**: Consumer side clearly positive; investment side clearly negative -- the net is uncertain.

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score**: 32 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "U.S. financial conditions eased over the intermeeting period: Major equity indexes rose, longer-term interest rates fell, credit spreads narrowed."
- "Credit conditions remained favorable: Mortgage rates had fallen further."
- Financial markets "recovered their earlier declines" post-Brexit.
**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions clearly loose/accommodative, having eased further over the intermeeting period.
**Minority views**: Narrow -- broad agreement that conditions eased.

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score**: 51 +-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Members generally agreed that... it was prudent to accumulate more data."
- "Many judged that it was appropriate to wait for additional information."
- "Some other members anticipated that economic conditions would soon warrant taking another step in removing policy accommodation."
- "One member preferred to raise the target range for the federal funds rate at the current meeting."
**Center-of-gravity**: Balanced to very slight hawkish lean -- hold now but with expectation of hiking in the near future for many members.
**Minority views**: One member wanted to hike now; several preferred waiting for more inflation evidence.

### DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score**: 30 +-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "One member preferred to raise the target range for the federal funds rate at the current meeting."
- Views varied on labor market implications: "some... believed that a convergence to a more moderate, sustainable pace of job gains would soon be necessary to prevent an unwanted increase in inflationary pressures. Other participants continued to judge that labor utilization remained below" max employment.
- Overall broad agreement to hold with varying rationales.
**Center-of-gravity**: One effective dissent with a range of views on timing but general agreement on the hold decision.
**Minority views**: Limited formal disagreement despite varied economic assessments.

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score**: 40 +-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants also discussed recent developments in financial markets and issues related to financial stability" -- dedicated paragraph.
- "Several participants commented on a few developments, including potential overvaluation in the market for CRE, the elevated level of equity values relative to expected earnings, and the incentives for investors to reach for yield."
- "Several expressed concern that an extended period of low interest rates risked intensifying incentives for investors to reach for yield."
**Center-of-gravity**: Moderate discussion of financial stability -- a dedicated section with several participants raising specific concerns, but not a dominant topic.
**Minority views**: Some participants more concerned about reach-for-yield dynamics.

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score**: 60 +-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The Committee expected that economic conditions would evolve in a manner that would warrant only gradual increases in the federal funds rate."
- "Some other members anticipated that economic conditions would soon warrant taking another step in removing policy accommodation."
- "Several suggested that the Committee would likely have ample time to react if inflation rose more quickly than they currently anticipated."
**Center-of-gravity**: Mild tightening bias forward -- gradual rate increases expected but with patience and data dependence.
**Minority views**: Some want to move sooner; several prefer extended patience.

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score**: 40 +-8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Some noted that the Brexit vote had created uncertainty about the medium- to longer-run outlook for foreign economies."
- "Participants judged that the incoming information, on the whole, had lowered the downside risks to the near-term economic outlook."
- "Some other participants indicated that they did not view the uncertainties attending the outlook to be unusually elevated and continued to see the risks to their economic forecasts as balanced."
**Center-of-gravity**: Somewhat elevated uncertainty driven by Brexit and global risks, but near-term risks seen as diminished. Split views on whether uncertainty is unusual.
**Minority views**: Some see risks as balanced/normal; others highlight China, European banks, and medium-term Brexit effects.
