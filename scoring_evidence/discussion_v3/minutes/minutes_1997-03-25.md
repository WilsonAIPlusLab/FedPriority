# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1997-03-25 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented (pre-2009), so I must focus on participant-attributed language ("members," "some," "a few," etc.) and ignore staff-attributed content.

**Era context**: Mid-1990s expansion. The economy is growing above trend, labor markets are tight, yet inflation remains subdued — the classic late-90s "new economy" puzzle. The Committee is beginning a preemptive tightening cycle (this meeting raises rates by 25bp).

**Quantifier distribution**: "members" (broad consensus) used frequently; "some" appears several times; "a few" used for those wanting larger tightening; "a majority" favored symmetric directive; "other members" wanted asymmetric. The "all" at conclusion indicates unanimous vote.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation risks as tilted upward but acknowledge current inflation is "remarkably subdued" and broad price measures show "slightly declining trend." The concern is forward-looking: "risks now seemed to be tilted more clearly toward higher inflation" due to tight labor markets and above-potential growth. Current data show no pickup; the risk is prospective. Direction: slightly below midpoint for current readings but above midpoint for risk perception. The rubric says "score what participants perceive NOW" — the gotcha says transitory language doesn't reduce score, but here the situation is reversed: inflation IS low now but risks are rising. The center of gravity is that inflation is currently subdued but risks have "increased appreciably." I'll score this as modestly above 50 — the concern is real and described as "more urgent" but actual inflation data are still favorable. Score ~55.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "economy that already was operating at high levels of resource utilization." Growth exceeds "estimated growth of potential." "Surprising strength in economic activity." Members can't find "signs of weakness or imbalances." This is clearly above potential. The bright-line rule: above potential = must be >50. The language "high levels of resource utilization" and growth exceeding potential growth suggests meaningfully above. Score ~58.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "labor markets already were tight" and "tightness in labor markets appeared to be intensifying." Widespread anecdotal reports, declining initial claims, high labor force participation inducing "marginal workers into the job market." Worker compensation shows "some acceleration." This is clearly tight and getting tighter. Score ~65.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending described as "ebullient," with "very high levels of consumer confidence," "recent surge in consumer demand." Business investment on "steep uptrend." Members see "major ongoing support" from consumers with risks "in the direction of more robust consumer spending." This is clearly above trend. Score ~63.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "financial conditions that, broadly considered, were not substantially different from those now prevailing" — suggesting conditions haven't tightened despite the economy running hot. Stock market wealth effects are boosting spending. Financing is "readily available" for commercial construction. Mortgage rates have risen somewhat but housing remains affordable. This suggests loose to neutral conditions. Score ~35.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Unanimous vote for a 25bp rate hike. This is described as "preemptive." "A few members argued that a more substantial tightening was needed." Others accepted the smaller move given uncertainties. The action is clearly hawkish — a rate increase with unanimous support. "A clear need for a preemptive policy action." Score ~65.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Unanimous vote (no dissents). But there was disagreement about the SIZE of the move ("a few" wanted more) and about the directive (majority wanted symmetric, "other members" wanted asymmetric but "could accept" symmetric). This is moderate internal discussion but no formal dissent. Score ~22.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Stock market wealth effects mentioned as boosting consumer spending. "Speculative building activity" noted in a few areas. Some concern about the "run-up in household net worth" from stock market. But financial stability per se is not a focus — these are mentioned as economic transmission mechanisms, not systemic risk concerns. Score ~18.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Symmetric directive adopted — "did not include a presumption about adjustments to policy during the intermeeting period." Majority felt developments wouldn't require further action in the intermeeting period. But "other members" believed additional tightening would be needed "sooner rather than later." The symmetric directive with a rate hike suggests a mild tightening lean but not a committed path. Score ~58.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "substantial uncertainty surrounded the timing and extent of such slowing." "Not possible to forecast with confidence when the period of favorable price behavior would end." "Outlook for economic activity and inflation remained subject to substantial uncertainties." The word "substantial" appears twice regarding uncertainty. This is somewhat elevated. Score ~42.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (58) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (65): Both above 50, labor tighter than output gap — consistent with the text emphasizing labor tightness more than capital constraints ("little evidence of growing demand pressures on capital resources").
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (65) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (58): Both lean hawkish, but forward guidance is less hawkish than current action due to symmetric directive. This makes sense — they hiked but signaled patience.

### Polarity verifications:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE: Higher = more pressure. Evidence shows rising risk but subdued current levels. 55 = modestly above midpoint. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP: Higher = more above potential. "High levels of resource utilization." 58 = clearly above. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter conditions. Conditions are described as not having tightened, financing readily available. 35 = somewhat loose. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 55 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "inflation had remained relatively subdued, and there were no signs in price data that it was picking up"
- "the risks of a rise in inflation down the road had increased appreciably"
- "inflationary developments in the economy had become a matter of more urgent concern for monetary policy"

**Center-of-gravity**: Current inflation is subdued, but members see appreciably increased forward risks due to tight resources and above-potential growth, making this a modestly above-midpoint score on net.

**Minority views**: No one argued inflation was actually rising currently; the debate was about the degree of forward risk.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 58 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the surprising strength in economic activity over the closing months of 1996 was persisting in 1997"
- "an economy that already was operating at high levels of resource utilization"
- "prospects for a substantial period of economic expansion at a rate that exceeded the estimated growth of potential"

**Center-of-gravity**: Members broadly agreed the economy was operating above potential with growth exceeding potential growth, placing this clearly above 50.

**Minority views**: Some noted eventual slowing was "inevitable" but timing uncertain.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 65 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "labor markets already were tight" and "tightness in labor markets appeared to be intensifying"
- "rise in labor force participation to a high percentage of the working age population had helped to keep the unemployment rate from falling, but the unexpected increase in participation was itself suggesting tight conditions"
- "data on worker compensation were somewhat mixed, but they suggested some acceleration on balance"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor markets described as tight and intensifying, with multiple data points and widespread anecdotal confirmation. Consistent with late-90s anchor of 65-75.

**Minority views**: None expressed a view that labor markets were slack.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 63 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "members emphasized the strength of consumer spending in recent months" with "an ebullient consumer sector"
- "Business fixed investment, which had remained on a steep uptrend for an extended period"
- "a number of members expressed the view...that the risks to such a forecast were in the direction of more robust consumer spending"

**Center-of-gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment described as strong, with upside risks to consumer spending. Clearly above trend.

**Minority views**: Some noted moderating influences (debt burdens, equipment spending growth expected to decline), but the net assessment was strongly positive.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 35 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "financial conditions that, broadly considered, were not substantially different from those now prevailing" (i.e., conditions have not tightened despite strong economy)
- "readily available financing" for commercial construction; "speculative building activity" emerging
- Stock market wealth effects boosting consumer spending; mortgage rates risen somewhat but housing still affordable

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions described as accommodative — financing available, wealth effects positive, no tightening despite strong economy. Somewhat loose.

**Minority views**: Mortgage rate increases noted as "somewhat inhibiting" for housing, but this is a minor offset.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 65 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all the members indicated that they supported or could accept a directive that called for a slight increase in the degree of pressure on reserve positions"
- "they saw a clear need for a preemptive policy action that would head off any pickup of inflation"
- "A few members argued that a more substantial tightening was needed"

**Center-of-gravity**: Unanimous support for a rate hike framed as preemptive. The center supported 25bp; a few wanted more. This is a moderate hawkish action.

**Minority views**: "A few" wanted larger tightening; "other members" felt caution warranted given uncertainties — but all voted for the hike.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 22 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Votes against this action: None" — unanimous
- "A few members argued that a more substantial tightening was needed"
- Debate over symmetric vs. asymmetric directive: "a majority...favored a symmetric directive" while "other members believed...additional tightening would be needed sooner rather than later"

**Center-of-gravity**: No formal dissent, but internal debate about size of move and directive tilt. Consistent with broad agreement with minor emphasis differences.

**Minority views**: "A few" wanting more, and "other members" wanting asymmetric directive, create moderate but contained disagreement.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 18 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Stock market wealth effects discussed as transmission mechanism to consumer spending: "run-up in household net worth stemming from the earlier buoyant performance of the stock market"
- "speculative building activity" noted in a few areas
- No discussion of systemic risk, asset bubbles as policy concern, or financial imbalances per se

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions mentioned as context for the economic outlook, not as a standalone stability concern. Consistent with 1990s-era low emphasis on financial stability.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 58 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Symmetric directive: "did not include a presumption about adjustments to policy during the intermeeting period"
- "additional policy tightening might be needed at some point, it did not appear very likely that developments during the intermeeting period would require a further policy move"
- "Other members believed...additional tightening would be needed sooner rather than later"

**Center-of-gravity**: The symmetric directive signals no preset path, but the context of a rate hike and discussion of possible further tightening creates a mild hawkish lean in forward tone.

**Minority views**: Some members explicitly wanted asymmetric (tightening-biased) directive.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 42 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "substantial uncertainty surrounded the timing and extent of such slowing in the quarters ahead"
- "it was not possible to forecast with confidence when the period of favorable price behavior would end"
- "the outlook for economic activity and inflation remained subject to substantial uncertainties"

**Center-of-gravity**: The word "substantial" is used twice to describe uncertainty, particularly about when the expansion would slow and when inflation would rise. This is somewhat elevated above normal but not extreme — the basic economic picture is clear even if timing is uncertain.
