## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 30 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Inflation was continuing to run below the Committee's longer-run objective, reflecting in part continued reductions in oil prices and falling import prices."
- "Most participants saw no clear evidence of a broad-based acceleration in wages."
- "Participants generally anticipated that inflation was likely to decline further in the near term, reflecting the reduction in oil prices and the effects of the rise in the foreign exchange value of the dollar on import prices."
- "A number of participants saw a risk that it could run persistently below their 2 percent objective."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Participants see inflation clearly below target and expected to decline further near term, driven by energy price declines and dollar strength, with no wage pressure emerging and downside risks to inflation persistence.

**Minority views**: "A couple of others noted that if the unemployment rate continued to decline quickly, wage and price inflation could rise more than generally anticipated" (upside inflation risk, small minority).

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 44 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Economic activity was expanding at a moderate pace."
- "Participants judged that the underutilization of labor resources was continuing to diminish."
- "Several participants, pointing to indicators of consumer and business confidence as well as to the solid record of payroll employment gains in 2014, suggested that the real economy may end up showing more momentum than anticipated."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The economy is expanding moderately and approaching potential, but underutilization persists — placing the output gap slightly below the midpoint with the gap narrowing.

**Minority views**: Several saw possible upside momentum; a few thought the energy price boost could be "quite large."

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 42 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants saw broad-based improvement in labor market conditions over the intermeeting period, including solid gains in payroll employment, a slight reduction in the unemployment rate, and increases in the rates of hiring and quits."
- "The levels of these measures suggested to some participants that there remained more labor market slack than was indicated by the unemployment rate alone."
- "Most participants saw no clear evidence of a broad-based acceleration in wages."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The labor market is improving meaningfully across multiple indicators but has not yet reached full employment, with most participants seeing remaining slack and no broad wage acceleration.

**Minority views**: "A few others continued to view the unemployment rate as a reliable indicator of overall labor market conditions and saw a narrower degree of labor underutilization remaining." "A few participants suggested that the recent uptick in the employment cost index or average hourly earnings could be a tentative sign of an upturn in wage growth."

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 55 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Household spending continued to advance over the intermeeting period, and reports from contacts in several parts of the country indicated that recent retail or auto sales had been robust."
- "Many participants pointed to relatively high levels of consumer confidence as signaling near-term strength in discretionary consumer spending, and most participants judged that the recent significant decline in energy prices would provide a boost to consumer spending."
- "Manufacturing activity was strong." "Information from some regions pointed to a pickup in capital investment."
- "Residential construction continued to be slow."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Consumer spending is robust and supported by energy savings, confidence, and job gains; business investment and manufacturing are solid; but housing remains a drag, netting to moderately above trend.

**Minority views**: Oil/gas capital investment expected to slow; housing sector sluggish.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 30 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:

- Discussion of "very low term premiums" in market-based measures.
- Participants noted asset price movements influenced by foreign growth concerns and expected ECB/BOJ easing, but no mention of tightening conditions domestically.

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Financial conditions appear accommodative, with low interest rates and low term premiums; no discussion of credit tightness or stress.

**Minority views**: None expressed regarding tight conditions.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 50 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Most participants agreed that it would be useful to state that the Committee judges that it can be patient in beginning to normalize the stance of monetary policy."
- "Most participants thought the reference to patience indicated that the Committee was unlikely to begin the normalization process for at least the next couple of meetings."
- "Two members thought that this forward guidance did not take sufficient account of the progress" (hawkish lean).
- "Some participants emphasized that policy will still be highly accommodative for a time after the first increase."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The Committee is balanced between maintaining accommodation and preparing the ground for normalization, with the shift from "considerable time" to "patient" representing a small hawkish evolution while still committing to patience.

**Minority views**: Two members wanted more hawkish guidance; some emphasized continued accommodation post-liftoff.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 35 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Most participants agreed" on the patient language, but "two members thought that this forward guidance did not take sufficient account of the progress."
- Disagreement on labor slack: "some participants" saw more slack vs. "a few others" saw narrower underutilization.
- "Some participants regarded the revised language as risking an unwarranted concentration of market expectations."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Moderate disagreement on forward guidance language and the degree of remaining labor market slack, with two members expressing explicit reservations but no outright fracture.

**Minority views**: Two members on the hawkish side; some concerned about language precision.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 48 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Shift from "considerable time" to "patient" — a small step toward normalization.
- "Most participants thought the reference to patience indicated that the Committee was unlikely to begin the normalization process for at least the next couple of meetings."
- "Participants generally stressed the need to communicate that the timing of the first increase in the federal funds rate would depend on the incoming data."
- "Some participants emphasized that policy will still be highly accommodative for a time after the first increase in the federal funds rate target."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: The forward signal is near-neutral with a mild tightening lean — normalization is on the horizon but data-dependent and not imminent, with "patient" implying at least two more meetings of inaction.

**Minority views**: Two members wanted to signal readiness sooner; a few wanted more condition-based language.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 38 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The risks to the outlook for economic activity and the labor market were seen as nearly balanced."
- "Many of them thought that a further deterioration in the foreign economic situation could result in slower domestic economic growth than they currently expected."
- "Participants generally agreed that it would take more time and analysis to draw definitive conclusions regarding the recent behavior of inflation compensation."
- "A number of participants saw a risk that [inflation] could run persistently below their 2 percent objective."

**Center-of-gravity summary**: Uncertainty is somewhat elevated, with genuine ambiguity about inflation dynamics (declining market-based compensation, oil price effects) and international risks, though growth risks are seen as nearly balanced.

**Minority views**: Some more confident about growth outlook; others more concerned about downside international risks.
