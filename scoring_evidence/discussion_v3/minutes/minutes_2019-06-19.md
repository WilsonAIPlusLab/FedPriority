# FOMC Meeting Scoring — 2019-06-19 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Post-2009 segmented format with clear separation of staff review and participant discussion sections. Quantifier language is used throughout.

**Era context**: June 2019 — late-cycle expansion. The Fed had paused its hiking cycle (last hike Dec 2018) and was about to pivot toward rate cuts. Trade tensions with China and Mexico had escalated sharply. Inflation was running persistently below 2%. Unemployment was near 50-year lows. This was the meeting where the Committee removed "patient" language and signaled readiness to cut.

**Quantifier distribution**: "many" appears frequently (inflation risks, momentum loss, yield curve, case for accommodation). "Several" used for specific observations (investment weakness, labor market revisions, yield curve concerns). "Nearly all" for rate path revisions down and for holding rates this meeting. "A few" for outlier views (slack remaining, inflation expectations already below target). "A couple" favored cutting at this meeting, one member actually voted for a cut.

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as BELOW target. Readings "lower than expected," "muted inflation pressures," PCE below 2%, core below 2%. "Many" viewed inflation risks weighted to the downside. "Global disinflationary pressures" cited. Market-based inflation compensation had declined. This is clearly below-target inflation, consistent with the 2019 environment. The rubric anchor for 2013-19 below target is 38-42. The language here is somewhat more concerned than typical 2013-19 — multiple references to downside risks, inflation expectations possibly slipping. Score: ~36. Higher = more pressure. Evidence says less pressure. Score below 50 confirmed.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants describe "sustained expansion" and "moderate pace" of growth as baseline. Labor market "strong," unemployment at 50-year low. But "economy appeared to have lost some momentum," business investment "soft," manufacturing "posted declines." The baseline is still positive growth near potential, but risks are tilting down. The economy hasn't opened a negative output gap yet — it's still operating near or modestly above potential (low unemployment suggests at or slightly above), but momentum is weakening. The bright-line rule: unemployment well below estimated natural rate, labor market "strong" — this implies at or slightly above potential. Score: ~50-52. Several participants revised down longer-run unemployment estimates, suggesting the gap may be smaller than previously thought. Score: 50.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Very low unemployment rate," "strong labor market conditions," job gains "solid on average." Business contacts report "continued strong labor demand, with many firms planning to hire more workers." However, May job gains weaker than expected, and "a few participants cited the combination of muted inflation pressures, moderate wage growth, and expanding employment as a possible indication that some slack remained." Center of gravity: tight labor market but with some questions emerging. Anchor for 2016-17 is 50; this is tighter — 50-year low unemployment. Score: ~57. A few see slack, so uncertainty band widens.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "solid," "rebounded from the weak patch earlier in the year," supported by strong labor market and rising incomes. But business investment is the big concern — "softness in various indicators of business fixed investment," "shipments and orders of new capital goods looked weak," "manufacturing surveys had dropped sharply," firms "putting capital expenditures or hiring plans on hold." Housing "continued softness." Net: consumer spending positive, business investment clearly negative. This is a mixed picture that nets to slightly below trend. Score: ~43.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Overall financial conditions remained supportive of growth" — but "premised importantly on expectations that the Federal Reserve would ease policy." Equity prices declined, corporate spreads widened, Treasury yields fell sharply. Yield curve inverted (10y-3m spread negative). But financing conditions for businesses and households "generally remained supportive." This is slightly tighter than neutral but still mostly accommodative. Anchor for 2017-19 normal is 45-55. Conditions have tightened somewhat from trade tensions but remain broadly supportive. Score: ~48.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: Held rates but strong dovish pivot. "Many participants indicated that the case for somewhat more accommodative policy had strengthened." "Many judged additional monetary policy accommodation would be warranted in the near term." Nearly all revised down rate paths. Removed "patient" language, replaced with "act as appropriate to sustain the expansion." A couple favored cutting at this meeting. One formal dissent for a 25bp cut. Only "some" suggested no strong case for a cut yet, and "a few" worried about overheating risk from easing. Center of gravity: clear dovish lean, leaning toward imminent cuts but not pulling the trigger yet. Anchor: 2014-15 pre-liftoff is 40-50. This is a hold-with-strong-easing-bias. Score: ~35.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: One formal dissent (Bullard for 25bp cut). Beyond that, "nearly all" agreed to hold. But within the hold camp, there's a range: "many" want to cut soon, "some" want to wait for more data, "a few" worry about easing. This is moderate disagreement — broad agreement to hold but meaningful differences about forward path. Score: ~35.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Discussed yield curve inversion at some length ("many" noted negative 10y-3m spread, "several" noted increased recession risk from yield curve). "A few" pointed to corporate debt growth and "generally high asset valuations" as warranting monitoring. The repo facility discussion at the beginning is more operational/technical. Financial stability gets moderate attention but is not a dominant topic. Score: ~35.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: Very strong easing signal forward. Removed "patient" language, replaced with "act as appropriate to sustain the expansion." Nearly all revised rate paths down. "Many judged additional monetary policy accommodation would be warranted in the near term." "Several" noted risk-management case for near-term cut. Markets pricing 85% chance of July cut. This is a clear easing lean forward. Anchor: 2019 mid-cycle cuts is 35-45. This is the meeting right before the first cut, with very strong signaling. Score: ~30.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Uncertainties and downside risks surrounding the economic outlook had increased significantly over recent weeks." "Many participants attached significant odds to scenarios with less favorable outcomes." Trade uncertainty is a dominant theme throughout. "Elevated levels of uncertainty regarding the global outlook." Multiple references to monitoring incoming data. This is clearly elevated uncertainty. Score: ~58.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (50) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (57): Slight divergence is explained by the fact that labor market remains tight from prior cycle strength while output momentum is weakening — a late-cycle pattern. Reasonable.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (35) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (30): Both dovish, with forward tone slightly more dovish — consistent with the "hold now but signal cuts ahead" pattern. Aligned.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 36 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants noted that readings on overall inflation and inflation for items other than food and energy had come in lower than expected over recent months."
- "Many participants viewed the risks to the outlook for inflation as weighted to the downside."
- "Inflation pressures remained muted and some readings on inflation expectations were at low levels."

**Center-of-gravity**: Inflation running below 2% target with downside risks; participants concerned about persistent undershooting and possible slippage in expectations.

**Minority views**: "Several participants commented that alternative measures of inflation that removed the influence of unusually large changes in the prices of individual items... were running around 2 percent." "Several participants observed that the trimmed mean measure... had stayed near 2 percent recently." These provide the upward uncertainty.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 50 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Under their baseline outlook, the labor market was likely to remain strong with economic activity growing at a moderate pace."
- "The economy appeared to have lost some momentum" — business investment soft, manufacturing declining.
- Unemployment at 50-year low but several participants revised down longer-run natural rate estimates.

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy operating near potential — strong labor market offsets weakening business investment and manufacturing. Growth at a "moderate pace" consistent with near-potential output.

**Minority views**: A few see some remaining slack in labor market; conversely, labor market tightness suggests slightly above potential.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 57 ±7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants cited evidence that conditions remained strong, including the very low unemployment rate and the fact that job gains had been solid, on average, in recent months."
- "Reports from business contacts pointed to continued strong labor demand, with many firms planning to hire more workers."
- "A few participants cited the combination of muted inflation pressures, moderate wage growth, and expanding employment as a possible indication that some slack remained in the labor market."

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market clearly tight with 50-year low unemployment, but not overheating — wage growth moderate, some questioning whether slack remains.

**Minority views**: A few see remaining slack; several revised down natural rate estimates, which would reduce perceived tightness.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 43 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Available data on consumer spending had been solid, supported by a strong labor market and rising incomes."
- "Incoming data on shipments and orders of new capital goods looked weak and recent readings from some manufacturing surveys had dropped sharply."
- "Contacts reported that softer export sales, weaker economic activity abroad, and elevated levels of uncertainty regarding the global outlook were weighing on business sentiment and leading firms to reassess plans for investment spending."

**Center-of-gravity**: Consumer spending solid but business investment notably weak; net picture is slightly below trend. Housing also soft.

**Minority views**: "A couple of participants, however, pointed to signs that investment might pick up."

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 48 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Overall financial conditions remained supportive of growth, those conditions appeared to be premised importantly on expectations that the Federal Reserve would ease policy in the near term."
- "Many participants noted that the spread between the 10-year and 3-month Treasury yields was now negative."
- Corporate spreads widened, equity prices declined, but financing conditions for businesses and households "generally remained supportive."

**Center-of-gravity**: Conditions broadly neutral — still supportive of growth but with some tightening signals (yield curve inversion, widened spreads, equity declines). Support contingent on expected policy easing.

**Minority views**: None prominent — broad agreement that conditions were supportive but conditional.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 35 ±8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Many participants indicated that the case for somewhat more accommodative policy had strengthened."
- "Many judged additional monetary policy accommodation would be warranted in the near term should these recent developments prove to be sustained."
- "A couple of participants favored a cut in the target range at this meeting." One formal dissent for a 25bp cut.
- "Some participants suggested... there was not yet a strong case for a rate cut from current levels."

**Center-of-gravity**: Clear dovish lean — held rates but strong signaling toward imminent easing. Majority sees accommodation warranted soon. Removed "patient" language.

**Minority views**: "Some" want to wait for more data. "A few" worry easing could overheat labor markets or fuel financial imbalances.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 35 ±5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Nearly all members agreed to maintain the target range" — one formal dissent (for a 25bp cut).
- Range of forward views: "many" want near-term accommodation, "some" prefer waiting, "a few" concerned about risks of easing.
- Broad agreement on holding but meaningful spread on forward path.

**Center-of-gravity**: One formal dissent plus moderate differences on timing of easing. Not a deep split but more than minor nuance.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 35 ±5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Many participants noted that the spread between the 10-year and 3-month Treasury yields was now negative, and several noted that their assessment of the risk of a slowing in the economic expansion had increased based on either the shape of the yield curve or other financial and economic indicators."
- "A few participants pointed to the growth in debt issuance by nonfinancial corporations and still generally high asset valuations as developments that warranted continued monitoring."
- Extensive technical discussion of standing repo facility (more operational than stability-focused).

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions/stability receive moderate attention — yield curve inversion discussed meaningfully, corporate debt and valuations flagged, but not a dominant concern relative to trade and growth risks.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 30 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Members agreed to remove the 'patient' language and to emphasize instead that... the Committee would closely monitor the implications of incoming information for the economic outlook and would act as appropriate to sustain the expansion."
- "Nearly all participants had revised down their assessment of the appropriate path for the federal funds rate over the projection period in their SEP submissions."
- "Many judged additional monetary policy accommodation would be warranted in the near term."

**Center-of-gravity**: Strong easing signal forward — removed patient language, signaled readiness to cut, nearly all marked down rate paths. Clear tilt toward imminent accommodation.

**Minority views**: "Some" see flatter trajectory but not yet a strong case for a cut.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 58 ±6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Participants judged that uncertainties and downside risks surrounding the economic outlook had increased significantly over recent weeks."
- "Many participants attached significant odds to scenarios with less favorable outcomes."
- "Many participants noted that they viewed the risks to their growth and inflation projections, such as those emanating from greater uncertainty about trade, as shifting notably over recent weeks."

**Center-of-gravity**: Elevated uncertainty driven by trade tensions, global growth concerns, and recent negative data surprises. Not extreme or unprecedented, but clearly above normal levels.

**Minority views**: Limited — near-unanimous agreement that uncertainty had risen significantly.
