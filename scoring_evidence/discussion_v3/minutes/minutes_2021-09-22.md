# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2021-09-22 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Post-2009 segmented format with clear participant sections separated from staff reviews.

**Era context**: September 2021 — the economy is recovering from the COVID-19 pandemic. Delta variant is spreading. Inflation has surged above target due to supply-demand imbalances from reopening. The FOMC is at the zero lower bound with ongoing QE ($120B/month), actively discussing tapering. This is a pivotal meeting where "all participants agreed" taper signaling language would be in the statement.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all participants" (taper language in statement), "most" (substantial further progress on price stability met), "many" (short-term inflation expectations rising; payroll shortfalls but continued improvement; risk-management considerations), "several" (participation rate lag; preferred faster taper; inflation pressures broader OR concentrated), "some" (elevated inflation feeding into expectations; labor supply constraints as main impediment; inflation risks upside), "a number of" (substantial further progress on employment not yet met; also number who said it was met), "a few" (no evidence wage-price pass-through yet; downside inflation risks; participation rate not recovering as expected), "various" (complete return to pre-pandemic labor unlikely), "a couple" (inventories at record lows; advance notice of taper).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as clearly above target. "Inflation rate was elevated, and they expected that it would likely remain so in coming months." Participants "marked up their inflation projections" due to supply constraints being "larger and likely to be longer lasting than previously anticipated." "Some participants expressed concerns that elevated rates of inflation could feed through into longer-term inflation expectations." However, "several other participants pointed out that the largest contributors...were a handful of COVID-related, pandemic-sensitive categories." The center of gravity is elevated inflation with debate about persistence. The rubric anchor for 2021 Q3 is 65-72. Score should be in the 67-70 range. Higher = more inflation pressure. Evidence says elevated, above target, broadening concerns. Score matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: The economy is expanding but "at a less rapid pace than in the first half of the year." Participants "marked down their projections of real GDP growth for the year." Supply constraints and Delta variant are restraining activity. However, the economy "had continued to expand in recent months" and showed "resilience." Consumer spending "decelerated" but was supported by savings/pent-up demand. The economy is still recovering — employment has only retraced "three-fourths" of pandemic losses. Firms facing challenges "keeping up with strong demand" due to supply bottlenecks suggests demand is strong but supply-constrained. The staff projects GDP "well above potential throughout the projection period." The output gap is modestly above potential given strong demand but supply constraints limiting actual output. Score around 52-55. The bright-line rule: staff says "well above potential" but participants focus more on the recovery being incomplete due to Delta/supply issues. Participants' own framing emphasizes slowing from earlier pace and uneven recovery but strong demand. Score: 53.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: Unemployment at 5.2%, down from 5.9% — still well above pre-pandemic ~3.5%. However, "District contacts had broadly reported having difficulty hiring workers." "Labor shortages were causing firms to reduce hours and scale back production." "Labor demand was extraordinarily high." Wage growth robust at 4.9% annualized. But "the economy was still well below maximum employment" per several participants. Labor force participation "little changed, remaining at a lower level than its pre-pandemic values." The labor market is tight in terms of hiring difficulty and wage pressure but slack remains in terms of unemployment and participation. This is the unusual combination of tight flow conditions with slack stock conditions. Center of gravity: moderately tight but not at full employment. Score ~53-56. The tightness signals (shortages, wages, difficulty hiring) are strong but the level indicators (5.2% unemployment, low LFPR) suggest not yet at full employment. Score: 55.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumer spending had decelerated in recent months after expanding at a very rapid pace in the first half of the year." Delta variant weighing on services, supply constraints restraining goods spending. But "accumulated stock of savings, the release of pent-up demand, and progress on vaccinations" expected to support spending. Business sector: firms "facing challenges keeping up with strong demand due to widespread supply chain bottlenecks." Residential construction restrained by shortages. Investment being held back by supply constraints, not lack of demand. The picture is above-trend demand being supply-constrained. Score around 57-60 — demand is strong but actual spending has decelerated from its earlier surge. The anchor for 2021 Q3 is 62-68. Given the deceleration noted, I'll score slightly below the top of that range: 60.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Overall financial conditions remained accommodative, in part reflecting policy measures to support the economy." This is stated clearly by participants. Financial conditions are loose/accommodative. The market discussion notes conditions "remained highly accommodative." Score should be low (loose = low tightness). Around 20-25. Higher = tighter. Evidence says accommodative = loose. Score should be low. Score: 22.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: This is the critical meeting where taper signaling crystallizes. "All participants agreed that it would be appropriate for the current meeting's postmeeting statement to relay...a moderation in the pace of asset purchases may soon be warranted." "Most participants remarked that the standard of 'substantial further progress' had been met with regard to the Committee's price-stability goal." On rates: "Various participants stressed that economic conditions were likely to justify keeping the rate at or near its lower bound over the next couple of years." But "a number of participants raised the possibility of beginning to increase the target range by the end of next year." "Several participants indicated that they preferred to proceed with a more rapid moderation of purchases." The action is hold rates + signal imminent taper. This is a lean-hawkish posture in the context of being at ZLB with QE. During QE debates, those arguing about tapering are moving from the 15-25 range toward 40-50+. The unanimous agreement on taper signaling + some wanting faster taper + some wanting rate hikes by end 2022 = solidly moving toward tightening. But rate hikes are still distant. Score: 52-55. The forward lean is clearly toward tightening (taper imminent) but actual action is still hold. Score: 53.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: There is notable disagreement. On employment progress: "a number of participants assessed that the standard...had not yet been attained" vs "a number of other participants indicated that they believed that the test...had been met." On inflation: some see broader pressures, several others see it as COVID-specific categories. On rates: various want rates low for couple years, others want hikes by end 2022. Several want faster taper. But there was no formal dissent — "all participants agreed" on the statement language. The spread of views is substantial but they converged on a common statement. Score: 40-45. Notable internal disagreement about interpretation but unified on the statement. Score: 42.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Limited discussion. "Some participants mentioned the risks associated with high asset valuations in the United States and abroad." "Several participants expressed concern that the high degree of accommodation being provided by monetary policy, including through continued asset purchases, could increase risks to financial stability." Also debt ceiling concerns mentioned by "a number of participants." This is moderate — a few mentions of asset valuations and financial stability risks, not a dominant theme. Score: 30-35. Score: 32.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: The forward signal is clearly toward tightening: taper imminent ("may soon be warranted"), with illustrative path ending QE by mid-2022. "A number of participants raised the possibility of beginning to increase the target range by the end of next year." But "various participants stressed that economic conditions were likely to justify keeping the rate at or near its lower bound over the next couple of years." The forward guidance is moving toward normalization — taper is the concrete near-term signal, rate hikes are debated but not imminent. The existing forward guidance on rates remains dovish (outcome-based). Score: 55-60. The taper signal is a mild tightening signal; rate hikes debated but not consensus. Score: 57.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Uncertainty remained high." "A number of participants judged that the uncertain course of the virus, supply chain disruptions, and labor shortages complicated the task of interpreting incoming economic data." "Risks to the outlook for economic activity as broadly balanced" but "most participants saw inflation risks as weighted to the upside." The uncertainty language is genuine — Delta variant, supply chains, labor supply dynamics all cited as sources. Score: 58-62. "Uncertainty remained high" is a clear signal. Score: 60.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:

- OUTPUT_GAP (53) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (55): Both modestly above 50, coherent. The economy is near or slightly above potential with labor market tighter in flow terms than stock terms.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (53) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (57): Both lean hawkish, with forward guidance slightly more hawkish than current stance (taper signaling is forward-looking). Coherent.
- INFLATION_PRESSURE (68) is high while HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (53) is only modestly hawkish — this is consistent with the meeting's context: participants acknowledge elevated inflation but many view it as transitory, and the labor market recovery is incomplete, so they are moving gradually toward tightening rather than aggressively.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 68 ±8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "The inflation rate was elevated, and they expected that it would likely remain so in coming months before moderating."
- "Participants marked up their inflation projections, as they assessed that supply constraints in product and labor markets were larger and likely to be longer lasting than previously anticipated."
- "Some participants expressed concerns that elevated rates of inflation could feed through into longer-term inflation expectations."
- Minority: "Several other participants pointed out that the largest contributors to the recent elevated measures of inflation were a handful of COVID-related, pandemic-sensitive categories."

Center-of-gravity: Inflation is clearly above target and perceived as persistent enough to warrant upward revisions, though debate exists about breadth and duration. Consistent with the 65-72 anchor for 2021 Q3.

Minority views: Several see it as narrow/transitory (would push toward 60), while some see broadening pressures and expectations risks (would push toward 75). Wide uncertainty band reflects this split.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 53 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Economic activity had continued to expand in recent months, though at a less rapid pace than in the first half of the year."
- "Firms in a number of industries were facing challenges keeping up with strong demand due to widespread supply chain bottlenecks as well as labor shortages."
- Staff projection (referenced by participants): GDP "well above potential throughout the projection period."
- Participants "marked down their projections of real GDP growth for the year."

Center-of-gravity: Demand is running above potential (firms can't keep up) but actual output is constrained by supply bottlenecks and Delta variant. Modestly above potential.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 55 ±8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "District contacts had broadly reported having difficulty hiring workers. The labor shortages were causing firms to reduce hours and scale back production."
- "Average hourly earnings were up 4.9 percent at an annualized rate over the past six months."
- "The economy was still well below maximum employment" (several participants).
- Labor force participation "remaining at a lower level than its pre-pandemic values."

Center-of-gravity: Unusual combination — very tight hiring conditions (difficulty hiring, strong wage growth) coexisting with elevated unemployment (5.2%) and depressed participation. The flow tightness dominates the discussion but level-based slack is acknowledged. Moderately tight overall.

Minority views: Those emphasizing labor supply constraints as main impediment (tighter reading) vs. those noting economy "well below maximum employment" (slacker reading) create the wide band.

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 60 ±7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Consumer spending had decelerated in recent months after expanding at a very rapid pace in the first half of the year."
- "Accumulated stock of savings, the release of pent-up demand, and progress on vaccinations to continue to support household spending."
- "Firms in a number of industries were facing challenges keeping up with strong demand."
- Residential construction "restrained by shortages of materials."

Center-of-gravity: Demand remains strong and above trend, but actual spending has decelerated from the earlier surge due to Delta and supply constraints. Above trend but not at the peak pace.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 22 ±5 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Overall financial conditions remained accommodative, in part reflecting policy measures to support the economy and the flow of credit to U.S. households and businesses."
- Market discussion: "Domestic financial conditions were little changed, on net, and remained highly accommodative."
- Staff review: "Market-based financing conditions were robust, and credit availability improved for riskier borrowers."

Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are clearly loose/accommodative. Very little disagreement on this assessment.

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 53 ±8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "All participants agreed that...a moderation in the pace of asset purchases may soon be warranted."
- "Several participants indicated that they preferred to proceed with a more rapid moderation of purchases."
- "Various participants stressed that economic conditions were likely to justify keeping the rate at or near its lower bound over the next couple of years."
- "A number of participants raised the possibility of beginning to increase the target range by the end of next year."

Center-of-gravity: Lean hawkish — unanimous agreement on taper signaling represents a shift toward tightening from the pure QE stance. But rates remain at zero and the taper is still ahead, not yet started. The lean is mild but clearly in the tightening direction.

Minority views: Dovish camp wants rates low for years; hawkish camp wants rate hikes by end-2022 and faster taper.

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 42 ±7 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- On employment progress: "A number of participants assessed that the standard...had not yet been attained" vs. "a number of other participants indicated that they believed that the test...had been met."
- On inflation interpretation: "Some participants expressed concerns that elevated rates of inflation could feed through into longer-term inflation expectations" vs. "Several other participants pointed out that the largest contributors...were a handful of COVID-related, pandemic-sensitive categories."
- On rate path: "various participants" favor low rates for years vs. "a number of participants" raise possibility of hikes by end-2022.
- No formal dissent: "All participants agreed" on statement language.

Center-of-gravity: Substantial internal disagreement on interpretation of economic conditions and appropriate pace of normalization, but participants converged on a unified statement. No formal dissent.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 32 ±6 [Moderate confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Some participants mentioned the risks associated with high asset valuations in the United States and abroad."
- "Several participants expressed concern that the high degree of accommodation being provided by monetary policy, including through continued asset purchases, could increase risks to financial stability."
- "A number of participants commented on the importance of resolving the issues involving the federal government budget and debt ceiling in a timely manner."

Center-of-gravity: Financial stability receives limited but meaningful attention — "some" and "several" mentions of asset valuations and monetary-policy-related financial stability risks. Not a dominant theme but more than passing reference.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 57 ±8 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- Taper signal: "If progress continued broadly as expected, a moderation in the pace of asset purchases may soon be warranted" — with illustrative path ending purchases "around the middle of next year."
- "A number of participants raised the possibility of beginning to increase the target range by the end of next year."
- Dovish counterweight: "Various participants stressed that economic conditions were likely to justify keeping the rate at or near its lower bound over the next couple of years."
- "The Committee's 'substantial further progress' standard regarding its asset purchases was distinct from the criteria given in its forward guidance on the federal funds rate."

Center-of-gravity: The forward signal is mildly hawkish — taper is imminent and clearly communicated, but rate hikes remain debated and distant. The explicit separation of taper from rate hikes moderates the hawkish forward signal.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 60 ±6 [High confidence]**

Evidence:
- "Uncertainty remained high."
- "A number of participants judged that the uncertain course of the virus, supply chain disruptions, and labor shortages complicated the task of interpreting incoming economic data and assessing progress toward the Committee's goals."
- "Most participants saw inflation risks as weighted to the upside."
- "Participants generally saw the risks to the outlook for economic activity as broadly balanced."

Center-of-gravity: Elevated uncertainty driven by Delta variant, supply chain dynamics, and labor supply questions. The language "uncertainty remained high" is genuine, and multiple sources of uncertainty are cited. Inflation risks skewed upside.

### Optional Dimensions

**INFLATION_GOODS**: Discussed prominently — supply chain bottlenecks for goods, especially motor vehicles/semiconductors, driving elevated goods prices. Score: 73 ±7.
Evidence: "Supply chain bottlenecks were creating challenges for a number of manufacturers; the problems were seen as especially acute for the motor vehicle industry." "Low inventories and high prices due to supply constraints were restraining spending on many goods."

**INFLATION_SERVICES**: Brief mentions — Delta weighing on services demand, "slowing in demand for services sensitive to social distancing." Not prominent enough for a confident score. Score: N/A.

**INFLATION_HOUSING**: Discussed by many participants. "Many participants pointed out that the owners' equivalent rent component of price indexes should be monitored carefully, as rising home prices could lead to upward pressure on rents." Score: 62 ±7.
Evidence: "Demand for housing appeared to have remained very strong." "Rising home prices could lead to upward pressure on rents."

**INFLATION_EXPECTATIONS**: Discussed prominently. "Several participants observed that measures of longer-term inflation expectations...had remained in ranges that were viewed as broadly consistent with the Committee's longer-run inflation goal." But "many participants noted the substantial rise in one- and three-year measures of inflation expectations." Score: 55 ±7.
Evidence: Longer-term expectations anchored, short-term expectations rising substantially. Net: modestly above anchored-at-target.

**CREDIT_STRESS**: N/A — credit conditions discussed in staff section, not prominently by participants. Participants note accommodative conditions broadly.

**EQUITY_STRESS**: N/A — "some participants mentioned the risks associated with high asset valuations" but this is an overvaluation concern (low on the scale), and it's only "some" with no detailed discussion.

**BANKING_STRESS**: N/A — no participant discussion of banking system stress.

**TREASURY_STRESS**: N/A — debt ceiling concerns mentioned but not Treasury market dysfunction per se.
