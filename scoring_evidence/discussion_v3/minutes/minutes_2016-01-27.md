# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2016-01-27

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized participant views). Segmented -- participant discussion is clearly separated from staff content.

**Era context**: January 2016 -- one meeting after the December 2015 liftoff (first rate hike since 2006). Backdrop includes: global financial market turbulence (China slowdown fears), oil price collapse, strong labor market but soft GDP and manufacturing, dollar appreciation, and inflation well below 2%. Markets were volatile and financial conditions had tightened notably since December.

**Quantifier distribution**: "most" appears frequently (medium-term inflation expectations, view that premature to alter outlook), "many" (downside risks, uncertainty increased), "several" (moderation in employment gains, inflation depending on labor market, importance of monitoring inflation), "some" (tighter financing for speculative-grade, concern about inflation expectations, mixed reports), "a few" (slack largely eliminated, direct evidence of inflation needed), "a couple" (mortgage regulations, equity valuations more in line).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target. "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective." Further declines in energy prices and dollar appreciation pushing inflation lower. "Most continued to anticipate that inflation would rise to 2 percent over the medium term" but "a number of participants indicated that...they viewed the outlook for inflation as somewhat more uncertain or saw the risks as being to the downside." Market-based inflation compensation at "historically low levels." Current inflation is clearly below target with transitory factors cited, but downside risks are growing. Direction: below midpoint. Magnitude: "below" with downside risks, energy and dollar headwinds -- somewhat below target = 30-38 range. Score ~33.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Mixed signals. Labor market is strong (unemployment at 5.0%, "close to or below most participants' estimates of its longer-run normal rate"), but GDP growth was slow and spending/production data disappointing. "Most policymakers thought...it was premature to alter appreciably their assessment of the medium-term economic outlook. They continued to anticipate that economic activity would expand at a moderate pace." The labor market is near full employment but output indicators are soft. Net assessment: economy is near potential but with some remaining slack given mixed signals. Unemployment near NAIRU suggests near potential. Score ~47-48. The bright-line rule: unemployment "close to or below" longer-run normal suggests at or slightly above potential on labor side, but GDP growth slowed and manufacturing weak suggest slightly below. Net: near potential, slight softness. Score: 47.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "unemployment rate, at 5.0 percent...at a level close to or below most participants' estimates of its longer-run normal rate." "Many viewed labor market underutilization as having been substantially reduced...a few saw slack as having been largely eliminated." Reports of "shortages of workers in various industries" and "firming in wage increases." Strong job gains. Direction: near or at full employment. Center of gravity is at/near NAIRU with some tightness appearing. Score ~50-52.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending "appeared to have slowed in the fourth quarter." Manufacturing "continued to weaken." Business investment mixed -- energy sector contracting, some services moderate. "Many participants indicated that they still expected consumer spending to contribute importantly to economic growth in the coming year." Housing "continued to trend up." But "several were concerned" about rising saving rate and equity decline. Net: below trend currently but expected to return to moderate pace. Score ~40-42.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: Very prominent discussion. "Almost all participants cited a number of recent events as indicative of tighter financial conditions" -- equity price declines, credit spread widening, dollar appreciation, increased volatility. "Some participants also pointed to significantly tighter financing conditions for speculative-grade firms and small businesses, and to reports of tighter standards at banks for C&I and CRE loans." The effects described as "roughly equivalent to those from further firming in monetary policy." This is clearly tighter than neutral. Score ~62-65.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: They held rates steady after December liftoff. "Several participants noted that...waiting for additional information...before taking the next step to reduce policy accommodation would be prudent." "While participants continued to expect that gradual adjustments...would be appropriate, they emphasized that the timing and pace of adjustments will depend on future economic and financial market developments." No urgency to hike further. Cautious hold with data-dependent posture. Still on a gradual normalization path but clearly pausing. Score ~48-50. Lean slightly dovish from the December action given increased uncertainty and caution. Score: 48.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Broad agreement on holding rates. "Participants expressed a range of views regarding the balance of risks" and "members expressed a range of views regarding the implications of recent...developments for the degree of uncertainty." Some thought risks balanced, others saw downside risks increased. But all agreed to hold and no dissents mentioned. Moderate differences about risk assessment but consensus on action. Score ~28-32.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Very substantial discussion. "Almost all participants cited" tighter financial conditions. Extended discussion of equity prices, credit spreads, CRE valuations, energy firm stress, bank net interest margins, yield curve effects. Financial developments discussed as potentially equivalent to further policy firming. This is a prominent topic. Score ~55-60.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Participants continued to expect that gradual adjustments in the stance of monetary policy would be appropriate" but "emphasized that the timing and pace of adjustments will depend on future economic and financial market developments." Several noted prudence in waiting. No signal of imminent further hikes. Mild tightening bias (still on normalization path) but clearly tempered by uncertainty. Score ~55-58.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "many members judging that uncertainty had increased." "many saw these developments as increasing the downside risks." "increased uncertainty about global economic prospects and financial market turbulence." "the implications of the available information were not sufficiently clear." Elevated uncertainty about the outlook. Score ~52-58.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (47) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (51): Slight divergence is explained by the text -- labor market is near full employment but GDP/spending data are soft. The document explicitly notes this divergence.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (48) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (56): Slight divergence makes sense -- current action is cautious hold (lean dovish) but forward path still envisions gradual normalization (mild hawkish lean forward).

### Polarity checks:
- INFLATION_PRESSURE 33: Higher = more pressure. Evidence says below target with downside risks. 33 < 50. Correct.
- OUTPUT_GAP 47: Higher = more above potential. Near potential but soft GDP. 47 slightly below 50. Correct.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS 63: Higher = tighter. "Almost all" see tighter conditions. 63 > 50. Correct.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN 48: Higher = more hawkish. Cautious hold, emphasis on patience. 48 slightly below 50. Correct.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE: 33 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Inflation continued to run below the Committee's 2 percent longer-run objective, partly reflecting declines in energy prices and in prices of non-energy imports."
- "a number of participants indicated that, in light of recent developments, they viewed the outlook for inflation as somewhat more uncertain or saw the risks as being to the downside."
- "Market-based measures of inflation compensation had declined to historically low levels."

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants see inflation clearly below target with additional downward forces from energy and the dollar; most expect a gradual return to 2% but downside risks are increasing.

**Minority views**: Some worried inflation expectations may be drifting lower; a few wanted direct evidence of inflation rising before further hikes. A few saw underlying inflation measures as relatively stable, providing mild offset.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP: 47 +/-6 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "labor market conditions had improved further in late 2015 even as economic growth slowed"
- "the spending and production data generally had been disappointing"
- "They continued to anticipate that economic activity would expand at a moderate pace over the medium term"

**Center-of-gravity**: Economy near potential on the labor side but GDP/spending data suggest slight remaining softness; participants see mixed signals.

**Minority views**: Some emphasize labor market strength as the more reliable signal (pushing closer to 50); others worry global/financial headwinds could pull activity lower.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 51 +/-5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the unemployment rate, at 5.0 percent...was at a level close to or below most participants' estimates of its longer-run normal rate"
- "Many viewed labor market underutilization as having been substantially reduced over the past year, and a few saw slack as having been largely eliminated"
- "reports of shortages of workers in various industries, or firming in wage increases"

**Center-of-gravity**: Labor market is approximately at full employment with early signs of tightness (shortages, wage firming), but some residual slack remains for the center of gravity.

**Minority views**: A few see slack largely eliminated (suggesting 53-55); residual underutilization indicators (participation rate just starting to recover) keep the center near 50-51.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 40 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Growth of consumer spending appeared to have slowed in the fourth quarter, with the December data showing a decline in nominal retail sales"
- "Manufacturing activity continued to weaken in late 2015"
- "many participants indicated that they still expected consumer spending to contribute importantly to economic growth in the coming year"

**Center-of-gravity**: Current spending and investment data are below trend -- consumer spending slowed, manufacturing contracted, energy investment declining. But fundamentals (income, employment) support a rebound, preventing a lower score.

**Minority views**: Several concerned rising saving rate and equity declines could damp spending further; a few note positive fundamentals as mitigating.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 63 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Almost all participants cited a number of recent events as indicative of tighter financial conditions in the United States; these events included declines in equity prices, a widening in credit spreads, a further rise in the exchange value of the dollar, and an increase in financial market volatility."
- "Some participants also pointed to significantly tighter financing conditions for speculative-grade firms and small businesses, and to reports of tighter standards at banks for C&I and CRE loans."
- "The effects of these financial developments, if they were to persist, may be roughly equivalent to those from further firming in monetary policy."

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial conditions have tightened notably across multiple dimensions; participants view this as a significant development with policy-equivalent implications.

**Minority views**: "A number of participants noted that the large magnitude of changes in domestic financial market conditions was difficult to reconcile with incoming information on U.S. economic developments" -- suggesting some saw the tightening as potentially overshooting fundamentals.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 48 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Several participants noted that monetary policy was less well positioned to respond effectively to shocks that reduce inflation or real activity than to upside shocks, and that waiting for additional information...before taking the next step to reduce policy accommodation would be prudent."
- "While participants continued to expect that gradual adjustments in the stance of monetary policy would be appropriate, they emphasized that the timing and pace of adjustments will depend on future economic and financial market developments."

**Center-of-gravity**: Participants are in a cautious hold posture -- still on a gradual normalization path but emphasizing patience and data dependence, with asymmetric risk concerns tilting slightly dovish.

**Minority views**: A couple of members wanted direct evidence of inflation rising before further hikes (more dovish); the broad expectation of continued gradual normalization prevents a more dovish score.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY: 30 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Participants expressed a range of views regarding the balance of risks to the medium-term economic outlook"
- "members expressed a range of views regarding the implications of recent economic and financial developments for the degree of uncertainty about the medium-term outlook, with many members judging that uncertainty had increased"
- Unanimous agreement to hold rates; no dissents mentioned.

**Center-of-gravity**: Broad agreement on the policy action (hold) but moderate differences in risk assessment and uncertainty views. No formal dissents.

**Minority views**: "Others judged that recent developments had increased the level of downside risks or that the risks were no longer balanced" -- a notable minority diverged on risk characterization.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 58 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Almost all participants cited a number of recent events as indicative of tighter financial conditions"
- Extended discussion of equity prices, credit spreads, CRE valuations, energy firm financial stress, bank margins, speculative-grade financing
- "a few participants cautioned that valuations in CRE markets should be closely monitored"

**Center-of-gravity**: Financial stability and conditions were a prominent discussion topic at this meeting, with almost all participants engaging and multiple dimensions of financial markets covered at length.

**Minority views**: None significant -- the discussion was widespread.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 56 +/-7 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "While participants continued to expect that gradual adjustments in the stance of monetary policy would be appropriate"
- "they emphasized that the timing and pace of adjustments will depend on future economic and financial market developments"
- Several participants stressed waiting for more information before "taking the next step to reduce policy accommodation"

**Center-of-gravity**: Forward signal still points toward gradual normalization (tightening direction) but is heavily tempered by data dependence and uncertainty, producing a mild tightening lean.

**Minority views**: A few participants wanted direct evidence of rising inflation before further hikes, pulling the forward signal toward neutral; the ongoing expectation of gradual rate increases keeps it above 50.

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 55 +/-6 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "many members judging that uncertainty had increased"
- "many saw these developments as increasing the downside risks to the outlook"
- "the implications of the available information were not sufficiently clear to allow members to assess the balance of risks to the economic outlook"
- "participants emphasized the importance of closely monitoring these developments"

**Center-of-gravity**: Uncertainty is elevated -- many participants explicitly note increased uncertainty driven by global economic and financial developments, and the Committee could not even characterize the balance of risks clearly.

**Minority views**: Some participants saw the situation as more clearly deteriorating (higher uncertainty), while "most" thought it premature to change the outlook materially.
