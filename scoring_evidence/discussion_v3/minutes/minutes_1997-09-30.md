# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 1997-09-30

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary of participant views). Unsegmented (pre-2009), so I must focus on participant-attributed language ("members commented," "members noted," "several indicated") and ignore staff-reported content.

**Era context**: Late 1990s boom. The economy is expanding strongly, unemployment is low (~4.9%), inflation remains subdued despite tight labor markets. The "new economy" productivity story is emerging. The Fed held rates steady at 5.5% at this meeting with a tightening bias.

**Quantifier distribution**: "all the members" (endorsed unchanged policy, retained asymmetry), "many members" (risks on side of demand pressing against resources), "several" (tightening could be needed in near future; preemptive tightening emphasis; credit amply available), "some" (credit overly available; money growth suggesting loose policy), "most" (risks might have increased).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Members describe inflation as "subdued" and "unexpectedly benign" currently. However, there is significant concern about future inflation risks: "widespread tautness of labor markets," "scattered indications of increased pressure on wages," "risks to the economy appeared to be strongly tilted toward rising inflation." The current level is below target-equivalent, but there is clear forward-looking concern. The center of gravity is that inflation is currently low but risks are building — scoring the perceived pressure NOW, this is in the 40-48 range. Members see favorable inflation but mounting upside risk. Score ~45.

Direction: Participants see inflation pressure as slightly below midpoint because current inflation is "subdued" but risks are "strongly tilted toward rising inflation." The current benign outcome dominates, but the risk perception pushes it toward the midpoint.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: "continued remarkable performance," "faster-than-expected growth," "economic activity and the associated demand for labor expanding at an unsustainable pace," "demands that would press more intensely against the available resources," "high levels of resource utilization." This is clearly above potential. Score must be >50. The language "unsustainable pace" and "press more intensely against available resources" suggests clearly above potential — 59-65 range. Score ~62.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "increasingly tight labor markets across the country," "growing tightness in labor markets in many parts of the country," "widespread tautness of labor markets," "businesses were reporting increasing difficulty in hiring and retaining qualified workers," "Growth in labor demand had been outpacing sustainable increases in labor supply," unemployment at 4.9% (near expansion low). This is very tight. Score 65-72. Score ~68.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Strength in consumer spending and further acceleration in capital investment sparked faster-than-expected growth," "rebound in consumer expenditures," "continued further rapid expansion in investment expenditures," "final demands had been unexpectedly strong." This is clearly above trend — robust/strong momentum. Score ~65.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "favorable financial conditions," "ready financing from both internal cash flows and external sources," "credit from a wide variety of lenders appeared to be amply available in financial markets on favorable terms, perhaps overly so," "ready availability of financing." Members explicitly describe conditions as loose/accommodative despite real rates being "fairly restrictive." The net assessment is that financial conditions are loose. Score ~25 (loose conditions = low financial tightness).

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: All members voted to hold rates. But: "several also indicated that economic developments could well require a tightening of policy in the relatively near future," "risks continued to be biased toward rising inflation," "most members that those risks might have increased," all retained asymmetric tightening bias. The center of gravity is hold with a clear tightening lean. This is lean hawkish to moderate hawkish — 55-63 range. Score ~58.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Unanimous vote (10-0). "All the members endorsed a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance." "All the members also agreed on the desirability of retaining a bias." While several wanted tightening "in the relatively near future" vs. the consensus to hold, this is within the range of broad agreement with minor emphasis differences. Score ~20.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Limited discussion of financial stability per se. There are mentions of "favorable financial conditions," equity prices reaching new highs, credit availability possibly being "overly" available, and the "unintended adverse effects on financial markets" from surprise tightening. These are brief/contextual mentions, not a prominent stability discussion. Score ~18.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "the next policy move as more likely to be in the direction of some firming than toward easing," asymmetric directive toward tightening, "several also indicated that economic developments could well require a tightening of policy in the relatively near future." This is a mild tightening bias with data dependence. Score ~62.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "The behavior of inflation had been unexpectedly benign for an extended period of time for reasons that were not fully understood," "Forecasts of an upturn in inflation were therefore subject to a considerable degree of uncertainty," "it was an open question, however, as to whether these influences would be sufficient to slow the growth." The word "considerable" is used, and there's genuine puzzlement about why inflation has been so low. Score ~45.

### Cross-dimension coherence check:
- OUTPUT_GAP (62) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (68): Both above 50, consistent with an overheating economy. Labor is tighter than the output gap score, which makes sense given the emphasis on labor shortages even more than general overheating.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (58) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (62): Both lean hawkish, with forward guidance slightly more hawkish than current action (hold). This makes sense — they held but signaled the next move is likely tightening.

### Polarity verification:
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: Higher = tighter. Evidence says conditions are loose/accommodative. Score 25 = low tightness. Correct.
- All others check out directionally.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### INFLATION_PRESSURE: 45 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "inflation and inflationary expectations had remained subdued to date, reflecting to some extent special influences"
- "while few signs of rising price inflation had surfaced, the widespread tautness of labor markets and the emergence of scattered indications of increased pressure on wages and other labor costs were cause for concern"
- "the risks to the economy appeared to be strongly tilted toward rising inflation"

**Center of gravity**: Members see current inflation as subdued/benign but perceive mounting upside risks from tight labor markets — slightly below midpoint reflecting low current inflation with acknowledged risk buildup.

**Minority views**: None significantly diverge; the range of views is about how soon risks materialize, not whether they exist.

---

### OUTPUT_GAP: 62 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "continued remarkable performance of the economy... faster-than-expected growth in the third quarter"
- "economic activity and the associated demand for labor expanding at an unsustainable pace"
- "demands that would press more intensely against the available resources"

**Center of gravity**: Economy clearly operating above potential with demand outpacing supply — "running hot" territory.

**Minority views**: Some expectation of slowing, but the current assessment is firmly above potential.

---

### LABOR_TIGHTNESS: 68 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "increasingly tight labor markets across the country and rising levels of manufacturing capacity utilization"
- "businesses were reporting increasing difficulty in hiring and retaining qualified workers"
- "Growth in labor demand had been outpacing sustainable increases in labor supply"
- Unemployment at 4.9%, "near its low for the current economic expansion"

**Center of gravity**: Very tight labor markets with widespread shortages and growing wage pressures, though wage increases have been moderate so far.

---

### CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT: 65 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Strength in consumer spending and further acceleration in capital investment sparked faster-than-expected growth"
- "continued further rapid expansion in investment expenditures by business firms for equipment and structures"
- "final demands had been unexpectedly strong"

**Center of gravity**: Both consumer spending and business investment are running well above trend — "robust" territory.

---

### FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS: 25 ±7 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "favorable financial conditions" and "ready financing from both internal cash flows and external sources"
- "credit from a wide variety of lenders appeared to be amply available in financial markets on favorable terms, perhaps overly so in present circumstances"
- "the strength in the broad measures of money in recent months suggested that policy was not restraining liquidity or financial conditions more generally"

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are clearly loose — ample credit, favorable terms, strong money growth. Members worry conditions may even be too easy.

**Minority views**: Some note real short-term rates are "fairly restrictive," creating tension with the overall assessment of loose conditions.

---

### HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN: 58 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "all the members endorsed a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance"
- "several also indicated that economic developments could well require a tightening of policy in the relatively near future"
- "the members continued to view the next policy move as more likely to be in the direction of some firming"
- "most members that those risks might have increased"

**Center of gravity**: Hold with a clear tightening lean — the committee is ready to tighten but not yet pulling the trigger. Lean hawkish.

---

### DISSENT_INTENSITY: 18 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "Votes against this action: None." (unanimous 10-0)
- "all the members endorsed a proposal to maintain an unchanged policy stance"
- "All the members also agreed on the desirability of retaining a bias"

**Center of gravity**: Near-unanimous agreement on both the decision and the directive. Minor differences in urgency (several wanted to tighten soon) but no formal disagreement.

---

### FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS: 18 ±5 [Moderate confidence]
**Evidence**:
- Brief mentions of equity prices reaching new highs and credit being "perhaps overly" available
- Concern that "a policy tightening... might therefore have unintended adverse effects on financial markets"

**Center of gravity**: Financial stability gets passing mentions but is not a focal topic. The discussion centers on the real economy and inflation risks.

---

### FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE: 62 ±5 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "the members continued to view the next policy move as more likely to be in the direction of some firming than toward easing"
- "a somewhat higher federal funds rate would be acceptable" (asymmetric directive)
- "several also indicated that economic developments could well require a tightening of policy in the relatively near future"

**Center of gravity**: Mild tightening bias — the next move is expected to be a hike, but timing is data-dependent. Consistent with early normalization/tightening signal.

---

### UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED: 45 ±8 [High confidence]
**Evidence**:
- "The behavior of inflation had been unexpectedly benign for an extended period of time for reasons that were not fully understood"
- "Forecasts of an upturn in inflation were therefore subject to a considerable degree of uncertainty"
- "It was an open question, however, as to whether these influences would be sufficient to slow the growth"

**Center of gravity**: Elevated uncertainty driven primarily by the puzzle of why inflation has been so low despite tight labor markets. "Considerable" uncertainty is expressed about the inflation outlook specifically.
