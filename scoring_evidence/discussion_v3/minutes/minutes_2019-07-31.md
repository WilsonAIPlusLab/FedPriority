# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2019-07-31 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

### Document Type
This is a **minutes** document (synthesized summary of participant views). It uses standard quantifier language: "most," "many," "several," "some," "a few," "a couple." The document is segmented, with clear participant discussion sections beginning at "Participants' Views on Current Conditions and the Economic Outlook" (line 491) and "Committee Policy Action" (line 737). Staff sections are clearly labeled and will be excluded from scoring.

### Era Context
July 2019 -- late-expansion period. The economy had been growing moderately, unemployment was near historic lows (3.7%), but inflation was persistently below 2%. Trade tensions (U.S.-China) and weak global growth were key headwinds. This was the first rate cut of the "mid-cycle adjustment" -- a 25bp cut to the fed funds rate target range to 2-2.25%. The Committee was shifting from a tightening to an easing posture.

### Quantifier Distribution
- "Participants agreed" / "participants generally" -- broad consensus language used frequently
- "Most" -- used for the 25bp cut framing ("most participants viewed a proposed quarter-point policy easing as part of a recalibration...mid-cycle adjustment")
- "Many" -- trade risks, inflation data views
- "Several" -- wage pressures for lower-wage workers, trade tensions, maintaining current rate
- "Some" -- trade uncertainties receded, inflation below target stressed, inflation expectations drift risk
- "A few" -- residential investment concern, inflation expectations anchored, yield curve inversion concern, 50bp cut preference (actually "a couple")
- "A couple" -- preferred 50bp cut, no change preferred (actually "two members")

### Dimension-by-Dimension Reasoning

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as below target. "Both overall and core PCE price inflation rates continued to run below the Committee's symmetric 2 percent objective; the latest reading on the 12-month change in the core PCE price index was 1.6 percent." Many noted the recent firming was transitory reversal, but the level was still below 2%. "Inflation pressures remained muted." This is clearly below target but not alarmingly so -- in the 35-42 range per anchors for 2013-19 period. Direction: below midpoint. Magnitude: "muted" and "running below" = somewhat below, about 10-15 points below 50. Score ~38.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy expanding at a "moderate rate," expected to "slow a bit to around its estimated potential rate." The economy is near potential -- no language about being "above potential" or "below potential" explicitly. Consumer spending is "robust," but business investment is "soft." The staff forecast GDP growing "at a rate a little above the staff's estimate of potential output growth in 2019 and 2020." Participants describe "the economy continued to expand at a moderate pace." The net position: economy at or very slightly above potential, with mixed signals. Score ~50-52. Bright-line rule: no clear "above" or "below" potential language from participants themselves. Staff says "a little above" but participants say "moderate" and "around potential." Score 50.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Labor market had remained strong," "unemployment rate near historical lows," "continued solid job gains," "many firms reporting difficulty finding workers to meet current demand," "several participants reported seeing notable wage pressures for lower-wage workers." But also: "several participants remarked that there seemed to be little sign of overheating in labor markets, citing the combination of muted inflation pressures and moderate wage growth." The labor market is tight but not overheating. Score: tight, around 55-60. Anchors: 50 for 2016-17; this is 2019, clearly tighter with 3.7% unemployment and "difficulty finding workers." Score 57.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: Consumer spending is "robust" -- strong positive signal. Business fixed investment is "soft" -- negative signal. "Household spending had picked up from earlier in the year" but "growth of business fixed investment had been soft." Manufacturing production "declined so far this year." Residential investment "declined again." Net: consumer spending strong, business investment and manufacturing weak, housing weak. Consumer spending gets more weight ("important role that household spending was currently playing in supporting the expansion"). Net is slightly above trend on consumer side, offset by weak investment. Score ~48-50. The mixed picture centers near trend. Score 48.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Financial conditions remained supportive of economic growth, with borrowing rates low and stock prices near all-time highs." "Financing conditions for nonfinancial businesses remained accommodative." Conditions are loose/accommodative. Yield curve inversion noted by a few as concern, but overall conditions supportive. Score: loose side, around 30-35. Anchors: 45-55 for 2017-19 normal. But the text emphasizes "accommodative" and "supportive" multiple times. Banks easing standards on C&I loans. Score 33.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: The Committee cut rates 25bp. "All but two members agreed to lower the target range." A couple preferred 50bp cut (more dovish). Two preferred no change (more hawkish). The majority framed this as "mid-cycle adjustment" and "recalibration," not the start of aggressive easing. Forward language emphasizes data dependence and "optionality." This is moderate dovish -- a rate cut but characterized as an insurance/adjustment move, not the beginning of a sustained easing cycle. Score: 35-40. Anchors: 40-50 for 2014-15 pre-liftoff. This is a cut but tentative. Score 37.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: Two formal dissents (for no change). A couple wanted 50bp cut. "Several participants favored maintaining the same target range." There's a three-way split: most for 25bp, a couple for 50bp, several for no change. This is moderate disagreement -- clear camps but the majority is solid. Score: 40-45. Anchors: 25-35 for typical one-dissent meeting; this has two dissenters plus a couple wanting more, so higher. Score 42.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: "Among those participants who commented on financial stability, most highlighted recent credit market developments, the elevated valuations in some asset markets, and the high level of nonfinancial corporate indebtedness." Several participants noted corporate debt/leveraged lending risks. A few discussed private credit markets. Several noted elevated equity and CRE valuations. A couple noted capital ratios at largest banks. One member noted "adverse implications for financial stability" from further easing. This is a meaningful but not dominant discussion. Score: 35-45. Anchors: 20-35 for 2013-19 typical. This meeting has more discussion than typical, with multiple specific concerns raised. Score 40.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "Most participants viewed a proposed quarter-point policy easing at this meeting as part of a recalibration of the stance of policy, or mid-cycle adjustment." "Participants generally favored an approach in which policy would be guided by incoming information...avoided any appearance of following a preset course." "Members generally agreed that it was important to maintain optionality." The forward signal is data-dependent with a mild easing lean -- they cut but don't commit to further cuts. This is an easing lean with heavy data dependence. Score: 35-40. Anchors: 35-45 for 2019 mid-cycle cuts. Score 38.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Significant risks and uncertainties attending the outlook remained." Trade policy uncertainty "remained elevated and looked likely to persist." "Many of the risks and uncertainties surrounding the economic outlook...had remained elevated." "The nature of many of the risks...and the absence of clarity regarding when those risks might be resolved." Downside risks noted to have "diminished somewhat" but still "important downside risks persisted." This is elevated uncertainty, somewhat above normal. Score: 45-55. Anchors: 40-50 for trade tensions. Score 50.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 38 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Both overall and core PCE price inflation rates continued to run below the Committee's symmetric 2 percent objective; the latest reading on the 12-month change in the core PCE price index was 1.6 percent."
- "Inflation pressures continued to be muted, notwithstanding the firming in the overall and core PCE price indexes in the three months ending in June relative to earlier in the year."
- "Many other participants...saw the recent inflation data as consistent with the view that the lower readings earlier this year were largely transitory, and noted that the trimmed mean measure of PCE price inflation...was running around 2 percent."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants see inflation running below the 2% target, with core PCE at 1.6%. While some took comfort from the recent firming and trimmed mean near 2%, the overall assessment is that inflation pressures are muted and below objective.

**Minority views:** Many participants saw recent data as consistent with a transitory dip resolving, which would place inflation closer to target; a few noted trimmed mean at 2%. This creates the upward uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 50 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Economic activity had risen at a moderate rate."
- "The economy continued to expand at a moderate pace, and participants generally expected GDP growth to slow a bit to around its estimated potential rate in the second half of the year."
- "Incoming data over the intermeeting period had been largely positive and...the economy had been resilient."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants describe an economy growing at a moderate pace, expected to converge to potential in the second half of 2019. No explicit "above potential" or "below potential" language from participants; the economy is essentially at potential.

**Minority views:** Some concern about deceleration from trade/global weakness could push slightly below potential; the staff forecast suggests slightly above. Narrow band.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 57 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Participants judged that conditions remained strong, with the unemployment rate near historical lows and continued solid job gains."
- "Reports from business contacts pointed to continued strong labor demand, with many firms reporting difficulty finding workers to meet current demand."
- "Several participants remarked that there seemed to be little sign of overheating in labor markets, citing the combination of muted inflation pressures and moderate wage growth."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The labor market is clearly tight -- low unemployment, firms have difficulty finding workers, wage pressures emerging for lower-wage workers -- but it is not overheating. Wage growth is moderate and consistent with low productivity growth, not generating inflationary pressure.

**Minority views:** Several noted no overheating, placing tightness in a more benign light. Several noted "notable wage pressures for lower-wage workers," suggesting somewhat greater tightness in segments.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 48 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Participants commented on the robust pace of consumer spending...household spending would likely continue to be supported by strong labor market conditions, rising incomes, and upbeat consumer sentiment."
- "Growth of business fixed investment had been soft." "Trade uncertainty...continuing to weigh on business confidence and firms' capital expenditure plans."
- "Manufacturing production had declined so far this year." "A few participants noted that the continued softness in residential investment was a concern."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Consumer spending is robust and the main engine of the expansion, but business fixed investment is soft, manufacturing is declining, and residential investment continues to fall. The net assessment is approximately at trend, with the strong consumer offset by weak investment.

**Minority views:** A few noted some improvement in business sentiment and new capital goods orders, while others highlighted ongoing agricultural and energy sector challenges.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 33 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Financial conditions remained supportive of economic growth, with borrowing rates low and stock prices near all-time highs."
- "Current financial conditions appeared to be premised importantly on expectations that the Federal Reserve would ease policy."
- "Financing conditions for nonfinancial businesses remained accommodative." Banks reported "continued to ease standards and terms on commercial and industrial loans."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial conditions are clearly accommodative/loose. Borrowing rates are low, equities near highs, credit freely available, banks easing lending standards. The only note of tightness is the inverted yield curve, mentioned by a few participants.

**Minority views:** A few expressed concern about yield curve inversion signaling future weakness, but the overwhelming assessment is that conditions are supportive.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 37 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "All but two members agreed to lower the target range for the federal funds rate to 2 to 2-1/4 percent at this meeting."
- "A couple of participants indicated that they would have preferred a 50 basis point cut...to better address the stubbornly low inflation rates."
- "Several participants favored maintaining the same target range at this meeting, judging that the real economy continued to be in a good place."
- "Most participants viewed a proposed quarter-point policy easing...as part of a recalibration of the stance of policy, or mid-cycle adjustment."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The majority favors a 25bp cut as an insurance/adjustment move, not the start of aggressive easing. The framing as "mid-cycle adjustment" rather than the beginning of an easing cycle moderates the dovish signal. Still clearly on the easing side.

**Minority views:** A couple wanted 50bp (more dovish); several/two wanted no change (hawkish). The range of views creates a wider uncertainty band.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 42 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "All but two members agreed to lower the target range." (Two formal dissents for no change.)
- "A couple of participants indicated that they would have preferred a 50 basis point cut."
- "Several participants favored maintaining the same target range at this meeting."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** There is a clear three-way split: most favor 25bp, several favor no change (two dissenting formally), and a couple favor 50bp. This represents moderate disagreement with two formal dissents and meaningful minority camps on both sides.

**Minority views:** The disagreement spans both the direction (no change) and magnitude (50bp), creating a wider-than-usual spread.

---

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 40 +/-5 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Among those participants who commented on financial stability, most highlighted recent credit market developments, the elevated valuations in some asset markets, and the high level of nonfinancial corporate indebtedness."
- "Several participants noted that high levels of corporate debt and leveraged lending posed some risks to the outlook."
- "One member also noted that a further easing in policy at a time when the economy is very strong and asset prices are elevated could have adverse implications for financial stability."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Financial stability receives a moderate discussion, with multiple specific concerns raised (corporate debt, leveraged lending, private credit, equity/CRE valuations, bank capital ratios). This is above the 2013-19 typical level due to the breadth of specific issues raised, but it is not a dominant topic of the meeting.

**Minority views:** Some participants saw risks as moderate and manageable; others were more concerned about specific sectors.

---

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 38 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Most participants viewed a proposed quarter-point policy easing at this meeting as part of a recalibration of the stance of policy, or mid-cycle adjustment."
- "Participants generally favored an approach in which policy would be guided by incoming information and its implications for the economic outlook and that avoided any appearance of following a preset course."
- "Members generally agreed that it was important to maintain optionality in setting the future target range."
- Statement: "act as appropriate to sustain the expansion."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** The forward signal is data-dependent with a mild easing lean. The "mid-cycle adjustment" framing explicitly avoids committing to further cuts, while the "act as appropriate" language preserves optionality in an easing direction. This is an easing lean with strong data dependence, not a commitment to further cuts.

**Minority views:** A couple wanted more aggressive action (suggesting more dovish forward path); several wanted no action (suggesting neutral/hawkish forward lean).

---

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 50 +/-6 [High confidence]**

**Evidence:**
- "Significant risks and uncertainties attending the outlook remained."
- "Trade policy uncertainty, although waning some over the intermeeting period, remained elevated and looked likely to persist."
- "Many of the risks and uncertainties surrounding the economic outlook that had been a source of concern in June had remained elevated, particularly those associated with the global economic outlook and international trade."
- "Participants generally judged that downside risks to the outlook for economic activity had diminished somewhat since their June meeting."

**Center-of-gravity summary:** Participants express elevated uncertainty, driven primarily by trade policy and global growth risks. While some risks diminished over the intermeeting period (G-20 truce, budget deal), participants judge that important downside risks persist and trade uncertainties are likely to continue. The uncertainty level is above normal but not extreme.

**Minority views:** Some saw risks as having diminished notably; others emphasized ongoing and potentially intensifying risks (no-deal Brexit, further trade escalation).
