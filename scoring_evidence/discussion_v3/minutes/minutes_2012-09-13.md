Conditions are broadly accommodative but not extremely loose.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 33 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Consumer price inflation remained subdued despite recent increases in the prices of some key commodities." "Participants generally anticipated that inflation over the medium run would likely run at or below the 2 percent rate." "Most participants saw the risks to the outlook for inflation as roughly balanced."
- Center-of-gravity: Inflation is subdued and expected to stay at or below the 2% target. The broad consensus is that slack is keeping prices in check.
- Minority views: "A few participants felt that maintaining a highly accommodative stance...could unmoor longer-term inflation expectations and...posed upside risks to inflation." Others saw risks "tilted to the downside" from persistent slack.

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 36 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Economic activity had continued to expand at a moderate pace in recent months." "The pace of the economic recovery would likely remain moderate over coming quarters." "They saw slack in resource utilization as remaining wide, indicating an important role for additional policy accommodation."
- Center-of-gravity: Economy is growing moderately but clearly below potential with persistent headwinds. Significant downside risks noted.
- Minority views: "A few participants...mentioned the possibility that economic growth could be more rapid than currently anticipated." A few argued structural factors reduce the effective output gap.

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 30 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "The available indicators pointed to continued weakness in overall labor market conditions." "Growth in employment had been disappointing, with the average monthly increases in payrolls so far this year below last year's pace." "The labor force participation rate and employment-to-population ratios were at or near post-recession lows."
- Center-of-gravity: The labor market is clearly slack with high unemployment, disappointing job growth, and declining participation. Well below full employment.
- Minority views: "A few participants reiterated their view that the persistently high level of unemployment reflected the effect of structural factors" (which would imply somewhat less cyclical slack).

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 37 +/-6 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Consumer spending had continued to advance" but "households were still in the process of deleveraging, confidence was low." "Growth in business fixed investment appeared to have slowed." Firms "remain reluctant to hire or expand capacity."
- Center-of-gravity: Below-trend spending and investment. Consumer spending slightly better than expected but investment slowing and business caution pervasive. Housing improving from depressed levels.
- Minority views: Retail sales "somewhat stronger than expected" provides a modest upside note.

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 35 +/-7 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Indicators of financial stress in the United States were not especially high and overall conditions in U.S. financial markets remained favorable." "Longer-term interest rates were low and supportive of economic growth, while equity prices had risen." But "still-tight credit conditions for some households and businesses."
- Center-of-gravity: Broadly accommodative financial conditions with low rates and rising equity prices, but pockets of tightness in credit for some borrowers. Somewhat loose overall.
- Minority views: One participant noted low rates could "ultimately lead to financial imbalances."

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 20 +/-8 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Many participants anticipated that such a program [LSAP] would provide support to the economic recovery." "Most participants thought these risks could be managed." The meeting is actively debating implementing QE3 -- additional large-scale asset purchases.
- Center-of-gravity: Strong dovish lean. The majority favors launching a new large-scale asset purchase program. This is classic QE-era dovishness (15-25 range per rubric).
- Minority views: "A few expressed skepticism that additional policy accommodation could help spur an economy." "Several participants reiterated their concern that additional purchases might complicate the Committee's efforts to withdraw monetary policy accommodation."

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 45 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: Extended debate on LSAP costs/benefits with multiple viewpoints. On labor: structural vs. cyclical unemployment debate ("a few" vs. "several"). On QE: "a few expressed skepticism," "several...reiterated their concern," but "most participants thought these risks could be managed."
- Center-of-gravity: Notable disagreement across multiple dimensions (efficacy of QE, nature of unemployment, inflation risks). The majority prevails but there is a meaningful minority with substantive objections.
- Minority views: The structural unemployment camp, QE skeptics, and inflation hawks form a notable minority bloc.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 33 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**
- Evidence: "Sentiment in financial markets improved notably during the intermeeting period." Discussion of ECB actions and euro-area risks. "One participant noted that...low interest rates could ultimately lead to financial imbalances." One participant raised "excessive risk-taking" concerns from additional asset purchases.
- Center-of-gravity: Financial conditions are discussed as context for the economic outlook and policy debate, but financial stability per se is not a prominent focus. A couple of individual participants flag stability concerns.
- Minority views: The financial imbalance/risk-taking concerns are raised by individual participants, not the center of gravity.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 8 +/-5 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Participants' forecasts for economic activity...were also associated with an outlook for the unemployment rate to remain close to recent levels through 2012 and then to decline gradually." "Additional purchases could reinforce the Committee's forward guidance regarding the federal funds rate." The meeting launched QE3 with open-ended purchases and extended the rate guidance to "at least through mid-2015."
- Center-of-gravity: Extremely dovish forward signal. Open-ended QE plus extended calendar-based (and soon threshold-based) forward guidance on rates. This is among the most dovish forward guidance configurations.
- Minority views: Skeptics exist but the forward signal from the majority and the Committee action is strongly accommodative.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 55 +/-8 [High confidence]**
- Evidence: "Many participants also noted that a high level of uncertainty regarding the European fiscal and banking crisis and the outlook for U.S. fiscal and regulatory policies was weighing on confidence." "Business contacts in many parts of the country were reported to be highly uncertain about the outlook." "A number of participants highlighted the uncertainty about the overall effects of additional purchases on financial markets and the real economy."
- Center-of-gravity: Elevated uncertainty. Multiple sources of uncertainty cited prominently: European crisis, U.S. fiscal cliff, efficacy of QE, structural vs. cyclical labor market debate. "Many" participants flag uncertainty explicitly.
- Minority views: "Others questioned the role of uncertainty about policy as a factor constraining aggregate demand."
