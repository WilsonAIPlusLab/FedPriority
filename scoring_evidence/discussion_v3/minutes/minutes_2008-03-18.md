# FOMC Meeting Scoring: 2008-03-18 (Minutes)

## Part 1: Thinking Process

**Document type**: Minutes (synthesized summary). This is a pre-2009 unsegmented minutes document with some staff content mixed in. I will focus on participant-attributed language ("participants noted," "members judged," "several," "some," "most").

**Era context**: March 2008 — early-to-mid Global Financial Crisis. Bear Stearns was rescued the same weekend. Housing contraction deepening, financial stress escalating, but the worst of the crisis (Lehman) is still six months away. The Committee cut 75bp at this meeting.

**Quantifier distribution**: "most" (members judged substantial easing warranted; most expected inflation to moderate), "many" (some contraction in H1 likely; lenders pulled back), "several" (price discovery difficult; business contacts on input costs; problems could be persistent), "some" (concern about severe downturn; emerging slack limiting pass-through; indicators of inflation expectations had risen; somewhat slower global growth), "one" (lender restricting home equity lines).

### Dimension-by-dimension reasoning:

**INFLATION_PRESSURE**: Participants see inflation as above target. "Recent readings on inflation had generally been elevated," "energy prices had risen sharply," "some indicators of inflation expectations had risen," "several participants stated that business contacts had emphasized that their input costs were rising and that they were seeking to pass on higher costs." However, "most participants still expected inflation to moderate later this year and in 2009." The current perception is elevated but with expectation of moderation. This is clearly above the midpoint — "elevated" with cost pass-through concerns — placing it in the 58-65 range. I'll score 62. Higher = more pressure, and the evidence says elevated — matches.

**OUTPUT_GAP**: Participants see the economy operating below potential and deteriorating. "Many participants thought some contraction in economic activity in the first half of 2008 now appeared likely." "Resource utilization was anticipated to follow a lower trajectory." "Some participants expressed concern that falling house prices and stresses in financial markets could lead to a more severe and protracted downturn." The economy is contracting — below potential. Score should be well below 50. The contraction is expected but early-stage — not yet deep recession levels. Score: 30. Below potential with contraction expected, but not yet catastrophic.

**LABOR_TIGHTNESS**: "Private payroll employment had fallen in February for the third consecutive month," "softening in labor markets," "increasing concerns among workers about prospects for employment and income." Labor market is weakening but not yet in deep distress. Score: 33. Below full employment, loosening.

**CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT**: "Consumer spending was decelerating noticeably, though it apparently had not yet actually declined substantially." "The outlook for business spending had also dimmed... retrenchment in capital spending." "Tightening supply of credit was seen as exacerbating this softness." Clearly below trend with retrenchment language. Score: 28. Between contracting and below trend — "retrenchment" and "pullback" in business investment, consumer spending decelerating toward decline.

**FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS**: "Stresses in financial markets had intensified noticeably since the January meeting." "Many lenders pulled back from risk taking." "Financial markets as unusually fragile." "Ongoing strains were likely to raise the price and reduce the availability of credit." "Adverse feedback loop was under way." "Financial markets remained under considerable stress." This is clearly very tight — crisis-level stress emerging, though not yet the full credit freeze of September 2008. Score: 78.

**HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN**: "Most members judged that a substantial easing in the stance of monetary policy was warranted." They cut 75bp. "Members viewed the downside risks to economic growth as having increased." "Some believed that a prolonged and severe economic downturn could not be ruled out." This is strongly dovish — a 75bp cut with most favoring it. Score: 15. Strong dovish action with large cut.

**DISSENT_INTENSITY**: "All in all, members judged that a 75 basis point easing of policy at this meeting was appropriate." The statement uses consensus language. However, there are tensions: inflation concerns coexist with growth concerns, "some" vs "most" on severity. There's a 2-dissent vote (Plosser and Fisher dissented at this meeting, though not stated in the extracted text). The text shows tension between inflation hawks and growth doves — "inflation pressures had apparently risen even as the outlook for growth had weakened." Score: 40. Moderate differences — the inflation/growth tension creates notable disagreement even within a consensus cut.

**FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS**: Enormous attention to financial conditions. An entire long paragraph on financial market stresses, adverse feedback loops, liquidity facilities, credit availability, price discovery problems, balance sheet pressures. Financial stability is a dominant theme alongside economic assessment. Score: 80.

**FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE**: "The Committee emphasized that it would continue to monitor inflation developments carefully." "Accommodative monetary policy and a recovery in financial markets... were expected to help foster a further pickup in economic growth in 2009." The statement signals continued dovish lean — they just cut 75bp and signal data dependence with easing bias, but inflation monitoring language adds caution. Score: 22. Easing bias with data dependence, but some caution from inflation.

**UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED**: "Considerable uncertainty surrounded this forecast." "The trajectory of house prices was a major source of uncertainty." "Significant uncertainty attended the near-term outlook for price pressures." "Uncertainties in the outlook for both economic activity and inflation elevated." Multiple explicit uncertainty statements from participants. Score: 72. High uncertainty — many participants emphasize difficulty, multiple sources of uncertainty identified.

### Cross-dimension coherence checks:
- OUTPUT_GAP (30) and LABOR_TIGHTNESS (33): Both below 50, both in the 30s — coherent. Economy weakening, labor softening.
- HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN (15) and FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE (22): Both dovish, lean aligned. The forward guidance is slightly less dovish than the action because of inflation monitoring language.
- FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS (78) and FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS (80): Consistent — high stress warrants high attention.

## Part 2: Evidence and Scores

### 1. INFLATION_PRESSURE
**Score: 62 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Recent readings on inflation had generally been elevated" and "energy prices had risen sharply"
- "Several participants stated that business contacts had emphasized that their input costs were rising and that they were seeking to pass on higher costs to their customers"
- "Most participants still expected inflation to moderate later this year and in 2009"

**Center of gravity**: Participants perceive inflation as clearly elevated with cost pass-through pressures, though most expect moderation ahead. The current assessment is above target with upside risks.

**Minority views**: "Some participants, however, expressed the view that emerging economic slack would limit the extent to which firms could pass on their higher costs" — this moderating view justifies the uncertainty band.

---

### 2. OUTPUT_GAP
**Score: 30 +/-8 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Many participants thought some contraction in economic activity in the first half of 2008 now appeared likely"
- "Some participants expressed concern that falling house prices and stresses in financial markets could lead to a more severe and protracted downturn in activity than currently anticipated"
- "The outlook for economic activity had weakened considerably since the January meeting"

**Center of gravity**: The economy is seen as contracting and operating well below potential, with the gap expected to widen further before recovery in H2.

**Minority views**: Some participants see risk of an even more severe downturn, pulling the uncertainty band downward.

---

### 3. LABOR_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 33 +/-7 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Private payroll employment had fallen in February for the third consecutive month"
- "Softening in the labor market" and "increasing concerns among workers about prospects for employment and income"

**Center of gravity**: Labor market is clearly weakening with consecutive payroll declines, but not yet at deep recession levels of mass layoffs.

**Minority views**: Limited divergence on labor assessment.

---

### 4. CONSUMPTION_INVESTMENT
**Score: 28 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Consumer spending was decelerating noticeably, though it apparently had not yet actually declined substantially"
- "Anecdotal reports from many regions of the country pointed to a retrenchment in capital spending in response to increased pessimism"
- "The tightening supply of credit was seen as exacerbating this softness in business outlays and contributing particularly to a pullback from nonresidential construction projects"

**Center of gravity**: Consumer spending decelerating sharply and business investment in active retrenchment/pullback. The combined picture is clearly contracting.

**Minority views**: Agricultural equipment investment was "quite strong" — a minor offset.

---

### 5. FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS
**Score: 78 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Stresses in financial markets had intensified noticeably since the January meeting" and "meeting participants regarded financial markets as unusually fragile"
- "An adverse feedback loop was under way, in which a restriction in credit availability prompts a deterioration in the economic outlook that, in turn, spurs additional tightening in credit conditions"
- "Ongoing strains were likely to raise the price and reduce the availability of credit to businesses and households"

**Center of gravity**: Financial conditions are very tight and worsening, with crisis-level dynamics (adverse feedback loops, liquidity problems, balance sheet pressures). Not yet full credit freeze, but approaching.

**Minority views**: Minimal — broad agreement on the severity.

---

### 6. HAWKISH_DOVISH_LEAN
**Score: 15 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Most members judged that a substantial easing in the stance of monetary policy was warranted at this meeting"
- "Members judged that a 75 basis point easing of policy at this meeting was appropriate to address the combination of risks"
- "Some believed that a prolonged and severe economic downturn could not be ruled out given the further restriction of credit availability"

**Center of gravity**: Strongly dovish — 75bp cut with broad support, driven by deteriorating growth outlook and financial stress. The action is aggressive easing.

**Minority views**: Inflation concerns create some tension but did not prevent the large cut. Two members (historically known to have dissented) may have preferred less easing, widening the band.

---

### 7. DISSENT_INTENSITY
**Score: 40 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- Tension between inflation concerns ("inflation pressures had apparently risen even as the outlook for growth had weakened") and growth/financial concerns
- "With the uncertainties in the outlook for both economic activity and inflation elevated, members noted that appropriately calibrating the stance of policy was difficult"
- The text does not explicitly mention dissents, but the inflation vs. growth tension and "difficult" calibration language suggest meaningful internal disagreement

**Center of gravity**: Moderate disagreement — the Committee reached consensus on 75bp but with evident tension between the inflation and growth camps. The difficulty of calibrating policy was explicitly acknowledged.

**Minority views**: Some participants leaned toward less easing given inflation concerns.

### 8. FINANCIAL_STABILITY_EMPHASIS
**Score: 80 +/-5 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:

- "Price discovery for mortgage-related financial assets had become increasingly difficult"
- "Many financial institutions experiencing significant balance sheet pressures"
- "Financial markets as unusually fragile"
- New Fed liquidity facilities discussed

**Center of gravity**: Financial stability is a dominant theme of this meeting, rivaling the economic assessment in emphasis. Nearly every aspect of the discussion references financial conditions.

**Minority views**: None — universal emphasis.

### 9. FORWARD_GUIDANCE_TONE
**Score: 22 +/-8 [Moderate confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "The Committee emphasized that it would continue to monitor inflation developments carefully" — a hawkish hedge within a dovish action
- "Accommodative monetary policy and a recovery in financial markets... were expected to help foster a further pickup in economic growth in 2009" — signals continued accommodation
- The statement noted inflation expectations had risen but reiterated inflation expected to moderate

**Center of gravity**: The forward signal is dovish with easing bias — the 75bp cut signals further accommodation as needed, but inflation monitoring language adds a cautionary note that moderates the forward dovishness somewhat.

**Minority views**: The inflation monitoring language represents hawkish members' influence on forward guidance.

### 10. UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED
**Score: 72 +/-7 [High confidence]**

**Evidence**:
- "Considerable uncertainty surrounded this forecast"
- "The trajectory of house prices was a major source of uncertainty in their economic outlook"
- "Significant uncertainty attended the near-term outlook for price pressures"
