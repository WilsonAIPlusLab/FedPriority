## Cross-Batch Summary: Systematic Patterns

**Dissent compression is consistent across all 12 meetings.** The average dissent gap is +9.3 points (transcript always higher). The mechanism is always the same: ANONYMIZATION removes names, QUANTIFIER_DOWNGRADE converts individual preferences to "some members" or "nearly all," and VOTE_DISCUSSION_GAP omits straw votes and near-dissent preferences. No meeting in this batch has a negative dissent gap (i.e., the minutes never overstate dissent relative to the transcript).

**FSE compression is largest in the pre-crisis meetings (2006-12 through 2007-05).** The three meetings where financial stability warnings were most consequentially omitted are 2006-12 (Bies subprime warning), 2007-01 (Dudley subprime vintage deterioration), and 2007-05 (Warsh/Geithner complete financial fragility briefings). The pattern reverses in the crisis and recovery phases where FSE content is more faithfully preserved.

**The FINANCIAL_TIGHTNESS reversal in 2006-08-08 is anomalous.** The gap of -32 (T:25 vs M:57) is not a compression artifact — it reflects a structural difference between what participants were actually discussing (financial market conditions = loose) and how the minutes framed the macroeconomic environment (Fed tightening = tighter). This is not dishonest but it is misleading about actual financial conditions.

**Uncertainty compression follows a consistent pattern: intensity preserved, vivid language stripped.** Across 12 meetings, the concept of elevated uncertainty typically survives into the minutes, but the qualitative intensity markers ("groping in the dark," "appalling," "uncharted territory," "undefinable tail risks") are replaced by institutionally appropriate "considerable uncertainty" constructions. The information is present but the alarm is removed.

**Reassurance insertion is most active in 2006-01 and 2006-12.** These are the two meetings where the minutes add positive framing not present in the same form in the transcript. The pattern fades as the crisis deepens — by late 2007, the minutes are no longer reassurance-inserting; they are instead uncertainty-projecting.

**The most consequential omissions are concentrated in 2006-12 through 2007-05** — the 18-month window before the August 2007 ABCP crisis. Multiple technically specific early warnings (Bies on subprime ARM resets, Dudley on 2006 vintage delinquency acceleration, Warsh on the LBO/covenant-lite bubble, Geithner's "significant risk of a sharp deterioration") are present in the transcripts and absent from the minutes. This is the window where the compression function had the highest counterfactual consequence.
