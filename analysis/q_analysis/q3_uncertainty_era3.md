## 300-Word Synthesis: Why Uncertainty Compression Worsens in 2012–2020

The worsening uncertainty gap in 2012–2020 is not primarily a drafting artifact or a change in meeting culture. It reflects a structural consequence of the FOMC's post-crisis communication apparatus. Three mechanisms compound each other across this era.

**Forward guidance and SEP publication create a binding projection commitment.** Beginning with the 2012 SEP framework and dot plot, the FOMC published individual rate path projections at quarterly meetings. The minutes — released three weeks after each meeting — must be read as consistent with those published projections. When participants express deep private uncertainty about the Phillips curve, the neutral rate, or whether models are functioning at all (as occurs in every one of the ten cases above), that uncertainty cannot be fully preserved in the minutes without calling into question the credibility of the recently published SEP path. The solution across all ten meetings is the same: individual uncertainty admissions are collapsed into standard "risks roughly balanced" language that is compatible with the published dot-plot trajectory.

**The policy instrument complexity of this era raises the stakes for uncertainty suppression.** Unlike single-instrument periods, 2013–2019 meetings routinely manage multiple simultaneous signals: QE tapering pace, forward guidance language, liftoff timing, and balance sheet normalization sequencing. Each instrument's credibility depends on the others. When Kashkari says "we don't understand inflation dynamics" at the June 2017 meeting where a rate hike and September balance sheet normalization are being pre-committed, recording that uncertainty at scale would unwind the entire forward guidance architecture. The minutes must project unified institutional confidence that none of the instruments is being misdirected.

**Dedicated financial stability sessions absorb the deliberative depth that might otherwise be channeled into uncertainty expression.** In eight of ten high-gap meetings, financial stability receives substantial dedicated discussion — often scoring 15–30 points higher in the transcript than in the minutes on that dimension alone. This crowding effect means genuine uncertainty about the macro outlook is already underrepresented in how participants allocate their speaking time, and then compressed a second time in the minutes. The result is a double-layer suppression unique to the 2013–2019 era that did not exist in earlier periods.
