## Synthesis (300 words)

The ten meetings in this batch — spanning 1994 to 2018 with DI gaps of 18 to 20 points — confirm and sharpen four structural compression mechanisms identified in earlier batches.

**The near-majority erasure problem.** The most striking finding across this batch is the frequency with which a near-majority or numerically large dissenting camp disappears entirely. At the February 1994 meeting, roughly ten of nineteen participants preferred a 50bp move; Greenspan made an extraordinary personal plea to achieve consensus on 25bp. The minutes record zero dissents and describe only that "a number of members" endorsed a slight adjustment. At July 1994, Melzer and Broaddus explicitly called for an immediate 50bp hike and Greenspan himself characterized the asymmetry outcome as only a "modest majority." Both facts vanish. The pattern repeats at March 1995, where Parry and Minehan wanted to actually tighten and Blinder challenged the entire asymmetry framework. None of this is named in the minutes.

**Reluctant acceptance rewritten as endorsement.** March 2000 provides a textbook case: McTeer explicitly stated "While I would have preferred 1/4 point, I can also agree to 1/2." The minutes render this as "all the members endorsed" the 50bp move — a conditional accommodation rewritten as enthusiastic agreement. The same mechanism is visible at October 2013, where Lacker's "irresistible attraction to alternative C" but willingness to accept B becomes a mere "a few" favoring tighter policy.

**Framework and process dissent is the most thoroughly erased category.** The June 2012 meeting is the extreme case: Kocherlakota's challenge to the Committee's policy loss function, Tarullo's invocation of his January dissent, Fisher and Dudley's interpersonal exchange, and Duke's explicit statement of reluctant support — all absent from the minutes. Only one formal dissent survives.

**Discussion dissent vs. vote dissent.** The November 2018 meeting illustrates a structural feature: when the formal vote is unanimous (a hold), Alt-A preferences expressed in discussion do not register as dissent even when two participants named themselves Alt-A supporters. The unanimity of the vote absorbs the substance of the discussion-level opposition.

Together, these cases show that the minutes instrument systematically underrepresents dissent intensity by at least 18–20 points on the 0–100 scale, and that this compression is not uniform — it operates most aggressively when dissent takes the form of magnitude preferences, framework challenges, reluctant acceptances, or discussion-only policy preferences that do not cross the vote-level dissent threshold.
