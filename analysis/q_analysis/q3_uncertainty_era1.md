## Synthesis: Uncertainty Compression in the 1993–2004 Era (~300 words)

The 1993–2004 era exhibits a consistent but moderate compression of expressed uncertainty, with UNCERTAINTY_EXPRESSED gaps typically running -10 to -15 points in the most extreme meetings. Several patterns repeat across the decade.

**First, individual speaker heterogeneity disappears.** The transcript's uncertainty is distributed across named participants who express different intensities, different objects of uncertainty, and sometimes different paradigmatic frameworks. Rivlin is "stuck between paradigms." Kohn has an "extremely wide confidence interval." Minehan says she has no answer. Boehne says uncertainty is increasing. The minutes synthesize these into a collective we, applying standard hedging language ("remained uncertain," "subject to risks") that conveys a uniform, lower-intensity epistemic state. The authentic disagreement among participants about how uncertain they are — itself a form of second-order uncertainty — is invisible in minutes.

**Second, model uncertainty is the dominant loss category.** When participants in this era expressed doubt about whether their frameworks even applied — Phillips curve breakdown in 1997, ZLB mechanics in 2003, forecasting at turning points in 2000 — these admissions did not survive into minutes. The minutes preserved object-level uncertainty (we are not sure if inflation will rise) while filtering out meta-level uncertainty (our models may not apply). Meta-uncertainty signals are the most compressed category in this era.

**Third, personal hedges are systematically depersonalized.** First-person formulations ("I don't have a clear answer," "I continue to be uncertain," "I'm more uncertain than usual") are replaced by passive constructions or nominal phrases. This is partly a stylistic convention of the minutes format but has a measurable effect: named admissions of ignorance appear as scored uncertainty; their nominalized replacements do not.

**Fourth, in the 2004 baseline meeting, no compression occurs** (UE gap = 0). This exception is instructive: when the uncertainty is well-defined, narrowly scoped (inflation pass-through mechanics), and resolved by data-dependence language in both formats, the minutes captures it accurately. Compression is primarily a property of diffuse or paradigm-level uncertainty, not routine forecast error.

The 1993–2004 era baseline thus shows the compression mechanism operating primarily through de-personalization and meta-uncertainty filtering, at a moderate (-10 to -15) intensity level. The later escalation to -9.4 mean in 2013–2019 likely reflects not a different mechanism but a higher volume of inputs flowing through the same editorial filter.
