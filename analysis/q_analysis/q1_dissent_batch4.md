## Synthesis (~300 words)

The ten meetings in this batch (DI gaps of −17 to −18) confirm and sharpen five compression mechanisms identified in earlier batches, while adding two structural features not previously prominent.

**The "all agreed" erasure.** Across multiple meetings — 2004, 1996-11, 2003 — the minutes record unanimous outcomes using formulas such as "all the members agreed that they could accept" or "all the members indicated that they could support," which technically describe the final vote but erase the prior deliberative reality in which three to six members preferred a different action. The phrase "could accept" (2004) is the most revealing: it is the minutes' weakest consensus formulation, but even this signal is easily overlooked amid the surrounding unanimity language.

**Six-to-one quantifier collapse.** The 1996-08-20 and 2008-04-30 meetings are the starkest cases. At 1996-08-20, six named participants openly advocated tightening; the minutes records one dissent (Stern). At 2008-04-30, six named Alt C advocates appear in the transcript; the minutes produces "most members" for the majority and "some participants" for the minority, with only Fisher and Plosser's formal dissents explicitly noted. The majority of the dissenting coalition becomes statistically invisible.

**Two-sided compression.** The 2010-06-23 meeting demonstrates that compression is not direction-specific. Hoenig's hawkish dissent and Rosengren/Dudley's dovish lean toward Alt A are both compressed: only "one member" (Hoenig) survives, and the dovish flank disappears entirely. This shows that the mechanism preserves the institutional outcome but flattens the full deliberative space, regardless of direction.

**Credibility critiques excised.** The 2015-09-17 meeting introduces a qualitatively distinct observation: Bullard's explicit "Yellen put" critique — a pointed claim about the Committee's institutional credibility — is entirely absent from the minutes. This suggests a higher suppression threshold for language that challenges the Committee's self-presentation rather than merely its policy choice.

**Procedural debates as a special target.** Across 2003, 2004, and 2010-01-27, extensive intra-meeting debates about statement wording and language are almost entirely erased. These debates engaged four to ten participants each and in several cases represented the most substantive disagreement at the meeting. Their absence from the minutes creates the impression that communication choices were straightforward when they were contested.

Taken together, this batch establishes that a DI gap of −17 to −18 points corresponds systematically to one of the following suppressed transcript features: (a) a hidden coalition of three or more named tightening/easing advocates who voted with the majority, (b) a formal dissent that was actually backed by five or more sympathizers whose preferences are invisible in the minutes, or (c) a broad procedural language debate that occupied much of the policy discussion but left no trace in the published record.
