## 300-Word Synthesis

**Pattern: Five Mechanisms of FSE Compression**

Across these ten meetings spanning 1997–2019, the compression of Financial Stability Emphasis from transcript to minutes follows five recurring mechanisms that operate simultaneously rather than interchangeably.

**Mechanism 1: Named-institution and named-instrument erasure.** Transcripts identify specific entities (Lehman Brothers, Bear Stearns, Federal Farm Credit Banks, specific option-ARM/subprime products, CCC-rated bonds at 8%) that function as anchors for risk assessment. Minutes either omit these references entirely or elevate them to generic category language ("some financial institutions," "certain mortgage products"). The precision loss is systematic and consistent.

**Mechanism 2: Minimization quantifiers.** Transcripts record six to ten participants substantively engaging with financial stability concerns; minutes render this as "a few participants," "some participants noted," or "several others expressed concern." The collapse from near-universal to marginal is the most pervasive compression mode across all ten meetings.

**Mechanism 3: Insertion of reassurance language.** Minutes do not merely omit — they actively introduce offsetting language absent from transcripts. The 2001-10-02 minutes insert "a sound banking system" as "source of underlying strength" directly against the transcript's financial tightening analysis. The 2007-08-07 minutes add "mortgage loans remained readily available to most potential borrowers" as a deliberate counterpoint to the transcript's credit-frozen framing.

**Mechanism 4: Mechanism-stripping.** Transcripts preserve the causal pathways: how CDO illiquidity transmits to ABCP conduits, how BBB downgrades cascade, how leverage dynamics interact with VAR constraints. Minutes preserve only conclusions ("financial stability risks") while removing the analytical scaffolding that made the concern credible.

**Mechanism 5: Foreshadowing elimination.** Six of ten meetings contain crisis-specific advance warnings subsequently vindicated by events (2005 ARM/subprime, 2007 CDO/ABCP, 2008 Lehman, 2010 Greek contagion, 2013 Treasury fragility, 2019 corporate debt cascade). In no case does the minutes' FSE language adequately convey the specificity of the concern recorded in the transcript. The public record systematically understates forward-looking risk assessment at precisely the meetings where such assessment was most granular.
