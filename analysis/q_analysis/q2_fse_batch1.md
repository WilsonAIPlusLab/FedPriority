## Synthesis (300 words)

Across these ten meetings, financial stability emphasis is compressed from transcripts to minutes through four distinct mechanisms operating in combination.

**Mechanism 1: Dedicated session erasure.** Six of the ten meetings (2020-07-29, 2016-04-27, 2019-05-01, 2018-11-08, 2013-09-18, 2019-07-31) featured structured dedicated financial stability go-rounds with multiple participants giving extended prepared remarks. In each case, the minutes reduced this multi-speaker session to a single paragraph beginning with hedging language ("Among those participants who commented…"), losing both the breadth of engagement and the urgency conveyed by concurrent, reinforcing participant voices.

**Mechanism 2: Quantitative specificity stripped.** Participants in transcripts routinely anchor their financial stability concerns to specific numbers: Rosengren's $2T leveraged loan comparison to 2007 subprime, Mester's debt-to-EBITDA ratios worse than the 2007 and 2014 cycle peaks, Kashkari's $1.235T banking recapitalization scenario. The minutes systematically replace these quantitative benchmarks with qualitative characterizations ("high level of indebtedness," "elevated valuations"), substantially reducing the alarm signal.

**Mechanism 3: Causal framing replaced by enumeration.** Speakers like Gramlich (2002), Brainard (multiple meetings), and McDonough (2002) develop explicit causal chains — how shock X propagates through channel Y to produce outcome Z. The minutes reduce these causal arguments to lists of concerns, eliminating the systemic-risk logic that made the original arguments compelling.

**Mechanism 4: Minority voices discarded.** In six of ten cases, the participant whose concern was most alarming (Minehan 1996, McDonough 2002, Greenspan 1995, Kashkari 2020) had their specific framing entirely absent from the minutes. The minutes practice of synthesizing to a center-of-gravity selectively excludes tail-risk voices.

The foreshadowing pattern is striking: eight of ten meetings have a YES or partial YES flag. In nearly every case, the financial stability concerns cut from the minutes proved prescient within two to five years. This systematic excision creates a public record that systematically understates the internal concern level at each of these moments.
