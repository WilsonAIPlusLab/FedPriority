## Synthesis: Dominant Compression Mechanisms (~300 words)

Across the ten meetings in this batch, five recurring mechanisms account for the systematic compression of dissent from transcript to minutes.

**Mechanism 1: Multi-dissent quantifier collapse.** The most consistent pattern is the reduction of multiple named dissenters to a single anonymous quantity word. In 2016-07-27, three participants explicitly said "I support alternative C"; the minutes record "one member preferred to raise the target range." In 2019-07-31, five named Alt-C supporters and one named partial dissent become "several participants favored maintaining" and "a couple indicated they would have preferred 50bp." This is not inaccuracy — the outcomes are correct — but the size signal is lost: three becomes one, five becomes several.

**Mechanism 2: Consensus-washing of contested process.** In 2007-08-07 and 2007-09-18, the minutes use outcome-consensus verbs ("the Committee agreed," "all members agreed") where the transcript records sharp multi-participant debate over statement language. The negotiation — which is itself evidence of disagreement — becomes invisible when only the agreed-upon output is reported. This is structural: minutes record decisions, not deliberations, so contested wording processes never appear.

**Mechanism 3: Vote/discussion gap absorption.** The minutes consistently record only formal voting dissents and omit stated discussion-phase preferences that were overridden. In 2010-04-28, Hoenig, Plosser, and Lacker each state hawkish preferences in discussion but only Hoenig's dissent survives. In 2009-06-24, Plosser's radically different terminal-rate vision appears nowhere. The minutes treat "voted for" as equivalent to "supported without reservation."

**Mechanism 4: Framework-level disagreement erasure.** Several meetings contain participants challenging the Committee's analytical framework — Bullard's Taylor-rule critique (2018-09-26), Evans's "bimodal beliefs" (2009-06-24), Tarullo's "disconnect" observation (2009-06-24). These are not captured as minority views because they do not produce a specific alternative vote. The minutes vocabulary of "some participants," "a few," and "one member" only maps onto policy-position disagreements, not epistemological ones.

**Mechanism 5: Reframing dissent as reluctant acceptance.** The 2000-12 and 2000-11 meetings illustrate how minutes transform "I prefer X" (active dissent) into "could accept a delay" or "indicated it was a close call" (passive acquiescence). The dissenter's expressed preference is replaced by their tolerance of the majority outcome, shifting the register from disagreement to accommodation.

Taken together, these mechanisms do not individually distort the record of *what was decided* but collectively suppress the record of *how contentious the decision was* and *how many distinct views were expressed*. The resulting minutes present a Committee that appears more unified than the deliberative record warrants.
