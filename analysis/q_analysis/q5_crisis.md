# Q5: Crisis-Period Editorial Compression Analysis

**Question**: Does the transcript-to-minutes editorial process operate differently during crisis periods? Does compression intensity, consensus manufacturing, urgency dampening, and vocabulary filtering vary by economic stress level?

**Sample**: 12 crisis meeting comparison documents across four distinct crisis episodes (LTCM/Russia 1998, Dot-com/9-11 2001, Global Financial Crisis 2007–2009, COVID-19 2020).

---

## Part 1: Per-Meeting Assessment

---

### 1998-09-29 (LTCM)

**DISSENT COMPRESSION**: MILD. Transcript scores dissent at 27; minutes at 22. Gap = 5. The transcript records McTeer's push for 50bp and the near-even directive split more vividly; minutes uses "small majority favored asymmetry" to aggregate.

**FSE COMPRESSION**: MILD. FSE gap = 3 (transcript 78, minutes 75). LTCM is explicitly discussed in both documents. McDonough's detailed LTCM presentation preserved in minutes summary. Both sources agree on "fulcrum" characterization of systemic risk.

**UE COMPRESSION**: NONE. Minutes actually scores 3 points *higher* (65 vs. 62), with language "impossible to predict" and "abnormally high volatility" exceeding any single transcript voice in intensity. No compression — the minutes amplifies uncertainty.

**CONSENSUS MANUFACTURING**: YES_MILD. McTeer's 50bp preference and the near-even directive split are collapsed into "small majority favored asymmetry." The 11-0 vote headline conceals real tactical divergence.

**URGENCY DAMPENING**: MILD. Transcript captures Greenspan's "turn... in the psychology of anticipatory evaluations" and McDonough's LTCM near-collapse narrative at length; minutes presents the same as structured summary paragraphs. Vivid individual framings ("very abnormal state of affairs," "drying up of liquidity in all markets") become "spreads of private rates over Treasury rates had gapped higher."

**SPECIFIC VOCABULARY FILTERED**: "Very abnormal state of affairs" (Poole); "well beyond a return to normalcy" (McDonough); "significant drying up of liquidity in all markets" (Minehan); "fear factor is pervasive" (Jordan); "fat tail" (Meyer — retained as "downside tail" language in substance but verbal idiom lost).

**PUBLIC REASSURANCE ADDITIONS**: YES. Minutes adds: "members did not believe that the tightness in credit markets were likely to lead to a 'credit crunch,' though some members expressed the view that such an outcome could not be ruled out." This hedged reassurance does not appear as a collective statement in the transcript — it reflects the minority hedge being elevated to institutional positioning.

### 1998-11-17 (Post-LTCM)

**DISSENT COMPRESSION**: MODERATE. Transcript dissent score = 44; minutes = 35. Gap = 9. Transcript records Broaddus, Hoenig, Poole, and Minehan all expressing substantial reluctance as non-dissenters; minutes records one formal dissent (Jordan) and "several members" with preferences for waiting. The depth of near-dissent is substantially compacted.

**FSE COMPRESSION**: MILD. Gap = 3 (transcript 65, minutes 62). Both documents give financial stability prominence. Hoenig's bubble concern and Kelley's crash warning are absorbed into "continuing fragility of financial markets."

**UE COMPRESSION**: NONE. Identical scores (55/55). Both documents treat uncertainty as a co-equal theme.

**CONSENSUS MANUFACTURING**: YES_STRONG. This is the meeting where Minehan said "I am nervous about it," Poole expressed "considerable reluctance" and feared "pouring gasoline onto this economy," and Hoenig preferred to wait — none of which appears in the minutes. The minutes creates a picture of "nearly all members" comfortably endorsing the action, while the transcript reveals a committee sharply divided, with the majority barely carrying the dissenters along.

**URGENCY DAMPENING**: MILD. Greenspan's "50/50 on Brazil" and Kelley's "could burgeon much more quickly, perhaps severely and without much warning" are absorbed into "the potential extent of such weakness was subject to considerable uncertainty."

**SPECIFIC VOCABULARY FILTERED**: "Pouring gasoline rather than water onto this economy" (Poole); "skating on thin ice" (Prell — staff, not participant); "50/50" on Brazilian default (Greenspan); "bubble economy syndrome" (Hoenig — partially retained in spirit but not literally); "without much warning" (Kelley).

**PUBLIC REASSURANCE ADDITIONS**: YES_MILD. The minutes frames the easing as a completion of the adjustment cycle ("further easing was not likely to be needed over the months ahead unless...") — a reassuring terminal framing not explicitly constructed in the transcript, where the picture was more open-ended and anxious.

### 2001-01-31 (Recession)

**DISSENT COMPRESSION**: NONE. Near-identical scores (12 vs. 10). Meeting was genuinely near-unanimous. Both documents capture the uniform enthusiasm for the 50bp cut. Negligible compression.

**FSE COMPRESSION**: MILD. Gap = 6 (transcript 38, minutes 32). Ferguson's detailed commercial paper analysis and Greenspan's Baa-Aaa spread references are compacted into brief mentions; the minutes adds "sound condition of the banking system" as a counterweight not present in the transcript's FSE framing.

**UE COMPRESSION**: NONE. Near-identical (58/57). Both documents use near-identical uncertainty language about the depth and duration of the slowdown.

**CONSENSUS MANUFACTURING**: NO. Meeting was genuinely consensual — the transcript itself is near-unanimous. No manufactured consensus needed.

**URGENCY DAMPENING**: MILD. Greenspan's "we have to move faster, sooner, quicker" and McTeer's "early and often" framing translate to more measured "front-loaded easing" language in the minutes. Jordan's "I have no idea what the national income accounts are going to add up to" disappears. Stern's explicit forecast confidence confession lost.

**SPECIFIC VOCABULARY FILTERED**: "Move faster, sooner, quicker" (Greenspan); "early and often" (McTeer); "business has fallen through the floor and is still falling" (Jordan anecdote); "zero or close to it" on Q1 GDP (Minehan).

**PUBLIC REASSURANCE ADDITIONS**: YES_MILD. The minutes adds "substantial underlying strength and resilience" of the economy as a counterpoint, which appears in the transcript only as a brief Minehan qualifier, not as a collective institutional framing.

### 2001-10-02 (Post-9/11)

**DISSENT COMPRESSION**: MODERATE. Gap = 10 (transcript 20, minutes 10). McTeer's "something less would be sufficient" reservation and Guynn's concern about reversibility both disappear in minutes' "all the members" formulation. The largest consistent pattern of the pre-2009 format.

**FSE COMPRESSION**: SEVERE. This is the single largest gap in the entire 12-meeting sample. Transcript FSE = 58, minutes FSE = 32. Gap = 26 points. Ferguson devotes substantial analysis to $1 trillion equity wealth loss, spread widening, below-investment-grade credit market dysfunction; Minehan, Gramlich, and Greenspan each devote extended remarks to financial stability as co-equal policy rationale. The minutes reduces this to brief contextual notation plus the reassuring phrase "a sound banking system." The compression ratio here is extraordinary.

**UE COMPRESSION**: MILD. Gap = 5 (transcript 80, minutes 75). The most vivid formulations — "order of magnitude above normal" (Meyer); "our knowledge is now obsolete" (McTeer); "I have no idea what the national income accounts will add up to" (Jordan) — do not survive in the minutes' "unusually wide range of uncertainty" phrasing.

**CONSENSUS MANUFACTURING**: YES_MILD. McTeer's and Guynn's reservations are elided. The unanimity is real in terms of voting; the minutes overstates its philosophical texture.

**URGENCY DAMPENING**: MODERATE. Several acute post-9/11 expressions disappear: Poole's "airlines literally within a few weeks of running out of cash" (a specific solvency claim); Duke's "banks have stopped doing business with one or more counterparties" type language; the general atmosphere of real-time shock and disorientation in the transcript is replaced by a structured retrospective account.

**SPECIFIC VOCABULARY FILTERED**: "Knowledge is now obsolete" (McTeer); "literally within a few weeks of running out of cash" (Poole on airlines); "order of magnitude above normal" on uncertainty (Meyer); "free fall" in spending; specific "$1 trillion equity loss" figure (Ferguson).

**PUBLIC REASSURANCE ADDITIONS**: YES_STRONG. "A sound banking system" as a countervailing source of "underlying strength" — this is structurally inserted by the minutes as a stabilizing counterpoint to what in the transcript was a dominant financial instability narrative.

### 2007-09-18 (First Cut)

**DISSENT COMPRESSION**: SEVERE. Gap = 22 (transcript 32, minutes 10). Fisher's stated preference for 25bp, Lacker's mild preference for 25bp, and the lengthy Plosser-Kohn debate about statement language are all collapsed into "all members favored," "all members agreed" with "unanimity on action" and no reservations noted. The transcript reveals meaningful internal disagreement that the minutes entirely suppresses.

**FSE COMPRESSION**: MODERATE. Gap = 15 (transcript 90, minutes 75). Warsh's multi-page phased analysis of financial distress stages, Kroszner's 1998-vs-2007 comparison, and Rosengren's ABCP quarter-end concern are compacted significantly. The minutes retains financial stability as a prominent theme but at a materially lower intensity level.

**UE COMPRESSION**: NONE. Minutes actually scores *higher* (75 vs. 72). The minutes' explicit refusal to provide a balance-of-risks assessment — framed as an acknowledgement that doing so "could give the mistaken impression that the Committee was more certain about the economic outlook than was in fact the case" — is a stronger uncertainty signal than any individual transcript expression.

**CONSENSUS MANUFACTURING**: YES_STRONG. "All members favored," "all members agreed" — language of complete uniformity — when the transcript shows Fisher firmly at 25bp, Lacker mildly at 25bp, and a substantive statement-language debate. The manufactured unanimity is most aggressive here relative to the actual transcript divergence.

**URGENCY DAMPENING**: MODERATE. Lacker's "any projections of real effects from events in financial markets seem like guesswork at this point" disappears. Pianalto's "forecast models are not well equipped to accommodate" the shock disappears. Rosengren's specific ABCP quarter-end warning — "many financial market participants are very concerned about the next two weeks" — becomes generic "strains."

**SPECIFIC VOCABULARY FILTERED**: "Guesswork" (Lacker on forecasting); "vicious spiral of foreclosures" (Yellen); "next two weeks" (Rosengren's temporal urgency); specific ABCP quarter-end risk mechanism; "free one-way bet on the dollar" (Warsh on dollar weakness); Warsh's three-phase financial distress framework (stripped to one-paragraph summary).

**PUBLIC REASSURANCE ADDITIONS**: YES_MILD. "Conditions seemed to have improved somewhat in the most recent days" — a reassuring footnote added to the minutes' financial conditions discussion, pulling the reader from crisis toward stabilization, not matched in the transcript's more uniformly alarmed tone.

### 2008-03-18 (Bear Stearns)

**DISSENT COMPRESSION**: SEVERE. Gap = 25 (transcript 65, minutes 40). Two formal voting dissents (Hoenig, Fisher) plus Lacker's and Plosser's explicit preference for ending/slowing cuts are present in the transcript. The minutes acknowledges the inflation-vs-growth tension but buries the individual dissenting voices in "some participants" language and does not name dissenters in the discussion sections. The minutes' "Most members judged" framing obscures that two members voted no.

**FSE COMPRESSION**: MODERATE. Gap = 10 (transcript 90, minutes 80). Mishkin's "worst financial crisis since WWII" — a landmark characterization — is not replicated in the minutes with equivalent specificity. Warsh's extended monologue on counterparty risk, investment bank viability, and sovereign wealth fund dynamics is compacted significantly. The minutes retains financial stability as dominant but at a lower absolute intensity.

**UE COMPRESSION**: MODERATE. Gap = 8 (transcript 80, minutes 72). "Incredibly unpleasant tradeoff" (Mishkin); "we don't know how financial markets will evolve" (Kohn); "increasingly difficult to separate liquidity from solvency" (Warsh) — the vivid individual formulations of a committee genuinely lost in unfamiliar terrain are replaced by generic "considerable uncertainty" language.

**CONSENSUS MANUFACTURING**: YES_STRONG. Two formal voting dissents exist. The minutes' discussion section does not clearly signal the magnitude of dissent — references to "most members" and "some believed" obscure that 2 of ~12 voting members formally opposed. The word "dissent" appears nowhere in the discussion section, only implicitly in the formal vote record.

**URGENCY DAMPENING**: SEVERE. "Worst financial crisis in the post-WWII era" (Mishkin) not replicated. "Old-fashioned bank run" (staff, on Bear Stearns) not in minutes discussion. "Dollar at post-Bretton Woods low" (staff transcript) softened to "declined notably." "Contagion effects" of Bear's collapse on other broker-dealers present in staff transcript but compressed in minutes.

**SPECIFIC VOCABULARY FILTERED**: "Worst financial crisis since WWII" (Mishkin); "old-fashioned bank run" (Bear Stearns characterization); "post-Bretton Woods low" (dollar); "free one-way bet on the dollar" (Warsh); "brave new world" (Mishkin on expanding safety net); "contagion" as applied to Bear's collapse; dollar concern discussion (transcript 18, minutes: not scored).

**PUBLIC REASSURANCE ADDITIONS**: YES_MILD. "Most participants still expected inflation to moderate later this year" — a forward-looking moderating statement that functions to anchor expectations and prevent the meeting minutes from reading as a full panic document.

### 2008-09-16 (Lehman Week)

**DISSENT COMPRESSION**: MODERATE. Gap = 10 (transcript 45, minutes 35). Note: minutes truncated mid-sentence — low confidence on minutes scoring. The transcript's hawks (Hoenig, Plosser preferring rate hikes; Evans and Stern preferring cuts) are absorbed into "participants agreed" on growth weakness without preserving the sharp ideological divergence.

**FSE COMPRESSION**: MINIMAL. Gap = 2 (transcript 92, minutes 90). This is the one meeting where the FSE compression is nearly absent. The sheer magnitude of the Lehman event — announced the previous day — made substantive compression impossible. Both documents acknowledge near-systemic conditions with nearly identical intensity. This is the "floor effect" of crisis severity.

**UE COMPRESSION**: MILD. Gap = 4 (transcript 82, minutes 78). "Decidedly confused and very muddled" (Bernanke) does not appear in minutes. "Downside risks almost too dispersed to characterize" (Evans) softens to "highly uncertain." "Oil price: $100 plus or minus $50" (industry CEO anecdote, cited by Kroszner) not in minutes.

**CONSENSUS MANUFACTURING**: YES_MILD. The internal hawkish-dovish split (Plosser "will need to raise rates"; Yellen "very concerned about downside risks") is dissolved into a general "participants agreed" on growth weakness. The hold at 2% looks unanimous when in fact it was achieved by hawks and doves reaching it from opposite directions.

**URGENCY DAMPENING**: MINIMAL. Given Lehman filing the previous day, the minutes cannot reduce urgency substantially. "Severely strained," "bankruptcy of Lehman Brothers," and "increasing difficulty in obtaining funding" retain the acute language. This is another floor effect.

**SPECIFIC VOCABULARY FILTERED**: "Decidedly confused and very muddled" (Bernanke self-description); "mother of all credit crunches" potential (Evans); specific CDS spread movements (+190, +340, +690 basis points for named institutions, per Warsh); "no more capital available in external markets — not common stock, not preferred, not anything" (Duke's full-market shutdown characterization).

**PUBLIC REASSURANCE ADDITIONS**: YES_MILD. "The announcement of government support for Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac appeared to have had a positive impact on financial markets" — a moderating addendum noting a positive development. Also: "Most continued to expect a gradual recovery in 2009" — optimistic baseline inserted amid the acute crisis description.

### 2008-10-29 (Peak Crisis)

**DISSENT COMPRESSION**: MODERATE. Gap = 17 (transcript 42, minutes 25). Hoenig explicitly said "I would just stay where we are"; Plosser said "my preference at this meeting would be to stand pat" and named his non-dissent choice explicitly; Bullard said "my preference based on this would be to leave rates alone." The minutes records "some expected an improving financial situation" and minor disagreements on timing of recovery — not the explicit preferences for no-cut that the transcript contains.

**FSE COMPRESSION**: MINIMAL. Gap = 2 (transcript 90, minutes 88). At peak crisis intensity, the minutes retains near-identical FSE emphasis. The "negative spiral" and "adverse feedback loop" language in the minutes matches the transcript's tone. Floor effect again.

**UE COMPRESSION**: MINIMAL. Gap = 3 (transcript 78, minutes 75). Both documents use "uncharted waters" language (Evans directly in transcript; absorbed into "uncertainty" framing in minutes). The compression of individual vivid formulations is present but the aggregate score barely moves.

**CONSENSUS MANUFACTURING**: YES_MODERATE. The three-participant no-cut preference group (Hoenig, Plosser, Bullard) is not reflected in the minutes' "broad agreement on the diagnosis." The minutes creates an impression of general consensus when approximately 25% of participants preferred not cutting.

**URGENCY DAMPENING**: MILD. "Baa corporate bonds up 200 basis points, low-grade up a staggering 700 basis points" (Yellen's specific numbers) become "risk aversion of investors increased, driving credit spreads sharply higher." Warsh's "confidence... in basic rules of doing business across financial markets, has been lost" — a fundamental diagnosis — disappears. Duke's "all the banks I talked to reported having stopped doing business with one or more counterparties" does not survive literally.

**SPECIFIC VOCABULARY FILTERED**: "Staggering 700 basis points" (Yellen on high-yield); "confidence in basic rules of doing business has been lost" (Warsh); "credit intermediation process is fundamentally broken" (Warsh); specific institutional failure names (AIG, Wachovia, Washington Mutual) present in staff minutes but not discussion minutes; "sailing in uncharted waters" (Evans, direct quote); "unpleasantly fat tail" (Kohn, not literal though similar substance retained).

**PUBLIC REASSURANCE ADDITIONS**: YES_MILD. "Global efforts to recapitalize banks and guarantee deposits had helped stabilize the situation" — a constructive framing absent from the transcript's more uniformly bleak tone.

### 2008-12-16 (ZLB)

**DISSENT COMPRESSION**: MODERATE. Gap = 13 (transcript 48, minutes 35). Fisher explicitly threatened to be "a minority of one" and initially could not support Alternative B, changing vote post-lunch. Plosser expressed "deep concerns" and proposed balance sheet cap. Lacker raised governance concerns. These appear in the minutes only as tactical debates about "communication" and "quantitative targets" — the personal intensity of the dissatisfaction is compacted.

**FSE COMPRESSION**: MODERATE. Gap = 7 (transcript 92, minutes 85). The ZLB transition meeting is a financial stability meeting at its core — the entire TALF design discussion, on/off-the-run Treasury spread concerns (Kohn: "at record levels"), and broken securitization analysis (Rosengren: "loss of the securitization market is really important") are compacted significantly. Both documents score this as dominant, but the transcript's absolute peak of 92 (the meeting where QE1 is launched) retreats to 85 in the minutes.

**UE COMPRESSION**: MINIMAL. Near-identical (80/78). Both documents document the unprecedented policy uncertainty with comparable force.

**CONSENSUS MANUFACTURING**: YES_MODERATE. Fisher's intra-meeting opposition and eventual mid-lunch conversion is a major dramatic event in the transcript. The minutes records tactical disagreements about format, not the near-collapse of consensus that the transcript reveals. The unanimous vote headline obliterates the drama.

**URGENCY DAMPENING**: MILD. The urgency of the ZLB transition is actually reinforced in both documents. The compression occurs in personal expression: Bernanke's "twice a century, or once a century so far" (on deep recession + financial crisis co-occurrence); Yellen's "the forecast is grim" as a personal statement; Rosengren's call for "maximum monetary policy stimulus as quickly as possible."

**SPECIFIC VOCABULARY FILTERED**: "A minority of one" (Fisher on his own dissent); "skating on thin ice" (Prell, November 1998 — not this meeting); "go as low as we can as fast as we can" (Yellen); "twice a century" (Bernanke on depth of crisis); "record levels" on on/off-the-run Treasury spreads (Kohn); "loss of the securitization market is really important" (Rosengren's TALF justification framing).

**PUBLIC REASSURANCE ADDITIONS**: YES_MILD. "Improvements were evident in some markets, particularly those for highly rated commercial paper and for interbank funds" — a constructive note the minutes inserts into an otherwise uniformly bleak picture. Not present with equivalent prominence in transcript.

### 2009-03-18 (QE1)

**DISSENT COMPRESSION**: MODERATE. Gap = 7 (transcript 42, minutes 35). Three participants firmly opposed Treasury purchases (Warsh: "quite uncomfortable"; Fisher: "I don't think it's advisable"; Hoenig: "prefer mortgage-backeds"). This composition disagreement is reduced to "one member preferred to focus on longer-term Treasury securities, whereas another member preferred to focus on agency MBS" — a characterization that strips the firmness of the opposition.

**FSE COMPRESSION**: NONE. Both documents score FSE at 78. At this depth of crisis, the minutes preserves the financial stability emphasis at full force. Duke's banking system analysis, Kohn's "perhaps the most troubling developments have been in the financial sector" — all reflected in the minutes at comparable intensity.

**UE COMPRESSION**: MODERATE. Gap = 9 (transcript 87, minutes 78). "Navigating in waters where there's not a particularly good map" (Tarullo); "the private sector is throwing out all of the old data" (Bullard); "we as a group don't have a clear view" (Lacker) — these individual confessions of framework breakdown disappear. The minutes retains high uncertainty but without the personal dimension of epistemic crisis.

**CONSENSUS MANUFACTURING**: YES_MILD. Three-participant Treasury-purchase opposition compacted. The minutes' "members expressed a range of views as to the preferred size" understates the degree of substantive disagreement among a significant minority.

**URGENCY DAMPENING**: MILD. Yellen's "the economy is just a disaster area. The economic outlook is utterly dreadful" — among the most vivid crisis characterizations in the entire dataset — disappears. The minutes equivalent is "nearly all meeting participants said that conditions had deteriorated relative to their expectations." The substance is preserved; the visceral force is not.

**SPECIFIC VOCABULARY FILTERED**: "Disaster area" and "utterly dreadful" (Yellen); "deflationary trap... you get stuck there, and you cannot get out" (Bullard); "private sector is throwing out all of the old data" (Bullard); "rules coming from Washington change every other day" (Mosser staff quote — staff not member); "Bank CDS spreads now significantly wider than they were... following the Lehman bankruptcy. This is truly extraordinary" (Mosser staff characterization).

**PUBLIC REASSURANCE ADDITIONS**: YES_MILD. "Some believed that the natural resilience of market forces would become evident later this year" — a recovery-optimist framing inserted alongside the dominant pessimism, moderating the minutes' tone from utterly bleak to bleak-with-minority-optimism.

### 2020-03-15 (COVID Emergency)

**DISSENT COMPRESSION**: MODERATE. Gap = 10 (transcript 42, minutes 32). Mester firmly preferred 50bp, citing real concerns about "using up interest rate policy space." Bostic tilted toward 50bp. Quarles voiced reservations. In minutes: "a few participants preferred a 50 basis point cut" with reasoning compacted to "preserve ammunition" — stripping the firmer personal conviction shown in the transcript.

**FSE COMPRESSION**: MILD. Gap = 4 (transcript 92, minutes 88). Financial stability remains near-dominant in both. The specific vivid characterizations (Powell: "famously liquid market for U.S. Treasury securities is experiencing high abnormal conditions of severe illiquidity"; Williams: "largest-ever daily amount of Treasury purchases, by three times, spanning six operations and taking five hours to complete") are absorbed into "extraordinary turbulence and stresses." The minutes retains the crisis label but loses the operational specificity.

**UE COMPRESSION**: MINIMAL. Gap = 3 (transcript 93, minutes 90). The "Knightian uncertainty" language appears explicitly in both the transcript (multiple speakers) and — by paraphrase — in the minutes' "profoundly uncertain." Remarkably tight preservation of uncertainty intensity.

**CONSENSUS MANUFACTURING**: YES_MILD. The four-participant 50bp preference group (Mester firmly, Bostic tilting, Quarles with reservations, Kaplan entering with 50bp preference) is reduced to "a few participants." The transcript reveals a more divided committee than the minutes conveys.

**URGENCY DAMPENING**: MILD. The operational reality language — Powell's "famously liquid market experiencing severe illiquidity" and the desk's six-operation, five-hour, failed-to-restore-market-functioning account — is flattened to "extraordinary turbulence." The real-time Sunday-afternoon emergency meeting atmosphere is not recoverable from the minutes alone.

**SPECIFIC VOCABULARY FILTERED**: "Knightian uncertainty" (multiple participants — partially preserved as paraphrase); "famously liquid market for U.S. Treasury securities" in severe dysfunction (Powell); five-hour/six-operations operational failure (Logan staff); "10 percent GDP decline at annual rate in Q2" (Bullard specific forecast); "60 to 70 percent sales decline across the nation" (Mester retail anecdote); speed of deterioration — "unprecedented" used by all in transcript, moderated in minutes.

**PUBLIC REASSURANCE ADDITIONS**: YES_MILD. "Several participants emphasized that the temporary nature of the shock...implied that the current situation was not directly comparable with the previous decade's financial crisis" — a deliberate reassurance insertion distinguishing COVID from GFC, more prominent in the minutes than in the transcript discussion, where most participants were focused on the acute crisis rather than reassurance.

### 2020-04-29 (COVID Lockdown)

**DISSENT COMPRESSION**: MODERATE. Gap = 10 (transcript 22, minutes 12). This meeting is relatively consensual — zero formal dissents — but the transcript reveals a meaningful range between Bullard's relative optimism (analogizes to natural disaster recovery) and Kashkari's 12–18 month pessimism. The minutes' "all participants judged" language overstates consensus.

**FSE COMPRESSION**: SEVERE. Gap = 22 (transcript 82, minutes 60). The formal financial stability go-around — seven participants giving extended structured remarks on banking capital adequacy, nonbank vulnerabilities, CECL provisioning, CRE stress, leveraged loans, money market funds — is compressed to "a number of participants commented on potential risks to financial stability." The minutes score of 60 reflects a "prominent secondary topic" while the transcript score of 82 reflects a "dominant topic." This is the largest FSE gap in the dataset alongside the September 2001 meeting.

**UE COMPRESSION**: MILD. Gap = 5 (transcript 90, minutes 85). Both documents use near-maximal uncertainty language. The "extraordinary amount of uncertainty" phrasing in the minutes is strong. The compression operates on individual colorful expressions: "more uncertain than six weeks ago" (Clarida); "standard models are not equipped to deal with a self-imposed deep recession" (Mester); "I chose to forecast as an average of two scenarios because the modal forecast is too uncertain to use alone" (Rosengren).

**CONSENSUS MANUFACTURING**: NO (weakly). The meeting was genuinely close to consensus. The Bullard-Kashkari spread exists but both support the statement. The minutes' "all participants judged" appropriately describes the actual policy outcome.

**URGENCY DAMPENING**: MILD. The specific anecdotal intensity — Brainard's "retail sales down 8.7%, by far the largest on record; spending down nearly 100% at restaurants and movie theaters"; Harker's credit card data "90% decline in travel" — is replaced by "consumer spending had plummeted across all parts of the country." The quantitative detail that makes the collapse real is stripped.

**SPECIFIC VOCABULARY FILTERED**: "Steepest decline in real economic activity since World War II" (Clarida); "Great Depression levels" of unemployment (Bullard, Brainard, Clarida — all used this phrase); "oil futures prices saw a record-breaking decline into negative territory" (Brainard); "spending down nearly 100 percent at restaurants and movie theaters" (Brainard); "largest one-month decline since World War II" in factory output (Brainard); specific WEI reading of −12% (Williams).

**PUBLIC REASSURANCE ADDITIONS**: YES_MILD. "The banking sector, including the large banks, was resilient coming into this period" — staff minutes; minutes gives this more prominent reassurance positioning than the transcript discussion, where it was one note among many alarm signals. Also: "some pockets of resilience" in spending noted more prominently in minutes.

## Part 3: Synthesis — The FOMC Editorial Process During Crises (~400 words)

The twelve crisis meetings analyzed here reveal a consistent but not uniform editorial logic. The minutes-production process applies compression in proportion to what would be most alarming or divisive in the public record — targeting vocabulary, dissent, and financial stability emphasis far more heavily than uncertainty expressions, economic forecasts, or policy lean.

**The floor effect at peak crisis.** When financial stress reaches extreme levels — the Lehman week (2008-09-16), the October 2008 peak, the ZLB transition — the FSE compression nearly disappears. The minutes cannot sanitize what was publicly known; a Lehman bankruptcy and AIG near-failure require acknowledgment. The compression floor appears to occur around FSE scores of 85+, where the minutes closes to within 2–4 points of the transcript. Below that threshold, the minutes systematically reduces FSE emphasis, most aggressively at moderate-crisis intensity levels (2001-10-02 at 26 points; 2007-09-18 at 15 points; 2020-04-29 at 22 points). The editorial process applies its strongest compression in the zone where financial stability concerns are severe but not so acute as to be externally documented — precisely the zone where editorial framing has maximum public effect.

**Dissent manufacturing is structural, not crisis-specific.** The suppression of internal disagreement — converting "I will not vote for this" into "several members preferred" and replacing individual reluctance with unanimous vote presentation — operates consistently across all crisis periods and escalates with the magnitude of internal division, not with the severity of the crisis. The September 2007 and March 2008 meetings show the largest dissent compression (22 and 25 points respectively), not because they were the most acute crises, but because those were the meetings with the most divided committees.

**Vocabulary selection is systematic and deliberate.** A consistent set of term-classes is filtered: (1) personal confessions of confusion or ignorance ("decidedly confused," "I have no idea," "guesswork"); (2) visceral economic descriptions ("disaster area," "utterly dreadful," "business has fallen through the floor"); (3) named institutional failures or near-failures in participant discussion sections; (4) temporal urgency phrases ("next two weeks," "within weeks of running out of cash"); and (5) historical superlatives that anchor fear ("worst since WWII," "post-Bretton Woods low," "twice a century"). These are replaced by institutionally calibrated but still alarming language — "severely strained," "considerable uncertainty," "extraordinary stress."

**Reassurance insertions are real and consistent.** Every crisis meeting minutes adds at least one mitigating note absent from or marginal in the transcript: a sound banking system, improving market conditions, expected inflation moderation, underlying economic resilience. These insertions do not change the document's overall diagnosis, but they provide a structural counterpoint — a sentence or phrase that media and market readers can anchor to when constructing the "and yet the Fed is not panicking" narrative. The pattern persists across all four crisis episodes and all twelve meetings, suggesting it is not situational but editorial policy.

The net result is a document that conveys crisis while managing the affective register of crisis. The minutes confirms that conditions were severe, that policy responded aggressively, and that the Committee was unified — while suppressing the evidence that participants were sometimes genuinely confused, sharply divided, or alarmed in ways that exceeded their official language.
